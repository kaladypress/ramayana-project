=== Sarga 6 ===
Ayodhyā Prosperity and Righteousness under the Rule of King Daśaratha

---
1.
puryāṁ tasyāmayōdhyāyāṁ vēdavitsarvasaṅgrahaḥ |
dīrghadarśī mahātējāḥ paurajānapadapriyaḥ ||

> **Meaning 1:** In that city of Ayodhyā lived Daśaratha, a knower of the Vedas
> who embraced all people, far-sighted, of great radiance, and beloved by the
> people of the city and the countryside.

---
2.
ikṣvākūṇāmatirathō yajvā dharmaratō vaśī |
maharṣikalpō rājarṣistriṣu lōkēṣu viśrutaḥ ||

> **Meaning 2:** He was an Atiratha among the Ikṣvākus — a supreme chariot warrior and performer of sacred yajñas. Firmly rooted in dharma and master of his passions, he ruled as a king yet lived with the saintly purity of a great maharṣi, renowned across all three worlds.

---
3.
balavānnihatāmitrō mitravānvijitēndriyaḥ |
dhanaiśca sañcayaiścānyaiḥ śakravaiśravaṇōpamaḥ ||

> **Meaning 3:** He was mighty, a destroyer of his enemies, rich in friends and
> allies, and a master of his senses. In wealth and treasure, he was the equal
> of Śakra (Indra) and Vaiśravaṇa (Kubera).

---
4.
yathā manurmahātējā lōkasya parirakṣitā |
tathā daśarathō rājā vasañjagadapālayat ||

> **Meaning 4:** Just as the radiant Manu once protected the world, so King
> Daśaratha protected the world and ruled Ayodhyā.

---
5.
tēna satyābhisandhēna trivargamanutiṣṭhatā |
pālitā sā purī śrēṣṭhēndrēṇa ivāmarāvatī ||

> **Meaning 5:** He was true to his word and followed the trivarga — dharma,
> artha and kāma. Under his care that city was protected like Amarāvatī under
> Indra, the greatest of the devatās.

---
6.
tasminpuravarē hr̥ṣṭā dharmātmanā bahuśrutāḥ |
narāstuṣṭādhanaiḥ svaiḥ svairalubdhāḥ satyavādinaḥ ||

> **Meaning 6:** In that finest of cities, the people were happy and learned.
> They had righteous hearts, were content with their own wealth, free of greed,
> and spoke only the truth.

---
7.
nālpasannicayaḥ kaścidāsīttasminpurōttamē |
kuṭumbī yō hyasiddhārthō'gavāśvadhanadhānyavān ||

> **Meaning 7:** In that excellent city there was no householder with meager
> savings, none whose goals had gone unmet, and none without cows, horses,
> wealth and grain.

---
8.
kāmī vā na kadaryō vā nr̥śaṁsaḥ puruṣaḥ kvacit |
draṣṭuṁ śakyamayōdhyāyāṁ nāvidvānna ca nāstikaḥ ||

> **Meaning 8:** Nowhere in Ayodhyā could one find a man ruled by lust, a miser,
> or a cruel person. There was no one uneducated and no nāstika, a denier of
> dharma and the Vedas.

---
9.
sarvē narāśca nāryaśca dharmaśīlāḥ susaṁyatāḥ |
muditāḥ śīlavr̥ttābhyāṁ maharṣaya ivāmalāḥ ||

> **Meaning 9:** All the men and women were of dharmic character and well
> restrained. They were cheerful, and through their good nature and conduct they
> were as pure as those of maharṣis.

---
10.
nākuṇḍalī nāmukuṭī nāsragvī nālpabhōgavān |
nāmr̥ṣṭō nānuliptāṅgō nāsugandhaśca vidyatē ||

> **Meaning 10:** In Ayodhyā, no one was without earrings, a fine turban, or a flower garland, and no one lived with meager comforts. No one went without their daily bath, without sandalwood paste upon the body, or without sweet fragrance.

---
11.
nāmr̥ṣṭabhōjī nādātā nāpyanaṅgadaniṣkadhr̥k |
nāhastābharaṇō vāpi dr̥śyatē nāpyanātmavān ||

> **Meaning 11:** No one ate poorly or was stingy with gifts, and no one went
> without armlets, a necklace (niṣka), or ornaments on the hands. No one lacked
> self-mastery either.

---
12.
nānāhitāgnirnāyajvā viprō nāpyasahasradaḥ |
kaścidāsīdayōdhyāyāṁ na ca nirvr̥ttasaṅkaraḥ ||

> **Meaning 12:** In Ayodhyā there was no brāhmaṇa who had not established the
> sacred fire (āhitāgni), who did not perform yajñas, or who did not give
> thousands in charity. And no one married outside their own varṇa

---
13.
svakarmaniratā nityaṁ brāhmaṇā vijitēndriyāḥ |
dānādhyayanaśīlāśca saṁyatāśca pratigrahē ||

> **Meaning 13:** The brāhmaṇas were always devoted to their own duties and had
> mastered their senses. They loved giving and study, and were restrained in
> accepting gifts.

---
14.
na nāstikō nānr̥takō na kaścidabahuśrutaḥ |
nāsūyakō na cāśaktō nāvidvānvidyatē tadā ||

> **Meaning 14:** At that time, no one was faithless toward the Vedas, untruthful, unlearned, envious, incapable, or ignorant.

---
15.
nāṣaḍaṅgavid atrāsīn nāvratō nāsahasradaḥ |
na dīnaḥ kṣiptacittō vā vyathitō vāpi kaścana ||

> **Meaning 15:** In that city, there was no one who did not know the six
> Vedāṅgas, none lacking in spiritual discipline, and none who failed to give
> gifts by the thousands. Not a single person was destitute, troubled in mind,
> or afflicted with grief.

---
16.
kaścinnarō vā nārī vā nāśrīmānnāpyarūpavān |
draṣṭuṁ śakyamayōdhyāyāṁ nāpi rājanyabhaktimān ||

> **Meaning 16:** Nowhere in Ayodhyā could any man or woman be seen who was
> lacking in grace and prosperity, devoid of beauty, or lacking devotion to
> the king.

---
17.
varṇēṣvagryacaturthēṣu dēvatātithipūjakāḥ |
kr̥tajñāśca vadānyāśca śūrāḥ vikrama-saṁyutāḥ ||

> **Meaning 17:** Across all four varṇas, beginning with the brāhmaṇas, the
> people worshipped the devatās and honored guests; all were grateful, generous,
> brave, and endowed with great prowess.

---
18.
dīrghāyuṣō narāḥ sarvē dharmaṁ satyaṁ ca saṁśritāḥ |
sahitāḥ putra-pautraiḥ ca nityaṁ strībhiḥ purōttamē ||

> **Meaning 18:** In that foremost of cities, all the people were blessed with
> long life, devoted to dharma and truth, and lived happily in the company of
> their wives, sons, and grandsons.

---
19.
kṣatraṁ brahmamukhaṁ cāsīdvaiśyāḥ kṣatramanuvratāḥ |
śūdrāḥ svadharmaniratāstrīnvarṇānupacāriṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 19:** The kṣatriyas looked to the brāhmaṇas as their guide, while the
> vaiśyas followed the lead of the kṣatriyas. Devoted to their own duty, the
> śūdras served the other three varṇas in mutual harmony.

---
20.
sā tēnēkṣvākunāthēna purī suparirakṣitā |
yathā purastānmanunā mānavēndrēṇa dhīmatā ||

> **Meaning 20:** That city was thoroughly protected King Daśaratha of the Ikṣvāku lineage, just as it had been safeguarded in ancient times by the wise Manu,
> ruler of mankind.

---
21.
yōdhānāmagnikalpānāṁ pēśalānāmamarṣiṇām |
sampūrṇākr̥tavidyānāṁ guhākēsariṇāmiva ||

> **Meaning 21:** The city was filled with warriors as fierce as fire — skilled,
> intolerant of insult, accomplished in every martial science, and formidable
> as lions in their mountain caves.

---
22.
kāmbōjaviṣayē jātairbāhlīkaiśca hayōttamaiḥ |
vanāyujairnadījaiśca pūrṇāharihayōpamaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 22:** Ayodhyā abounded in the finest horses — bred in Kāmboja, Bāhlīka, Vanāyu, and along the banks of the Sindhu — noble steeds that rivaled Indra's celestial mount, Ucchaiḥśravas.

---
23.
vindhyaparvatajairmattaiḥ pūrṇā haimavatairapi |
madānvitairatibalairmātaṅgaiḥ parvatōpamaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 23:** The city also teemed with mighty, mountain-like elephants,
> born in the Vindhya ranges and the snow-clad Himālayas, ferocious in rut and
> endowed with immense strength.

---
24.
airāvatakulīnaiśca mahāpadmakulaistathā |
añjanādapi niṣkrāntairvāmanādapi ca dvipaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 24:** It was populated by great elephants descended from the noble
> celestial lines of the four Diggajas — Airāvata, Mahāpadma, Añjana, and
> Vāmana.

---
25.
bhadraiḥ mandraiḥ mr̥gaiḥ ca ēva bhadra-mandra-mr̥gaiḥ tathā |
bhadra-mandraiḥ bhadra-mr̥gaiḥ mr̥ga-mandraiḥ ca sā purī |
nityamattaiḥ sadā pūrṇā nāgairacalasannibhaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 25:** That city was perpetually filled with mountain-like elephants
> ever in rut — belonging to the three pure breeds: Bhadra, Mandra, and Mṛga;
> those combining all three; and the mixed breeds: Bhadra-Mandra, Bhadra-Mṛga,
> and Mṛga-Mandra.

---
26.
sā yōjanē ca dvē bhūyaḥ satyanāmā prakāśatē |
yasyāṁ daśarathō rājā vasañjagadapālayat ||

> **Meaning 26:** Extending for two more yojanas all around, that city shone
> forth, truly worthy of its name Ayodhyā (the Unconquerable) — dwelling within
> which King Daśaratha protected the entire world.

---
27.
tāṁ purīṁ saḥ mahātējāḥ rājā daśarathō mahān |
śaśāsa śamita-amitraḥ nakṣatrāṇi iva candramāḥ ||

> **Meaning 27:** Having subdued all his enemies, the great and glorious King
> Daśaratha ruled over that magnificent city, shining in their midst just as the
> moon presides over the stars.

---
28.
tāṁ satyanāmāṁ dr̥ḍhatōraṇārgalā;mgr̥hairvicitrairupaśōbhitāṁ śivām |
purīmayōdhyāṁ nr̥sahasrasaṅkulāṁ; śaśāsa vai śakrasamō mahīpatiḥ ||

> **Meaning 28:** With its sturdy gateways and firm crossbars, adorned with
> wondrous mansions and teeming with thousands of citizens, that auspicious
> city of Ayodhyā — truly unassailable as its name proclaimed — was ruled by
> that king Daśaratha, an equal to Indra himself.

---
