=== Sarga 4 ===
The Recitation of the Rāmāyaṇa by Kuśa and Lava

---
1.
prāptarājyasya rāmasya vālmīkirbhagavānr̥ṣiḥ |
cakāra caritaṁ kr̥tsnaṁ vicitrapadamātmavān ||

> **Meaning 1:** After Rāma had regained his kingdom, the venerable and
> steadfast sage Vālmīki composed his entire life story in rich and varied
> verse.

---
2.
caturviṁśatsahasrāṇi ślōkānāmuktavānr̥ṣiḥ |
tathā sargaśatān pañca ṣaṭ kāṇḍāni tathōttaram ||

> **Meaning 2:** The sage composed twenty-four thousand verses, arranged in five
> hundred sargas, across six kāṇḍas as well as the Uttara Kāṇḍa.

---
3.
kr̥tvā tu tanmahāprājñaḥ sabhaviṣyaṁ sahōttaram |
cintayāmāsa kō nvētatprayuñjīyāditi prabhuḥ ||

> **Meaning 3:** Having composed the work, along with the future events in the
> Uttara Kāṇḍa, the wise master thought to himself, "Who indeed could recite and
> sing this poem?"

---
4.
tasya cintayamānasya maharṣērbhāvitātmanaḥ |
agr̥hṇītāṁ tataḥ pādau munivēṣau kuśīlavau ||

> **Meaning 4:** As the great-souled sage was reflecting on this, Kuśa and Lava,
> dressed in the simple garb of ascetics, came forward and touched his feet in reverence.

---
5.
kuśīlavau tu dharmajñau rājaputrau yaśasvinau |
bhrātarau svarasampannau dadarśāśramavāsinau ||

> **Meaning 5:** He looked upon the two brothers, Kuśa and Lava — princes living
> in his hermitage who knew dharma, possessed great renown, and were blessed with
> melodious voices.

---
6.
sa tu mēdhāvinau dr̥ṣṭvā vēdēṣu pariniṣṭhitau |
vēdōpabr̥hmaṇārthāya tāvagrāhayata prabhuḥ ||

> **Meaning 6:** Seeing that they were brilliant and thoroughly grounded in the
> Vedas, the master taught them the poem to illuminate and reinforce Vedic wisdom.

---
7.
kāvyaṁ rāmāyaṇaṁ kr̥tsnaṁ sītāyāścaritaṁ mahat |
paulastya vadhamityēva cakāra caritavrataḥ ||

> **Meaning 7:** The sage of steadfast vows composed this entire epic, known as
> the Rāmāyaṇa, which is also titled "The Great Story of Sītā" and "The Slaying
> of Paulastya (Rāvaṇa)."

---
8.
pāṭhyē gēyē ca madhuraṁ pramāṇaistribhiranvitam |
jātibhiḥ saptabhirbaddhaṁ tantrīlayasamanvitam ||

> **Meaning 8:** The work was sweet whether recited or sung, set to the three
> tempos, structured in the seven melodic modes, and tuned to the rhythm of
> stringed instruments.

---
9.
rasaiḥ śr̥ṅgārakaruṇahāsyaraudrabhayānakaiḥ |
vīrādibhī rasairyuktaṁ kāvyamētadagāyatām ||

> **Meaning 9:** The two sang this epic poem, which was rich in all the poetic
> rasas: the romantic, compassionate, comic, furious, fearful, heroic, and the
> rest.

---
10.
tau tu gāndharvatattvajñau mūrcchanāsthānakōvidau |
bhrātarau svarasampannau gandharvāviva rūpiṇau ||

> **Meaning 10:** Masters of the science of music, skilled in modulations and
> pitch registers, the melodious brothers appeared like two Gandharvas in visible
> form.

---
11.
rūpalakṣaṇasampannau madhurasvarabhāṣiṇau |
bimbādivōddhr̥tau bimbau rāmadēhāttathāparau ||

> **Meaning 11:** Endowed with beauty and auspicious marks, speaking in sweet
> tones, they seemed like two living reflections cast straight from Rāma's own
> form.

---
12.
tau rājaputrau kārtsnyēna dharmyamākhyānamuttamam |
vācō vidhēyaṁ tatsarvaṁ kr̥tvā kāvyamaninditau ||

> **Meaning 12:** Those two blameless princes committed the entire poem to memory
> — this supreme narrative rooted in dharma, making every word readily obedient
> to their speech.

---
13.
r̥ṣīṇāṁ ca dvijātīnāṁ sādhūnāṁ ca samāgamē |
yathōpadēśaṁ tattvajñau jagatustau samāhitau ||

> **Meaning 13:** In gatherings of rishis, brāhmaṇas, and righteous people, the
> two youths, deeply understanding its true essence, sang with deep concentration
> exactly as they had been taught.

---
14.
mahātmānau mahābhāgau sarvalakṣaṇalakṣitau |
tau kadācitsamētānāmr̥ṣīṇāṁ bhāvitātmanām |
āsīnānāṁ samīpasthāvidaṁ kāvyamagāyatām ||

> **Meaning 14:** One day, those two noble, fortunate youths, endowed with every
> auspicious mark, stood before an assembly of seated, purified rishis and sang
> this epic poem.

---
15.
tacchrutvā munayaḥ sarvē bāṣpaparyākulēkṣaṇāḥ |
sādhu sādhvitytāvūcatuḥ paraṁ vismayamāgatāḥ ||

> **Meaning 15:** Hearing it, all the sages were filled with great wonder; with
> eyes brimming with tears, they exclaimed to them, "Splendid! Splendid!"

---
16.
tē prītamanasaḥ sarvē munayō dharmavatsalāḥ |
praśaśaṁsuḥ praśastavyau gāyamānau kuśīlavau ||

> **Meaning 16:** Delighted in their hearts, those dharma-loving sages showered
> praise on Kuśa and Lava, who were so worthy of acclaim, as they sang.

---
17.
ahō gītasya mādhuryaṁ ślōkānāṁ ca viśēṣataḥ |
ciranirvr̥ttamapyētatpratyakṣamiva darśitam ||

> **Meaning 17:** "Ah, what sweetness in the singing, and especially in the
> verses! Though these events took place long ago, they seem to unfold right before
> our eyes!"

---
18.
praviśya tāvubhau suṣṭhu tadā bhāvamagāyatām |
sahitau madhuraṁ raktaṁ sampannaṁ svarasampadā ||

> **Meaning 18:** Entering deeply into the inner emotion of the poem, the two
> sang together in unison, their performance sweet, captivating, and rich in
> melodic perfection.

---
19.
ēvaṁ praśasyamānau tau tapaḥślāghyairmaharṣibhiḥ |
saṁraktataramatyarthaṁ madhuraṁ tāvagāyatām ||

> **Meaning 19:** Praised in this way by the great rishis celebrated for their
> austerities, the two sang with even greater sweetness and charm.

---
20.
prītaḥ kaścinmunistābhyāṁ saṁsthitaḥ kalaśaṁ dadau |
prasannō valkalaṁ kaściddadau tābhyāṁ mahāyaśāḥ ||

> **Meaning 20:** One delighted sage stood up and gave them a kalaśa, while
> another illustrious sage gladly presented them with bark garments.

---
21.
anyaḥ kr̥ṣṇājinamadādyajñasūtraṁ tathāparaḥ |
kaścitkamaṇḍaluṁ prādānmauñjīmanyō mahāmuniḥ ||

> **Meaning 21:** Another sage gave a black antelope skin, and another gave a
> sacred thread; someone gave a kamaṇḍalu, and another great sage gave a girdle
> of muñjā grass.

---
22.
audumbarīṁ br̥sīmanyaḥ svasti kēcittadāvadan |
br̥sīmanyastadā prādātkaupīnamaparō muniḥ ||

> **Meaning 22:** Another sage gave a seat made of audumbara wood, while some
> uttered words of blessing, wishing them well; another then gave a woven grass
> seat, and yet another sage gave a loincloth.

---
23.
tābhyāṁ dadau tadā hr̥ṣṭaḥ kuṭhāramaparō muniḥ |
kāṣāyamaparō vastraṁ cīramanyō dadau muniḥ ||

> **Meaning 23:** Another sage, overjoyed, gave them an axe; another gave an ochre
> robe, and yet another sage gave bark cloth.

---
24.
jaṭābandhanamanyastu kāṣṭharajjuṁ mudānvitaḥ |
yajñabhāṇḍamr̥ṣiḥ kaścit kāṣṭhabhāraṁ tathāparaḥ ||

> **Meaning 24:** One joyful sage gave a cord for tying their matted hair, another
> gave a rope for tying firewood, a certain rishi gave a yajñabhāṇḍa, and
> another gave a bundle of firewood.

---
25.
āyuṣyamaparē prāhurmudā tatra maharṣayaḥ |
daduścaivaṁ varānsarvē munayaḥ satyavādinaḥ ||

> **Meaning 25:** Other great rishis there happily blessed them with long life,
> and in this manner all those truth-speaking sages bestowed boons upon them.

---
26.
āścaryamidamākhyānaṁ muninā samprakīrtitam |
paraṁ kavīnāmādhāraṁ samāptaṁ ca yathākramam ||

> **Meaning 26:** The sages praised them, saying: "Wondrous is this narrative
> proclaimed by the sage! It will be the supreme foundation for all poets,
> brought to completion in perfect order."

---
27.
abhigītamidaṁ gītaṁ sarvagītēṣu kōvidau |
āyuṣyaṁ puṣṭijanakaṁ sarvaśrutimanōharam ||


> **Meaning 27:** "Skilled as you are in every style of song, you have sung this
> wonderfully! It bestows long life and vitality, and is delightful to all ears."

---
28.
praśasyamānau sarvatra kadācittatra gāyakau |
rathyāsu rājamārgēṣu dadarśa bharatāgrajaḥ ||

> **Meaning 28:** As the two singers were being praised everywhere, Bharata's
> elder brother, Rāma, noticed them one day singing along the streets and royal
> avenues.

---
29.
svavēśma cānīya tatō bhrātarau sakuśīlavau |
pūjayāmāsa pūjārhau rāmaḥ śatrunibarhaṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 29:** Inviting the two brothers, Kuśa and Lava, into his palace,
> Rāma, destroyer of foes, honored those two who were so worthy of respect.

---
30.
āsīnaḥ kāñcanē divyē sa ca siṁhāsanē prabhuḥ |
upōpaviṣṭaiḥ sacivairbhrātr̥bhiśca parantapaḥ ||

> **Meaning 30:** That scorcher of foes, Rāma, sat upon an exquisite golden throne,
> surrounded by his ministers and brothers seated near him.

---
31.
dr̥ṣṭvā tu rūpasampannau tāvubhau vīṇinau tataḥ |
uvāca lakṣmaṇaṁ rāmaḥ śatrughnaṁ bharataṁ tathā ||

> **Meaning 31:** Seeing the two handsome youths holding their vīṇās, Rāma said to
> Lakṣmaṇa, Bharata, and Śatrughna:

---
32.
śrūyatāmidamākhyānamanayōrdēvavarcasōḥ |
vicitrārthapadaṁ samyaggāyatōrmadhurasvaram ||

> **Meaning 32:** "Listen to this narrative sung so beautifully in melodious
> voices by these two youths of divine radiance, rich in profound meaning and
> expressive words."

---
33.
tau cāpi madhuraṁ raktaṁ svañcitāyataniḥsvanam |
tantrīlayavadatyarthaṁ viśrutārthamagāyatām ||

> **Meaning 33:** And they sang in a sweet, impassioned manner, with
> well-inflected and prolonged tones, perfectly aligned with the rhythm of the
> strings and conveying clear meaning.

---
34.
hlādayatsarvagātrāṇi manāṁsi hr̥dayāni ca |
śrōtrāśrayasukhaṁ gēyaṁ tadbabhau janasaṁsadi ||

> **Meaning 34:** Delighting every part of their listeners' being, their minds,
> and their hearts, that music, so soothing to the ears, shone brilliantly in the
> royal assembly.

---
35.
imau munī pārthivalakṣmaṇānvitau; kuśīlavau caiva mahātapasvinau |
mamāpi tadbhūtikaraṁ pracakṣatē; mahānubhāvaṁ caritaṁ nibōdhata ||

> **Meaning 35:** "Though living as ascetics of great austerity, these two youths,
> Kuśa and Lava, bear all the royal marks of kings. Listen closely to this
> glorious story they recite, which brings great well-being even to me."

---
36.
tatastu tau rāmavacaḥ pracōditā;vagāyatāṁ mārgavidhānasampadā |
sa cāpi rāmaḥ pariṣadgataḥ śanai;rbubhūṣayāsaktamanā babhūva ha ||

> **Meaning 36:** Prompted by Rāma's encouraging words, the two continued singing in the classical style with flawless technique; and Rāma, seated amid his assembly, gradually became completely captivated in his heart.

---
