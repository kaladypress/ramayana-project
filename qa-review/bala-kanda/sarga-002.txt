=== Sarga 2 ===
The Genesis of the Rāmāyaṇa Epic

---
1.
nāradasya tu tadvākyaṁ śrutvā vākyaviśāradaḥ |
pūjayāmāsa dharmātmā sahaśiṣyō mahāmuniḥ ||

> **Meaning 1:** After hearing Nārada's words, the great sage Vālmīki — a master
> of speech and a soul devoted to dharma — honored him, together with his
> disciples.

---
2.
yathāvatpūjitastēna dēvarṣirnāradastadā |
āpr̥ṣṭvaivābhyanujñātaḥ sa jagāma vihāyasam ||

> **Meaning 2:** Honored in the proper way, the divine sage Nārada asked leave
> of Vālmīki, and once he was given permission, he rose into the sky.

---
3.
sa muhūrtaṁ gatē tasmindēvalōkaṁ munistadā |
jagāma tamasātīraṁ jāhnavyāstvavidūrataḥ ||

> **Meaning 3:** A short while after Nārada had left for the world of the
> devatās, the sage Vālmīki went to the bank of the Tamasā, not far from the
> Jāhnavī (Gaṅgā).

---
4.
sa tu tīraṁ samāsādya tamasāyā mahāmuniḥ |
śiṣyamāha sthitaṁ pārśvē dr̥ṣṭvā tīrthamakardamam ||

> **Meaning 4:** Reaching the bank of the Tamasā, the great sage saw a bathing
> place free of mud, and he spoke to the disciple standing beside him:

---
5.
akardamamidaṁ tīrthaṁ bharadvāja niśāmaya |
ramaṇīyaṁ prasannāmbu sanmanuṣyamanō yathā ||

> **Meaning 5:** "Look, Bharadvāja — this bathing place has no mud at all. It is
> serene, and its water is as clear as the mind of a good person.

---
6.
nyasyatāṁ kalaśastāta dīyatāṁ valkalaṁ mama |
idamēvāvagāhiṣyē tamasātīrthamuttamam ||

> **Meaning 6:** Set down the water pot, my son, and hand me my bark garment.
> I will bathe right here, in this fine spot on the Tamasā."

---
7.
ēvamuktō bharadvājō vālmīkēna mahātmanā |
prāyacchata munēstasya valkalaṁ niyatō gurōḥ ||

> **Meaning 7:** When the great-souled Vālmīki said this, the disciplined
> Bharadvāja handed his teacher, the sage, his bark garment.

---
8.
sa śiṣyahastādādāya valkalaṁ niyatēndriyaḥ |
vicacāra ha paśyaṁstatsarvatō vipulaṁ vanam ||

> **Meaning 8:** Taking the bark garment from his disciple's hand, the sage,
> calm and centered, walked about, gazing at the vast forest all around him.

---
9.
tasyābhyāśē tu mithunaṁ carantamanapāyinam |
dadarśa bhagavāṁstatra krauñcayōścārunisvanam ||

> **Meaning 9:** Close by, the revered sage saw a pair of Krauñca birds moving
> about together, never apart from each other, calling sweetly to one another.

---
10.
tasmāttu mithunādēkaṁ pumāṁsaṁ pāpaniścayaḥ |
jaghāna vairanilayō niṣādastasya paśyataḥ ||

> **Meaning 10:** Then, as the sage watched, a Niṣāda hunter — full of
> hostility and set on doing evil — struck down the male of that pair.

---
11.
taṁ śōṇitaparītāṅgaṁ vēṣṭamānaṁ mahītalē |
bhāryā tu nihataṁ dr̥ṣṭvā rurāva karuṇāṁ giram ||

> **Meaning 11:** Seeing her mate struck down and writhing on the ground, his
> body covered in blood, the female cried out in a pitiful voice.

---
12.
viyuktā patinā tēna dvijēna sahacāriṇā |
tāmraśīrṣēṇa mattēna patriṇā sahitēna vai ||

> **Meaning 12:** Separated from that mate of hers — her constant winged
> companion, copper-headed and intoxicated with love, who had been united with
> her — she cried out in sorrow.

---
13.
tathā tu taṁ dvijaṁ dr̥ṣṭvā niṣādēna nipātitam |
r̥ṣērdharmātmanastasya kāruṇyaṁ samapadyata ||

> **Meaning 13:** When the sage, whose soul was rooted in dharma, saw the bird
> struck down by the Niṣāda in this way, he was overcome with compassion.

---
14.
tataḥ karuṇavēditvādadharmō'yamiti dvijaḥ |
niśāmya rudatīṁ krauñcīmidaṁ vacanamabravīt ||

> **Meaning 14:** Moved by that compassion, and seeing the female Krauñca
> weeping, sage Vālmīki thought, "This is wrong," and spoke these words:

---
15.
mā niṣāda pratiṣṭhāṁ tvamagamaḥ śāśvatīḥ samāḥ |
yatkrauñcamithunādēkamavadhīḥ kāmamōhitam ||

> **Meaning 15:** "Niṣāda, may you never find a place of rest for endless
> years, because you killed one of this pair of Krauñcas while he was lost in
> love."

---
16.
tasyaivaṁ bruvataścintā babhūva hr̥di vīkṣataḥ |
śōkārtēnāsya śakunēḥ kimidaṁ vyāhr̥taṁ mayā ||

> **Meaning 16:** Even as he spoke these words and looked on, a thought arose in
> his heart: "Overcome with grief for this bird, what is this I have said?"

---
17.
cintayansa mahāprājñaścakāra matimānmatim |
śiṣyaṁ caivābravīdvākyamidaṁ sa munipuṅgavaḥ ||

> **Meaning 17:** Reflecting on it, the wise and thoughtful sage reached a clear
> understanding. Then that foremost of sages said this to his disciple:

---
18.
pādabaddhō'kṣarasamastantrīlayasamanvitaḥ |
śōkārtasya pravr̥ttō mē ślōkō bhavatu nānyathā ||

> **Meaning 18:** "Bound in metrical feet, with an equal number of syllables in
> each, and fit to be sung to the rhythm of the strings — let what came from me
> in my grief (śōka) be called a śloka, and nothing else."

---
19.
śiṣyastu tasya bruvatō munērvākyamanuttamam |
pratijagrāha saṁhr̥ṣṭastasya tuṣṭō'bhavadguruḥ ||

> **Meaning 19:** As the sage spoke those peerless words, his disciple joyfully
> committed them to memory. His teacher was well pleased with him.

---
20.
sō'bhiṣēkaṁ tataḥ kr̥tvā tīrthē tasminyathāvidhi |
tamēva cintayannarthamupāvartata vai muniḥ ||

> **Meaning 20:** Then the sage bathed at that spot in the prescribed manner,
> and turned back, still absorbed in the verse he had uttered.

---
21.
bharadvājastataḥ śiṣyō vinītaḥ śrutavāngurōḥ |
kalaśaṁ pūrṇamādāya pr̥ṣṭhatō'nujagāma ha ||

> **Meaning 21:** Then his humble disciple Bharadvāja, having heard and learned
> the verse from his teacher, took up the full water pot and followed behind him.

---
22.
sa praviśyāśramapadaṁ śiṣyēṇa saha dharmavit |
upaviṣṭaḥ kathāścānyāścakāra dhyānamāsthitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 22:** Once back at the hermitage with his disciple, the sage, ever
> faithful to dharma, sat down to his customary rites and holy discourse, though
> his thoughts remained fixed in deep reflection.

---
23.
ājagāma tatō brahmā lōkakartā svayaṁ prabhuḥ |
caturmukhō mahātējā draṣṭuṁ taṁ munipuṅgavam ||

> **Meaning 23:** Then the creator of the worlds, the four-faced and radiant
> Lord Brahmā himself, came there to see that foremost of sages.

---
24.
vālmīkiratha taṁ dr̥ṣṭvā sahasōtthāya vāgyataḥ |
prāñjaliḥ prayatō bhūtvā tasthau paramavismitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 24:** When Vālmīki saw him, he rose at once, speechless and utterly
> amazed. He stood with folded hands, composed and reverent.

---
25.
pūjayāmāsa taṁ dēvaṁ pādyārghyāsanavandanaiḥ |
praṇamya vidhivaccainaṁ pr̥ṣṭvānāmayamavyayam ||

> **Meaning 25:** He honored that god with water for his feet, the respectful
> offering (arghya), a seat, and words of praise. He bowed to him in the proper
> way and inquired with reverence after his well-being.

---
26.
athōpaviśya bhagavānāsanē paramārcitē |
vālmīkayē maharṣayē sandidēśāsanaṁ tataḥ ||

> **Meaning 26:** The revered Brahmā then sat on a seat of the highest honor
> and gestured for the great sage Vālmīki to take a seat as well.

---
27.
upaviṣṭē tadā tasminsākṣāllōkapitāmahē |
tadgatēnaiva manasā vālmīkirdhyānamāsthitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 27:** Even as Brahmā, grandsire of the worlds, sat there before him
> in person, Vālmīki sank into thought, his mind fixed on what had happened:

---
28.
pāpātmanā kr̥taṁ kaṣṭaṁ vairagrahaṇabuddhinā |
yastādr̥śaṁ cāruravaṁ krauñcaṁ hanyādakāraṇāt ||

> **Meaning 28:** "What a terrible thing that evil man did, his mind set on
> hostility — to kill such a sweetly singing Krauñca for no reason at all!"

---
29.
śōcannēva muhuḥ krauñcīmupaślōkamimaṁ punaḥ |
jagāvantargatamanā bhūtvā śōkaparāyaṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 29:** Grieving again and again for the female Krauñca, lost in sorrow
> and turned inward, he sang that verse once more.

---
30.
tamuvāca tatō brahmā prahasanmunipuṅgavam |
ślōka ēva tvayā baddhō nātra kāryā vicāraṇā ||

> **Meaning 30:** Then Brahmā smiled and said to that foremost of sages, "What you
> have composed is truly a śloka, bound in meter (chandas). There's no need to doubt it.

---
31.
macchandādēva tē brahmanpravr̥ttēyaṁ sarasvatī |
rāmasya caritaṁ sarvaṁ kuru tvamr̥ṣisattama ||

> **Meaning 31:** O Brāhmaṇa, this flow of speech, this Sarasvatī, came to you by
> my will alone. O best of r̥ṣis, now compose the whole story of Rāma.

---
32.
dharmātmanō guṇavatō lōkē rāmasya dhīmataḥ |
vr̥ttaṁ kathaya dhīrasya yathā tē nāradācchrutam ||

> **Meaning 32:** Tell the story of Rāma — his virtues, his steadfastness,
> and his devotion to dharma — just as you heard it from Nārada.

---
33.
rahasyaṁ ca prakāśaṁ ca yadvr̥ttaṁ tasya dhīmataḥ |
rāmasya saha saumitrē rākṣasānāṁ ca sarvaśaḥ ||

34.
vaidēhyāścaiva yadvr̥ttaṁ prakāśaṁ yadi vā rahaḥ |
taccāpyaviditaṁ sarvaṁ viditaṁ tē bhaviṣyati ||

> **Meaning 33-34:** Everything that happened to the wise Rāma, to Saumitri
> (Lakṣmaṇa), to all the Rākṣasas, and to Vaidēhī (Sītā) — both their public
> deeds and what took place in private solitude — even what has remained
> unknown to the world will all be revealed to you.
---

35.
na tē vāganr̥tā kāvyē kācidatra bhaviṣyati |
kuru rāmakathāṁ puṇyāṁ ślōkabaddhāṁ manōramām ||

> **Meaning 35:** Nothing you say in this poem will be untrue. Compose the
> sacred and beautiful story of Rāma in ślokas.

---
36.
yāvatsthāsyanti girayaḥ saritaśca mahītalē |
tāvadrāmāyaṇakathā lōkēṣu pracariṣyati ||

> **Meaning 36:** As long as the mountains stand and the rivers flow on the
> earth, the story of the Rāmāyaṇa will endure throughout the worlds.

---
37.
yāvadrāmasya ca kathā tvatkr̥tā pracariṣyati |
tāvadūrdhvamadhaśca tvaṁ mallōkēṣu nivatsyasi ||

> **Meaning 37:** "And as long as the story of Rāma composed by you continues to
> be told, your renown will spread throughout all three worlds."

---
38.
ityuktvā bhagavānbrahmā tatraivāntaradhīyata |
tataḥ saśiṣyō vālmīkirmunirvismayamāyayau ||

> **Meaning 38:** After saying this, the revered Brahmā vanished right there.
> Then the sage Vālmīki and his disciples were filled with wonder.

---
39.
tasya śiṣyāstataḥ sarvē jaguḥ ślōkamimaṁ punaḥ |
muhurmuhuḥ prīyamāṇāḥ prāhuśca bhr̥śavismitāḥ ||

> **Meaning 39:** Then all his disciples sang that śloka again and again,
> delighting in it, and in their deep amazement they said:

---
40.
samākṣaraiścaturbhiryaḥ pādairgītō maharṣiṇā |
sō'nuvyāharaṇādbhūyaḥ śōkaḥ ślōkatvamāgataḥ ||

> **Meaning 40:** "The grief the great sage sang, bound in four quarters of
> equal syllables, has, by being repeated, become a śloka."

---
41.
tasya buddhiriyaṁ jātā vālmīkērbhāvitātmanaḥ |
kr̥tsnaṁ rāmāyaṇaṁ kāvyamīdr̥śaiḥ karavāṇyaham ||

> **Meaning 41:** Then a resolve formed in the mind of Vālmīki, whose soul was
> purified: "I will compose the entire Rāmāyaṇa poem in verses like these."

---
42.
udāravr̥ttārthapadairmanōramaiḥ stadāsya rāmasya cakāra kīrtimān |
samākṣaraiḥ ślōkaśatairyaśasvinō yaśaskaraṁ kāvyamudāradhīrmuniḥ ||

> **Meaning 42:** Then that renowned and noble-minded sage composed a poem that
> brings glory to the glorious Rāma — hundreds (twenty four thousand) of ślokas with equal syllables,
> with beautiful words and lofty meaning and style.

---
43.
tadupagatasamāsasandhiyōgaṁ
samamadhurōpanatārthavākyabaddham |
raghuvaracaritaṁ munipraṇītaṁ
daśaśirasaśca vadhaṁ niśāmayadhvam ||

> **Meaning 43:** Listen with devotion to this epic composed by the sage Vālmīki— richly
> adorned with elegant compounds and euphony, sweet and profound in meaning —
> recounting the noble life of the foremost of the Raghus and the destruction of
> the ten-headed Rāvaṇa.
