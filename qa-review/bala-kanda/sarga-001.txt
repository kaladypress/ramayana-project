=== Sarga 1 ===
Saṅkṣēpa Rāmāyaṇa: Nārada Narrates the Story of Rāma to Vālmīki

---

Om

1.
tapaḥsvādhyāyanirataṁ tapasvī vāgvidāṁ varam |
nāradaṁ paripapraccha vālmīkirmunipuṅgavam ||

> **Meaning 1:** Vālmīki, the ascetic, questioned Nārada — the foremost of
> sages, devoted to tapas and the study of the Vedas, and the finest of those
> skilled in speech.

---
2.
kō nvasminsāmprataṁ lōkē guṇavānkaśca vīryavān |
dharmajñaśca kr̥tajñaśca satyavākyō dr̥ḍhavrataḥ ||

> **Meaning 2:** "Who in this world today is truly virtuous and truly valiant?
> Who knows dharma, who is grateful, truthful in speech, and firm in his vows?

---
3.
cāritrēṇa ca kō yuktaḥ sarvabhūtēṣu kō hitaḥ |
vidvānkaḥ kaḥ samarthaśca kaścaikapriyadarśanaḥ ||

> **Meaning 3:** Who lives by good conduct, and who is devoted to the welfare of
> all beings? Who is learned, who is capable, and who alone is a delight to
> everyone who sees him?

---
4.
ātmavānkō jitakrōdhō dyutimānkō'nasūyakaḥ |
kasya bibhyati dēvāśca jātarōṣasya saṁyugē ||

> **Meaning 4:** Who is steadfast, who has conquered anger, who is radiant
> and free of envy? And whom do even the devatās fear when his anger is roused
> in battle?

---
5.
ētadicchāmyahaṁ śrōtuṁ paraṁ kautūhalaṁ hi mē |
maharṣē tvaṁ samarthō'si jñātumēvaṁvidhaṁ naram ||

> **Meaning 5:** I desire to hear this, for my eagerness is immense. Great sage, you are capable of knowing such a man."

---
6.
śrutvā caitattrilōkajñō vālmīkērnāradō vacaḥ |
śrūyatāmiti cāmantrya prahr̥ṣṭō vākyamabravīt ||

> **Meaning 6:** Nārada, conversant with all three worlds, heard Vālmīki's words.
> Delighted, he said, "Listen," and began to speak.

---
7.
bahavō durlabhāścaiva yē tvayā kīrtitā guṇāḥ |
munē vakṣyāmyahaṁ buddhvā tairyuktaḥ śrūyatāṁ naraḥ ||

> **Meaning 7:** "Sage, the qualities you have named are many, and rare to find
> together in a single person. I will reflect and tell you about the man who has them. Listen.

---
8.
ikṣvākuvaṁśaprabhavō rāmō nāma janaiḥ śrutaḥ |
niyatātmā mahāvīryō dyutimāndhr̥timānvaśī ||

> **Meaning 8:** There is one born in the dynasty of Ikṣvāku, known to the people
> as Rāma. He is self-disciplined, of great valor, radiant, steadfast, and master
> of his senses.

---
9.
buddhimān nītimān vāgmī śrīmāñ śatrunibarhaṇaḥ |
vipulāṁsō mahābāhuḥ kambugrīvō mahāhanuḥ ||

> **Meaning 9:** He is wise, skilled in statecraft, eloquent, and glorious — a
> destroyer of enemies. His shoulders are broad, his arms mighty, his neck
> shaped like a conch, and his jaw strong.

---
10.
mahōraskō mahēṣvāsō gūḍhajatrurarindamaḥ |
ājānubāhuḥ suśirāḥ sulalāṭaḥ suvikramaḥ ||

> **Meaning 10:** He is broad-chested and a great archer; his collarbones lie
> deep beneath strong muscle; he subdues his foes. His arms reach to his knees,
> his head and brow are finely shaped, and his stride is full of grace.

---
11.
samaḥ samavibhaktāṅgaḥ snigdhavarṇaḥ pratāpavān |
pīnavakṣā viśālākṣō lakṣmīvāñśubhalakṣaṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 11:** He is perfectly balanced and exquisitely formed, with a
> glowing complexion and great majesty. His chest is full, his eyes are wide, he
> is graced with fortune, and he bears every auspicious mark.

---
12.
dharmajñaḥ satyasandhaśca prajānāṁ ca hitē rataḥ |
yaśasvī jñānasampannaḥ śucirvaśyaḥ samādhimān ||

> **Meaning 12:** He knows dharma, keeps his word, and is devoted to the welfare
> of his people. He is renowned, rich in knowledge, pure, disciplined, and
> composed in mind.

---
13.
prajāpatisamaḥ śrīmān dhātā ripuniṣūdanaḥ |
rakṣitā jīvalōkasya dharmasya parirakṣitā ||

> **Meaning 13:** Like the creator Prajāpati, he brings forth and cares for his
> people; endowed with boundless prosperity, he sustains the entire world
> like Viṣṇu; and like Hara, he destroys all enemies. He is the protector of
> all living beings and the steadfast guardian of dharma.

--- 

14.
rakṣitā svasya dharmasya svajanasya ca rakṣitā |
vēdavēdāṅgatattvajñō dhanurvēdē ca niṣṭhitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 14:** He upholds his own dharma and ensures his people abide by
> theirs. He knows the true essence of the Vedas and Vedāṅgas, and is fully
> accomplished in Dhanurveda, the science of warfare.

---
15.
sarvaśāstrārthatattvajña smr̥timānpratibhānavān |
sarvalōkapriyaḥ sādhuradīnātmā vicakṣaṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 15:** He understands the true purpose of all the śāstras. His memory is sharp and his insight quick. Loved by everyone, he is kind, large-hearted, and discerning.

---
16.
sarvadābhigataḥ sadbhiḥ samudra iva sindhubhiḥ |
āryaḥ sarvasamaścaiva sadaikapriyadarśanaḥ ||

> **Meaning 16:** Good people always seek him out, just as rivers flow to the
> ocean. He is noble, treats everyone equally, and is always a delight to see.

---
17.
sa ca sarvaguṇōpētaḥ kausalyānandavardhanaḥ |
samudra iva gāmbhīryē dhairyēṇa himavāniva ||

> **Meaning 16:** Endowed with every virtue, he is the joy of Kausalyā. In profound depth he is like Samudra, and in steadiness like the Himavān.

---
18.
viṣṇunā sadr̥śō vīryē sōmavatpriyadarśanaḥ |
kālāgnisadr̥śaḥ krōdhē kṣamayā pr̥thivīsamaḥ |
dhanadēna samastyāgē satyē dharma ivāparaḥ ||

> **Meaning 18:** In valor he is equal to Viṣṇu, and he is as lovely to see as
> the moon. In anger he is like the fire that ends the world, and in patience he
> is like the earth.In generosity he equals Kubera, the giver of wealth, and in
> truthfulness he is like a second Dharma himself.

---
19.
tamēvaṅguṇasampannaṁ rāmaṁ satyaparākramam |
jyēṣṭhaṁ śrēṣṭhaguṇairyuktaṁ priyaṁ daśarathaḥ sutam ||

> **Meaning 19:** Rāma was endowed with all these virtues, and his valor was
> unfailing. He was Daśaratha's eldest and most beloved son, rich in the highest
> qualities.

---
20.
prakr̥tīnāṁ hitairyuktaṁ prakr̥tipriyakāmyayā |
yauvarājyēna saṁyōktumaicchatprītyā mahīpatiḥ ||

> **Meaning 19:**   Rāma was devoted to the welfare of the people; and wishing to please his subjects, King Daśaratha, out of deep affection, desired to install him as crown prince (yuvarāja).

---
21.
tasyābhiṣēkasambhārāndr̥ṣṭvā bhāryātha kaikayī ||

> **Meaning 21:** Then Queen Kaikeyī, the king's wife, saw the preparations
> being made for Rāma's consecration.

---
22.
pūrvaṁ dattavarā dēvī varamēnamayācata |
vivāsanaṁ ca rāmasya bharatasyābhiṣēcanam ||

> **Meaning 22:** Having been granted boons long before, she now demanded them of
> the king: the exile of Rāma and the consecration of Bharata.

---
23.
sa satyavacanādrājā dharmapāśēna saṁyataḥ |
vivāsayāmāsa sutaṁ rāmaṁ daśarathaḥ priyam ||

> **Meaning 23:** The king was bound by his promise and by the noose of dharma.
> So Daśaratha sent his beloved son Rāma into exile.

---
24.
sa jagāma vanaṁ vīraḥ pratijñāmanupālayan |
piturvacananirdēśātkaikēyyāḥ priyakāraṇāt ||

> **Meaning 24:** The hero went to the forest, keeping the promise, obeying his
> father's command, and to please Kaikeyī.

---
25.
taṁ vrajantaṁ priyō bhrātā lakṣmaṇō'nujagāma ha |
snēhādvinayasampannaḥ sumitrānandavardhanaḥ ||

> **Meaning 25:** As he left, his beloved brother Lakṣmaṇa — humble, and the joy
> of Sumitrā — followed him out of love.

---

26.
rāmasya dayitā bhāryā nityaṁ prāṇasamā hitā |
janakasya kulē jātā dēvamāyēva nirmitā ||

> **Meaning 26:** Rāma's beloved wife was ever dear to him as his own life and devoted to his welfare. Born into the noble house of Janaka, she was like divine Māyā made manifest.

27.
sarvalakṣaṇasampannā nārīṇāmuttamā vadhūḥ |
sītāpyanugatā rāmaṁ śaśinaṁ rōhiṇī yathā ||

> **Meaning 27:** Endowed with every auspicious mark, the foremost of women and
> daughter-in-law of Daśaratha, Sītā also followed Rāma, just as Rohiṇī follows
> the moon.
---
28.
paurairanugatō dūraṁ pitrā daśarathēna ca ||

> **Meaning 28:** The citizens of Ayodhyā and his father Daśaratha followed him a long distance as he departed.
--
29.
śr̥ṅgavērapurē sūtaṁ gaṅgākūlē vyasarjayat |
guhamāsādya dharmātmā niṣādādhipatiṁ priyam ||

> **Meaning 29:** At Śr̥ṅgavērapura, on the bank of the Gaṅgā, he sent his
> charioteer back; and there the righteous Rāma met his dear friend Guha, the
> king of the Niṣādas.
---
30.
guhēna sahitō rāmō lakṣmaṇēna ca sītayā |
tē vanēna vanaṁ gatvā nadīstīrtvā bahūdakāḥ ||

> **Meaning 30:** Accompanied by Guha, Rāma journeyed on with Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa from forest to forest, crossing rivers brimming with deep waters.

---
31.
citrakūṭamanuprāpya bharadvājasya śāsanāt |
ramyamāvasathaṁ kr̥tvā ramamāṇā vanē trayaḥ |
dēvagandharvasaṅkāśāstatra tē nyavasansukham ||

> **Meaning 31:** Reaching Citrakūṭa by the direction of Sage Bharadvāja, the three built a delightful dwelling and lived there happily, enjoying the forest like devatās and Gandharvas.

---
32.
citrakūṭaṁ gatē rāmē putraśōkāturastadā |
rājā daśarathaḥ svargaṁ jagāma vilapansutam ||

> **Meaning 32:** After Rāma had gone to Citrakūṭa, King Daśaratha, broken with
> grief for his son, went to heaven, weeping for him.

---
33.
mr̥tē tu tasminbharatō vasiṣṭhapramukhairdvijaiḥ |
niyujyamānō rājyāya naicchadrājyaṁ mahābalaḥ ||

> **Meaning 33:** When Daśaratha passed away, the brāhmaṇas led by Vasiṣṭha urged
> Bharata to accept the kingship; but the mighty Bharata did not want the
> kingdom.
---

34.
sa jagāma vanaṁ vīrō rāmapādaprasādakaḥ ||

> **Meaning 34:** Instead, that heroic prince went to the forest to propitiate
> Rāma and win his favor at his feet.
---
35.
gatvā tu sa mahātmānaṁ rāmaṁ satyaparākramam |
ayācadbhrātaraṁ rāmamāryabhāvapuraskr̥taḥ |
tvamēva rājā dharmajña iti rāmaṁ vacō'bravīt ||

> **Meaning 35:** Approaching his noble brother Rāma, whose valor was unfailing,
> Bharata spoke with heartfelt devotion and pleaded: "You know what dharma
> requires — you alone must rule." 

---
36.
rāmō'pi paramōdāraḥ sumukhaḥ sumahāyaśāḥ |
na caicchatpiturādēśādrājyaṁ rāmō mahābalaḥ ||

> **Meaning 36:** But Rāma, supremely magnanimous, gracious-faced, and of
> illustrious renown, refused to accept the throne, holding firm to his father's
> command.

---
37.
pādukē cāsya rājyāya nyāsaṁ dattvā punaḥ punaḥ |
nivartayāmāsa tatō bharataṁ bharatāgrajaḥ ||

> **Meaning 37:** Rāma, Bharata's elder brother, gave him his sandals (pādukās)
> as a sacred trust for the kingdom, and gently persuading and consoling him
> again and again, sent Bharata back.

---
38.
sa kāmamanavāpyaiva rāmapādāvupaspr̥śan |
nandigrāmē'karōdrājyaṁ rāmāgamanakāṅkṣayā ||

> **Meaning 38:** Unable to fulfill his wish to bring Rāma back, Bharata bowed at Rāma's feet. He ruled the kingdom from Nandigrāma, longing for Rāma's return.

---
39.
gatē tu bharatē śrīmān satyasandhō jitēndriyaḥ ||

> **Meaning 39:** When Bharata had departed, the glorious Rāma remained there,
> steadfast in his vow and master of his senses.
---

40.
rāmastu punarālakṣya nāgarasya janasya ca |
tatrāgamanamēkāgrē daṇḍakānpravivēśa ha ||

> **Meaning 40:** Realizing that the citizens and townspeople would come there again, Rāma entered the Daṇḍaka forest with single-minded resolve.

---
41.
praviśya tu mahāraṇyaṁ rāmō rājīvalōcanaḥ |
virādhaṁ rākṣasaṁ hatvā śarabhaṅgaṁ dadarśa ha |
sutīkṣṇaṁ cāpyagastyaṁ ca agastya bhrātaraṁ tathā ||

> **Meaning 41:** Entering the great forest, the lotus-eyed Rāma killed the
> Rākṣasa Virādha, and visited the sages Śarabhaṅga, Sutīkṣṇa, Agastya, and
> Agastya's brother.

---
42.
agastyavacanāccaiva jagrāhaindraṁ śarāsanam |
khaḍgaṁ ca paramaprītastūṇī cākṣayasāyakau ||

> **Meaning 42:** At Agastya's word, and with great joy, he accepted Indra's
> bow, a sword, and two quivers whose arrows never run out.

---
43.
vasatastasya rāmasya vanē vanacaraiḥ saha |
r̥ṣayō'bhyāgamansarvē vadhāyāsurarakṣasām ||

> **Meaning 43:** While Rāma was living in the forest among the forest dwellers,
> all the r̥ṣis came to him, pleading with him to destroy the Asuras and
> Rākṣasas who were tormenting them.
---
44.
sa tēṣāṁ pratiśuśrāva rākṣasānāṁ tathā vanē ||

> **Meaning 44:** He gave his solemn promise to the sages that he would destroy
> the Rākṣasas in that forest.

---
45.
pratijñātaśca rāmēṇa vadhaḥ saṁyati rakṣasām |
r̥ṣīṇāmagnikalpānāṁ daṇḍakāraṇyavāsinām ||

> **Meaning 45:** To those sages of the Daṇḍaka forest, whose spiritual power was like blazing fire, Rāma pledged that he would destroy the Rākṣasas in battle.

---
46.
tēna tatraiva vasatā janasthānanivāsinī |
virūpitā śūrpaṇakhā rākṣasī kāmarūpiṇī ||

> **Meaning 46:** While he lived there, Śūrpaṇakhā — a Rākṣasī of Janasthāna who
> could take any form she wished — was disfigured.

---
47.
tataḥ śūrpaṇakhāvākyādudyuktānsarvarākṣasān |
kharaṁ triśirasaṁ caiva dūṣaṇaṁ caiva rākṣasam |
nijaghāna raṇē rāmastēṣāṁ caiva padānugān ||

> **Meaning 47:** Then, incited by Śūrpaṇakhā's words, all the Rākṣasas marched
> out against him; and Rāma killed them in battle — Khara, Triśiras, the
> Rākṣasa Dūṣaṇa, and all their followers.

---
48.
vanē tasminnivasatā janasthānanivāsinām |
rakṣasāṁ nihatānyāsansahasrāṇi caturdaśa ||

> **Meaning 48:** While living in that forest, he destroyed fourteen thousand
> Rākṣasas who inhabited Janasthāna.

---
49.
tatō jñātivadhaṁ śrutvā rāvaṇaḥ krōdhamūrchitaḥ |
sahāyaṁ varayāmāsa mārīcaṁ nāma rākṣasam ||

> **Meaning 49:** When Rāvaṇa heard of the slaughter of his kinsmen, he was
> overcome with rage, and he chose a Rākṣasa named Mārīca as his ally.

---
50.
vāryamāṇaḥ subahuśō mārīcēna sa rāvaṇaḥ |
na virōdhō balavatā kṣamō rāvaṇa tēna tē ||

> **Meaning 50:** Mārīca tried many times to hold Rāvaṇa back: "Rāvaṇa, you
> cannot afford to make an enemy of someone that powerful."

---
51.
anādr̥tya tu tadvākyaṁ rāvaṇaḥ kālacōditaḥ |
jagāma sahamārīcastasyāśramapadaṁ tadā ||

> **Meaning 51:** But Rāvaṇa ignored his words. Driven by his fate, he went with
> Mārīca to Rāma's āśrama.

---
52.
tēna māyāvinā dūramapavāhya nr̥pātmajau |
jahāra bhāryāṁ rāmasya gr̥dhraṁ hatvā jaṭāyuṣam ||

> **Meaning 52:** Using Mārīca, that master of illusion, Rāvaṇa lured the two
> princes far away. Then he carried off Rāma's wife, after killing the vulture
> Jaṭāyu.

---
53.
gr̥dhraṁ ca nihataṁ dr̥ṣṭvā hr̥tāṁ śrutvā ca maithilīm |
rāghavaḥ śōkasantaptō vilalāpākulēndriyaḥ ||

> **Meaning 53:** When Rāghava saw the vulture slain and heard that Maithilī had been taken, he was consumed with grief and wept, utterly distraught.

---
54.
tatastēnaiva śōkēna gr̥dhraṁ dagdhvā jaṭāyuṣam ||


> **Meaning 54:** Overcome with sorrow, he cremated the vulture Jaṭāyu.
---

55.
mārgamāṇō vanē sītāṁ rākṣasaṁ sandadarśa ha |
kabandhaṁ nāma rūpēṇa vikr̥taṁ ghōradarśanam |
taṁ nihatya mahābāhurdadāha svargataśca saḥ ||

> **Meaning 55:** As he searched the forest for Sītā, he came upon a Rākṣasa
> named Kabandha, misshapen in form and terrible to behold. The mighty-armed
> Rāma killed and cremated him, whereupon Kabandha ascended to heaven.

---
56.
sa cāsya kathayāmāsa śabarīṁ dharmacāriṇīm |
śramaṇīṁ dharmanipuṇāmabhigacchēti rāghava ||

> **Meaning 56:** Kabandha told him, "Rāghava, go to Śabarī, the ascetic woman
> who lives by dharma and knows it deeply." So the radiant destroyer of enemies
> went to Śabarī.

---
57.
sō'bhyagacchanmahātējāḥ śabarīṁ śatrusūdanaḥ |
śabaryā pūjitaḥ samyagrāmō daśarathātmajaḥ ||

> **Meaning 57:** Rāma, Daśaratha's radiant son and destroyer of foes, went to Śabarī, who welcomed and honored him with deep devotion.

---
58.
pampātīrē hanumatā saṅgatō vānarēṇa ha |
hanumadvacanāccaiva sugrīvēṇa samāgataḥ ||

> **Meaning 58:** On the bank of the Pampā, he met the Vānara Hanumān; and on
> Hanumān's advice, he came together with Sugrīva.

---
59.
sugrīvāya ca tatsarvaṁ śaṁsadrāmō mahābalaḥ |
āditastadyathāvr̥ttaṁ sītāyāśca viśēṣataḥ ||

> **Meaning 59:** The mighty Rāma recounted everything to Sugrīva, telling
> exactly what had happened from the very beginning, especially concerning
> Sītā.

60.
sugrīvaścāpi tatsarvaṁ śrutvā rāmasya vānaraḥ |
cakāra sakhyaṁ rāmēṇa prītaścaivāgnisākṣikam ||

> **Meaning 60:** Having heard the whole of Rāma's story, the Vānara Sugrīva was
> delighted, and sealed his friendship with Rāma before the sacred fire.

61.
tatō vānararājēna vairānukathanaṁ prati |
rāmāyāvēditaṁ sarvaṁ praṇayādduḥkhitēna ca ||

> **Meaning 61:** Then, out of friendship and stricken with grief, the king of the
> Vānaras related to Rāma the entire story of his feud.

---
62.
vālinaśca balaṁ tatra kathayāmāsa vānaraḥ |
pratijñātaṁ ca rāmēṇa tadā vālivadhaṁ prati ||

> **Meaning 62:** There the Vānara described Vāli's formidable strength; and
> Rāma then pledged to slay Vāli.

---
63.
sugrīvaḥ śaṅkitaścāsīnnityaṁ vīryēṇa rāghavē ||

> **Meaning 63:** Yet Sugrīva constantly harbored doubts about Rāghava's
> prowess.

---
64.
rāghavaḥ pratyayārthaṁ tu dundubhēḥ kāyamuttamam |
darśayāmāsa sugrīvō mahāparvatasannibham ||


> **Meaning 64:** To test Rāghava and be convinced of his strength, Sugrīva
> showed him the colossal carcass of the demon Dundubhi, which looked like a
> massive mountain.

---

65.
utsmayitvā mahābāhuḥ prēkṣya cāsthi mahābalaḥ |
pādāṅguṣṭhēna cikṣēpa sampūrṇaṁ daśayōjanam ||

> **Meaning 65:** Smiling calmly, the mighty-armed and powerful Rāma looked at
> the skeleton, and with a flick of his big toe hurled it a full ten yojanas
> away.

---
66.
bibhēda ca punaḥ sālānsaptaikēna mahēṣuṇā |
giriṁ rasātalaṁ caiva janayanpratyayaṁ tadā ||

> **Meaning 66:** Then, to inspire complete confidence in Sugrīva, Rāma shot a single mighty arrow that pierced through seven sāla trees, penetrated the mountain, and plunged deep into the netherworld.

---
67.
tataḥ prītamanāstēna viśvastaḥ sa mahākapiḥ |
kiṣkindhāṁ rāmasahitō jagāma ca guhāṁ tadā ||

> **Meaning 67:** His mind at ease and his trust won, the great Kapi, Sugrīva,
> went with Rāma to Kiṣkindhā, the cave city.

---

68.
tatō'garjaddharivaraḥ sugrīvō hēmapiṅgalaḥ |
tēna nādēna mahatā nirjagāma harīśvaraḥ ||

> **Meaning 68:** Then Sugrīva, the best of the Vānaras, golden-tawny in color,
> let out a roar. At that great roar, the king of the Vānaras, Vāli, came out.

---
69.
anumānya tadā tārāṁ sugrīvēṇa samāgataḥ |
nijaghāna ca tatrainaṁ śarēṇaikēna rāghavaḥ ||


> **Meaning 69:** Then, reassuring Tārā, Vāli went out and clashed with
> Sugrīva; and there, Rāghava struck Vāli down with a single arrow.

---

70.
tataḥ sugrīvavacanāddhatvā vālinamāhavē |
sugrīvamēva tadrājyē rāghavaḥ pratyapādayat ||

> **Meaning 70:** Then, at Sugrīva's request, Rāghava killed Vāli in combat and
> installed Sugrīva as king of that realm.

---

71.
sa ca sarvānsamānīya vānarānvānararṣabhaḥ |
diśaḥ prasthāpayāmāsa didr̥kṣurjanakātmajām ||

> **Meaning 71:** Sugrīva, the bull among Vānaras, gathered all the Vānaras and
> sent them out in every direction, eager to find Janaka's daughter.

---
72.
tatō gr̥dhrasya vacanātsampātērhanumānbalī |
śatayōjanavistīrṇaṁ pupluvē lavaṇārṇavam ||

> **Meaning 72:** Then, on the word of the vulture Sampāti, the mighty Hanumān
> leapt across the salt ocean, a hundred yojanas wide.

---
73.
tatra laṅkāṁ samāsādya purīṁ rāvaṇapālitām |
dadarśa sītāṁ dhyāyantīmaśōkavanikāṁ gatām ||

> **Meaning 73:** Reaching Laṅkā, the city ruled by Rāvaṇa, he found Sītā in the Aśoka grove, deeply absorbed in thoughts of Rāma.

---
74.
nivēdayitvābhijñānaṁ pravr̥ttiṁ ca nivēdya ca |
samāśvāsya ca vaidēhīṁ mardayāmāsa tōraṇam ||

> **Meaning 74:** He gave her the token of recognition, told her all the news,
> and comforted Vaidēhī. Then he shattered the arched gateway of the grove.

---
75.
pañca sēnāgragānhatvā sapta mantrisutānapi |
śūramakṣaṁ ca niṣpiṣya grahaṇaṁ samupāgamat ||

> **Meaning 75:** He killed five army commanders and seven sons of ministers,
> crushed the heroic Akṣa, and then allowed himself to be captured.

---
76.
astrēṇōnmuhamātmānaṁ jñātvā paitāmahādvarāt |
marṣayanrākṣasānvīrō yantriṇastānyadr̥cchayā ||

> **Meaning 76:**  Knowing that by the boon of Brahmā he was freed from the weapon, the hero willingly tolerated the Rākṣasas who bound him with ropes.

---
77.
tatō dagdhvā purīṁ laṅkāmr̥tē sītāṁ ca maithilīm |
rāmāya priyamākhyātuṁ punarāyānmahākapiḥ ||

> **Meaning 77:** Then the great Kapi, Hanumān burned the city of Laṅkā — all except
> Sītā, Maithilī — and came back to give Rāma the good news.

---
78.
sō'bhigamya mahātmānaṁ kr̥tvā rāmaṁ pradakṣiṇam |
nyavēdayadamēyātmā dr̥ṣṭā sītēti tattvataḥ ||

> **Meaning 78:** Approaching the great-souled Rāma, Hanumān of boundless spirit
> circled him reverently and reported exactly what had happened: "I have seen
> Sītā."

---
79.
tataḥ sugrīvasahitō gatvā tīraṁ mahōdadhēḥ |
samudraṁ kṣōbhayāmāsa śarairādityasannibhaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 79:** Then Rāma, with Sugrīva, went to the shore of the great ocean
> and churned up the sea with arrows blazing like the sun.

---
80.
darśayāmāsa cātmānaṁ samudraḥ saritāṁ patiḥ |
samudravacanāccaiva nalaṁ sētumakārayat ||

> **Meaning 80:** Samudra, the lord of rivers, appeared before him. Following his
> advice, Rāma had Nala build a bridge across the waters.

---
81.
tēna gatvā purīṁ laṅkāṁ hatvā rāvaṇamāhavē |
rāmaḥ sītāmanuprāpya parāṁ vrīḍāmupāgamat ||

> **Meaning 81:** Crossing by that bridge to the city of Laṅkā and slaying Rāvaṇa
> in battle, Rāma regained Sītā, but felt deep reserve before the gathering.

---
82.
tāmuvāca tatō rāmaḥ paruṣaṁ janasaṁsadi |
amr̥ṣyamāṇā sā sītā vivēśa jvalanaṁ satī ||

> **Meaning 82:** Rāma then spoke harsh words to her in the presence of the
> assembly. Unable to bear them, the virtuous Sītā entered the blazing fire.

---
83.
tatō'gnivacanātsītāṁ jñātvā vigatakalmaṣām |
babhūva rāmaḥ saṁprahr̥ṣṭaḥ pūjitaḥ sarvadaivataiḥ ||

> **Meaning 83:** Then, knowing from Agni's testimony that Sītā was completely
> free from blemish, Rāma was overjoyed and was honored by all the devatās.
---
84.
karmaṇā tēna mahatā trailōkyaṁ sacarācaram |
sadēvarṣigaṇaṁ tuṣṭaṁ rāghavasya mahātmanaḥ ||

> **Meaning 84:** That great deed of the great-souled Rāghava, of killing Rāvaṇa
> and restoring Sītā, delighted the three worlds — all creation, moving and still,
> along with the devas and r̥ṣis.

---
85.
abhyaṣiñcatsa laṅkāyāṁ rākṣasēndraṁ vibhīṣaṇam |
kr̥takr̥tyastadā rāmō vijvaraḥ pramumōda ha ||

> **Meaning 85:** In Laṅkā, he consecrated Vibhīṣaṇa as king of the Rākṣasas.
> His purpose accomplished and his grief dispelled, Rāma rejoiced.

---
86.
dēvatābhyō varānprāpya samutthāpya ca vānarān |
ayōdhyāṁ prasthitō rāmaḥ puṣpakēṇa suhr̥dvr̥taḥ ||

> **Meaning 86:** Receiving boons from the devatās, he brought the fallen Vānaras back to life. Then, surrounded by his devoted friends, Rāma set out for Ayodhyā in the Puṣpaka vimāna.

---
87.
bharadvājāśramaṁ gatvā rāmaḥ satyaparākramaḥ |
bharatasyāntikaṁ rāmō hanumantaṁ vyasarjayat ||

> **Meaning 87:** Having reached the hermitage of Bharadvāja, the valorous
Rāma sent Hanumān to Bharata.

---
88.
punarākhyāyikāṁ jalpansugrīvasahitaśca saḥ |
puṣpakaṁ tatsamāruhya nandigrāmaṁ yayau tadā ||

> **Meaning 88:** Then, boarding the Puṣpaka once again and recounting past
> events in the company of Sugrīva, he proceeded to Nandigrāma.

---
89.
nandigrāmē jaṭāṁ hitvā bhrātr̥bhiḥ sahitō'naghaḥ |
rāmaḥ sītāmanuprāpya rājyaṁ punaravāptavān ||

> **Meaning 89:** At Nandigrāma the sinless Rāma, together with his brothers,
> cut off his matted hair. Having won back Sītā, he regained his kingdom.

---
90.
prahr̥ṣṭamuditō lōkastuṣṭaḥ puṣṭaḥ sudhārmikaḥ |
nirāyamō arōgaśca durbhikṣabhayavarjitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 90:** Under his rule, the people are joyous and glad, content and
> thriving, deeply devoted to dharma, free from all affliction and disease, and
> untouched by the fear of famine.

---
91.
na putramaraṇaṁ kēciddrakṣyanti puruṣāḥ kvacit |
nāryaścāvidhavā nityaṁ bhaviṣyanti pativratāḥ ||

> **Meaning 91:** Nowhere will men see the death of their sons. Women will never be
> widowed, and they will always be devoted to their husbands.

---
92.
na cāgnijaṁ bhayaṁ kiñcinnāpsu majjanti jantavaḥ |
na vātajaṁ bhayaṁ kiñcinnāpi jvarakr̥taṁ tathā |
na cāpi kṣudbhayaṁ tatra na taskarabhayaṁ tathā ||

> **Meaning 92:** There is no danger from fire, and no fear of drowning in
> water; there is no danger from storms, nor affliction from fever; neither is
> there any fear of hunger, nor of thieves.

---
93.
nagarāṇi ca rāṣṭrāṇi dhanadhānyayutāni ca |
nityaṁ pramuditāḥ sarvē yathā kr̥tayugē tathā ||

> **Meaning 93:** The cities and provinces abound in wealth and grain, and all the people are ever joyful — just as it was in the Kr̥tayuga.

---
94.
aśvamēdhaśatairiṣṭvā tathā bahusuvarṇakaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 94:** He will perform a hundred Aśvamēdha sacrifices and many other rites rich in gifts of gold.

---
95.
gavāṁ kōṭyayutaṁ dattvā vidvadbhyō vidhipūrvakam ||
asaṅkhyēyaṁ dhanaṁ dattvā brāhmaṇēbhyō mahāyaśāḥ ||

> **Meaning 95:** Giving ten thousand crores of cows to learned scholars
> according to sacred rules, the illustrious Rāma will bestow immeasurable wealth
> upon the brāhmaṇas.

---
96.
rājavaṁśāñśataguṇānsthāpayiṣyati rāghavaḥ |
cāturvarṇyaṁ ca lōkē'sminsvē svē dharmē niyōkṣyati ||

> **Meaning 96:** Rāghava will establish royal dynasties a hundredfold, and he
> will set the four varṇas in this world each in its own dharma.

---
97.
daśavarṣasahasrāṇi daśavarṣaśatāni ca |
rāmō rājyamupāsitvā brahmalōkaṁ gamiṣyati ||

> **Meaning 97:** After ruling the kingdom for ten thousand years and ten
> hundred years more, Rāma will go to Brahmalōka.

---
98.
idaṁ pavitraṁ pāpaghnaṁ puṇyaṁ vēdaiśca sammitam |
yaḥ paṭhēdrāmacaritaṁ sarvapāpaiḥ pramucyatē ||

> **Meaning 98:** This narrative of Rāma is purifying, auspicious, and equal to the Vedas. Whoever reads it is liberated from all sins.

---
99.
ētadākhyānamāyuṣyaṁ paṭhanrāmāyaṇaṁ naraḥ |
saputrapautraḥ sagaṇaḥ prētya svargē mahīyatē ||

> **Meaning 99:** Whoever reads this sacred epic of the Rāmāyaṇa attains long life, thrives surrounded by children, grandchildren, and family, and in the hereafter is exalted in heaven.

---
100.
paṭhandvijō vāgr̥ṣabhatvamīyā;tsyātkṣatriyō bhūmipatitvamīyāt |
vaṇigjanaḥ paṇyaphalatvamīyā;jjanaśca śūdrō'pi mahattvamīyāt ||

> **Meaning 100:** By reading this, each of the four varṇas attains excellence in its own sphere: a Brāhmaṇa gains foremost mastery in the Vedas and śāstras; a Kṣatriya attains sovereignty over the land; a Vaiśya reaps abundant prosperity in trade; and a Śūdra earns high honor and preeminence among his people."

---
