=== Sarga 24 ===
Crossing the Gaṅgā; The Forest of Tāṭakā and the Story of Malada and Karūṣa

---
1.
tataḥ prabhātē vimalē kr̥tāhnikamarindamau |
viśvāmitraṁ puraskr̥tya nadyāstīramupāgatau ||

> **Meaning 1:** Then, at the break of a clear dawn, the two subduers of enemies
> (Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa) completed their morning rites and, placing Viśvāmitra
> ahead of them, came to the bank of the Gaṅgā.

---
2.
tē ca sarvē mahātmānō munayaḥ saṁśitavratāḥ |
upasthāpya śubhāṁ nāvaṁ viśvāmitramathābruvan ||

> **Meaning 2:** All those great-souled sages of strict vows brought up a fine
> boat and said to Viśvāmitra:

---
3.
ārōhatu bhavānnāvaṁ rājaputrapuraskr̥taḥ |
ariṣṭaṁ gaccha panthānaṁ mā bhūtkālasya paryayaḥ ||

> **Meaning 3:** "Please step into the boat with the princes leading the way.
> Travel your path safely without mishap, and let no time be lost."

---
4.
viśvāmitrastathētyuktvā tānr̥ṣīnabhipūjya ca |
tatāra sahitastābhyāṁ saritaṁ sāgaraṁ gamām ||

> **Meaning 4:** Saying "So be it" and honoring those sages, Viśvāmitra crossed
> the ocean-bound river together with the two princes.

---
5.
tatra śuśrāva vai śabdaṃ toyasaṃrambhavardhitam |
madhyamāgamya toyasya tasya śabdasya niścayam ||

> **Meaning 5:** Reaching the middle of the river, Rāma heard a loud roar
> swelled by the rushing waters, and sought to know what caused that sound.

---
6.
atha rāmaḥ sarinmadhyē papraccha munipuṅgavam |
vāriṇō bhidyamānasya kimayaṁ tumulō dhvaniḥ ||

> **Meaning 6:** Then, in midstream, Rāma asked that foremost of sages, "What is
> this tumultuous roar of clashing waters?"

---
7.
rāghavasya vacaḥ śrutvā kautūhala samanvitam |
kathayāmāsa dharmātmā tasya śabdasya niścayam ||

> **Meaning 7:** Hearing Rāghava's curious question, the righteous sage
> explained the true cause of that sound:

---
8.
kailāsaparvatē rāma manasā nirmitaṁ saraḥ |
brahmaṇā naraśārdūla tēnēdaṁ mānasaṁ saraḥ ||

> **Meaning 8:** "Rāma, tiger among men, on Mount Kailāsa, Brahmā created a lake
> by the resolve of his mind (manas) — and for that reason it is called Lake
> Mānasa.

---
9.
tasmātsusrāva sarasaḥ sāyōdhyāmupagūhatē |
saraḥpravr̥ttā sarayūḥ puṇyā brahmasaraścyutā ||

> **Meaning 9:** "From that lake (saras) flows the river that embraces Ayodhyā
> — called Sarayū because it issues from that lake, and sacred because it
> sprang from Brahmā's lake.

---
10.
tasyāyamatulaḥ śabdō jāhnavīmabhivartatē |
vārisaṅkṣōbhajō rāma praṇāmaṁ niyataḥ kuru ||

> **Meaning 10:** "This matchless roar comes from the Sarayū rushing into the
> Jāhnavī (Gaṅgā), born from the churning of their waters. Rāma, offer your
> reverence to this confluence with a composed mind."

---
11.
tābhyāṁ tu tāvubhau kr̥tvā praṇāmamatidhārmikau |
tīraṁ dakṣiṇamāsādya jagmaturlaghuvikramau ||

> **Meaning 11:** Those two deeply righteous princes bowed in reverence to both
> rivers, and, reaching the southern bank, walked on with swift, light steps.

---
12.
sa vanaṁ ghōrasaṅkāśaṁ dr̥ṣṭvā nr̥pavarātmajaḥ |
aviprahatamaikṣvākaḥ papraccha munipuṅgavam ||

> **Meaning 12:** Seeing a terrifying, untamed forest, Rāma, the Ikṣvāku
> prince, asked that foremost of sages:

---
13.
ahō vanamidaṁ durgaṁ jhillikāgaṇanāditam |
bhairavaiḥ śvāpadaiḥ kīrṇaṁ śakuntairdāruṇārutaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 13:** "How impassable this forest is! It rings with swarms of
> crickets, and is overrun with fierce beasts of prey and birds with harsh,
> frightening cries.

---
14.
nānāprakāraiḥ śakunairvāśyadbhirbhairavasvanaiḥ |
siṁhavyāghravarāhaiśca vāraṇaiścāpi śōbhitam ||

> **Meaning 14:** "It is filled with all kinds of birds screeching with dreadful
> sounds, and marked by lions, tigers, wild boars, and elephants.

---
15.
dhavāśvakarṇakakubhairbilvatindukapāṭalaiḥ |
saṅkīrṇaṁ badarībhiśca kiṁ nvidaṁ dāruṇaṁ vanam ||

> **Meaning 15:** "Crowded with dhava, aśvakarṇa, kakubha, bilva, tinduka,
> pāṭala, and badarī trees — what is this dreadful forest?"

---
16.
tamuvāca mahātējā viśvāmitrō mahāmuniḥ |
śrūyatāṁ vatsa kākutstha yasyaitaddāruṇaṁ vanam ||

> **Meaning 16:** The radiant great sage Viśvāmitra replied to him, "Listen,
> dear Kākutstha, to whom this dreadful forest belongs.

---
17.
ētau janapadau sphītau pūrvamāstāṁ narōttama |
maladāśca karūṣāśca dēvanirmāṇanirmitau ||

> **Meaning 17:** "Best of men, there once were two prosperous lands here —
> Malada and Karūṣa — built by the design of the devatās themselves.

---
18.
purā vr̥travadhē rāma malēna samabhiplutam |
kṣudhā caiva sahasrākṣaṁ brahmahatyā yadāviśat ||

> **Meaning 18:** "Long ago, Rāma, when the thousand-eyed Indra slew Vṛtra,
> the sin of brahmahatyā (killing a brāhmaṇa) entered him, leaving him
> overwhelmed with impurity and hunger.

---
19.
tamindraṁ snāpayandēvā r̥ṣayaśca tapōdhanāḥ |
kalaśaiḥ snāpayāmāsurmalaṁ cāsya pramōcayan ||

> **Meaning 19:** "Then the devatās and the sages rich in austerity bathed Indra
> with pitchers of Gaṅgā water consecrated with mantras, and washed away his
> impurity.

---
20.
iha bhūmyāṁ malaṁ dattvā dattvā kārūṣamēva ca |
śarīrajaṁ mahēndrasya tatō harṣaṁ prapēdirē ||

> **Meaning 20:** "Having freed Mahendra's body of its impurity (mala) and
> filth (kārūṣa) by casting it here on this land, they were filled with joy.

---
21.
nirmalō niṣkarūṣaśca śucirindrō yadābhavat |
dadau dēśasya suprītō varaṁ prabhuranuttamam ||

> **Meaning 21:** "When Indra became free of impurity and filth, and pure once
> more, the mighty one was deeply pleased and granted the region an incomparable
> boon:

---
22.
imau janapadau sthītau khyātiṁ lōkē gamiṣyataḥ |
maladāśca karūṣāśca mamāṅgamaladhāriṇau ||

> **Meaning 22:** "'These two prosperous lands, having borne the impurity of my
> body, will gain fame throughout the world as Malada and Karūṣa.'

---
23.
sādhu sādhviti taṁ dēvāḥ pākaśāsanamabruvan |
dēśasya pūjāṁ tāṁ dr̥ṣṭvā kr̥tāṁ śakrēṇa dhīmatā ||

> **Meaning 23:** "Seeing the honor thus bestowed on the land by the wise Śakra
> (Indra), the devatās praised him, saying, 'Well done, well done!'

---
24.
ētau janapadau sthītau dīrghakālamarindama |
maladāśca karūṣāśca muditau dhanadhānyataḥ ||

> **Meaning 24:** "Rāma, subduer of enemies, for a long time these two
> prosperous lands, Malada and Karūṣa, flourished happily with abundant wealth
> and grain.

---
25.
kasyacittvatha kālasya yakṣī vai kāmarūpiṇī |
balaṁ nāgasahasrasya dhārayantī tadā hyabhūt ||

> **Meaning 25:** "After some time, however, there appeared a yakṣī able to take
> any form at will, possessing the strength of a thousand elephants.

---
26.
tāṭakā nāma bhadraṁ tē bhāryā sundasya dhīmataḥ |
mārīcō rākṣasaḥ putrō yasyāḥ śakraparākramaḥ ||

> **Meaning 26:** "May all be well with you, Rāma — her name is Tāṭakā, the wife
> of the powerful Sunda; her son is the rākṣasa Mārīca, who equals Śakra in
> prowess.

---
27.
vṛttabāhurmahāśīrṣo vipulāsyatanurmahān |
rākṣaso bhairavākāro nityaṃ trāsayate prajāḥ ||

> **Meaning 27:** "With round, massive arms, a huge head, a vast mouth and body,
> that giant rākṣasa of terrifying form constantly terrorizes the people.

---
28.
imau janapadau nityaṁ vināśayati rāghava |
maladāṁśca karūṣāṁśca tāṭakā duṣṭacāriṇī ||

> **Meaning 28:** "Rāghava, this wicked Tāṭakā continually ravages these two
> lands, Malada and Karūṣa.

---
29.
sēyaṁ panthānamāvārya vasatyatyardhayōjanē |
ata ēva ca gantavyaṁ tāṭakāyā vanaṁ yataḥ ||

> **Meaning 29:** "She lives just over half a yojana from here, blocking the
> road — and we must pass right through Tāṭakā's forest.

---
30.
svabāhubalamāśritya jahīmāṁ duṣṭacāriṇīm |
manniyōgādimaṁ dēśaṁ kuru niṣkaṇṭakaṁ punaḥ ||

> **Meaning 30:** "Relying on the strength of your arms, slay this wicked
> creature; at my command, rid this land of its thorn once more.

---
31.
na hi kaścidimaṁ dēśaṁ śakrōtyāgantumīdr̥śam |
yakṣiṇyā ghōrayā rāma utsāditamasahyayā ||

> **Meaning 31:** "No one dares to enter this region as it is now, Rāma,
> devastated by that dreadful, unbearable yakṣiṇī.

---
32.
ētattē sarvamākhyātaṁ yathaitaddaruṇaṁ vanam |
yakṣyā cōtsāditaṁ sarvamadyāpi na nivartatē ||

> **Meaning 32:** "I have told you everything — how this dreadful forest was
> completely laid waste by the yakṣī, and her destruction still continues even
> today."

---
