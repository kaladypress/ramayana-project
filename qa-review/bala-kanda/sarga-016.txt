=== Sarga 16 ===
Viṣṇu's Resolve to Incarnate and Emergence of the Divine Pāyasa

---
1.
tatō nārāyaṇō viṣṇurniyuktaḥ surasattamaiḥ |
jānannapi surānēvaṁ ślakṣṇaṁ vacanamabravīt ||

> **Meaning 1:** Then Nārāyaṇa, Viṣṇu, entreated by the foremost of the devas,
> spoke gently to them, though he already knew everything:

---
2.
upāyaḥ kō vadhē tasya rākṣasādhipatēḥ surāḥ |
yamahaṁ taṁ samāsthāya nihanyāmr̥ṣikaṇṭakam ||

> **Meaning 2:** "Devas, what is the way to kill that king of the Rākṣasas?
> Tell me the means by which I can destroy that tormentor of the ṛṣis."

---
3.
ēvamuktāḥ surāḥ sarvē pratyūcurviṣṇumavyayam |
mānuṣīṁ tanumāsthāya rāvaṇaṁ jahi saṁyugē ||

> **Meaning 3:** Asked this, all the devas replied to the imperishable Viṣṇu:
> "Incarnate as human and kill Rāvaṇa in battle.

---
4.
sa hi tēpē tapastīvraṁ dīrghakālamarindama |
yēna tuṣṭō'bhavadbrahmā lōkakr̥llōkapūjitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 4:** Subduer of enemies, he performed fierce tapas for a very long
> time. That pleased Brahmā, the creator of the worlds, who is worshipped by all
> the worlds.

---
5.
santuṣṭaḥ pradadau tasmai rākṣasāya varaṁ prabhuḥ |
nānāvidhēbhyō bhūtēbhyō bhayaṁ nānyatra mānuṣāt ||

> **Meaning 5:** Pleased, the mighty Brahmā gave that Rākṣasa a boon: he would
> have no fear from beings of every kind, except from humans.

---
6.
ēvaṁ pitāmahāttasmādvaradānēna garvitaḥ |
utsādayati lōkāṁstrīn striyaścāpyapakarṣati ||

> **Meaning 6:** Proud of the boon he received from Brahmā, he is destroying
> the three worlds and tormenting even women.

---
7.
avajñātāḥ purā tēna varadānēna mānavāḥ |
tasmāt tasya vadhō dr̥ṣṭō mānuṣēbhyaḥ parantapa ||

> **Meaning 7:** When he asked for that boon, he looked down on humans and left
> them out. So, scorcher of foes, his death can come only from humans."

---
8.
ityētadvacanaṁ śrutvā surāṇāṁ viṣṇurātmavān |
pitaraṁ rōcayāmāsa tadā daśarathaṁ nr̥pam ||

> **Meaning 8:** Hearing these words of the devas, the self-possessed Viṣṇu
> chose King Daśaratha to be his father.

---
9.
sa kr̥tvā niścayaṁ viṣṇurāmantrya ca pitāmaham |
antardhānaṁ gatō dēvaiḥ pūjyamānō maharṣibhiḥ ||

> **Meaning 9:** Having made his decision and taken leave of the Brahmā,
> Viṣṇu vanished from sight, honored by the devas and the great ṛṣis.

---
10.
sa cāpyaputrō nr̥patistasminkālē mahādyutiḥ |
ayajatputriyāmiṣṭiṁ putrēpsurarisūdanaḥ ||

> **Meaning 10:** At that time the radiant King Daśaratha, slayer of enemies,
> who had no son, was performing the Putrakāmeṣṭi yajña for sons, longing
> to have children.

---
11.
tatō vai yajamānasya pāvakādatulaprabham |
prādurbhūtaṁ mahadbhūtaṁ mahāvīryaṁ mahābalam ||

> **Meaning 11:** Then, as the king was performing the yajña, a great being
> of unmatched splendor, tremendous power, and great strength rose up out of the
> sacred fire.

---
12.
kr̥ṣṇaṁ raktāmbaradharaṁ raktāsyaṁ dundubhisvanam |
snigdhaharyakṣatanujaśmaśrupravaramūrdhajam ||

> **Meaning 12:** He was dark, dressed in red, with a red face and a voice like
> the boom of a dundubhi drum. The hair on his body, his beard, and the fine
> hair on his head were glossy, like a lion's mane.

---
13.
śubhalakṣaṇasampannaṁ divyābharaṇabhūṣitam |
śailaśr̥ṅgasamutsēdhaṁ dr̥ptaśārdūlavikramam ||

> **Meaning 13:** He bore auspicious marks and was adorned with divine
> ornaments. He was as tall as a mountain peak, with the prowess of a proud
> tiger.

---
14.
divākarasamākāraṁ dīptānalaśikhōpamam |
taptajāmbūnadamayīṁ rājatāntaparicchadām ||

> **Meaning 14:** He shone like the sun and blazed like the flames of a fire. He
> carried a large vessel made of refined Jāmbūnada gold, with a lid edged in
> silver,

---
15.
divyapāyasasampūrṇāṁ pātrīṁ patnīmiva priyām |
pragr̥hya vipulāṁ dōrbhyāṁ svayaṁ māyāmayīmiva ||

> **Meaning 15:** full of divine pāyasa. He held it in both arms, close to him,
> like a beloved wife, as though it were divine and auspicious itself.

---
16.
samavēkṣyābravīdvākyamidaṁ daśarathaṁ nr̥pam |
prājāpatyaṁ naraṁ viddhi māmihābhyāgataṁ nr̥pa ||

> **Meaning 16:** Looking at King Daśaratha, he said: "King, know that I am a
> being sent by Brahmā, and I have come here to you."

---
17.
tataḥ paraṁ tadā rājā pratyuvāca kr̥tāñjaliḥ |
bhagavansvāgataṁ tē'stu kimahaṁ karavāṇi tē ||

> **Meaning 17:** Then the king, with folded hands, replied: "Welcome,
> Bhagavān! What is my duty? What must I do?"

---
18.
athō punaridaṁ vākyaṁ prājāpatyō narō'bravīt |
rājannarcayatā dēvānadya prāptamidaṁ tvayā ||

> **Meaning 18:** The divine being sent by Prajāpati spoke again: "O King,
> because the devas are pleased with your yajñas, you have received this today.

---
19.
idaṁ tu naraśārdūla pāyasaṁ dēvanirmitam |
prajākaraṁ gr̥hāṇa tvaṁ dhanyamārōgyavardhanam ||

> **Meaning 19:** O King, tiger among men, accept this pāyasa. It bestows
> children, confers blessings, and enhances health.

---
20.
bhāryāṇāmanurūpāṇāmaśnītēti prayaccha vai |
tāsu tvaṁ lapsyasē putrānyadarthaṁ yajasē nr̥pa ||

> **Meaning 20:** Give it to your worthy wives and tell them to eat it. Through
> them you will have the sons for whom you are performing this yajña."

---
21.
tathēti nr̥patiḥ prītaḥ śirasā pratigr̥hya tām |
pātrīṁ dēvānnasampūrṇāṁ dēvadattāṁ hiraṇmayīm ||

> **Meaning 21:** "So be it," said the delighted king. Bowing his head in
> reverence, he accepted the golden vessel, given by the devas and filled with
> divine pāyasa.

---
22.
abhivādya ca tadbhūtamadbhutaṁ priyadarśanam |
mudā paramayā yuktaścakārābhipradakṣiṇam ||

> **Meaning 22:** King bowed to that wonderful being, so pleasing to look at, and,
> filled with great joy, walked around him in reverence.

---
23.
tatō daśarathaḥ prāpya pāyasaṁ dēvanirmitam |
babhūva paramaprītaḥ prāpya vittamivādhanaḥ ||

> **Meaning 23:** Then Daśaratha, having received the pāyasa blessed by the devas,
> was overjoyed, like a poor man who suddenly comes into wealth.

---
24.
tatastadadbhutaprakhyaṁ bhūtaṁ paramabhāsvaram |
saṁvartayitvā tatkarma tatraivāntaradhīyata ||

> **Meaning 24:** Then that wondrous, brilliantly shining being, having finished
> his task, vanished right there.

---
25.
harṣaraśmibhirudyōtaṁ tasyāntaḥpuramābabhau |
śāradasyābhirāmasya candrasyēva nabhōṁ'śubhiḥ ||

> **Meaning 25:** The king's inner palace glowed with rays of joy, the way the
> sky glows with the rays of the lovely autumn moon.

---
26.
sō'ntaḥpuraṁ praviśyaiva kausalyāmidamabravīt |
pāyasaṁ pratigr̥hṇīṣva putrīyaṁ tvidamātmanaḥ ||

> **Meaning 26:** Entering the inner palace, he said to Queen Kausalyā and his
> other wives: "Take this pāyasa. It will give you sons."

---
27.
kausalyāyai narapatiḥ pāyasārdhaṁ dadau tadā |
ardhādardhaṁ dadau cāpi sumitrāyai narādhipaḥ ||

> **Meaning 27:** The king then gave half of the pāyasa to Kausalyā. Of the
> remaining half, he gave half (1/4) to Sumitrā.

---
28.
kaikēyyai cāvaśiṣṭārdhaṁ dadau putrārthakāraṇāt |
pradadau cāvaśiṣṭārdhaṁ pāyasasyāmr̥tōpamam ||

> **Meaning 28:** For the sake of having sons, he gave half of what remained (1/8) to
> Kaikeyī; and the rest of that nectar-like pāyasa (1/8) he gave—

---
29.
anucintya sumitrāyai punarēva mahīpatiḥ |
ēvaṁ tāsāṁ dadau rājā bhāryāṇāṁ pāyasaṁ pr̥thak ||

> **Meaning 29:** after thinking it over, once again to Sumitrā. In this way,
> the king gave the pāyasa to each of his wives separately.

---
30.
tā stvētatpāyasaṁ prāpya narēndrasyōttamāḥ striyaḥ |
sammānaṁ mēnirē sarvāḥ praharṣōditacētasaḥ ||

> **Meaning 30:** When the king's noble wives received this pāyasa, they all
> felt deeply honored, and their hearts were filled with joy.

---
31.
tatastu tāḥ prāśya taduttamastriyō
mahīpatēruttamapāyasaṁ pr̥thak |
hutāśanādityasamānatējasō-
'cirēṇa garbhān pratipēdirē tadā ||

> **Meaning 31:** Then those noble women, each having eaten her share of the
> divine pāyasa, shone with a splendor like that of fire and the sun,
> and before long they conceived.

---
32.
tatastu rājā prativīkṣya tāḥ striyaḥ
prarūḍhagarbhāḥ pratilabdhamānasaḥ |
babhūva hr̥ṣṭastridivē yathā hariḥ
surēndrasiddharṣigaṇābhipūjitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 32:** Seeing that his wives had conceived, the king felt his
> heart's wish fulfilled. He was as joyful as Viṣṇu in heaven, who is adored
> by Indra, the Siddhas, and the hosts of ṛṣis.

---
