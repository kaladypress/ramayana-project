=== Sarga 5 ===
The Splendor of the City of Ayodhyā

---
1.
sarvāpūrvamiyaṁ yēṣāmāsītkr̥tsnā vasundharā |
prajāpatimupādāya nr̥pāṇāṁ jayaśālinām ||

> **Meaning 1:** From the very beginning, starting with Prajāpati himself, this
> entire earth belonged to that line of ever-victorious kings.

---
2.
yēṣāṁ sa sagarō nāma sāgarō yēna khānitaḥ |
ṣaṣṭiḥ putrasahasrāṇi yaṁ yāntaṁ paryavārayan ||

> **Meaning 2:** Among them was Sagara, at whose command the ocean was excavated
> (from whom the ocean took its name, Sāgara). Sixty thousand sons accompanied
> him as his royal retinue wherever he went.

---
3.
ikṣvākūṇāmidaṁ tēṣāṁ rājñāṁ vaṁśē mahātmanām |
mahadutpannamākhyānaṁ rāmāyaṇamiti śrutam ||

> **Meaning 3:** In the dynasty of those noble Ikṣvāku monarchs arose this grand
> epic, celebrated across the world as the Rāmāyaṇa.

---
4.
tadidaṁ vartayiṣyāmi sarvaṁ nikhilamāditaḥ |
dharmakāmārthasahitaṁ śrōtavyamanasūyayā ||

> **Meaning 4:** I will recount this epic in its entirety, right from the
> beginning. Filled with the teachings of dharma, kāma, and artha, it should be
> listened to with an undoubting mind.

---
5.
kōsalō nāma muditaḥ sphītō janapadō mahān |
niviṣṭaḥ sarayūtīrē prabhūtadhanadhānyavān ||

> **Meaning 5:** Along the banks of the sacred Sarayū lay a vast and joyous
> realm named Kosala — prosperous, thriving, and overflowing with wealth and
> grain.

---
6.
ayōdhyā nāma nagarī tatrāsīllōkaviśrutā |
manunā mānavēndrēṇa yā purī nirmitā svayam ||

> **Meaning 6:** Within that realm stood the world-renowned city of Ayodhyā,
> built by Manu himself, the ruler of mankind.

---
7.
āyatā daśa ca dvē ca yōjanāni mahāpurī |
śrīmatī trīṇi vistīrṇā suvibhaktamahāpathā ||

> **Meaning 7:** That glorious city stretched twelve yojanas in length and three
> yojanas in breadth, laid out with broad, stately avenues.

---
8.
rājamārgēṇa mahatā suvibhaktēna śōbhitā |
muktapuṣpāvakīrṇēna jalasiktēna nityaśaḥ ||

> **Meaning 8:** "It was graced by a broad, well-planned royal highway, sprinkled daily with water and strewn with loose blossoms.

---
9.
tāṁ tu rājā daśarathō mahārāṣṭravivardhanaḥ |
purīmāvāsayāmāsa divi dēvapatiryathā ||

> **Meaning 9:** King Daśaratha, under whose reign the great empire flourished,
> dwelt in that city, reigning over it as the king of the devatās reigns in
> heaven.

---
10.
kapāṭatōraṇavatīṁ suvibhaktāntarāpaṇām |
sarvayantrāyudhavatīmupētāṁ sarvaśilpibhiḥ ||

> **Meaning 10:** The city was fortified with strong gates and arched portals,
> laid out with orderly markets, equipped with every manner of weapons and mechanical defenses, and enriched by master craftsmen of every kind.

---
11.
sūtamāgadhasambādhāṁ śrīmatīmatulaprabhām |
uccāṭṭāladhvajavatīṁ śataghnīśatasaṅkulām ||

> **Meaning 11:** It hummed with bards and chroniclers, radiant with peerless
> splendor, crowned with high watchtowers and fluttering banners, and bristling
> with hundreds of deadly śataghnī engines.

---
12.
vadhūnāṭakasaṅghaiśca saṁyuktāṁ sarvataḥ purīm |
udyānāmravaṇōpētāṁ mahatīṁ sālamēkhalām ||

> **Meaning 12:** The city was alive throughout with theatrical troupes and performing artists. It was adorned with pleasure gardens and mango groves, and encircled by a great belt of sāla trees like a majestic girdle..

---
13.
durgagambhīraparikhāṁ durgāmanyairdurāsadām |
vājivāraṇasampūrṇāṁ gōbhiruṣṭraiḥ kharaistathā ||

> **Meaning 13:** A deep, impassable moat encircled the city, making it a fortress no enemy could approach. It was abundant in horses and elephants, as well as cattle, camels, and donkeys (or mules)

---
14.
sāmantarājasaṅghaiśca balikarmabhirāvr̥tām |
nānādēśanivāsaiśca vaṇigbhirupaśōbhitām ||

> **Meaning 14:** Gatherings of tributary kings constantly arrived to present
> their tribute, while merchants hailing from distant lands graced the capital
> with commerce.

---
15.
prasādai ratnavikr̥taiḥ parvatairupaśōbhitām |
kūṭāgāraiśca sampūrṇāmindrasyēvāmarāvatīm ||

> **Meaning 15:** Adorned with jewel-inlaid palaces rising like mountain peaks and filled with lofty pavilions, the city resembled Indra's celestial capital, Amarāvatī.

---
16.
citrāmaṣṭāpadākārāṁ varanārīgaṇairyutām |
sarvaratnasamākīrṇāṁ vimānagr̥haśōbhitām ||

> **Meaning 16:** Laid out in an intricate grid like an aṣṭāpada board, the
> wondrous city was filled with assemblies of graceful women, gleaming with all
> varieties of jewels, and adorned with palaces that rose like celestial
> vimānas.

---
17.
gr̥hagāḍhāmavicchidrāṁ samabhūmau nivēśitām |
śālitaṇḍulasampūrṇāmikṣukāṇḍarasōdakām ||

> **Meaning 17:** Its mansions stood close together in unbroken harmony upon
> level terrain. Granaries were filled to the brim with polished rice, and the
> waters flowing through it tasted as sweet as sugarcane juice.

---
18.
dundubhībhirmr̥daṅgaiśca vīṇābhiḥ paṇavaistathā |
nāditāṁ bhr̥śamatyarthaṁ pr̥thivyāṁ tāmanuttamām ||

> **Meaning 18:** The city continually reverberated with the vibrant sound of
> dundubhis and mr̥daṅgas, and the melodies of vīṇās and paṇavas, standing
> peerless among all the cities on earth.

---
19.
vimānamiva siddhānāṁ tapasādhigataṁ divi |
sunivēśitavēśmāntāṁ narōttamasamāvr̥tām ||

> **Meaning 19:** With its beautifully arranged mansions and courtyards,
> populated by the finest of men, the city appeared like a celestial realm
> attained by the Siddhas through their tapas.

---
20.
yē ca bāṇairna vidhyanti viviktamaparāparam |
śabdavēdhyaṁ ca vitataṁ laghuhastā viśāradāḥ ||

> **Meaning 20:** In that city dwelt swift-handed, seasoned warriors who could
> strike a target from afar by sound alone, yet whose martial code forbade them
> from ever shooting an arrow at a solitary foe or one retreating in disarray.

---
21.
siṁhavyāghravarāhāṇāṁ mattānāṁ nadatāṁ vanē |
hantārō niśitaiḥ śastrairbalādbāhubalairapi ||

> **Meaning 21:** These heroes hunted down ferocious lions, tigers, and roaring
> boars in the deep forests, slaying them with keen-edged weapons or wrestling
> them down with the raw strength of their arms.

---
22.
tādr̥śānāṁ sahasraistāmabhipūrṇāṁ mahārathaiḥ |
purīmāvāsayāmāsa rājā daśarathastadā ||

> **Meaning 22:** Filled with thousands of such formidable mahārathas, that was
> the city where King Daśaratha made his royal abode.

---
23.
tāmagnimadbhirguṇavadbhirāvr̥tāṁ; dvijōttamairvēdaṣaḍaṅgapāragaiḥ |
sahasradaiḥ satyaratairmahātmabhi;rmaharṣikalpairr̥ṣibhiśca kēvalaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 23:** The city was filled with the foremost brāhmaṇas and great-souled sages who rivaled the ancient maharṣis — Agnihotrīs of noble character, masters of the Vedas and the six Vedāṅgas, generous givers of thousandfold charities, steadfast in truth, and pure ascetics dedicated to solitary contemplation.

---
