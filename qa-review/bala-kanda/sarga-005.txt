=== Sarga 5 ===
Description of the City of Ayodhyā

---
1.
sarvāpūrvamiyaṁ yēṣāmāsītkr̥tsnā vasundharā |
prajāpatimupādāya nr̥pāṇāṁ jayaśālinām ||

> **Meaning 1:** In former times, this entire earth belonged to a line of
> victorious kings, beginning with Prajāpati (Manu).

---
2.
yēṣāṁ sa sagarō nāma sāgarō yēna khānitaḥ |
ṣaṣṭiḥ putrasahasrāṇi yaṁ yāntaṁ paryavārayan ||

> **Meaning 2:** Among them was King Sagara, who had the ocean dug out, and who
> was accompanied by sixty thousand sons wherever he went.

---
3.
ikṣvākūṇāmidaṁ tēṣāṁ rājñāṁ vaṁśē mahātmanām |
mahadutpannamākhyānaṁ rāmāyaṇamiti śrutam ||

> **Meaning 3:** In the dynasty of these noble kings of the Ikṣvāku lineage,
> this great narrative arose, renowned as the Rāmāyaṇa.

---
4.
tadidaṁ vartayiṣyāmi sarvaṁ nikhilamāditaḥ |
dharmakāmārthasahitaṁ śrōtavyamanasūyayā ||

> **Meaning 4:** I will now recount this entire story from the very beginning.
> Encompassing dharma, artha, and kāma, it should be heard with an open heart,
> free from envy.

---
5.
kōsalō nāma muditaḥ sphītō janapadō mahān |
niviṣṭaḥ sarayūtīrē prabhūtadhanadhānyavān ||

> **Meaning 5:** Along the banks of the Sarayū river lay a great, prosperous,
> and joyful realm named Kosala, rich in abundant wealth and grain.

---
6.
ayōdhyā nāma nagarī tatrāsīllōkaviśrutā |
manunā mānavēndrēṇa yā purī nirmitā svayam ||

> **Meaning 6:** There stood the world-renowned city named Ayodhyā, built by
> Manu himself, the first ruler of mankind.

---
7.
āyatā daśa ca dvē ca yōjanāni mahāpurī |
śrīmatī trīṇi vistīrṇā suvibhaktamahāpathā ||

> **Meaning 7:** That splendid city was twelve yojanas long and three yojanas
> wide, laid out with spacious and well-planned grand avenues.

---
8.
rājamārgēṇa mahatā suvibhaktēna śōbhitā |
muktapuṣpāvakīrṇēna jalasiktēna nityaśaḥ ||

> **Meaning 8:** It was graced by a magnificent royal highway that was carefully
> planned, constantly sprinkled with water, and strewn with fresh flowers.

---
9.
tāṁ tu rājā daśarathō mahārāṣṭravivardhanaḥ |
purīmāvāsayāmāsa divi dēvapatiryathā ||

> **Meaning 9:** In that city ruled King Daśaratha, expanding the greatness of
> his realm, residing there like Indra, ruler of the gods, in heaven.

---
10.
kapāṭatōraṇavatīṁ suvibhaktāntarāpaṇām |
sarvayantrāyudhavatīmupētāṁ sarvaśilpibhiḥ ||

> **Meaning 10:** The city was fortified with massive gates and ornamental
> archways, lined with well-arranged markets, equipped with all kinds of
> mechanical devices and weapons, and populated by skilled artisans of every
> trade.

---
11.
sūtamāgadhasambādhāṁ śrīmatīmatulaprabhām |
uccāṭṭāladhvajavatīṁ śataghnīśatasaṅkulām ||

> **Meaning 11:** Crowded with bards and chroniclers, glorious and of matchless
> splendor, it was adorned with high watchtowers flying banners and defended by
> hundreds of Śataghnī weapons.

---
12.
vadhūnāṭakasaṅghaiśca saṁyuktāṁ sarvataḥ purīm |
udyānāmravaṇōpētāṁ mahatīṁ sālamēkhalām ||

> **Meaning 12:** The city was filled on all sides with theatrical troupes of
> women actors and dancers, endowed with gardens and mango groves, and encircled
> by a vast girdle of Sāla trees.

---
13.
durgagambhīraparikhāṁ durgāmanyairdurāsadām |
vājivāraṇasampūrṇāṁ gōbhiruṣṭraiḥ kharaistathā ||

> **Meaning 13:** Protected by a deep and impassable moat, it was impregnable
> and unassailable by any enemy, filled with horses, elephants, cattle, camels,
> and donkeys.

---
14.
sāmantarājasaṅghaiśca balikarmabhirāvr̥tām |
nānādēśanivāsaiśca vaṇigbhirupaśōbhitām ||

> **Meaning 14:** It was constantly attended by crowds of tributary kings
> bringing tribute, and graced by merchants arriving from many different lands.

---
15.
prasādai ratnavikr̥taiḥ parvatairupaśōbhitām |
kūṭāgāraiśca sampūrṇāmindrasyēvāmarāvatīm ||

> **Meaning 15:** Adorned with mountain-high palaces encrusted with gems and
> filled with upper pavilions, it shone like Amarāvatī, the celestial city of
> Indra.

---
16.
citrāmaṣṭāpadākārāṁ varanārīgaṇairyutām |
sarvaratnasamākīrṇāṁ vimānagr̥haśōbhitām ||

> **Meaning 16:** Laid out in an orderly grid like a chessboard, the city was
> filled with beautiful women, overflowing with all kinds of precious gems, and
> adorned with mansions that looked like celestial vimānas.

---
17.
gr̥hagāḍhāmavicchidrāṁ samabhūmau nivēśitām |
śālitaṇḍulasampūrṇāmikṣukāṇḍarasōdakām ||

> **Meaning 17:** Built on level ground with houses standing side by side in
> unbroken rows, it had storehouses full of fine rice grain, and its water was
> as sweet as the juice of sugarcane.

---
18.
dundubhībhirmr̥daṅgaiśca vīṇābhiḥ paṇavaistathā |
nāditāṁ bhr̥śamatyarthaṁ pr̥thivyāṁ tāmanuttamām ||

> **Meaning 18:** It continuously echoed with the sounds of kettledrums,
> mr̥daṅgas, vīṇās, and paṇava drums, standing without an equal anywhere on
> earth.

---
19.
vimānamiva siddhānāṁ tapasādhigataṁ divi |
sunivēśitavēśmāntāṁ narōttamasamāvr̥tām ||

> **Meaning 19:** Resembling a celestial vimāna won by Siddhas in heaven through
> their austerities, the city was lined with finely designed residences and
> inhabited by the noblest of people.

---
20.
yē ca bāṇairna vidhyanti viviktamaparāparam |
śabdavēdhyaṁ ca vitataṁ laghuhastā viśāradāḥ ||

> **Meaning 20:** Its skilled, swift-handed warriors could strike targets by
> sound alone, yet would never shoot their arrows at a lonely, defenseless, or
> fleeing opponent.

---
21.
siṁhavyāghravarāhāṇāṁ mattānāṁ nadatāṁ vanē |
hantārō niśitaiḥ śastrairbalādbāhubalairapi ||

> **Meaning 21:** They were capable of slaying enraged, roaring lions, tigers,
> and wild boars in the forest, whether with sharp weapons or by the sheer power
> of their bare arms.

---
22.
tādr̥śānāṁ sahasraistāmabhipūrṇāṁ mahārathaiḥ |
purīmāvāsayāmāsa rājā daśarathastadā ||

> **Meaning 22:** Filled with thousands of such heroic chariot-warriors, that
> city was ruled at that time by King Daśaratha.

---
23.
tāmagnimadbhirguṇavadbhirāvr̥tāṁ; dvijōttamairvēdaṣaḍaṅgapāragaiḥ |
sahasradaiḥ satyaratairmahātmabhi;rmaharṣikalpairr̥ṣibhiśca kēvalaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 23:** The city was filled with virtuous brāhmaṇas who tended the
> sacred fires and had mastered the Vedas and their six auxiliary sciences —
> noble souls devoted to truth, generous donors of thousands, who lived like
> great rishis or were pure sages themselves.

---
