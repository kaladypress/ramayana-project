=== Sarga 41 ===

---
1.
sa ca vāgbhiḥ praśastābhiḥ gamiṣyan pūjitastayā |
tasmād deśād apakramya cintayām āsa vānaraḥ ||

> **Meaning 1:** Sītā honored him with words of praise as he got ready to leave.
> The Vānara moved away from that spot and began to think.

---
2.
alpaśeṣam idaṁ kāryaṁ dr̥ṣṭeyam asitekṣaṇā |
trīn upāyān atikramya caturtha iha dr̥śyate ||

> **Meaning 2:** "Only a small part of this task is left. I have seen this
> dark-eyed lady. The first three means (sāma, dāna, bheda) can be set aside;
> here only the fourth (daṇḍa, force) seems right.

---
3.
na sāma rakṣaḥsu guṇāya kalpate na danam arthopaciteṣu vartate |
na bhedasādhyā baladarpitā janāḥ parākramastv eṣa mameha rocate ||

> **Meaning 3:** Conciliation (sāma) does no good with Rākṣasas. Gifts (dāna)
> mean nothing to people who are already rich. People proud of their strength
> can't be won over by dividing them (bheda). So force (parākrama) is what
> appeals to me here.

---
4.
na cāsya kāryasya parākramād r̥te viniścayaḥ kaścid ihopapadyate |
hr̥tapravīrāstu raṇe hi rākṣasāḥ kathaṁ cid īyuryad ihādya mārdavam ||

> **Meaning 4:** Without force, nothing decisive can come of this task. Only
> when their best warriors have been killed in battle might the Rākṣasas soften
> a little today.

---
5.
kārye karmaṇi nirdiṣṭaḥ yo bahūny api sādhayet |
pūrvakāryavirodhena sa kāryaṁ kartum arhati ||

> **Meaning 5:** A person sent on a task who achieves many more things as well,
> without undermining the original task, is the right one to do the work.

---
6.
na hy ekaḥ sādhako hetuḥ svalpasyāpīha karmaṇaḥ |
yo hy arthaṁ bahudhā veda sa samarthaḥ arthasādhane ||

> **Meaning 6:** Even for a small task there is no single means of success.
> Whoever understands a matter from many angles is the one able to achieve it.

---
7.
ihaiva tāvat kr̥taniścayo hy ahaṁ yadi vrajeyaṁ plavageśvarālayam |
parātmasaṁmarda viśeṣatattvavit tataḥ kr̥taṁ syān mama bhartr̥śāsanam ||

> **Meaning 7:** I have settled on this right here. If I go back to the home of
> the king of the Plavagas (Sugrīva) knowing the real difference between our
> strength and the enemy's, then I will truly have carried out my master's
> command.

---
8.
kathaṁ nu khalv adya bhavet sukhāgataṁ prasahya yuddhaṁ mama rākṣasaiḥ saha |
tathaiva khalv ātmabalaṁ ca sāravat samānayen māṁ ca raṇe daśānanaḥ ||

> **Meaning 8:** How can I make my coming here today truly worthwhile? By
> forcing a battle with the Rākṣasas. In that battle Daśānana will come to know
> both his own real strength and mine.

---
9.
tatassamāsādya raṇē daśānanaṁ
samaṁtrivargaṁ sabalaprayāyinam |
hr̥di sthitaṁ tasya mataṁ balaṁ ca vai
sukhēna mattvāhamitaḥ punarvrajē ||

10.
idam asya nr̥śaṁsasya nandanopamam uttamam |
vanaṁ netramanaḥkāntaṁ nānādrumalatāyutam ||

> **Meaning 9-10:** Then, facing Daśānana in battle along with his ministers and
> his marching army, I will easily learn the plans in his heart and his
> strength, and then go back from here. This splendid garden of that cruel one
> is like Nandana itself, delightful to the eyes and the mind, and filled with
> many kinds of trees and creepers.

---
11.
idaṁ vidhvaṁsayiṣyāmi śuṣkaṁ vanam ivānalaḥ |
asmin bhagne tataḥ kopaṁ kariṣyati sa rāvaṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 11:** I will destroy it, the way fire destroys a dry forest. Once it
> is wrecked, Rāvaṇa will become angry.

---
12.
tato mahat sāśvamahārathadvipaṁ balaṁ samāneṣv api rākṣasādhipaḥ |
triśūlakālāyasapaṭṭiśāyudhaṁ tato mahad yuddham idaṁ bhaviṣyati ||

> **Meaning 12:** Then the lord of the Rākṣasas will send a great army with
> horses, great chariots, and elephants, armed with tridents, black-iron
> weapons, and paṭṭiśas. Then there will be a great battle.

---
13.
ahaṁ tu taiḥ saṁyati caṇḍavikramaiḥ sametya rakṣobhirasaṁgavikramaḥ |
nihatya tad rāvaṇacoditaṁ balaṁ sukhaṁ gamiṣyāmi kapīśvarālayam ||

> **Meaning 13:** And I, whose valor nothing can stop, will meet those fiercely
> bold Rākṣasas in combat, destroy the army Rāvaṇa sends, and then return
> happily to the home of the Kapīśvara (Sugrīva)."

---
14.
tato mārutavat kruddhaḥ mārutirbhīmavikramaḥ |
ūruvegena mahatā drumān kṣeptum athārabhat ||

> **Meaning 14:** Then Māruti, terrible in valor and furious like the wind
> itself, began to hurl down the trees with the tremendous force of his thighs.

---
15.
tatastad dhanumān vīraḥ babhañja pramadāvanam |
mattadvijasamāghuṣṭaṁ nānādrumalatāyutam ||

> **Meaning 15:** Then the hero Hanumān wrecked the Pramadāvana, the pleasure
> garden, which rang with the calls of excited birds and was filled with many
> trees and creepers.

---
16.
tad vanaṁ mathitairvr̥kṣaiḥ bhinnaiśca salilāśayaiḥ |
cūrṇitaiḥ parvatāgraiśca babhūvāpriyadarśanam ||

> **Meaning 16:** With its trees torn apart, its ponds broken open, and its
> hilltops smashed to powder, that garden became an ugly sight.

---
17.
nānāśakuṁtavirutaiḥ prabhinnai ssalilāśayaiḥ |
tāmraiḥ kisalayaiḥ klāṁtaiḥ klāṁtadruma latāyutam ||

18.
na babhau tadvanaṁ tatra dāvānalahataṁ yathā |
vyākulāvaraṇā rējuḥ vihvalā iva tā latāḥ ||


19.
latāgr̥haiścitragr̥haiśca nāśitaiḥ mahoragairvyālamr̥gaiśca nirdhutaiḥ |
śilāgr̥hairunmathitaistathā gr̥haiḥ pranaṣṭarūpaṁ tad abhūn mahad vanam ||

> **Meaning 17-19:** With its birds crying out, its ponds shattered, and its
> coppery young leaves wilting on drooping trees and creepers, that garden no
> longer shone; it looked like a forest struck by wildfire. The creepers, their
> coverings torn loose, looked like women in distress. Its creeper-bowers and
> painted halls were destroyed, its great serpents and wild beasts driven out,
> and its stone houses and other buildings torn down. That great garden lost all
> its former beauty.

---
20.
sā vihvalā’śōka latāpratānā
vanasthalī śōkalatāpratānā |
jātā daśāsyapramadāvanasya
kapērbalāddhi pramadāvanasya ||

21.
sa tasya kr̥tvārthapatermahākapiḥ mahad vyalīkaṁ manaso mahātmanaḥ |
yuyutsureko bahubhirmahābalaiḥ śriyā jvalaṁstoraṇam āśritaḥ kapiḥ ||

> **Meaning 20-21:** Through the Kapi's strength, the grounds of Daśāsya's
> pleasure garden, once spread with Aśoka creepers, became shaken and spread
> instead with creepers of sorrow (śoka). Having done this great injury to the
> mind of the mighty king, the great Kapi, eager to fight alone against many
> powerful foes, took his stand at the gateway, blazing with splendor.

---
