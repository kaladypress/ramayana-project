=== Sarga 9 ===

---
1.
tasyālayavariṣṭhasya madhye vipulam āyatam |
dadarśa bhavanaśreṣṭhaṁ hanūmān mārutātmajaḥ ||

2.
ardhayojanavistīrṇam āyataṁ yojanaṁ hi tat |
bhavanaṁ rākṣasendrasya bahuprāsādasaṁkulam ||

3.
mārgamāṇastu vaidehīṁ sītām āyatalocanām |
sarvataḥ paricakrāma hanūmān arisūdanaḥ ||

4.
uttamaṁ rākṣasāvāsaṁ hanumā navalōkayan |
āsasādātha lakṣmīvān rākṣasēṁdra nivēśanam ||

> **Meaning 1-4:** In the middle of that most excellent aerial chariot (Puṣpaka),
> Hanumān, the son of Vāyu, saw a vast and expansive building. That sprawling
> mansion of the Rākṣasa king was half a yojana wide and a full yojana long,
> crowded with numerous palatial structures. Searching everywhere for the
> wide-eyed Sītā, the princess of Videha, Hanumān, the destroyer of enemies,
> roamed around in all directions. Looking at the supreme dwellings of the
> Rākṣasas, the glorious Hanumān finally approached the magnificent palace of
> the lord of the Rākṣasas located within it.
---

5.
caturviṣāṇairdviradaiḥ triviṣāṇaistathaiva ca |
parikṣiptam asaṁbādhaṁ rakṣyamāṇam udāyudhaiḥ ||

6.
rākṣasībhiśca patnībhī rāvaṇasya niveśanam |
āhr̥tābhiśca vikramya rājakanyābhirāvr̥tam ||

7.
tan nakramakarākīrṇaṁ timiṁgilajhaṣākulam |
vāyuvegasamādhūtaṁ pannagairiva sāgaram ||

8.
yā hi vaiśvaraṇe lakṣmīḥ yā cendre harivāhane |
sā rāvaṇagr̥he sarvā nityam evānapāyinī ||

9.
yā ca rājñaḥ kuberasya yamasya varuṇasya ca |
tādr̥śī tad viśiṣṭā vā r̥ddhī rakṣo gr̥heṣv iha ||

> **Meaning 5-9:** The expanded mansion was closely guarded by Rākṣasas holding upraised
> weapons and elephants with four tusks and three tusks. Rāvaṇa's residence was filled with his Rākṣasa wives as well as the royal
> princesses he had abducted by his valor. The mansion was like an ocean agitated by the force of the wind, teeming with
> crocodiles, whales, and great serpents. Whatever opulence exists in the abode of Kubera, or in the house of Indra,
> all of that was constantly present in Rāvaṇa's house. The kind of prosperity that belongs to king Kubera,
> Yama, and Varuṇa was seen in the house of that Rākṣasa, and perhaps even greater.
---

10.
tasya harmyasya madhyasthaṁ veśma cānyat sunirmitam |
bahuniryūha saṁkīrṇaṁ dadarśa pavanātmajaḥ ||

11.
brahmaṇo 'rthe kr̥taṁ divyaṁ divi yad viśvakarmaṇā |
vimānaṁ puṣpakaṁ nāma sarvaratnavibhūṣitam ||

12.
pareṇa tapasā lebhe yat kuberaḥ pitāmahāt |
kuberam ojasā jitvā lebhe tad rākṣaseśvaraḥ ||

13.
īhā mr̥gasamāyuktaiḥ kāryasvarahiraṇmayaiḥ |
sukr̥tairācitaṁ stambhaiḥ pradīptam iva ca śriyā ||

14.
merumandarasaṁkāśaiḥ ullikhadbhirivāmbaram |
kūṭāgāraiḥ śubhākāraiḥ sarvataḥ samalaṁkr̥tam ||

15.
jvalanārkapratīkāśaṁ sukr̥taṁ viśvakarmaṇā |
hemasopānasaṁyuktaṁ cārupravaravedikam ||

16.
jālavātāyanairyuktaṁ kāñcanaiḥ sthāṭikairapi |
indranīlamahānīlamaṇipravaravedikam |

> **Meaning 10-16:** In the middle of that mansion, the son of Vāyu saw another
> beautifully constructed building crowded with many turrets. It was the divine
> aerial chariot named Puṣpaka, adorned with all kinds of gems, originally
> crafted in heaven by Viśvakarmā for the sake of Lord Brahmā. Kubera had
> obtained it from Lord Brahmā (the grandfather of the worlds) through supreme
> austerities, and the lord of the Rākṣasas acquired it by conquering Kubera
> with his might. Supported by well-crafted golden pillars shaped like wolves
> and other beasts, it blazed with splendor. It was entirely decorated with
> beautiful structures resembling Mount Meru and Mount Mandara, seeming to
> scrape the sky itself. Constructed flawlessly by Viśvakarmā, it was radiant
> like the blazing sun, equipped with golden staircases and elegant, prominent
> raised platforms. It featured latticed windows made of gold and crystal, with
> excellent platforms studded with sapphires and great blue gems.
---

17.
vidrumēṇa vicitrēṇa maṇibhiśca mahāghanaiḥ |
nistulābhiśca muktābhiḥ talēnābhi virājitam ||

18.
caṁdanēna ca raktēna tapanīyanibhēna ca |
supuṇyagaṁdhinā yuktaṁ ādityataruṇōpamam ||

19.
kūṭāgārairvarākāraiḥ vividhaissamalaṁkr̥tam |
vimānaṁ puṣpakaṁ divyaṁ ārurōha mahākapiḥ ||

20.
tatrasthaḥ sa tadā gandhaṁ pānabhakṣyānnasaṁbhavam |
divyaṁ saṁmūrchitaṁ jighran rūpavantam ivānilam ||

21.
sa gandhastaṁ mahāsattvaṁ bandhurbandhum ivottamam |
ita ehīty uvāceva tatra yatra sa rāvaṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 17-21:** Its floors were brilliantly embellished with wonderful
> coral, highly precious gems, and matchless pearls. Anointed with red
> sandalwood paste that shone like gold and emitting a highly meritorious
> fragrance, it resembled the rising sun. Adorned with various excellent
> towers, the Mahākapi ascended that divine Puṣpaka Vimāna. Standing there,
> he inhaled a divine, overwhelming fragrance arising from food and drinks,
> which felt as though the wind itself had taken a physical form. That
> fragrance, like an excellent relative calling out to a dear one, seemed to
> invite that great-souled one, saying "Come here," drawing him toward where
> Rāvaṇa was.
---

22.
tatastāṁ prasthitaḥ śālāṁ dadarśa mahatīṁ śubhām |
rāvaṇasya manaḥkāntāṁ kāntām iva varastriyam ||

23.
maṇisopānavikr̥tāṁ hemajālavirājitām |
sphāṭikairāvr̥tatalāṁ dantāntaritarūpikām ||

24.
muktābhiśca pravālaiśca rūpyacāmīkarairapi |
vibhūṣitāṁ maṇistambhaiḥ subahustambhabhūṣitām ||

25.
samairr̥jubhiratyuccaiḥ samantāt suvibhūṣitaiḥ |
stambhaiḥ pakṣairivātyuccaiḥ divaṁ saṁprasthitām iva ||

26.
mahatyā kuthayāstrīṇaṁ pr̥thivīlakṣaṇāṅkayā |
pr̥thivīm iva vistīrṇāṁ sarāṣṭragr̥hamālinīm ||

> **Meaning 22-26:** Proceeding towards it, he saw a vast and splendid hall that
> was as dear to Rāvaṇa's heart as a beautiful, beloved woman. It was crafted
> with gem-studded staircases, illuminated by golden latticework, with floors
> covered in crystal and decorated with ivory carvings. Embellished with pearls,
> corals, silver, and gold, it was adorned with numerous pillars studded with
> gems. These exceedingly tall, straight, and perfectly proportioned pillars,
> beautifully decorated all around, looked like massive wings, making the hall
> appear as if it were about to take flight into the sky. It was spread with a
> massive carpet featuring cities, and rows of houses.
---

27.
nāditāṁ mattavihagaiḥ divyagandhādhivāsitām |
parārdhyāstaraṇopetāṁ rakṣo’dhipaniṣevitām ||

28.
dhūmrām agarudhūpena vimalāṁ haṁsapāṇḍurām |
citrāṁ puṣpopahāreṇa kalmāṣīm iva suprabhām ||

29.
manaḥsaṁhlādajananīṁ varṇasyāpi prasādinīm |
tāṁ śokanāśinīṁ divyāṁ śriyaḥ saṁjananīm iva ||

30.
indriyāṇīndriyārthaistu pañca pañcabhiruttamaiḥ |
tarpayām āsa māteva tadā rāvaṇapālitā ||

31.
svargo 'yaṁ devaloko 'yam indrasyeyaṁ purī bhavet |
siddhirveyaṁ parā hi syād ity amanyata mārutiḥ ||

32.
pradhyāyata ivāpaśyat pradīpāṁstatra kāñcanān |
dhūrtān iva mahādhūrtaiḥ devanena parājitān ||

33.
dīpānāṁ ca prakāśena tejasā rāvaṇasya ca |
arcirbhirbhūṣaṇānāṁ ca pradīptety abhyamanyata ||

> **Meaning 27-33:** The hall resonated with the cries of intoxicated birds and
> was permeated with divine fragrances. Furnished with the most excellent carpets
> and cushions, it was enjoyed by the lord of the Rākṣasas. Purified by the
> smoke of Agaru incense, it was spotless and as white as a swan. Decorated with
> floral offerings, it looked like Vasishta's kalmashi (cow) which fulfills every wish. It brought
> delight to the mind, enhanced one's complexion, destroyed sorrow, and seemed
> like the very birthplace of divine splendor. That hall, protected by Rāvaṇa,
> satisfied the five senses with the five most excellent sense objects, nurturing
> them like a mother. Māruti thought to himself, "Is this heaven? Is this the
> realm of the Devas? Could this be the city of Indra? Or is this the ultimate
> attainment of perfection?" Gazing thoughtfully, he saw the golden lamps there
> standing motionless, looking like expert gamblers who were lost in thought, after being defeated by even greater
> tricksters in a game of dice. He felt that the hall was ablaze with the light
> of the lamps, the personal radiance of Rāvaṇa, and the brilliance of the
> ornaments.
---

34.
tato 'paśyat kuthāsīnaṁ nānāvarṇāmbarasrajam |
sahasraṁ varanārīṇāṁ nānāveṣavibhūṣitam ||

35.
parivr̥tte 'rdharātre tu pānanidrāvaśaṁ gatam |
krīḍitvoparataṁ rātrau suṣvāpa balavat tadā ||

36.
tat prasuptaṁ viruruce niḥśabdāntarabhūṣaṇam |
niḥśabdahaṁsabhramaraṁ yathā padmavanaṁ mahat ||

37.
tāsāṁ saṁvr̥tadantāni mīlitākṣāṇi mārutiḥ |
apaśyat padmagandhīni vadanāni suyoṣitām ||

38.
prabuddhānīva padmāni tāsāṁ bhūtvā kṣapākṣaye |
punaḥsaṁvr̥tapatrāṇi rātrāv iva babhustadā ||

39.
imāni mukhapadmāni niyataṁ mattaṣaṭpadāḥ |
ambujānīva phullāni prārthayanti punaḥ punaḥ ||

40.
iti cāmanyata śrīmān upapattyā mahākapiḥ |
mene hi guṇatastāni samāni salilodbhavaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 34-40:** Then he saw a thousand excellent women, adorned in diverse
> attire and wearing garlands and garments of various colors, sleeping on the
> carpets. Having spent the time until past midnight in drinking and
> entertainment, those women became tired and came under the sway of sleep and
> intoxication; wobbling from exhaustion, they ceased their revelries and fell
> into a deep sleep. That hall of sleeping women, devoid of the sound of
> ornaments, shone like a vast lotus lake where the swans and bees have
> fallen silent. Māruti saw the faces of those beautiful women, carrying the
> fragrance of lotuses, with their eyes closed and teeth concealed. Their faces,
> which had bloomed like awakened lotuses earlier, now looked like lotuses that
> had closed their petals at the end of the day. The glorious Mahākapi logically deduced, "Intoxicated bees would surely mistake these lotus-like faces for real blooming lotuses and seek them out again and again." Thus, he considered their faces to be equal in every quality to the lotuses born in water.
---

41.
sā tasya śuśubhe śālā tābhiḥ strībhirvirājitā |
śāradīva prasannā dyauḥ tārābhirabhiśobhitā ||

42.
sa ca tābhiḥ parivr̥taḥ śuśubhe rākṣasādhipaḥ |
yathā hy uḍupatiḥ śrīmāṁḥ tārābhirabhisaṁvr̥taḥ ||

43.
yāścyavante 'mbarāt tārāḥ puṇyaśeṣasamāvr̥tāḥ |
imāstāḥ saṁgatāḥ kr̥tsnā iti mene haristadā ||

44.
tārāṇām iva suvyaktaṁ mahatīnāṁ śubhārciṣām |
prabhāvarṇaprasādāśca virejustatra yoṣitām ||

45.
vyāvr̥ttagurupīnasrakprakīrṇavarabhūṣaṇāḥ |
pānavyāyāmakāleṣu nidrāpahr̥tacetasaḥ ||

> **Meaning 41-45:** That hall, illuminated by those women, shone brilliantly
> like the clear autumn sky adorned with stars. Surrounded by them, the king of
> the Rākṣasas looked as splendid as the glorious moon encircled by stars. The
> Hari (Hanumān) then thought, "These must be the stars that fall from the sky
> when their merit is exhausted, gathered here in their entirety." Indeed, the
> radiance, complexion, and grace of those women shone beautifully, just like the
> brilliant light of large, auspicious stars. Having been exhausted by drinking
> and physical exertion, their minds were stolen by sleep, with their heavy,
> thick garlands displaced and their excellent ornaments scattered.
---

46.
vyāvr̥ttatilakāḥ kāścit kāścid udbhrāntanūpurāḥ |
pārśve galitahārāśca kāścit paramayoṣitaḥ ||

47.
mukhā hāravr̥tāścānyāḥ kāścit prasrastavāsasaḥ |
vyāviddharaśanā dāmāḥ kiśorya iva vāhitāḥ ||

48.
sukuṇḍaladharāścānyā vicchinnamr̥ditasrajaḥ |
gajendramr̥ditāḥ phullā latā iva mahāvane ||

49.
candrāṁśukiraṇābhāśca hārāḥ kāsāṁ cid utkaṭāḥ |
haṁsā iva babhuḥ suptāḥ stanamadhyeṣu yoṣitām ||

50.
aparāsāṁ ca vaidūryāḥ kādambā iva pakṣiṇaḥ |
hemasūtrāṇi cānyāsāṁ cakravākā ivābhavan ||

51.
hamsakāraṇḍavākīrṇāḥ cakravākopaśobhitāḥ |
āpagā iva tā rejuḥ jaghanaiḥ pulinairiva ||

52.
kiṅkiṇījālasaṁkāśāḥ tā hemavipulāmbujāḥ |
bhāvagrāhā yaśastīrāḥ suptā nadya ivābabhuḥ ||

> **Meaning 46-52:** Some of those supreme women had their tilakas smudged, some
> had their anklets displaced, and others had their pearl necklaces slipping off
> to the side. Others had their faces covered by their broken necklaces, their
> garments slipping off, and their waistbands unfastened, tossing and turning in
> their sleep like young mares rolling on the ground after being exhausted from
> carrying heavy loads. Some wore beautiful earrings but had their garlands torn
> and crushed, looking like blossoming creepers in a great forest trampled by a
> majestic elephant. The prominent pearl necklaces on some women, shining like
> moonbeams, rested between their breasts like sleeping swans. The Vaidūrya gem
> necklaces of some looked like Kādamba birds, while the golden chains of others
> resembled Cakravāka birds. Filled with women resembling swans and Kāraṇḍavas,
> and adorned with others resembling Cakravākas, they shone like rivers, with
> their hips resembling the sandy banks. Those women, with their golden, large
> lotus-like faces, sleeping peacefully, appeared like rivers whose shores were
> fame and whose crocodiles were their expressions of love, glistening like
> networks of small bells.
---

53.
mr̥duṣv aṅgeṣu kāsāṁ cit kucāgreṣu ca saṁsthitāḥ |
babhūvurbhūṣaṇānīva śubhā bhūṣaṇarājayaḥ ||

54.
aṁśukāntāśca kāsāṁ cin mukhamārutakampitāḥ |
upary upari vaktrāṇāṁ vyādhūyante punaḥ punaḥ ||

55.
tāḥ pātākā ivoddhūtāḥ patnīnāṁ ruciraprabhāḥ |
nānāvarṇasuvarṇānāṁ vaktramūleṣu rejire ||

56.
vavalguścātra kāsāṁ cit kuṇḍalāni śubhārciṣām |
mukhamārutasaṁsargān mandaṁ mandaṁ suyoṣitām ||

57.
śarkarāsavagandhaḥ sa prakr̥tyā surabhiḥ sukhaḥ |
tāsāṁ vadananiḥśvāsaḥ siṣeve rāvaṇaṁ tadā ||

> **Meaning 53-57:** The beautiful marks left by the ornaments resting on the
> tender limbs and breasts of some women looked like ornaments themselves
> (implying that even if they didn't wear ornaments, these arrays of marks
> made it seem as if they were wearing them). Some women had their faces
> covered with the edges of their garments (pallu), which fluttered repeatedly
> over their faces, stirred by the wind of their inhaling and exhaling breath.
> These beautiful, multicolored, and golden garments, fluttering at the base of
> their faces, shone like waving flags. As the wind from their mouths gently
> moved the beautiful earrings of some women, they swayed gracefully. The breath
> of those women, naturally fragrant and pleasant, smelling of sweet wine,
> served Rāvaṇa at that time.
---

58.
rāvaṇānanaśaṅkāśca kāścid rāvaṇayoṣitaḥ |
mukhāni sma sapatnīnām upājighran punaḥ punaḥ ||

59.
atyarthaṁ saktamanasaḥ rāvaṇe tā varastriyaḥ |
asvatantrāḥ sapatnīnāṁ priyam evācaraṁstadā ||

60.
bāhūn upanidhāyānyāḥ pārihārya vibhūṣitāḥ |
aṁśukāni ca ramyāṇi pramadāstatra śiśyire ||

61.
anyā vakṣasi cānyasyāḥ tasyāḥ kā cit punarbhujam |
aparā tv aṅkam anyasyāḥ tasyāścāpy aparā bhujau ||

62.
ūrupārśvakaṭīpr̥ṣṭham anyonyasya samāśritāḥ |
parasparaniviṣṭāṅgyaḥ madasnehavaśānugāḥ ||

> **Meaning 58-62:** Some of Rāvaṇa's wives, mistaking the faces of their
> co-wives for Rāvaṇa's face, kissed them again and again. Those excellent
> women, whose minds were deeply attached to Rāvaṇa, were no longer in control
> of themselves and thus did what was pleasing to their co-wives. Other women
> slept there using their bracelet-adorned arms and their beautiful fine
> garments as pillows. One woman rested on another's chest, another on her
> arm; one rested on another's lap, and yet another on her arms. Resting on
> each other's thighs, sides, waists, and backs, their bodies intertwined,
> completely under the sway of intoxication and affection.
---

63.
anyonyabhujasūtreṇa strīmālāgrathitā hi sā |
māleva grathitā sūtre śuśubhe mattaṣaṭpadā ||

64.
latānāṁ mādhave māsi phullānāṁ vāyusevanāt |
anyonyamālāgrathitaṁ saṁsaktakusumoccayam ||

65.
vyativeṣṭitasuskantham anyonyabhramarākulam |
āsīd vanam ivoddhūtaṁ strīvanaṁ rāvaṇasya tat ||

66.
uciteṣv api suvyaktaṁ na tāsāṁ yoṣitāṁ tadā |
vivekaḥ śakya ādhātuṁ bhūṣaṇāṅgāmbarasrajām ||

67.
rāvaṇe sukhasaṁviṣṭe tāḥ striyo vividhaprabhāḥ |
jvalantaḥ kāñcanā dīpāḥ prekṣantānimiṣā iva ||

> **Meaning 63-67:** That multitude of women formed a "forest of women," which
> shone like a forest of blossoming creepers in the spring season. Just as a
> forest of creepers sways in the cool spring breeze, the women undulated gently
> with the fragrant breath from their mouths. Like creepers intertwined with rows
> of flowers, the women's arms were intertwined with each other like garlands.
> Just as flowers rub against each other on the creepers, the flower garlands
> worn by the women were entangled together. Like creepers wrapping around tree
> trunks, their arms were wrapped around one another. And just as a forest is
> resplendent with swarms of bees gathered together, that forest of women shone
> brightly with their dark, intertwined curls of hair resembling swarms of bees.
> Their limbs, garments, ornaments, and garlands were so completely tangled
> together that it was impossible to distinguish them from one another. As Rāvaṇa
> slept comfortably, the brightly burning golden lamps seemed to gaze at those
> splendid women with unblinking eyes.
---
68.
rājarṣipitr̥daityānāṁ gandharvāṇāṁ ca yoṣitaḥ |
rakṣasāṁ cābhavan kanyāḥ tasya kāmavaśaṁ gatāḥ ||

> **Meaning 68:** They were the daughters of Rājarṣis, Pitr̥s, Daityas,
> Gandharvas, and Rākṣasas, who had come under his control due to their
> desire for him.
---

69.
yuddhakāmēna tāssarvāḥ rāvaṇēna hr̥tāsstriyaḥ |
samadā madanēnaiva mōhitāḥ kāścidāgatāḥ ||

70.
na tatra kāścit pramadā prasahya
vīryōpapannēna guṇēna labdhāḥ |
na cānyakāmāpi na cānyapūrvā
vinā varārhāṁ janakātmajāṁ tām ||

71.
na cākulīnā na ca hīnarūpā
nādakṣiṇā nānupacārayuktā |
bhāryābhavattasya na hīnasattvā
na cāpi kāṁtasya na kāmanīyā ||

72.
babhūva buddhistu harīśvarasya
yadīdr̥śī rāghavadharmapatnī |
imā yathā rākṣasarājabhāryāḥ
sujātamasyēti hi sādhubuddhēḥ ||

73.
punaśca sō’ciṁtayā dārtarūpaḥ
dhruvaṁ viśiṣṭā guṇatō hi sītā |
athāyamasyāṁ kr̥tavān mahātmā
laṁkēśvaraḥ kaṣṭa manāryakarma ||

> **Meaning 69-73:** All those women had been abducted by Rāvaṇa, who was eager
> for battle, while some had come of their own accord, intoxicated by passion
> for him. Except for the supremely worthy daughter of Janaka, there was no woman
> there who had been acquired by force simply by his might; neither was there
> any who desired another, nor any who is wife of someone else. None of
> them were of low birth, devoid of beauty, lacking in skill, or devoid of
> courtesy. Not a single wife of his lacked intelligence, nor was there anyone
> who was not dear to her husband. A thought struck the mind of the intelligent
> Kapīśvara: "Just as all these wives of the Rākṣasa king are happily united
> with their husband, if Sītā, the lawful wife of Rāghava, were also happily
> united with her husband, it would be auspicious for Rāvaṇa (meaning, if he
> had not abducted her or if he returned her, he would be spared). But then,
> thinking further, Hanumān became grief-stricken: "Mother Sītā is absolutely
> endowed with the most excellent virtues. Rāvaṇa was born into a great lineage,
> is the lord of Laṅkā, and a great devotee of Śiva. Yet, in a manner completely
> unworthy of his lineage, status, and devotion, he has committed a highly
> inappropriate and miserable act against her. How sorrowful this is!"
---
