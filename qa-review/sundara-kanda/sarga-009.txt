=== Sarga 9 ===

---
1.
tasyālayavariṣṭhasya madhye vipulam āyatam |
dadarśa bhavanaśreṣṭhaṁ hanūmān mārutātmajaḥ ||

> **Meaning 1:** In the center of that supreme palace complex, Hanumān, the son
> of Māruta, saw an immense and spacious mansion of extraordinary beauty.

---
2.
ardhayojanavistīrṇam āyataṁ yojanaṁ hi tat |
bhavanaṁ rākṣasendrasya bahuprāsādasaṁkulam ||

> **Meaning 2:** Half a yojana wide and a full yojana long, that residence of
> the king of Rākṣasas was filled with numerous palace towers.

---
3.
mārgamāṇastu vaidehīṁ sītām āyatalocanām |
sarvataḥ paricakrāma hanūmān arisūdanaḥ ||

> **Meaning 3:** Searching everywhere for the wide-eyed Vaidehī Sītā, Hanumān,
> the destroyer of foes, explored in every direction.

---
4.
uttamaṁ rākṣasāvāsaṁ hanumā navalōkayan |
āsasādātha lakṣmīvān rākṣasēṁdra nivēśanam ||

> **Meaning 4:** Looking all around that magnificent Rākṣasa dwelling, the
> illustrious Hanumān reached the inner palace of the king of Rākṣasas.

---
5.
caturviṣāṇairdviradaiḥ triviṣāṇaistathaiva ca |
parikṣiptam asaṁbādhaṁ rakṣyamāṇam udāyudhaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 5:** It was surrounded by elephants with four tusks and others with
> three, spacious and uncrowded, guarded by warriors with weapons raised.

---
6.
rākṣasībhiśca patnībhī rāvaṇasya niveśanam |
āhr̥tābhiśca vikramya rājakanyābhirāvr̥tam ||

> **Meaning 6:** That dwelling was filled with Rāvaṇa's Rākṣasī wives and with
> princesses abducted by force through his valor.

---
7.
tan nakramakarākīrṇaṁ timiṁgilajhaṣākulam |
vāyuvegasamādhūtaṁ pannagairiva sāgaram ||

> **Meaning 7:** It resembled the ocean filled with crocodiles and sea monsters,
> swarming with giant fish and timiṅgilas, agitated by fierce winds and
> inhabited by sea serpents.

---
8.
yā hi vaiśvaraṇe lakṣmīḥ yā cendre harivāhane |
sā rāvaṇagr̥he sarvā nityam evānapāyinī ||

> **Meaning 8:** Whatever splendor resides with Vaiśravaṇa, and whatever
> splendor belongs to Indra of tawny steeds, all of it dwelt permanently and
> without fail in Rāvaṇa's abode.

---
9.
yā ca rājñaḥ kuberasya yamasya varuṇasya ca |
tādr̥śī tad viśiṣṭā vā r̥ddhī rakṣo gr̥heṣv iha ||

> **Meaning 9:** The prosperity found in the mansions of King Kubera, of Yama,
> and of Varuṇa was matched or even surpassed by the opulence here in this
> Rākṣasa palace.

---
10.
tasya harmyasya madhyasthaṁ veśma cānyat sunirmitam |
bahuniryūha saṁkīrṇaṁ dadarśa pavanātmajaḥ ||

> **Meaning 10:** In the center of that palace complex, the son of Pavana saw
> another exquisitely built mansion, adorned with numerous turrets and arched
> gateways.

---
11.
brahmaṇo ’rthe kr̥taṁ divyaṁ divi yad viśvakarmaṇā |
vimānaṁ puṣpakaṁ nāma sarvaratnavibhūṣitam ||

> **Meaning 11:** It was the divine aerial chariot named Puṣpaka, adorned with
> all kinds of gems, crafted in heaven by Viśvakarmā for Brahmā.

---
12.
pareṇa tapasā lebhe yat kuberaḥ pitāmahāt |
kuberam ojasā jitvā lebhe tad rākṣaseśvaraḥ ||

> **Meaning 12:** Kubera had obtained it from the Grandfather through severe
> austerity, and the king of Rākṣasas had seized it by defeating Kubera with his
> might.

---
13.
īhā mr̥gasamāyuktaiḥ kāryasvarahiraṇmayaiḥ |
sukr̥tairācitaṁ stambhaiḥ pradīptam iva ca śriyā ||

> **Meaning 13:** Supported by well-crafted pillars carved with figures of
> mythical beasts and wrought of pure gold, it seemed ablaze with splendor.

---
14.
merumandarasaṁkāśaiḥ ullikhadbhirivāmbaram |
kūṭāgāraiḥ śubhākāraiḥ sarvataḥ samalaṁkr̥tam ||

> **Meaning 14:** It was adorned on all sides with graceful roof pavilions
> resembling Mount Meru and Mandara, appearing to scrape the very sky.

---
15.
jvalanārkapratīkāśaṁ sukr̥taṁ viśvakarmaṇā |
hemasopānasaṁyuktaṁ cārupravaravedikam ||

> **Meaning 15:** Resplendent like fire and the sun, masterfully created by
> Viśvakarmā, it was fitted with golden stairways and lovely, elevated
> platforms.

---
16.
jālavātāyanairyuktaṁ kāñcanaiḥ sthāṭikairapi |
indranīlamahānīlamaṇipravaravedikam |

> **Meaning 16:** It had latticed windows of gold and crystal, and raised daises
> studded with sapphires, large azure gems, and precious jewels.

---
17.
vidrumēṇa vicitrēṇa maṇibhiśca mahāghanaiḥ |
nistulābhiśca muktābhiḥ talēnābhi virājitam ||

18.
caṁdanēna ca raktēna tapanīyanibhēna ca |
supuṇyagaṁdhinā yuktaṁ ādityataruṇōpamam ||

19.
kūṭāgārairvarākāraiḥ vividhaissamalaṁkr̥tam |
vimānaṁ puṣpakaṁ divyaṁ ārurōha mahākapiḥ ||

20.
tatrasthaḥ sa tadā gandhaṁ pānabhakṣyānnasaṁbhavam |
divyaṁ saṁmūrchitaṁ jighran rūpavantam ivānilam ||

> **Meaning 17-20:** Its floors shone with variegated coral, priceless jewels,
> and flawless pearls. Anointed with fragrant red sandalwood of molten-gold hue,
> it glowed like the morning sun. Adorned with various magnificent domed
> pavilions, the great Kapi ascended that divine Puṣpaka Vimāna. Standing there,
> he inhaled the rich, heavenly fragrance of foods, drinks, and roasted
> delicacies, thick as if the wind itself had taken solid form.

---
21.
sa gandhastaṁ mahāsattvaṁ bandhurbandhum ivottamam |
ita ehīty uvāceva tatra yatra sa rāvaṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 21:** That aroma seemed to beckon the mighty hero like a dear
> kinsman calling out, "Come this way," drawing him toward the chamber where
> Rāvaṇa was.

---
22.
tatastāṁ prasthitaḥ śālāṁ dadarśa mahatīṁ śubhām |
rāvaṇasya manaḥkāntāṁ kāntām iva varastriyam ||

> **Meaning 22:** Moving in that direction, he saw an immense and splendid hall,
> dear to Rāvaṇa's heart like a beloved and noble lady.

---
23.
maṇisopānavikr̥tāṁ hemajālavirājitām |
sphāṭikairāvr̥tatalāṁ dantāntaritarūpikām ||

> **Meaning 23:** It had staircases inlaid with jewels, latticework of gold,
> floors paved with crystal, and decorative reliefs inlaid with ivory.

---
24.
muktābhiśca pravālaiśca rūpyacāmīkarairapi |
vibhūṣitāṁ maṇistambhaiḥ subahustambhabhūṣitām ||

> **Meaning 24:** Adorned with pearls, corals, silver, and gold, it was
> supported and embellished by countless jewel-encrusted pillars.

---
25.
samairr̥jubhiratyuccaiḥ samantāt suvibhūṣitaiḥ |
stambhaiḥ pakṣairivātyuccaiḥ divaṁ saṁprasthitām iva ||

> **Meaning 25:** With its tall, straight, and evenly set pillars standing like
> lofty wings on all sides, the hall seemed ready to take flight into the
> heavens.

---
26.
mahatyā kuthayāstrīṇaṁ pr̥thivīlakṣaṇāṅkayā |
pr̥thivīm iva vistīrṇāṁ sarāṣṭragr̥hamālinīm ||

> **Meaning 26:** It was carpeted with an immense rug patterned with maps of the
> earth, spreading out like the vast earth itself with all its kingdoms and
> settlements.

---
27.
nāditāṁ mattavihagaiḥ divyagandhādhivāsitām |
parārdhyāstaraṇopetāṁ rakṣo’dhipaniṣevitām ||

> **Meaning 27:** Resounding with the calls of pet birds, perfumed with
> celestial scents, furnished with the finest couches, it was frequented by the
> king of Rākṣasas.

---
28.
dhūmrām agarudhūpena vimalāṁ haṁsapāṇḍurām |
citrāṁ puṣpopahāreṇa kalmāṣīm iva suprabhām ||

> **Meaning 28:** Veiled in the fragrant smoke of burning agaru incense, pure
> and white as a swan, yet variegated with offerings of flowers, it glowed with
> mottled brilliance.

---
29.
manaḥsaṁhlādajananīṁ varṇasyāpi prasādinīm |
tāṁ śokanāśinīṁ divyāṁ śriyaḥ saṁjananīm iva ||

> **Meaning 29:** It delighted the heart, enhanced the radiance of one's
> complexion, dispelled all sorrow, and seemed to generate divine fortune
> itself.

---
30.
indriyāṇīndriyārthaistu pañca pañcabhiruttamaiḥ |
tarpayām āsa māteva tadā rāvaṇapālitā ||

> **Meaning 30:** Maintained by Rāvaṇa, that hall nurtured all five senses with
> the finest of fivefold sensory pleasures, like an affectionate mother.

---
31.
svargo ’yaṁ devaloko ’yam indrasyeyaṁ purī bhavet |
siddhirveyaṁ parā hi syād ity amanyata mārutiḥ ||

> **Meaning 31:** "Is this heaven? Is this the realm of the devatās? Can this be
> Indra's city? Or is this supreme divine accomplishment?" thought the son of
> Māruta.

---
32.
pradhyāyata ivāpaśyat pradīpāṁstatra kāñcanān |
dhūrtān iva mahādhūrtaiḥ devanena parājitān ||

> **Meaning 32:** He saw golden lamps burning motionless, as if deep in thought,
> resembling gamblers who had lost everything to sharper gamblers at dice.

---
33.
dīpānāṁ ca prakāśena tejasā rāvaṇasya ca |
arcirbhirbhūṣaṇānāṁ ca pradīptety abhyamanyata ||

> **Meaning 33:** Between the blaze of the lamps, the personal aura of Rāvaṇa,
> and the brilliance of jewels, he felt the whole hall was blazing with light.

---
34.
tato ’paśyat kuthāsīnaṁ nānāvarṇāmbarasrajam |
sahasraṁ varanārīṇāṁ nānāveṣavibhūṣitam ||

> **Meaning 34:** Then he saw thousands of beautiful women resting on the
> carpet, wearing colorful garments and garlands, adorned in diverse styles of
> dress.

---
35.
parivr̥tte ’rdharātre tu pānanidrāvaśaṁ gatam |
krīḍitvoparataṁ rātrau suṣvāpa balavat tadā ||

> **Meaning 35:** As midnight passed, overcome by wine and exhaustion after
> reveling in the night, they had ceased their play and fallen into deep sleep.

---
36.
tat prasuptaṁ viruruce niḥśabdāntarabhūṣaṇam |
niḥśabdahaṁsabhramaraṁ yathā padmavanaṁ mahat ||

> **Meaning 36:** That sleeping assembly, with all ornaments fallen silent,
> looked like a vast lotus pool where swans and bees had fallen quiet.

---
37.
tāsāṁ saṁvr̥tadantāni mīlitākṣāṇi mārutiḥ |
apaśyat padmagandhīni vadanāni suyoṣitām ||

> **Meaning 37:** The son of Māruta saw the faces of those beautiful women, with
> their eyes closed, their teeth covered by their lips, smelling sweet as
> lotuses.

---
38.
prabuddhānīva padmāni tāsāṁ bhūtvā kṣapākṣaye |
punaḥsaṁvr̥tapatrāṇi rātrāv iva babhustadā ||

> **Meaning 38:** Their faces looked like lotuses that had bloomed at day's
> close and then folded their petals again for the night.

---
39.
imāni mukhapadmāni niyataṁ mattaṣaṭpadāḥ |
ambujānīva phullāni prārthayanti punaḥ punaḥ ||

> **Meaning 39:** "Surely intoxicated bees seek out these lotus-faces again and
> again, mistaking them for blooming water lilies,"

---
40.
iti cāmanyata śrīmān upapattyā mahākapiḥ |
mene hi guṇatastāni samāni salilodbhavaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 40:** reasoned the illustrious great Kapi, for he judged those faces
> to match water-born lotuses in every pleasant quality.

---
41.
sā tasya śuśubhe śālā tābhiḥ strībhirvirājitā |
śāradīva prasannā dyauḥ tārābhirabhiśobhitā ||

> **Meaning 41:** That hall shone resplendent with those women, like a clear
> autumn sky adorned with constellations of stars.

---
42.
sa ca tābhiḥ parivr̥taḥ śuśubhe rākṣasādhipaḥ |
yathā hy uḍupatiḥ śrīmāṁḥ tārābhirabhisaṁvr̥taḥ ||

> **Meaning 42:** Surrounded by them, the king of Rākṣasas shone like the
> glorious moon encircled by stars.

---
43.
yāścyavante ’mbarāt tārāḥ puṇyaśeṣasamāvr̥tāḥ |
imāstāḥ saṁgatāḥ kr̥tsnā iti mene haristadā ||

> **Meaning 43:** Then the Hari thought: "These must be all the stars that fall
> from the sky when their merit is exhausted, gathered together here in one
> place."

---
44.
tārāṇām iva suvyaktaṁ mahatīnāṁ śubhārciṣām |
prabhāvarṇaprasādāśca virejustatra yoṣitām ||

> **Meaning 44:** The radiance, color, and serene grace of those women shone
> clearly like the bright beams of great stars.

---
45.
vyāvr̥ttagurupīnasrakprakīrṇavarabhūṣaṇāḥ |
pānavyāyāmakāleṣu nidrāpahr̥tacetasaḥ ||

> **Meaning 45:** With heavy, thick garlands displaced and fine jewelry
> scattered during their drinking and revelry, their senses had been stolen by
> sleep.

---
46.
vyāvr̥ttatilakāḥ kāścit kāścid udbhrāntanūpurāḥ |
pārśve galitahārāśca kāścit paramayoṣitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 46:** Some noble women had their forehead tilakas smudged, some had
> their anklets slipped out of place, and some had their necklaces fallen to
> their sides.

---
47.
mukhā hāravr̥tāścānyāḥ kāścit prasrastavāsasaḥ |
vyāviddharaśanā dāmāḥ kiśorya iva vāhitāḥ ||

> **Meaning 47:** Some had their faces covered by pearl necklaces, some had
> their clothes slipped off, and some had their girdle strings broken, looking
> like young mares exhausted after a long ride.

---
48.
sukuṇḍaladharāścānyā vicchinnamr̥ditasrajaḥ |
gajendramr̥ditāḥ phullā latā iva mahāvane ||

> **Meaning 48:** Others wearing beautiful earrings had their garlands crushed
> and broken, looking like blooming creepers trampled by a lordly elephant in a
> vast forest.

---
49.
candrāṁśukiraṇābhāśca hārāḥ kāsāṁ cid utkaṭāḥ |
haṁsā iva babhuḥ suptāḥ stanamadhyeṣu yoṣitām ||

> **Meaning 49:** Bright necklaces shining like moonbeams lay upon the breasts
> of some women, looking like swans sleeping between their curves.

---
50.
aparāsāṁ ca vaidūryāḥ kādambā iva pakṣiṇaḥ |
hemasūtrāṇi cānyāsāṁ cakravākā ivābhavan ||

> **Meaning 50:** The cat's-eye chains on others looked like dark-winged kādamba
> birds, while the golden cords on some resembled cakravāka birds.

---
51.
hamsakāraṇḍavākīrṇāḥ cakravākopaśobhitāḥ |
āpagā iva tā rejuḥ jaghanaiḥ pulinairiva ||

> **Meaning 51:** Resembling rivers filled with swans and ducks and adorned with
> cakravākas, those women shone with their hips looking like sandy riverbanks.

---
52.
kiṅkiṇījālasaṁkāśāḥ tā hemavipulāmbujāḥ |
bhāvagrāhā yaśastīrāḥ suptā nadya ivābabhuḥ ||

> **Meaning 52:** With their tiny girdle bells resembling ripples, their gold
> ornaments like large lotuses, their amorous sentiments like whirlpools, they
> appeared like slumbering rivers.

---
53.
mr̥duṣv aṅgeṣu kāsāṁ cit kucāgreṣu ca saṁsthitāḥ |
babhūvurbhūṣaṇānīva śubhā bhūṣaṇarājayaḥ ||

> **Meaning 53:** The imprints left by jewelry upon their soft forms and breasts
> looked just like lovely ornaments themselves.

---
54.
aṁśukāntāśca kāsāṁ cin mukhamārutakampitāḥ |
upary upari vaktrāṇāṁ vyādhūyante punaḥ punaḥ ||

> **Meaning 54:** The hems of the fine garments of some women, stirred by the
> breath from their mouths, fluttered repeatedly over their faces.

---
55.
tāḥ pātākā ivoddhūtāḥ patnīnāṁ ruciraprabhāḥ |
nānāvarṇasuvarṇānāṁ vaktramūleṣu rejire ||

> **Meaning 55:** Fluttering over the faces of those wives of golden complexion,
> those lovely multicolored cloth hems shone like raised banners.

---
56.
vavalguścātra kāsāṁ cit kuṇḍalāni śubhārciṣām |
mukhamārutasaṁsargān mandaṁ mandaṁ suyoṣitām ||

> **Meaning 56:** The radiant earrings of some beautiful women swayed gently in
> the soft breeze of their breath.

---
57.
śarkarāsavagandhaḥ sa prakr̥tyā surabhiḥ sukhaḥ |
tāsāṁ vadananiḥśvāsaḥ siṣeve rāvaṇaṁ tadā ||

> **Meaning 57:** The breath from their mouths, naturally sweet and carrying the
> aroma of sugar-wine, gently wafted over Rāvaṇa.

---
58.
rāvaṇānanaśaṅkāśca kāścid rāvaṇayoṣitaḥ |
mukhāni sma sapatnīnām upājighran punaḥ punaḥ ||

> **Meaning 58:** Some of Rāvaṇa's wives, mistaking their co-wives' faces for
> Rāvaṇa's, kissed and smelled their lips again and again in their sleep.

---
59.
atyarthaṁ saktamanasaḥ rāvaṇe tā varastriyaḥ |
asvatantrāḥ sapatnīnāṁ priyam evācaraṁstadā ||

> **Meaning 59:** So deeply devoted were their minds to Rāvaṇa that, lost in
> unconscious sleep, they acted lovingly toward their rivals.

---
60.
bāhūn upanidhāyānyāḥ pārihārya vibhūṣitāḥ |
aṁśukāni ca ramyāṇi pramadāstatra śiśyire ||

> **Meaning 60:** Restful women lay there using their arms adorned with
> bracelets as pillows, nestled in their lovely garments.

---
61.
anyā vakṣasi cānyasyāḥ tasyāḥ kā cit punarbhujam |
aparā tv aṅkam anyasyāḥ tasyāścāpy aparā bhujau ||

> **Meaning 61:** One woman slept on another's chest, another rested on a
> companion's arm, one lay in another's lap, and another held a friend's arms.

---
62.
ūrupārśvakaṭīpr̥ṣṭham anyonyasya samāśritāḥ |
parasparaniviṣṭāṅgyaḥ madasnehavaśānugāḥ ||

> **Meaning 62:** Supported by one another's thighs, sides, waists, and backs,
> their bodies were entwined under the spell of wine and affection.

---
63.
anyonyabhujasūtreṇa strīmālāgrathitā hi sā |
māleva grathitā sūtre śuśubhe mattaṣaṭpadā ||

> **Meaning 63:** Strung together by the thread of their intertwined arms, that
> garland of women shone like a floral wreath threaded on a string and settled
> with intoxicated bees.

---
64.
latānāṁ mādhave māsi phullānāṁ vāyusevanāt |
anyonyamālāgrathitaṁ saṁsaktakusumoccayam ||

> **Meaning 64:** Like a grove of creepers in the month of Mādhava, blossoming
> and swaying in the breeze, with flowers clustered and tangled with one
> another,

---
65.
vyativeṣṭitasuskantham anyonyabhramarākulam |
āsīd vanam ivoddhūtaṁ strīvanaṁ rāvaṇasya tat ||

> **Meaning 65:** their stems entwined and bees hovering between them, that
> forest of Rāvaṇa's women appeared like a blooming woodland stirred by the
> wind.

---
66.
uciteṣv api suvyaktaṁ na tāsāṁ yoṣitāṁ tadā |
vivekaḥ śakya ādhātuṁ bhūṣaṇāṅgāmbarasrajām ||

> **Meaning 66:** Even on close inspection, it was impossible to distinguish
> between the ornaments, features, garments, and garlands of those sleeping
> women.

---
67.
rāvaṇe sukhasaṁviṣṭe tāḥ striyo vividhaprabhāḥ |
jvalantaḥ kāñcanā dīpāḥ prekṣantānimiṣā iva ||

> **Meaning 67:** While Rāvaṇa slept peacefully, the burning golden lamps seemed
> to gaze with unblinking eyes upon those women of diverse beauty.

---
68.
rājarṣipitr̥daityānāṁ gandharvāṇāṁ ca yoṣitaḥ |
rakṣasāṁ cābhavan kanyāḥ tasya kāmavaśaṁ gatāḥ ||

> **Meaning 68:** There were daughters of royal sages, pitr̥s, Daityas,
> Gandharvas, and Rākṣasas, all brought under the power of his desire.

---
69.
yuddhakāmēna tāssarvāḥ rāvaṇēna hr̥tāsstriyaḥ |
samadā madanēnaiva mōhitāḥ kāścidāgatāḥ ||

> **Meaning 69:** All those women had been carried off by Rāvaṇa in his lust for
> conquest; some, overcome by passion, had come to him of their own desire.

---
70.
na tatra kāścit pramadā prasahya
vīryōpapannēna guṇēna labdhāḥ |
na cānyakāmāpi na cānyapūrvā
vinā varārhāṁ janakātmajāṁ tām ||

> **Meaning 70:** Not a single woman there had been taken by force without being
> won over by his valor and accomplishments; none loved another man, and none
> had belonged to another before — except that noble daughter of Janaka.

---
71.
na cākulīnā na ca hīnarūpā
nādakṣiṇā nānupacārayuktā |
bhāryābhavattasya na hīnasattvā
na cāpi kāṁtasya na kāmanīyā ||

> **Meaning 71:** There was no wife of his who was of low birth, deficient in
> beauty, devoid of grace, unskilled in serving him, lacking in spirit, or
> undesirable to her lover.

---
72.
babhūva buddhistu harīśvarasya
yadīdr̥śī rāghavadharmapatnī |
imā yathā rākṣasarājabhāryāḥ
sujātamasyēti hi sādhubuddhēḥ ||

> **Meaning 72:** The thought arose in the noble mind of the Hari chief: "If
> Rāghava's lawful wife were safe and happy like these wives of the Rākṣasa
> king, all would be well with him."

---
73.
punaśca sō’ciṁtayā dārtarūpaḥ
dhruvaṁ viśiṣṭā guṇatō hi sītā |
athāyamasyāṁ kr̥tavān mahātmā
laṁkēśvaraḥ kaṣṭa manāryakarma ||

> **Meaning 73:** Then, sorrowful at heart, he reflected: "Sītā is surely
> superior to all of them in virtue; yet this powerful king of Laṅkā has
