=== Sarga 11 ===

---
1.
avadhūya ca tāṁ buddhiṁ babhūvāvasthitastadā |
jagāma cāparāṁ cintāṁ sītāṁ prati mahākapiḥ ||


---
2.
na rāmeṇa viyuktā sā svaptum arhati bhāminī |
na bhoktuṁ nāpy alaṁkartuṁ na pānam upasevitum ||


---
3.
nānyaṁ naram upasthātuṁ surāṇām api ceśvaram |
na hi rāmasamaḥ kaścid vidyate tridaśeṣv api |



---
4.
anyeyam iti niścitya pānabhūmau cacāra saḥ |
pānabhūmau hariśreṣṭhaḥ sītāsaṁdarśanotsukaḥ ||


> **Meaning 1-4:** Shaking off that thought (that the sleeping woman was Sītā),
> the great Kapi then became steady and entertained another thought regarding
> Sītā. He thought, "Separated from Rāma, that noble lady would not sleep, nor
> eat, nor adorn herself, nor drink wine. She would not approach another man,
> even if he were the lord of the gods. Indeed, there is no one equal to Rāma
> even among the gods. Therefore, this must be someone else (she is definitely
> not Sītā)." Deciding thus, he wandered about the banquet hall. Eager to see
> Sītā, the foremost of Vānaras roamed the banquet hall.
---


5.
krīḍitenāparāḥ klāntā gītena ca tathā parāḥ |
nr̥ttena cāparāḥ klāntāḥ pānaviprahatāstathā ||


---
6.
murajeṣu mr̥daṅgeṣu pīṭhikāsu ca saṁsthitāḥ |
tathāstaraṇamukhyyeṣu saṁviṣṭāścāparāḥ striyaḥ ||


---
7.
aṅganānāṁ sahasreṇa bhūṣitena vibhūṣaṇaiḥ |
rūpasaṁlāpaśīlena yuktagītārthabhāṣiṇā ||


---
8.
deśakālābhiyuktena yuktavākyābhidhāyinā |
ratābhiratasaṁsuptaṁ dadarśa hariyūthapaḥ ||

9.
anyatrāpi varastrīṇāṁ rūpasaṁlāpaśālinām |
sahasraṁ yuvatīnāṁ tu prasuptaṁ sa dadarśa ha ||

10.
tāsāṁ madhye mahābāhuḥ śuśubhe rākṣaseśvaraḥ |
goṣṭhe mahati mukhyānāṁ gavāṁ madhye yathā vr̥ṣaḥ ||


---
11.
sa rākṣasendraḥ śuśubhe tābhiḥ parivr̥taḥ svayam |
kareṇubhiryathāraṇyaṁ parikīrṇo mahādvipaḥ ||


---
12.
sarvakāmairupetāṁ ca pānabhūmiṁ mahātmanaḥ |
dadarśa kapiśārdūlaḥ tasya rakṣaḥpatergr̥he ||


> **Meaning 5-12:** He saw some women exhausted from amorous play, some from
> singing, some from dancing, and others overcome by the effects of wine. Some
> women were sleeping on Murajas, Mṛdaṅgas, and wooden seats, while others
> rested on excellent carpets. The leader of the Vānaras saw Rāvaṇa sleeping,
> deeply engrossed in amorous pleasures, accompanied by thousands of women
> adorned with ornaments, who were skilled in engaging in pleasant
> conversations, capable of speaking appropriately about the meaning of songs,
> and who spoke suitable words according to the time and place. He also saw
> thousands of other excellent young women, skilled in beautiful conversations,
> sleeping elsewhere. In the midst of them, the great-armed lord of the Rākṣasas
> shone brightly, like a great bull in a large pen amidst foremost cows.
> Surrounded by them, the lord of the Rākṣasas shone like a massive elephant in
> a forest surrounded by female elephants. In the house of that lord of the
> Rākṣasas, the tiger among Vānaras saw the banquet hall, endowed with all
> objects of desire.
---



---
13.
mr̥gāṇāṁ mahiṣāṇāṁ ca varāhāṇāṁ ca bhāgaśaḥ |
tatra nyastāni māṁsāni pānabhūmau dadarśa saḥ ||


---
14.
raukmeṣu ca viśaleṣu bhājaneṣv ardhabhakṣitān |
dadarśa kapiśārdūla mayūrān kukkuṭāṁstathā ||


---
15.
varāhavārdhrāṇasakān dadhisauvarcalāyutān |
śalyān mr̥gamayūrāṁśca hanūmān anvavaikṣata ||


---
16.
kr̥karān vividhān siddhāṁḥ cakorān ardhabhakṣitān |
mahiṣān ekaśalyāṁśca chāgāṁśca kr̥taniṣṭhitān |



---
17.
lekhyam uccāvacaṁ peyaṁ bhojyāni vividhāni ca ||
tathāmlalavaṇottaṁsaiḥ vividhai rāgaṣāḍavaiḥ |



---
18.
hāra nūpurakeyūraiḥ apaviddhairmahādhanaiḥ |
pānabhājanavikṣiptaiḥ phalaiśca vividhairapi ||


> **Meaning 13-18:** In that banquet hall, he saw the meats of deer, buffaloes,
> and boars, arranged separately. The tiger among Vānaras saw half-eaten
> peacocks and roosters in large golden vessels. Hanumān also observed the meats
> of boars, rhinoceroses, porcupines, deer, and peacocks,
> seasoned with yogurt and Sauvarcala salt. He saw various types of partridges
> (Kṛkaras and Cakoras), buffaloes, fish or sea food,
> and goats half eaten. He saw foods meant to be licked, various
> drinks, and diverse edibles, along with various Rāga-ṣāḍavas (sweet and sour
> sauces) garnished with sour and salty ingredients. The place was scattered
> with highly valuable necklaces, anklets, and armlets that had been cast off,
> as well as drinking vessels and various fruits.
---





---
19.
kr̥tapuṣpopahārā bhūḥ adhikaṁ puṣyati śriyam |
tatra tatra ca vinyastaiḥ suśliṣṭaiḥ śayanāsanaiḥ ||



---
20.
pānabhūmirvinā vahniṁ pradīptevopalakṣyate |
bahuprakārairvividhaiḥ varasaṁskārasaṁskr̥taiḥ ||



---
21.
māṁsaiḥ kuśalasaṁyuktaiḥ pānabhūmigataiḥ pr̥thak |
divyāḥ prasannā vividhāḥ surāḥ kr̥tasurā api ||

22.
śarkarāsavamādhvīkāḥ puṣpāsavaphalāsavāḥ |
vāsacūrṇaiśca vividhaiḥ mr̥ṣṭāstaistaiḥ pr̥thakpr̥thak ||


---
23.
saṁtatā śuśubhe bhūmiḥ mālyaiśca bahusaṁsthitaiḥ |
hiraṇmayaiśca karakaiḥ bhājanaiḥ sphāṭikairapi ||


> **Meaning 19-23:** The floor, decorated with offerings of scattered flowers,
> enhanced the beauty of the place even more. With well-arranged beds and seats
> placed here and there, the banquet hall appeared to be blazing with various
> flames even without a fire, due to the various excellently prepared meats
> that were expertly cooked to be highly fragrant and placed separately. Māruti
> saw divine and delicious natural wines, as well as artificial wines: wines
> prepared from sugar (Śarkarāsava), honey/Madhūka flowers (Mādhvīka), flower
> nectar (Puṣpāsava), and fruits (Phalāsava). These were separately mixed with
> various fragrant powders. The expansive floor shone exceedingly, filled with
> numerous flower garlands, golden pitchers, and crystal vessels.
---
---
24.
jāmbūnadamayaiścānyaiḥ karakairabhisaṁvr̥tā |
rājateṣu ca kumbheṣu jāmbūnadamayeṣu ca ||



---
25.
pānaśreṣṭhaṁ tadā bhūri kapistatra dadarśa ha |
so ’paśyac chātakumbhāni śīdhormaṇimayāni ca ||



---
26.
rājatāni ca pūrṇāni bhājanāni mahākapiḥ |
kva cid ardhāvaśeṣāṇi kva cit pītāni sarvaśaḥ ||



---
27.
kva cin naiva prapītāni pānāni sa dadarśa ha |
kva cid bhakṣyāṁśca vividhān kva cit pānāni bhāgaśaḥ |
kva cid annāvaśeṣāṇi paśyan vai vicacāra ha ||


> **Meaning 24-27:** It was covered with other horn-shaped pitchers made of
> gold. At that time, the Kapīśvara saw abundant and excellent wine fully
> filled in silver and golden pots. The Mahākapi saw golden, gem-studded, and
> silver vessels full of Śīdhu wine (wine prepared from sugarcane juice). He
> observed that the wine in some vessels was half-consumed, in some it was
> completely drunk, and in others it was entirely untouched. Seeing various
> types of edibles in some places, drinks divided into portions in others, and
> leftover food elsewhere, Māruti wandered everywhere.
---



---
28.
kva cit prabhinnaiḥ karakaiḥ kva cid āloḍitairghaṭaiḥ |
kva cit saṁpr̥ktamālyāni jalāni ca phalāni ca ||


---
29.
śayanāny atra nārīṇāṁ śūnyāni bahudhā punaḥ |
parasparaṁ samāśliṣya kāścit suptā varāṅganāḥ ||


---
30.
kā cic ca vastram anyasyā apahr̥tyopaguhya ca |
upagamyābalā suptā nidrābalaparājitā ||


---
31.
tāsām ucchvāsavātena vastraṁ mālyaṁ ca gātrajam |
nātyarthaṁ spandate citraṁ prāpya mandam ivānilam ||


---
32.
candanasya ca śītasya śīdhormadhurasasya ca |
vividhasya ca mālyasya puṣpasya vividhasya ca ||


---
33.
bahudhā mārutastatra gandhaṁ vividham udvahan |
snānānāṁ candanānāṁ ca dhūpānāṁ caiva mūrchitaḥ ||



---
34.
pravavau surabhirgandhaḥ vimāne puṣpake tadā |
śyāmāvadātāstatrānyāḥ kāścit kr̥ṣṇā varāṅganāḥ ||



---
35.
kāścit kāñcanavarṇāṅgyaḥ pramadā rākṣasālaye |
tāsāṁ nidrāvaśatvāc ca madanena vimūrchitam ||



---
36.
padminīnāṁ prasuptānāṁ rūpam āsīd yathaiva hi |
evaṁ sarvam aśeṣeṇa rāvaṇāntaḥpuraṁ kapiḥ |
dadarśa sumahātejā na dadarśa ca jānakīm ||


> **Meaning 28-36:** In some places there were broken pitchers, in others
> overturned pots, and in some places water and fruits were mixed with garlands.
> In that place, the beds of the women were empty in many ways, while some
> beautiful women slept embracing each other. Women, entirely subdued by
> sleepiness, pulled the blankets of other women sleeping next to them and
> wrapped themselves in them, moving close to them. As they breathed, the
> blankets and garlands on their bodies gently fluttered, as if moved by a
> gentle breeze. The breeze blowing there carried various fragrances—of cool
> sandalwood, sweet Śīdhu wine, diverse garlands, and various flowers—and,
> heavily laden with the scents of baths, sandalwood, and incense, the fragrant
> breeze blew in the Puṣpaka Vimāna. In that abode of the Rākṣasas, some women
> were fair-complexioned, some were dark, and others were golden-hued. Due to
> their subjugation by sleep and being overcome by intoxication, their
> appearance was exactly like that of a pond of closed lotuses. Thus, the
> immensely powerful Kapi completely saw the entire inner apartment of Rāvaṇa,
> but he did not see Jānakī.
---



---
37.
nirīkṣamāṇaśca tataḥ tāḥ striyaḥ sa mahākapiḥ |
jagāma mahatīṁ cintāṁ dharmasādhvasaśaṅkitaḥ ||


---
38.
paradārāvarodhasya prasuptasya nirīkṣaṇam |
idaṁ khalu mamātyarthaṁ dharmalopaṁ kariṣyati ||


---
39.
na hi me paradārāṇāṁ dr̥ṣṭirviṣayavartinī |
ayaṁ cātra mayā dr̥ṣṭaḥ paradāraparigrahaḥ ||


---
40.
tasya prādurabhūc cintāpunar anyā manasvinaḥ |
niścitaikāntacittasya kāryaniścayadarśinī ||


---
41.
kāmaṁ dr̥ṣṭvā mayā sarvā viśvastā rāvaṇastriyaḥ |
na tu me manasaḥ kiṁ cid vaikr̥tyam upapadyate ||


---
42.
mano hi hetuḥ sarveṣām indriyāṇāṁ pravartate |
śubhāśubhāsv avasthāsu tac ca me suvyavasthitam ||


> **Meaning 37-42:** Then, gazing at those women, the Mahākapi fell into deep
> thought, alarmed by the fear of violating Dharma (righteousness). "Gazing upon
> the sleeping wives of another man will surely cause a great destruction of my
> Dharma. My gaze does not wander towards the wives of others, but here I have
> also seen the man who embraced other women." Then, another thought arose in the
> mind of that intelligent one, who had a firm and focused mind, which helped
> him determine his course of action. "Even though I have seen all the wives of
> Rāvaṇa freely, no distortion or impure thought has arisen in my mind. The mind
> is indeed the cause for all the senses to engage in auspicious or inauspicious
> actions, but my mind is well-established in righteousness."
---



---
43.
nānyatra hi mayā śakyā vaidehī parimārgitum |
striyo hi strīṣu dr̥śyante sadā saṁparimārgaṇe ||


---
44.
yasya sattvasya yā yoniḥ tasyāṁ tat parimārgyate |
na śakyaṁ pramadā naṣṭā mr̥gīṣu parimārgitum ||


---
45.
tad idaṁ mārgitaṁ tāvac chuddhena manasā mayā |
rāvaṇāntaḥpuraṁ saraṁ dr̥śyate na ca jānakī ||


---
46.
devagandharvakanyāśca nāgakanyāśca vīryavān |
avekṣamāṇo hanumān naivāpaśyata jānakīm ||


---
47.
tām apaśyan kapistatra paśyaṁścānyā varastriyaḥ |
apakramya tadā vīraḥ pradhyātum upacakrame ||


---
48.
sa bhūyastu paraṁ śrīmān mārutiryatnamāsthitaḥ |
āpānabhūmimutsṛjya tāṁ vicetuṁ pracakrame ||


> **Meaning 43-48:** "How could I search for Vaidehī in any other place? A
> search for a woman must certainly be made among women. A creature is always
> sought within its own species; one would not search for a lost woman among
> female deer. Therefore, I have searched this entire inner apartment of
> Rāvaṇa with a pure mind, but Jānakī is not to be seen." Though the valiant
> Hanumān saw the daughters of Devas, Gandharvas, and Nāgas, he did not see
> Jānakī. Not seeing her there while seeing other excellent women, the heroic
> son of Vāyu stepped away and began to think deeply. Then, the highly
> illustrious Māruti, maintaining his great effort, left the banquet hall and
> set out to search for her again.
