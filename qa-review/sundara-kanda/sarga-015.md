=== Sarga 15 ===

---

1.
sa vīkṣamāṇas tatrastho mārgamāṇaś ca maithilīm |
avekṣamāṇaś ca mahīṁ sarvāṁ tām anvavaikṣata ||

2.
santāna kalatābhiś ca pādapair upaśobhitām |
divyagandharasopetāṁ sarvataḥ samalaṅkr̥tām ||

3.
tāṁ sa nandanasaṅkāśāṁ mr̥gapakṣibhir āvr̥tām |
harmyaprāsādasambādhāṁ kokilākulaniḥsvanām ||

4.
kāñcanotpalapadmābhir vāpībhir upaśobhitām |
bahvāsanakuthopetāṁ bahubhūmigr̥hāyutām ||

5.
sarvartukusumai ramyaiḥ phalavadbhiś ca pādapaiḥ |
puṣpitānām aśokānāṁ śriyā sūryodayaprabhām ||

> **Meaning 1-5:** Standing there, searching for the daughter of Janaka and
> observing the entire land, he looked all around. He beheld that Aśoka
> grove, which was adorned with Santānaka trees and creepers, filled with
> divine fragrances and juices, and beautifully decorated on all sides. It
> resembled the Nandana garden of Indra, was filled with animals and birds,
> crowded with mansions and palaces, and resonant with the sweet notes of
> cuckoos. It was graced by ponds adorned with golden lotuses and water
> lilies, provided with many resting places and carpets, and featured
> numerous subterranean chambers. That forest was resplendent with
> delightful trees bearing flowers and fruits of all seasons, and with the
> beauty of blooming Aśoka trees, it radiated the brilliance of the rising
> sun.
---

6.
pradīptām iva tatrastho mārutiḥ samudaikṣata |
niṣpatraśākhāṁ vihagaiḥ kriyamāṇām ivāsakr̥t ||

7.
viniṣpatadbhiḥ śataśaś citraiḥ puṣpāvataṁsakaiḥ |
āmūlapuṣpanicitair aśokaiḥ śokanāśanaiḥ ||

8.
puṣpabhārātibhāraiś ca spr̥śadbhir iva medinīm |
karṇikāraiḥ kusumitaiḥ kiṁśukaiś ca supuṣpitaiḥ ||

9.
sa deśaḥ prabhayā teṣāṁ pradīpta iva sarvataḥ |
punnāgāḥ saptaparṇāś ca campakoddālakās tathā ||

10.
vivr̥ddhamūlā bahavaḥ śobhante sma supuṣpitāḥ |
śātakumbhanibhāḥ ke cit ke cid agniśikhopamāḥ ||

11.
nīlāñjananibhāḥ ke cit tatrāśokāḥ sahasraśaḥ |
nandanaṁ vividhodyānaṁ citraṁ caitrarathaṁ yathā ||

> **Meaning 6-11:** Standing there, the son of Vāyu gazed at the grove,
> which was illuminated as if by fire. Due to hundreds of birds repeatedly
> perching on them, the branches appeared as if they had only birds and no
> leaves. As those birds flew away carrying flowers in their beaks, they
> looked as if they were adorned with wonderful floral ornaments. Hanumān
> watched the birds perching on the trees and flying away from there. The
> Aśoka trees, blossoming from their roots upwards, destroyed the sorrow of
> the onlookers. Māruti looked all around that Aśoka grove, which was
> resplendent with Karṇikāra trees bent under the heavy burden of flowers as
> if touching the earth, and beautifully blooming Kiṁśuka trees. That
> entire forest region shone brilliantly with the splendor of the trees.
> Countless Punnāga trees with thick trunks, Saptaparṇa trees, Campaka
> trees, and Uddālaka trees were shining full of flowers. Among the
> thousands of Aśoka trees there, some shone with golden colors, some with
> fiery brilliance, and some with the dark color of collyrium. That
> wonderful forest shone brilliantly, evoking wonder like the divine Nandana
> garden and the enchanting Caitraratha garden of Kubera.
---

12.
ativr̥ttam ivācintyaṁ divyaṁ ramyaṁ śriyā vr̥tam |
dvitīyam iva cākāśaṁ puṣpajyotirgaṇāyutam ||

13.
puṣparatnaśataiś citraṁ pañcamaṁ sāgaraṁ yathā |
sarvartupuṣpair nicitaṁ pādapair madhugandhibhiḥ ||

14.
nānāninādair udyānaṁ ramyaṁ mr̥gagaṇair dvijaiḥ |
anekagandhapravahaṁ puṇyagandhaṁ manoramam |
śailendram iva gandhāḍhyaṁ dvitīyaṁ gandhamādanam ||

> **Meaning 12-14:** That divine and delightful grove, adorned with
> unimaginable splendor and surpassing all others, looked like a second sky
> studded with multitudes of stars. With its hundreds of floral
> jewels, it appeared wonderful like a fifth ocean. That enchanting grove
> was filled with trees blossoming in every season, emitting the fragrance
> of honey, echoing with various sounds, and inhabited by flocks of animals
> and birds. It carried manifold fragrances, possessed a pure and
> captivating scent, and, being rich in aromas, resembled a second Mount
> Gandhamādana, the king of mountains.
---

15.
aśokavanikāyāṁ tu tasyāṁ vānarapuṅgavaḥ |
sa dadarśāvidūrasthaṁ caityaprāsādam ūrjitam ||

16.
madhye stambhasahasreṇa sthitaṁ kailāsapāṇḍuram |
pravālakr̥tasopānaṁ taptakāñcanavedikam ||

17.
muṣṇantam iva cakṣūṁṣi dyotamānam iva śriyā |
vimalaṁ prāṁśubhāvatvād ullikhantam ivāmbaram ||

> **Meaning 15-17:** In that Aśoka grove, the foremost of the Vānaras beheld
> a magnificent Caitya-palace situated not far away. It was supported in the
> middle by a thousand pillars, was as white as Mount Kailāśa, featured
> steps made of coral, and had platforms of refined gold. It appeared as if
> stealing the eyes, shining with brilliance; being spotless and
> exceptionally tall, it seemed to scrape the sky.
---

18.
tato malinasaṁvītāṁ rākṣasībhiḥ samāvr̥tām ||

19.
upavāsakr̥śāṁ dīnāṁ niḥśvasāntīṁ punaḥ punaḥ |
dadarśa śuklapakṣādau candrarekhām ivāmalām ||

20.
mandaprakhyāyamānena rūpeṇa ruciraprabhām |
pinaddhāṁ dhūmajālena śikhām iva vibhāvasoḥ ||

21.
pītenaikena saṁvītāṁ kliṣṭenottamavāsasā |
sapaṅkām analaṅkārāṁ vipadmām iva padminīm ||

22.
vrīḍitāṁ duḥkhasantaptāṁ parimlānāṁ tapasvinīm |
graheṇāṅgārakeṇaiva pīḍitām iva rohiṇīm ||

23.
aśrupūrṇamukhīṁ dīnāṁ kr̥śām ananaśena ca |
śokadhyānaparāṁ dīnāṁ nityaṁ duḥkhaparāyaṇām ||

24.
priyaṁ janam apaśyantīṁ paśyantīṁ rākṣasīgaṇam |
svagaṇena mr̥gīṁ hīnāṁ śvagaṇābhivr̥tām iva ||

> **Meaning 18-24:** Then, he beheld a lady clad in a soiled garment,
> surrounded by Rākṣasīs, emaciated from fasting, utterly distressed, and
> sighing repeatedly. She appeared like the spotless crescent moon at the
> beginning of the bright fortnight. With her beauty shining faintly, she
> possessed a radiant aura but looked like a flame of fire enveloped by
> smoke. Wrapped in a single, worn-out, excellent yellow garment, she was
> covered in dust and devoid of ornaments, resembling a lotus pond without
> lotuses. Modest, tormented by sorrow, faded, and leading a life of
> austerities, she appeared like the star Rohiṇī afflicted by the planet
> Mars. With a face bathed in tears, she was distressed, emaciated by
> fasting, immersed in sorrowful contemplation, and perpetually given to
> grief. Not seeing her beloved ones but beholding the terrifying horde of
> Rākṣasīs, she resembled a doe separated from its herd and surrounded by a
> pack of hounds.
---

25.
nīlanāgābhayā veṇyā jaghanaṁ gatayaikayā |
nīlayā nīradāpāye vanarājya mahimiva||

26.
sukhārhāṁ duḥkhasantaptāṁ vyasanānām akodivām |
tāṁ samīkṣya viśālākṣīm adhikaṁ malināṁ kr̥śām ||

27.
tarkayām āsa sīteti kāraṇair upapādibhiḥ |
hriyamāṇā tadā tena rakṣasā kāmarūpiṇā ||

28.
yathārūpā hi dr̥ṣṭā vai tathārūpeyam aṅganā ||

pūrṇacandrānanāṁ subhrūṁ cāruvr̥ttapayodharām |

29.
kurvantīṁ prabhayā devīṁ sarvā vitimirā diśaḥ ||

tāṁ nīlakeśīṁ bimbauṣṭhīṁ sumadhyāṁ supratiṣṭhitām |

30.
sītāṁ padmapalāśākṣīṁ manmathasya ratiṁ yathā ||

iṣṭāṁ sarvasya jagataḥ pūrṇacandraprabhām iva |

31.
bhūmau sutanum āsīnāṁ niyatām iva tāpasīm ||

niḥśvāsabahulāṁ bhīruṁ bhujagendravadhūm iva |

32.
śokajālena mahatā vitatena na rājatīm ||

saṁsaktāṁ dhūmajālena śikhām iva vibhāvasoḥ |

33.
tāṁ smr̥tīm iva sandigdhām r̥ddhiṁ nipatitām iva ||

vihatām iva ca śraddhām āśāṁ pratihatām iva |

34.
sopasargāṁ yathā siddhiṁ buddhiṁ sakaluṣām iva |
abhūtenāpavādena kīrtiṁ nipatitām iva ||

35.
rāmoparodhavyathitāṁ rakṣoharaṇakarśitām |
abalāṁ mr̥gaśāvākṣīṁ vīkṣamāṇāṁ tatas tataḥ ||

> **Meaning 25-35:** Wearing her hair in a single braid that resembled a
> dark serpent, she looked like the earth adorned with a line of dark
> forests when the monsoon clouds have vanished. She who was deserving of
> happiness and unaccustomed to suffering was now deeply tormented by
> sorrow. Observing that wide-eyed lady, who was exceedingly soiled and
> emaciated, Hanumān reasoned her to be Sītā through logical deductions. He
> concluded, "This lady possesses the same exquisite form as the one seen
> when she was being carried away by that Rākṣasa who can assume any form at
> will." With a face like the full moon, beautiful eyebrows, and lovely,
> rounded breasts, that divine lady illuminated all directions with her
> radiance. With dark hair, lips like the Bimba fruit, a slender waist, and
> a well-established presence, whose eyes were like lotus petals, she
> resembled Rati, the consort of Manmatha—she is Sītā. Beloved by the entire
> world like the radiance of the full moon, that lady with a beautiful form
> sat firmly on the ground like a disciplined ascetic. Sighing heavily, she
> was timid like a captive female serpent, and her brilliance was obscured
> by the vast, spreading net of sorrow. She resembled a flame of fire
> enveloped in smoke, a memory that has become doubtful, or prosperity that
> has plummeted. She looked like faith that has been shattered, hope that
> has been frustrated, success obstructed by obstacles, or an intellect made
> turbid. She was like a pristine reputation ruined by baseless false
> accusations. Distressed by the separation from Śrī Rāma and weakened by
> the Rākṣasa's abduction, that helpless lady with eyes like a fawn was
> looking hither and thither with timid glances; Māruti beheld that Sītā
> Devī.
---

36.
bāṣpāmbupratipūrṇena kr̥ṣṇavaktrākṣipakṣmaṇā |
vadanenāprasannena niḥśvasantīṁ punaḥ punaḥ ||

37.
malapaṅkadharāṁ dīnāṁ maṇḍanārhām amaṇḍitām |
prabhāṁ nakṣatrarājasya kālameghair ivāvr̥tām ||

38.
tasya sandidihe buddhir muhuḥ sītāṁ nirīkṣya tu |
āmnāyānām ayogena vidyāṁ praśithilām iva ||

39.
duḥkhena bubudhe sītāṁ hanumān analaṅkr̥tām |
saṁskāreṇa yathāhīnāṁ vācam arthāntaraṁ gatām ||

> **Meaning 36-39:** With eyes brimming with tears, dark eyelashes, and a
> cheerless face, she was sighing repeatedly. She who was worthy of
> adornments remained unadorned and covered in dirt; looking like the
> radiance of the moon obscured by dark clouds. Having observed Sītā
> repeatedly, Māruti fell into doubt. Just as knowledge diminishes without
> continuous study of the Vedas, and just as a sentence's true meaning is
> altered due to a lack of refinement, Hanumān recognized that unadorned
> Sītā with great difficulty.
---

40.
tāṁ samīkṣya viśālākṣīṁ rājaputrīm aninditām |
tarkayām āsa sīteti kāraṇair upapādayan ||

41.
vaidehyā yāni cāṅgeṣu tadā rāmo ’nvakīrtayat |
tāny ābharaṇajālāni gātraśobhīny alakṣayat ||

42.
sukr̥tau karṇaveṣṭau ca śvadaṁṣṭrau ca susaṁsthitau |
maṇividrumacitrāṇi hasteṣv ābharaṇāni ca ||

43.
śyāmāni cirayuktatvāt tathā saṁsthānavanti ca |
tāny evaitāni manye ’haṁ yāni rāmo ’vnakīrtayat ||

44.
tatra yāny avahīnāni tāny ahaṁ nopalakṣaye |
yāny asyā nāvahīnāni tānīmāni na saṁśayaḥ ||

45.
pītaṁ kanakapaṭṭābhaṁ srastaṁ tad vasanaṁ śubham |
uttarīyaṁ nagāsaktaṁ tadā dr̥ṣṭaṁ plavaṅgamaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 40-45:** Having carefully observed that flawless princess with
> wide eyes, he reasoned her to be Sītā, establishing his conclusion through
> clear evidence. Śrī Rāma had told Hanumān about the ornaments she was
> wearing. He saw all of them hanging on the branches. He saw beautifully
> crafted earrings, well-arranged ear ornaments named Śvadaṁṣṭra, and
> wonderful jewelry on her hands studded with gems and corals. Having been
> worn for a long time, they had turned dark, blackened by the heat of her
> body; likewise, those resting on the branches had also turned dark. He
> thought to himself, "I believe these are the very same ornaments that Śrī
> Rāma described that day. The ornaments which were dropped by her on the
> R̥ṣyamūka mountain when Rāvaṇa was abducting her are not seen here. There
> is no doubt that these are the ornaments not dropped by her. That day, an
> excellent yellow upper garment with golden borders, which was dropped on
> the R̥ṣyamūka mountain and snagged on a tree, was seen by the Vānaras."
---

46.
bhūṣaṇāni ca mukhyāni dr̥ṣṭāni dharaṇītale |
anayaivāpaviddhāni svanavanti mahānti ca ||

47.
idaṁ ciragr̥hītatvād vasanaṁ kliṣṭavattaram |
tathā hi nūnaṁ tad varṇaṁ tathā śrīmad yathetarat ||

48.
iyaṁ kanakavarṇāṅgī rāmasya mahiṣī priyā |
pranaṣṭāpi satī yasya manaso na praṇaśyati ||

49.
iyaṁ sā yat kr̥te rāmaś caturbhiḥ paritapyate |
kāruṇyenānr̥śaṁsyena śokena madanena ca ||

50.
strī pranaṣṭeti kāruṇyād āśritety ānr̥śaṁsyataḥ |
patnī naṣṭeti śokena priyeti madanena ca ||

51.
asyā devyā yathā rūpam aṅgapratyaṅgasauṣṭhavam |
rāmasya ca yathārūpaṁ tasyeyam asitekṣaṇā ||

> **Meaning 46-51:** "Those magnificent, prominent ornaments that fell to
> the earth sounding sweetly were also dropped by her. Since this garment
> has been worn for a long time, it is quite worn out; nevertheless, its
> color and splendor remain exactly as the other one. This lady with a
> golden complexion is the beloved queen of Rāma; though lost to sight, she,
> being devoted, has never vanished from his heart. This is she for whose
> sake Śrī Rāma is intensely tormented by four things. Women must be
> protected in times of distress; thinking 'I could not protect her,' he
> suffers out of compassion. As it is his nature to protect those who seek
> refuge in him, he suffers out of tenderness. He suffers out of grief for
> his lost wife, and out of love, longing for his beloved. Sītā Devī is a
> beauty surpassing all worlds with the perfection of every limb, and Śrī
> Rāma is captivating among men with his handsome features. In every way,
> they are perfectly suited for each other. Thus, this slender lady is the
> rightful wife of Śrī Rāma."
---

52.
asyā devyā manas tasmiṁs tasya cāsyāṁ pratiṣṭhitam |
teneyaṁ sa ca dharmātmā muhūrtam api jīvati ||

53.
duṣkaraṁ kṛtavān rāmo hīno yadanayā prabhuḥ |
dhārayatyātmano dehaṁ na śokenāvasīdati ||

54.
duṣkaraṁ kurute rāmo ya imāṁ mattakāśinīm |
sītāṁ vinā mahābāhur muhūrtam api jīvati ||

55.
evaṁ sītāṁ tadā dr̥ṣṭvā hr̥ṣṭaḥ pavanasambhavaḥ |
jagāma manasā rāmaṁ praśaśaṁsa ca taṁ prabhum ||

> **Meaning 52-55:** "The form of Sītā completely fills the mind of Śrī Rāma,
> and in the mind of Sītā Devī, the form of Śrī Rāma is perfectly complete.
> Therefore, both of these righteous souls are able to live; without each
> other, neither of them could survive even for a moment. Even though
> separated from the great chaste lady Sītā Devī, Śrī Rāmacandra sustains
> his life and performs an impossibly difficult task. His maintaining
> courage and steadfastness in such a state of grief is praiseworthy. It is
> truly an impossible task that Śrī Rāma, who cannot endure being without
> this enchanting lady Sītā Devī even for a moment, has survived until now."
> Having thus seen Sītā, Māruti rejoiced immensely. Then he remembered Śrī
> Rāmacandra and praised that Prabhu profusely. (It is only proper for Śrī
> Rāma to be intensely tormented for the sake of such a highly chaste and
> noble wife. Only he is worthy of having such a virtuous woman as his
> wife.)
---
