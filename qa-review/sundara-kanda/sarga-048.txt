=== Sarga 48 ===

---
1.
tatastu rakṣaḥ adhipatirmahātmā hanūmatākṣe nihate kumāre |
manaḥ samādhāya tadendrakalpaṁ samādideśendrajitaṁ sa roṣāt ||

> **Meaning 1:** Then the great Rākṣasa king, after Hanumān killed Prince Akṣa,
> steadied his mind. In his anger he gave his orders to Indrajit, who was equal
> to Indra himself.

---
2.
tvam astravicchastrabhr̥tāṁ variṣṭhaḥ surāsurāṇām api śokadātā |
sureṣu sendreṣu ca dr̥ṣṭakarmā pitāmahārādhanasaṁcitāstraḥ ||

> **Meaning 2:** Rāvaṇa said: You know the astras and are the best of those who
> bear weapons. You have brought grief even to the devatās and asuras. You have
> shown your deeds against the devatās, even with Indra among them, and you
> gained your astras by worshipping Pitāmaha (brahmā).

---
3.
tavāstrabalam āsādya nāsurā na marudgaṇāḥ |
na kaścit triṣu lokeṣu saṁyuge na gataśramaḥ ||

> **Meaning 3:** Against the power of your astras, neither the asuras nor the
> Marut hosts could stand. No one in the three worlds has ever fought you in
> battle without being worn out.

---
4.
bhujavīryābhiguptaśca tapasā cābhirakṣitaḥ |
deśakālavibhāgajñaḥ tvam eva matisattamaḥ ||

> **Meaning 4:** You are protected by the strength of your arms and guarded by
> your austerities. You understand the right place and time for action, and you
> are truly the wisest of all.

---
5.
na te asty aśakyaṁ samareṣu karmaṇā na te asty akāryaṁ matipūrvamantraṇe |
na saḥ asti kaścit triṣu saṁgraheṣu vai na veda yaste astrabalaṁ balaṁ ca te ||

> **Meaning 5:** Nothing in battle is beyond your power, and nothing is beyond
> you in careful counsel. No one in the three worlds fails to know your astra
> power and your strength.

---
6.
mamānurūpaṁ tapaso balaṁ ca te parākramaścāstrabalaṁ ca saṁyuge |
na tvāṁ samāsādya raṇāvamarde manaḥ śramaṁ gacchati niścitārtham ||

> **Meaning 6:** Your austerity, your strength, your courage and your astra
> power in battle all match mine. When I meet you in the heat of war, my mind
> feels no worry about the outcome.

---
7.
nihatā iṁkarāḥ sarve jambumālī ca rākṣasaḥ |
amātyaputrā vīrāśca pañca senāgrayāyinaḥ ||

> **Meaning 7:** All the Kiṅkaras have been killed, and so has the Rākṣasa
> Jambumālī. The sons of my ministers are dead, and so are the five heroic
> generals who led the army.

---
8.
sahodaraste dayitaḥ kumāraḥ akṣaśca sūditaḥ |
na tu teṣv eva me sāraḥ yastvayy ariniṣūdana ||

> **Meaning 8:** Your dear brother, Prince Akṣa, has also been killed. But I
> don't place my hope in them as I place it in you, conqueror of enemies.

---
9.
idaṁ hi dr̥ṣṭvā matiman mahad balaṁ kapeḥ prabhāvaṁ ca parākramaṁ ca |
tvam ātmanaścāpi samīkṣya sāraṁ kuruṣva vegaṁ svabalānurūpam ||

> **Meaning 9:** Wise one, see this great strength of the Kapi, his power and
> his courage. Weigh your own strength too, and then make an effort that matches
> your power.

---
10.
balāvamardastvayi saṁnikr̥ṣṭe yathā gate śāmyati śāntaśatrau |
tathā samīkṣyātmabalaṁ paraṁ ca samārabhasvāstravidāṁ variṣṭha ||

> **Meaning 10:** Once you are close, the Kapi's crushing force will fade, just
> as a fire dies down when its enemy is gone. So judge your own strength and the
> enemy's, and then begin, best of those who know astras.

---
11.
na vīra sēnā gaṇaśōcyavaṁti
na vajramādāya viśālasāram |
na mārutasyāsya gatēḥ pramāṇaṁ
na cāgnikalpaḥ karaṇēna haṁtum || 11 ||

> **Meaning 11:** Hero, armies in great numbers are not the answer, nor is
> taking up a heavy thunderbolt. Nor can his wind-like speed be measured, and no weapon blazing like fire can kill him.

---
12.
tamēva marthaṁ prasamīkṣya samyak
svakarmasāmyāddhi samāhitātmā |
smaraṁśca divyaṁ dhanuṣaḥ astravīryaṁ
vrajā’kṣataṁ karma samārabhasva ||

> **Meaning 12:** Consider this very matter carefully, matching it to your own
> proven deeds. Keep your mind steady and remember the divine power of your bow
> and astras. Then go and begin an action that cannot fail.

---
13.
na khalv iyaṁ matiḥ śreṣṭhā yat tvāṁ saṁpreṣayāmy aham |
iyaṁ ca rājadharmāṇāṁ kṣatrasya ca matirmatā ||

> **Meaning 13:** This is not the best course, that I am sending a youth like you, but it is how kings and kṣatriyas see their duty.

---
14.
nānāśastraiśca saṁgrāme vaiśāradyam ariṁdama |
avaśyam eva boddhavyaṁ kāmyaśca vijayo raṇe ||

> **Meaning 14:** Conqueror of foes, in battle one must know skill with many
> kinds of weapons, and victory in war is something to be wished for.

---
15.
tataḥ pitustad vacanaṁ niśamya pradakṣiṇaṁ dakṣasutaprabhāvaḥ |
cakāra bhartāram adīnasattvaḥ raṇāya vīraḥ pratipannabuddhiḥ ||

> **Meaning 15:** Hearing his father's words, the heroic and highly capable Indrajit, circled his father in respect. Fearless and
> firm in resolve, he set out for battle.

---
16.
tatastaiḥ svagaṇairiṣṭaiḥ indrajit pratipūjitaḥ |
yuddhoddhatakr̥totsāhaḥ saṁgrāmaṁ pratipadyata ||

> **Meaning 16:** Then his dear companions honored Indrajit. Eager for the fight
> and full of enthusiasm, he went into the battle.

---
17.
śrīmān padmapalāśākṣaḥ rākṣasādhipateḥ sutaḥ |
nirjagāma mahātejāḥ samudra iva parvasu ||

> **Meaning 17:** The glorious son of the Rākṣasa king, lotus-eyed and mighty, surged forth like the ocean swelling on a full-moon day.

---
18.
sa pakṣi rājopamatulyavegaiḥ vyālaiścaturbhiḥ sitatīkṣṇadaṁṣṭraiḥ |
rathaṁ samāyuktam asaṁgavegaṁ samārurohendrajid indrakalpaḥ ||

> **Meaning 18:** Indrajit, equal to Indra, climbed into a chariot yoked with
> four fierce lions with white, sharp fangs. They matched the speed of the king
> of birds (garuḍa), and the chariot moved without hindrance.

---
19.
sa rathī dhanvināṁ śreṣṭhaḥ śastrajñaḥ astravidāṁ varaḥ |
rathenābhiyayau kṣipraṁ hanūmān yatra saḥ abhavat ||

> **Meaning 19:** The great chariot-warrior, the best of archers, knowing
> weapons and the finest among astra-masters, quickly drove his chariot to where
> Hanumān was.

---
20.
sa tasya rathanirghoṣaṁ jyāsvanaṁ kārmukasya ca |
niśamya harivīraḥ asau saṁprahr̥ṣṭataraḥ abhavat ||

> **Meaning 20:** Hearing the rumble of his chariot and the twang of his
> bowstring, the Vānara hero grew even more delighted.

---
21.
sumahaccāpam ādāya śitaśalyāṁśca sāyakān |
hanūmantam abhipretya jagāma raṇapaṇḍitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 21:** Taking a huge bow and sharp-tipped arrows,
> Indrajit, a master of warfare, moved toward Hanumān.

---
22.
tasmiṁstataḥ saṁyati jātaharṣe raṇāya nirgacchati bāṇapāṇau |
diśaśca sarvāḥ kaluṣā babhūvuḥ mr̥gāśca raudrā bahudhā vineduḥ ||

> **Meaning 22:** AAs Indrajit, eager for combat, marched forth with arrows in hand, all the quarters turned dark, and wild beasts howled in myriad fearsome tones.

---
23.
samāgatāstatra tu nāgayakṣā maharṣayaścakracarāśca siddhāḥ |
nabhaḥ samāvr̥tya ca pakṣisaṁghā vineduruccaiḥ paramaprahr̥ṣṭāḥ ||

> **Meaning 23:** Nāgas and Yakṣas gathered there, along with great ṛṣis,
> Cakracaras and Siddhas. Flocks of birds covered the sky and cried out loudly
> in great joy.

---
24.
āyantaṁ sarathaṁ dr̥ṣṭvā tūrṇam indrajitaṁ kapiḥ |
vinanāda mahānādaṁ vyavardhata ca vegavān ||

> **Meaning 24:** Seeing Indrajit coming quickly in his chariot, the Kapi roared
> a mighty roar, and in his speed he grew larger.

---
25.
indrajit tu rathaṁ divyam āsthitaścitrakārmukaḥ |
dhanurvisphārayām āsa taḍidūrjitaniḥsvanam ||

> **Meaning 25:** Indrajit stood on his divine chariot with a bow of wondrous
> design. He twanged it, and the sound was loud like lightning.

---
26.
tataḥ sametāv atitīkṣṇavegau mahābalau tau raṇanirviśaṅkau |
kapiśca rakṣaḥ adhipateśca putraḥ surāsurendrāv iva baddhavairau ||

> **Meaning 26:** Then the two powerful ones, Hanumān and the Rākṣasa king's
> son, met in battle. Both were fearless, swift and fierce, like Indra and the
> chief of the asuras locked in enmity.

---
27.
sa tasya vīrasya mahārathasyā dhanuṣmataḥ saṁyati saṁmatasya |
śarapravegaṁ vyahanat pravr̥ddhaḥ cacāra mārge pituraprameyaḥ ||

> **Meaning 27:** Hanumān, growing larger, dodged the stream of arrows shot by
> that heroic, great-chariot-warrior archer, who was respected in battle. He
> moved through the air, his father's path, and none could measure him.

---
28.
tataḥ śarān āyatatīkṣṇaśalyān supatriṇaḥ kāñcanacitrapuṅkhān |
mumoca vīraḥ paravīrahantā susaṁtatān vajranipātavegān ||

> **Meaning 28:** Then Indrajit, the destroyer of enemy heroes, unleashed arrows with long, sharp heads, fine feathers, and brilliantly colored golden shafts. They fell in a dense stream, striking with the speed of a thunderbolt.

---
29.
sa tasya tat syandananiḥsvanaṁ ca mr̥daṅgabherīpaṭahasvanaṁ ca |
vikr̥ṣyamāṇasya ca kārmukasya niśamya ghoṣaṁ punarutpapāta ||

> **Meaning 29:** Hearing the rumbling of Indrajit's chariot, the beating of
> drums, kettle-drums and tabors, and the twang of his bow, Hanumān sprang up
> once more.

---
30.
śarāṇām antareṣv āśu vyavartata mahākapiḥ |
haristasyābhilakṣasya mokṣayam̐l lakṣyasaṁgraham ||

> **Meaning 30:** The great Kapi quickly slipped through the gaps between the
> arrows. Indrajit tried to aim at him, but Hanumān kept making that aim fail.

---
31.
śarāṇām agratastasya punaḥ samabhivartata |
prasārya hastau hanumān utpapātānilātmajaḥ ||

> **Meaning 31:** As if sporting with the volley, Hanumān, the son of the Wind, stretched out his arms and bounded upward again, playfully darting in and out of the arrows' path.

---
32.
tāv ubhau vegasaṁpannau raṇakarmaviśāradau |
sarvabhūtamanogrāhi cakraturyuddham uttamam ||

> **Meaning 32:** Both were swift and skilled in battle. Together they fought a
> magnificent fight that captured the hearts of all beings.

---
33.
hanūmato veda na rākṣasaḥ antaraṁ na mārutistasya mahātmanaḥ antaram |
parasparaṁ nirviṣahau babhūvatuḥ sametya tau devasamānavikramau ||

> **Meaning 33:** The Rākṣasa could not find an opening against Hanumān, and
> Māruti could not find one against him. The two met, each with the strength of
> a devatā, and neither could overpower the other.

---
34.
tatastu lakṣye sa vihanyamāne śareṣu mogheṣu ca saṁpatatsu |
jagāma cintāṁ mahatīṁ mahātmā samādhisaṁyogasamāhitātmā ||

> **Meaning 34:** Then, as his target kept slipping away and his arrows kept
> failing, the great Indrajit fell into deep thought, though his mind stayed
> focused and collected.

---
35.
tato matiṁ rākṣasarājasūnuḥ cakāra tasmin harivīramukhye |
avadhyatāṁ tasya kapeḥ samīkṣya kathaṁ nigacched iti nigrahārtham ||

> **Meaning 35:** Seeing that this chief of the Vānara heroes could not be
> killed, the Rākṣasa king's son made up his mind how to bring about his
> capture.

---
36.
tataḥ paitāmahāṁ vīraḥ saḥ astram astravidāṁ varaḥ |
saṁdadhe sumahātejāḥ taṁ haripravaraṁ prati ||

> **Meaning 36:** Then the mighty hero, best among astra-masters, fitted the
> pitāmaha astra, Brahmā's weapon, and aimed it at that best of Vānaras.

---
37.
avadhyaḥ ayam iti jñātvā tam astreṇāstratattvavit |
nijagrāha mahābāhuḥ mārutātmajam indrajit ||

> **Meaning 37:** Knowing that Hanumān could not be killed, Indrajit, the
> mighty-armed master of astra-lore, bound the son of Māruta with that astra.

---
38.
tena baddhastataḥ astreṇa rākṣasena sa vānaraḥ |
abhavan nirviceṣṭaśca papāta ca mahītale ||

> **Meaning 38:** Bound by the Rākṣasa's astra, the Vānara became unable to move
> and fell to the ground.

---
39.
tataḥ atha buddhvā sa tadāstrabandhaṁ prabhoḥ prabhāvād vigatālpavegaḥ |
pitāmahānugraham ātmanaśca vicintayām āsa haripravīraḥ ||

> **Meaning 39:** Then the Vānara hero realized he was bound by the astra. By
> the power of the weapon his speed had faded. He thought about the boon
> Pitāmaha had given him.

---
40.
tataḥ svāyambhuvairmantraiḥ brahmāstram abhimantritam |
hanūmāṁścintayām āsa varadānaṁ pitāmahāt ||

> **Meaning 40:** The Brahmāstra had been charged with the mantras of Svayambhū.
> Hanumān recalled the boon Pitāmaha had granted him.

---
41.
na me astrabandhasya ca śaktirasti vimokṣaṇe lokaguroḥ prabhāvāt |
ity evam evaṁvihitaḥ astrabandhaḥ mayātmayoneranuvartitavyaḥ ||

> **Meaning 41:** Hanumān thought: 'Indrajit assumes I have no strength to break free of this astra's bond. Yet this binding has come about by the power of the Guru of the worlds, and I must simply respect the wish of Brahmā, the self-born.'

---
42.
sa vīryam astrasya kapirvicārya pitāmahānugraham ātmanaśca |
vimokṣaśaktiṁ paricintayitvā pitāmahājñām anuvartate sma ||

> **Meaning 42:** The Kapi weighed the power of the astra and the favor Pitāmaha
> had shown him. Thinking also of his own ability to break free, he chose to
> honor Pitāmaha's command.

---
43.
astreṇāpi hi baddhasya bhayaṁ mama na jāyate |
pitāmahamahendrābhyāṁ rakṣitasyānilena ca ||

> **Meaning 43:** Even bound by this astra, I feel no fear, since I am protected
> by Pitāmaha, by Mahendra and by Vāyu.

---
44.
grahaṇe cāpi rakṣobhiḥ mahan me guṇadarśanam |
rākṣasendreṇa saṁvādaḥ tasmād gr̥hṇantu māṁ pare ||

> **Meaning 44:** Being captured by the Rākṣasas will in fact bring me great
> benefit, since I will get to speak with the Rākṣasa king. So let the enemy
> seize me.

---
45.
sa niścitārthaḥ paravīrahantā samīkṣya karī vinivr̥ttaceṣṭaḥ |
paraiḥ prasahyābhigatairnigr̥hya nanāda taistaiḥ paribhartsyamānaḥ ||

> **Meaning 45:** Firm in his purpose, Hanumān, destroyer of enemy heroes, looked on and stopped all movement. He cried out as the enemies surged wildly toward him, binding him tightly and fiercely threatening him.

---
46.
tatastaṁ rākṣasā dr̥ṣṭvā nirviceṣṭam ariṁdamam |
babandhuḥ śaṇavalkaiśca drumacīraiśca saṁhataiḥ ||

> **Meaning 46:** Then the Rākṣasas saw him lying still, the conqueror of foes,
> and bound him with hempen cords and with tree-bark ropes twisted together.

---
47.
sa rocayām āsa paraiśca bandhanaṁ prasahya vīrairabhinigrahaṁ ca |
kautūhalān māṁ yadi rākṣasendraḥ draṣṭuṁ vyavasyed iti niścitārthaḥ ||

> **Meaning 47:** He welcomed being bound and roughly seized by the enemy
> heroes. His purpose was settled: out of curiosity the Rākṣasa king might want
> to see him.

---
48.
sa baddhastena valkena vimuktaḥ astreṇa vīryavān |
astrabandhaḥ sa cānyaṁ hi na bandham anuvartate ||

> **Meaning 48:** He was bound with that bark, and the astra let go of the
> powerful Hanumān, since the Brahmāstra's bond does not hold when another
> binding is put on.

---
49.
athendrajit taṁ drumacīrabandhaṁ vicārya vīraḥ kapisattamaṁ tam |
vimuktam astreṇa jagāma cintām anyena baddho hy anuvartate astram ||

> **Meaning 49:** Indrajit, the hero, saw the Kapi leader tied with bark ropes
> and freed from the astra. He fell into worry, since an astra does not work on
> one already bound by something else.

---
50.
aho mahat karma kr̥taṁ nirarthakaṁ na rākṣasairmantragatirvimr̥ṣṭā |
punaśca nāstre vihate astram anyat pravartate saṁśayitāḥ sma sarve ||

> **Meaning 50:** He thought: Alas, this great effort was wasted! The Rākṣasas
> have wrongly broken the power of the mantra. When an astra fails, another one
> cannot work, and now all of us are in danger.

---
51.
astreṇa hanumān muktaḥ nātmānam avabudhyate |
kr̥ṣyamāṇastu rakṣobhiḥ taiśca bandhairnipīḍitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 51:** Though freed from the astra, Hanumān did not let it show. He
> was dragged along by the Rākṣasas and pained by those bindings.

---
52.
hanyamānastataḥ krūrai rākṣasaiḥ kāṣṭhamuṣṭibhiḥ |
samīpaṁ rākṣasendrasya prākr̥ṣyata sa vānaraḥ ||

> **Meaning 52:** Beaten by the cruel Rākṣasas with wooden clubs and fists, the
> Vānara was dragged toward the Rākṣasa king.

---
53.
athendrajit taṁ prasamīkṣya muktam astreṇa baddhaṁ drumacīrasūtraiḥ |
vyadarśayat tatra mahābalaṁ taṁ haripravīraṁ sagaṇāya rājñe ||

> **Meaning 53:** Then Indrajit, seeing that the Vānara was free of the astra
> and tied with bark ropes, presented that mighty Vānara hero to the king and
> his court.

---
54.
taṁ mattam iva mātaṅgaṁ baddhaṁ kapivarottamam |
rākṣasā rākṣasendrāya rāvaṇāya nyavedayan ||

> **Meaning 54:** Like a wild elephant in rut, the best of Kapis was brought
> bound, and the Rākṣasas presented him to their king Rāvaṇa.

---
55.
kaḥ ayaṁ kasya kuto vāpi kiṁ kāryaṁ ko vyapāśrayaḥ |
iti rākṣasavīrāṇāṁ tatra saṁjajñire kathāḥ ||

> **Meaning 55:** Who is this? Whose is he? Where is he from? What is his
> mission? Who is his support? Such talk arose among the Rākṣasa heroes there.

---
56.
hanyatāṁ dahyatāṁ vāpi bhakṣyatām iti cāpare |
rākṣasāstatra saṁkruddhāḥ parasparam athābruvan ||

> **Meaning 56:** Some said, Kill him! Others said, Burn him! Still others said,
> Eat him! The furious Rākṣasas argued among themselves.

---
57.
atītya mārgaṁ sahasā mahātmā sa tatra rakṣaḥ adhipapādamūle |
dadarśa rājñaḥ paricāravr̥ddhān gr̥haṁ mahāratnavibhūṣitaṁ ca ||

> **Meaning 57:** Hanumān, the great one, quickly crossed the path and came to
> the feet of the Rākṣasa king. There he saw the old attendants of the king and
> his house, adorned with great jewels.

---
58.
sa dadarśa mahātejā rāvaṇaḥ kapisattamam |
rakṣobhirvikr̥tākāraiḥ kr̥ṣyamāṇam itastataḥ ||

> **Meaning 58:** The mighty Rāvaṇa saw that best of Kapis being dragged here
> and there by Rākṣasas with grotesque forms.

---
59.
rākṣasādhipatiṁ cāpi dadarśa kapisattamaḥ |
tejobalasamāyuktaṁ tapantam iva bhāskaram ||

> **Meaning 59:** The best of Kapis also saw the Rākṣasa king, full of splendor
> and power, shining like the blazing sun.

---
60.
sa roṣasaṁvartitatāmradr̥ṣṭiḥ daśānanastaṁ kapim anvavekṣya |
athopaviṣṭān kulaśīlavr̥ddhān samādiśat taṁ prati mantramukhyān ||

> **Meaning 60:** His coppery eyes turned red with rage, Daśānana looked at the
> Kapi. Then he ordered his chief counselors, who were seated there, men of good
> family and character, to question him.

---
61.
yathākramaṁ taiḥ sa kapiśca pr̥ṣṭaḥ kāryārtham arthasya ca mūlam ādau |
nivedayām āsa harīśvarasya dūtaḥ sakāśād aham āgataḥ asmi ||

> **Meaning 61:** In order, they questioned him about the purpose of his coming
> and the root of the matter. Hanumān replied: I am a messenger who has come
> from the king of the Vānaras, Sugrīva.

---
