=== Sarga 16 ===

---

1.
praśasya tu praśastavyāṁ sītān tāṁ haripuṅgavaḥ |
guṇābhirāmam rāmañ ca punaś cintāparo ’bhavat ||

2.
sa muhūrtam iva dhyātvā bāṣpaparyākulekṣaṇaḥ |
sītām āśritya tejasvī hanumān vilalāpa ha ||

3.
mānyā guruvinītasya lakṣmaṇasya gurupriyā |
yadi sītāpi duḥkhārtā kālo hi duratikramaḥ ||

4.
rāmasya vyavasāyajñā lakṣmaṇasya ca dhīmataḥ |
nātyarthaṅ kṣubhyate devī gaṅgeva jaladāgame ||

5.
tulyaśīlavayovr̥ttān tulyābhijanalakṣaṇām |
rāghavo ’rhati vaidehīn tañ ceyam asitekṣaṇā ||

> **Meaning 1-5:** Having praised the praiseworthy Sītā and Śrī Rāma, whose
> nature is pleasing by his virtues, that foremost among Vānaras again
> became absorbed in thought. Having reflected for a moment, his eyes
> brimming with tears, the radiant Hanumān lamented over Sītā: "If even
> Sītā, who is honorable, beloved to her elders, and the elder sister-in-law
> of the disciplined Lakṣmaṇa, is afflicted by sorrow, then indeed time is
> insurmountable! Knowing the determination of Śrī Rāma and the wise
> Lakṣmaṇa, this divine lady does not become excessively agitated, just as
> the river Gaṅgā does not overflow during the rainy season. Śrī Rāmacandra
> deserves Vaidehī, who matches him in character, age, conduct, noble
> lineage, and auspicious marks, and similarly, this dark-eyed lady is
> perfectly suited for him."
---

6.
tān dr̥ṣṭvā navahemābhāṁ lokakāntām iva śriyam |
jagāma manasā rāmaṁ vacanañ cedam abravīt ||

7.
asyā hetor viśālākṣyā hato vālī mahābalaḥ |
rāvaṇapratimo vīrye kabandhaś ca nipātitaḥ ||

8.
virādhaś ca hataḥ saṅkhye rākṣaso bhīmavikramaḥ |
vane rāmeṇa vikramya mahendreṇeva śambaraḥ ||

9.
caturdaśasahasrāṇi rakṣasām bhīmakarmaṇām |
nihatāni janasthāne śarair agniśikhopamaiḥ ||

10.
kharaś ca nihataḥ saṅkhye triśirāś ca nipātitaḥ |
dūṣaṇaś ca mahātejā rāmeṇa viditātmanā ||

11.
aiśvaryaṁ vānarāṇāñ ca durlabhaṁ vālipālitam |
asyā nimitte sugrīvaḥ prāptavām̐l lokasatkr̥tam ||

> **Meaning 6-11:** Seeing her, who possessed the splendor of pure gold and
> resembled Goddess Śrī, beloved by the world, he approached Śrī Rāma in his
> mind and spoke these words: "It is for the sake of this wide-eyed lady
> that the mighty Vālī was killed, and Kabandha, equal to Rāvaṇa in prowess,
> was struck down. The Rākṣasa Virādha of terrible valor was slain in battle
> in the forest by Śrī Rāma, just as the demon Śambara was slain by Indra.
> Fourteen thousand Rākṣasas of terrifying deeds were annihilated in
> Janasthāna by arrows resembling leaping flames of fire. Khara was killed
> in battle, Triśirā was struck down, and the highly radiant Dūṣaṇa was
> slain by the self-realized Śrī Rāma. It is for her sake that Sugrīva
> obtained the wealth of the Vānaras, which was fiercely protected by Vālī,
> difficult to obtain, and honored by the world."
---

12.
sāgaraś ca mayā krāntaḥ śrīmān nadanadīpatiḥ |
asyā hetor viśālākṣyāḥ purī ceyan nirīkṣitā ||

13.
yadi rāmaḥ samudrāntām medinīm parivartayet |
asyāḥ kr̥te jagac cāpi yuktam ity eva me matiḥ ||

14.
rājyaṁ vā triṣu lokeṣu sītā vā janakātmajā |
trailokyarājyaṁ sakalaṁ sītāyā nāpnuyāt kalām ||

15.
iyaṁ sā dharmaśīlasya maithilasya mahātmanaḥ |
sutā janakarājasya sītā bhartr̥dr̥ḍhavratā ||

> **Meaning 12-15:** "It is for the sake of this wide-eyed lady that the
> magnificent ocean, the king of rivers and streams, was crossed by me, and
> this city was thoroughly searched. If Śrī Rāma were to turn upside down
> the entire earth bounded by the oceans, or even the entire universe for
> her sake, I believe it would be entirely justified! If one were to weigh
> the sovereignty over the three worlds against Sītā, the daughter of
> Janaka, the kingdom of all three worlds combined would not equal even a
> fraction of Sītā. She is the daughter of the righteous, noble-souled King
> Janaka of Mithilā, and she is Sītā, firmly devoted to her husband."
---

16.
utthitā medinīm bhittvā kṣetre halamukhakṣate |
padmareṇunibhaiḥ kīrṇā śubhaiḥ kedārapāṁsubhiḥ ||

17.
vikrāntasyāryaśīlasya saṁyugeṣv anivartinaḥ |
snuṣā daśarathasyaiṣā jyeṣṭhā rājño yaśasvinī ||

18.
dharmajñasya kr̥tajñasya rāmasya viditātmanaḥ |
iyaṁ sā dayitā bhāryā rākṣasī vaśam āgatā ||

19.
sarvān bhogān parityajya bhartr̥snehabalāt kr̥tā |
acintayitvā duḥkhāni praviṣṭā nirjanaṁ vanam ||

20.
santuṣṭā phalamūlena bhartr̥śuśrūṣaṇe ratā |
yā parām bhajate prītiṁ vane ’pi bhavane yathā ||

21.
seyaṅ kanakavarṇāṅgī nityaṁ susmitabhāṣiṇī |
sahate yātanām etām anarthānām abhāginī ||

> **Meaning 16-21:** "She arose by breaking through the earth when the field
> was furrowed by the plow, covered in the auspicious, lotus-pollen-like
> dust of the fields. She is the renowned eldest daughter-in-law of King
> Daśaratha, who was valiant, of noble character, and one who never
> retreated in battles. She is the beloved wife of the righteous, grateful,
> and self-realized Śrī Rāma, and she has now fallen under the control of
> Rākṣasīs! Driven by the power of her love for her husband, she abandoned
> all comforts, disregarded all hardships, and entered the desolate forest.
> Content with fruits and roots, wholly devoted to serving her husband, she
> experienced the highest joy even in the forest, as if she were in a
> palace. That lady with a golden complexion, who always speaks with a
> gentle smile, is now enduring this agony, completely undeserving of such
> misfortunes."
---

22.
imān tu śīlasampannān draṣṭum icchati rāghavaḥ |
rāvaṇena pramathitām prapām iva pipāsitaḥ ||

23.
asyā nūnam punar lābhād rāghavaḥ prītim eṣyati |
rājā rājyaparibhraṣṭaḥ punaḥ prāpyeva medinīm ||

24.
kāmabhogaiḥ parityaktā hīnā bandhujanena ca |
dhārayaty ātmano dehan tatsamāgamakāṅkṣiṇī ||

25.
naiṣā paśyati rākṣasyo nemān puṣpaphaladrumān |
ekasthahr̥dayā nūnam rāmam evānupaśyati ||

26.
bhartā nāma paran nāryā bhūṣaṇam bhūṣaṇād api |
eṣā hi rahitā tena śobhanārhā na śobhate ||

> **Meaning 22-26:** "Śrī Rāma yearns to see her, endowed with noble
> character and oppressed by Rāvaṇa, just as a thirsty man longs for a
> watering place. Surely, upon regaining her, Rāghava will attain the same
> joy as a king who, having lost his kingdom, reclaims the earth. Deprived
> of desires, comforts, and separated from her kin, she sustains her body
> only out of the desire to be reunited with him. She does not truly see
> these Rākṣasīs, nor these trees laden with flowers and fruits; with her
> heart singularly focused, she perceives only Śrī Rāma. A husband is indeed
> the ultimate ornament for a woman, greater than any other adornment;
> separated from him, this lady, though worthy of radiant beauty, no longer
> shines."
---

27.
duṣkaraṅ kurute rāmo hīno yad anayā prabhuḥ |
dhārayaty ātmano dehan na duḥkhenāvasīdati ||

28.
imām asitakeśāntāṁ śatapatranibhekṣaṇām |
sukhārhān duḥkhitān dr̥ṣṭvā mamāpi vyathitam manaḥ ||

29.
kṣitikṣamā puṣkarasannibhākṣī yā rakṣitā rāghavalakṣmaṇābhyām |
sā rākṣasībhir vikr̥tekṣaṇābhiḥ saṁrakṣyate samprati vr̥kṣamūle ||

30.
himahatanalinīva naṣṭaśobhā vyasanaparamparayā nipīḍyamānā |
sahacararahiteva cakravākī janakasutā kr̥paṇān daśām prapannā ||

31.
asyā hi puṣpāvanatāgraśākhāḥ śokan dr̥ḍhaṁ vai janayaty aśokāḥ |
himavyapāyena ca mandaraśmir abhyutthito naikasahasraraśmiḥ ||

32.
ity evam arthaṅ kapir anvavekṣya sīteyam ity eva niviṣṭabuddhiḥ |
saṁśritya tasmin niṣasāda vr̥kṣe balī harīṇām r̥ṣabhas tarasvī ||

> **Meaning 27-32:** "Śrī Rāma is performing an impossible task by
> sustaining his body and not perishing from sorrow despite being separated
> from her. Seeing this lady with beautiful dark hair and eyes like lotus
> petals, who is worthy of happiness but is now grief-stricken, even my mind
> is deeply distressed! She who is as forgiving as the earth, with eyes
> resembling lotuses, who was guarded by Śrī Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, is now being
> guarded under a tree by Rākṣasīs with deformed eyes! Like a lotus
> destroyed by frost, her splendor vanished; continuously tormented by a
> series of calamities, like a Cakravāka bird bereft of its mate, the
> daughter of Janaka has reached a pitiable state. These Aśoka trees, with
> their branch tips bent with flowers, and the newly risen moon with its
> thousands of rays emerging after the passing of winter, indeed severely
> intensify her sorrow." Having reflected on these matters and firmly
> convinced that this was indeed Sītā, the mighty and swift hero, a bull
> among Vānaras, Hanuman, sat taking refuge in that tree.
---
