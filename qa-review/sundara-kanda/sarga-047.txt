=== Sarga 47 ===

---
1.
senāpatīn pañca sa tu pramāpitān hanūmatā sānucarān savāhanān |
samīkṣya rājā samaroddhatonmukhaṁ kumāram akṣaṁ prasamaikṣatāgrataḥ ||

> **Meaning 1:** The king saw that Hanumān had killed his five commanders along
> with their followers and vehicles. Then he looked at Prince Akṣa, who stood
> before him eager for battle.

---
2.
sa tasya dr̥ṣṭyarpaṇasaṁpracoditaḥ pratāpavān kāñcanacitrakārmukaḥ |
samutpapātātha sadasy udīritaḥ dvijātimukhyairhaviṣeva pāvakaḥ ||

> **Meaning 2:** Spurred by that glance, the glorious Akṣa, carrying a bow
> decorated with gold, sprang up from the assembly, like a fire rising when the
> best of brahmins feed it with offerings.

---
3.
tato mahad bāladivākaraprabhaṁ prataptajāmbūnadajālasaṁtatam |
rathāṁ samāsthāya yayau sa vīryavān mahāhariṁ taṁ prati nairr̥tarṣabhaḥ ||

> **Meaning 3:** Then that mighty bull among the Rākṣasas mounted a huge
> chariot, radiant as the young sun and covered with a net of molten gold, and
> rode out toward the great Hari.

---
4.
tatastapaḥsaṁgrahasaṁcayārjitaṁ prataptajāmbūnadajālaśobhitam |
patākinaṁ ratnavibhūṣitadhvajaṁ manojavāṣṭāśvavaraiḥ suyojitam ||

> **Meaning 4:** The chariot had been won by the force of his accumulated
> austerities. It shone with a net of molten gold and carried flags and banners,
> with a jeweled standard. Eight excellent horses, swift as thought, were
> harnessed to it.

---
5.
surāsurādhr̥ṣyam asaṁgacāriṇaṁ raviprabhaṁ vyomacaraṁ samāhitam |
satūṇam aṣṭāsinibaddhabandhuraṁ yathākramāveśitaśaktitomaram ||

> **Meaning 5:** Neither gods nor asuras could stand against it. Nothing could
> block its path, and it moved through the sky blazing like the sun. It was
> fully equipped, with quivers, eight swords firmly fastened in place, and
> spears and tomaras arranged in proper order.

---
6.
virājamānaṁ pratipūrṇavastunā sahemadāmnā śaśisūryavarvasā |
divākarābhaṁ ratham āsthitastataḥ sa nirjagāmāmaratulyavikramaḥ ||

> **Meaning 6:** The chariot gleamed, packed with supplies and hung with golden
> garlands, with the brilliance of the moon and sun. Akṣa, whose valor matched
> that of the immortals, climbed into that sun-bright chariot and set out.

---
7.
sa pūrayan khaṁ ca mahīṁ ca sācalāṁ turaṁgamataṅgamahārathasvanaiḥ |
balaiḥ sametaiḥ sa hi toraṇasthitaṁ samartham āsīnam upāgamat kapim ||

> **Meaning 7:** With the roar of his horses, elephants and great chariots he
> filled the sky and the earth with its mountains. Together with his troops, he
> approached the able Kapi, who sat on the gateway arch.

---
8.
sa taṁ samāsādya hariṁ harīkṣaṇaḥ yugāntakālāgnim iva prajākṣaye |
avasthitaṁ vismitajātasaṁbhramaḥ samaikṣatākṣo bahumānacakṣuṣā ||

> **Meaning 8:** Akṣa came up to Hari, who stood there like the fire at the end
> of an age when all creatures perish. Astonished and uneasy, the tawny-eyed
> prince looked at him with respect.

---
9.
sa tasya vegaṁ ca kapermahātmanaḥ parākramaṁ cāriṣu pārthivātmajaḥ |
vicārayan khaṁ ca balaṁ mahābalaḥ himakṣaye sūrya ivābhivardhate ||

> **Meaning 9:** Akṣa, mighty son of the king, weighed the speed of the
> great-souled Kapi and his prowess against enemies, and measured his own
> strength against it. Then he swelled with energy, like the sun at the end of
> winter.

---
10.
sa jātamanyuḥ prasamīkṣya vikramaṁ sthiraḥ sthitaḥ saṁyati durnivāraṇam |
samāhitātmā hanumantam āhave pracodayām āsa śaraistribhiḥ śitaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 10:** Provoked to anger, Akṣa saw Hanumān's valor, a force no one
> could hold back in battle. Steady and focused, he struck Hanumān in the fight
> with three sharp arrows.

---
11.
tataḥ kapiṁ taṁ prasamīkṣya garvitaṁ jitaśramaṁ śatruparājayorjitam |
avaikṣatākṣaḥ samudīrṇamānasaḥ sabāṇapāṇiḥ pragr̥hītakārmukaḥ ||

> **Meaning 11:** Then Akṣa looked at the proud Kapi, who had shaken off his
> fatigue and was swollen with his triumph over the enemy. His own spirit
> surged, and he stood with arrows in hand and his bow drawn.

---
12.
sa hemaniṣkāṅgadacārukuṇḍalaḥ samāsasādāśu parākramaḥ kapim |
tayorbabhūvāpratimaḥ samāgamaḥ surāsurāṇām api saṁbhramapradaḥ ||

> **Meaning 12:** Wearing gold chains, armlets and lovely earrings, the valiant
> prince quickly closed in on Kapi. The meeting of those two was beyond compare,
> enough to shake even gods and asuras.

---
13.
rarāsa bhūmirna tatāpa bhānumān vavau na vāyuḥ pracacāla cācalaḥ |
kapeḥ kumārasya ca vīkṣya saṁyugaṁ nanāda ca dyaurudadhiśca cukṣubhe ||

> **Meaning 13:** The earth roared, the sun gave no heat, and the wind stopped
> blowing. The mountains trembled, the sky thundered and the ocean churned, as
> they watched the battle between Kapi and the prince.

---
14.
tataḥ sa vīraḥ sumukhān patatriṇaḥ suvarṇapuṅkhān saviṣān ivoragān |
samādhisaṁyogavimokṣatattvavic charān atha trīn kapimūrdhny apātayat ||

> **Meaning 14:** Then the hero, who knew the secrets of aiming and releasing,
> shot three arrows into Kapi's head. They had fine tips and golden feathers,
> and they were like venomous serpents.

---
15.
sa taiḥ śarairmūrdhni samaṁ nipātitaiḥ kṣarann asr̥gdigdhavivr̥ttalocanaḥ |
navoditādityanibhaḥ śarāṁśumān vyarājatāditya ivāṁśumālikaḥ ||

> **Meaning 15:** Struck on the head by those arrows all at once, Hanumān
> streamed blood and his eyes rolled, smeared with it. Wreathed in the rays of
> the arrows, he blazed like the newly risen sun with its garland of light.

---
16.
tataḥ sa piṅgādhipamantrisattamaḥ samīkṣya taṁ rājavarātmajaṁ raṇe |
udagracitrāyudhacitrakārmukaṁ jaharṣa cāpūryata cāhavonmukhaḥ ||

> **Meaning 16:** Then Hanumān, the finest counselor of the Vānara king, saw the
> prince in battle with his colorful weapons and bow raised. He rejoiced and
> grew eager for the fight.

---
17.
sa mandarāgrastha ivāṁśumālī vivr̥ddhakopo balavīryasaṁyutaḥ |
kumāram akṣaṁ sabalaṁ savāhanaṁ dadāha netrāgnimarīcibhistadā ||

> **Meaning 17:** Like the sun on the peak of Mandara, with his anger swelling
> and full of strength and valor, Kapi seemed to burn Prince Akṣa, with his army
> and vehicles, in the rays of fire from his eyes.

---
18.
tataḥ sa bāṇāsanaśakrakārmukaḥ śarapravarṣo yudhi rākṣasāmbudaḥ |
śarān mumocāśu harīśvarācale balāhako vr̥ṣṭim ivācalottame ||

> **Meaning 18:** Then Akṣa, whose bow was like Indra's, a cloud of Rākṣasas
> raining arrows, loosed shaft after shaft at Hanumān, who stood like a
> mountain, as a rain cloud pours over the greatest of mountains.

---
19.
tataḥ kapistaṁ raṇacaṇḍavikramaṁ vivr̥ddhatejobalavīryasāyakam |
kumāram akṣaṁ prasamīkṣya saṁyuge nanāda harṣād ghanatulyavikramaḥ ||

> **Meaning 19:** Seeing Akṣa in battle, fierce in valor, with his arrows,
> energy, strength and prowess grown huge, Kapi roared with joy, his valor like
> that of a thundercloud.

---
20.
sa bālabhāvād yudhi vīryadarpitaḥ pravr̥ddhamanyuḥ kṣatajopamekṣaṇaḥ |
samāsasādāpratimaṁ raṇe kapiṁ gajo mahākūpam ivāvr̥taṁ tr̥ṇaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 20:** Out of youth and pride in his strength, Akṣa's anger rose and
> his eyes turned red as blood. He charged the matchless Kapi like an elephant
> rushing at a deep well covered over with grass.

---
21.
sa tena bāṇaiḥ prasabhaṁ nipātitaiḥ cakāra nādaṁ ghananādaniḥsvanaḥ |
samutpapātāśu nabhaḥ sa mārutiḥ bhujoruvikṣepaṇa ghoradarśanaḥ ||

> **Meaning 21:** Struck by his arrows, loosed with great force, Māruti gave out
> a roar like a thundercloud. He swung his arms and thighs and leapt up quickly
> into the sky, a terrifying sight.

---
22.
samutpatantaṁ samabhidravad balī sa rākṣasānāṁ pravaraḥ pratāpavān |
rathī rathaśreṣṭhatamaḥ kirañ śaraiḥ payodharaḥ śailam ivāśmavr̥ṣṭibhiḥ ||

> **Meaning 22:** As Hanumān rose, the mighty and glorious Akṣa, foremost of the
> Rākṣasas and the best of charioteers, rushed at him, showering arrows like a
> cloud pouring hail on a mountain.

---
23.
sa tāñ śarāṁstasya vimokṣayan kapiḥ cacāra vīraḥ pathi vāyusevite |
śarāntare mārutavad viniṣpatan manojavaḥ saṁyati caṇḍavikramaḥ ||

> **Meaning 23:** The hero Kapi evaded his arrows and ranged along the path the
> wind frequents. Swift as thought and fierce in battle, he darted out through the
> gaps between the arrows, just as the wind would.

---
24.
tam āttabāṇāsanam āhavonmukhaṁ kham āstr̥ṇantaṁ vividhaiḥ śarottamaiḥ |
avaikṣatākṣaṁ bahumānacakṣuṣā jagāma cintāṁ ca sa mārutātmajaḥ ||

> **Meaning 24:** Hanumān watched Akṣa, who had taken up his bow, eager for
> battle, covering the sky with many fine arrows. He looked at him with respect,
> and Māruti's son fell into thought.

---
25.
tataḥ śarairbhinnabhujāntaraḥ kapiḥ kumāravaryeṇa mahātmanā nadan |
mahābhujaḥ karmaviśeṣatattvavid vicintayām āsa raṇe parākramam ||

> **Meaning 25:** Then Kapi, his chest pierced by arrows from that noble prince,
> roared. The mighty-armed one, who understood the true nature of every kind of
> deed, thought carefully about how to fight him in this battle.

---
26.
abālavad bāladivākaraprabhaḥ karoty ayaṁ karma mahan mahābalaḥ |
na cāsya sarvāhavakarmaśobhinaḥ pramāpaṇe me matiratra jāyate ||

> **Meaning 26:** "He doesn't fight like a boy. He shines like the young sun and
> performs great deeds with great strength. When I see him shine in every kind
> of battle, I don't feel inclined to kill him.

---
27.
ayaṁ mahātmā ca mahāṁśca vīryataḥ samāhitaścātisahaśca saṁyuge |
asaṁśayaṁ karmaguṇodayād ayaṁ sanāgayakṣairmunibhiśca pūjitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 27:** "He is noble and mighty. In battle he is focused and can take
> any blow. There is no doubt that Nāgas, Yakṣas and sages honor him for the
> merit of his deeds.

---
28.
parākramotsāhavivr̥ddhamānasaḥ samīkṣate māṁ pramukhāgataḥ sthitaḥ |
parākramo hy asya manāṁsi kampayet surāsurāṇām api śīghrakāriṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 28:** "His mind has grown strong with valor and zeal, and he stands
> facing me and watches me. His prowess could shake the minds of even gods and
> asuras, swift as they are.

---
29.
na khalv ayaṁ nābhibhaved upekṣitaḥ parākramo hy asya raṇe vivardhate |
pramāpaṇaṁ tv eva mamāsya rocate na vardhamānaḥ agnirupekṣituṁ kṣamaḥ ||

> **Meaning 29:** "If I ignore him, he will surely overpower me, because his
> prowess keeps growing in this fight. I prefer to kill him. A fire that is
> growing should never be ignored."

---
30.
iti pravegaṁ tu parasya tarkayan svakarmayogaṁ ca vidhāya vīryavān |
cakāra vegaṁ tu mahābalastadā matiṁ ca cakre asya vadhe mahākapiḥ ||

> **Meaning 30:** Judging the enemy's onrush this way, and settling his own plan
> of action, mighty Kapi gathered up his speed and fixed his mind on killing
> him.

---
31.
sa tasya tān aṣṭahayān mahājavān samāhitān bhārasahān vivartane |
jaghāna vīraḥ pathi vāyusevite talaprahālaiḥ pavanātmajaḥ kapiḥ ||

> **Meaning 31:** Then the hero Hanumān, son of the Wind, killed Akṣa's eight
> great horses in the wind-swept sky with blows of his palm. They were swift and
> steady, able to carry the load when the chariot turned.

---
32.
tatastalenābhihato mahārathaḥ sa tasya piṅgādhipamantrinirjitaḥ |
sa bhagnanīḍaḥ parimuktakūbaraḥ papāta bhūmau hatavājirambarāt ||

> **Meaning 32:** Struck by the palm of the counselor of the Vānara king, the
> great chariot fell from the sky to the ground. Its seat was shattered, its
> pole had come loose and its horses were dead.

---
33.
sa taṁ parityajya mahāratho rathaṁ sakārmukaḥ khaḍgadharaḥ kham utpatat |
tapaḥ abhiyogād r̥ṣirugravīryavān vihāya dehaṁ marutām ivālayam ||

> **Meaning 33:** Akṣa left that chariot behind and sprang into the sky, holding
> his bow and sword. He was a great chariot-warrior, like a sage of fierce power
> who leaves his body through austerity and goes to the realm of the Maruts.

---
34.
tataḥ kapistaṁ vicarantam ambare patatrirājānilasiddhasevite |
sametya taṁ mārutavegavikramaḥ krameṇa jagrāha ca pādayordr̥ḍham ||

> **Meaning 34:** Then Kapi, with the speed and valor of the wind, went after
> him as he moved through the sky, where the king of birds, the wind and the
> Siddhas travel. He caught him and seized him firmly by the feet.

---
35.
sa taṁ samāvidhya sahasraśaḥ kapiḥ mahoragaṁ gr̥hya ivāṇḍajeśvaraḥ |
mumoca vegāt pitr̥tulyavikramaḥ mahītale saṁyati vānarottamaḥ ||

> **Meaning 35:** Whirling him around thousands of times, as Garuḍa, lord of
> birds, seizes a great serpent, Kapi, whose valor matched his father's, hurled
> him down onto the ground in the fight, with great speed.

---
36.
sa bhagnabāhūrukaṭīśiro dharaḥ kṣarann asr̥n nirmathitāsthilocanaḥ |
sa bhinnasaṁdhiḥ pravikīrṇabandhanaḥ hataḥ kṣitau vāyusutena rākṣasaḥ ||

> **Meaning 36:** Akṣa's arms, thighs, waist and head were broken. Blood
> streamed from him, his bones and eyes were crushed, his joints were smashed
> and his fastenings scattered. The Rākṣasa lay dead on the ground, killed by
> the son of Vāyu.

---
37.
mahākapirbhūmitale nipīḍya taṁ cakāra rakṣaḥ adhipatermahad bhayam |
maharṣibhiścakracarairmahāvrataiḥ sametya bhūtaiśca sayakṣapannagaiḥ |
suraiśca sendrairbhr̥śajātavismayaiḥ hate kumāre sa kapirnirīkṣitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 37:** Mahākapi pressed him into the ground and struck great fear
> into the lord of the Rākṣasas. Great sages, Cakracaras, those firm in their
> vows, beings with Yakṣas and Nāgas, and the devatās with Indra all watched the
> Kapi in deep amazement when the prince was killed.

---
38.
nihatya taṁ vajrasutopamaprabhaṁ kumāram akṣaṁ kṣatajopamekṣaṇam |
tad eva vīraḥ abhijagāma toraṇaṁ kr̥takṣaṇaḥ kāla iva prajākṣaye ||

> **Meaning 38:** Having killed Prince Akṣa, who shone like the son of
> Vajra-wielding Indra and whose eyes were red as blood, the hero went back to
> the gateway arch, like Kāla ready to strike at the end of creation.

---
