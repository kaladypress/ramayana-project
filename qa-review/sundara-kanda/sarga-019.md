=== Sarga 19 ===

---

1.
tasminn eva tataḥ kāle rājaputrī tv aninditā |
rūpayauvanasampannaṁ bhūṣaṇottamabhūṣitam ||

2.
tato dr̥ṣṭvaiva vaidehī rāvaṇaṁ rākṣasādhipam |
prāvepata varārohā pravāte kadalī yathā ||

3.
ūrubhyām udaraṁ chādya bāhubhyāṁ ca payodharau |
upaviṣṭā viśālākṣī rudantī varavarṇinī ||

4.
daśagrīvas tu vaidehīṁ rakṣitāṁ rākṣasīgaṇaiḥ |
dadarśa dīnāṁ duḥkhārtāṁ nāvaṁ sannām ivārṇave ||

> **Meaning 1-4:** At that very time, the flawless princess, endowed with beauty
> and youth, adorned with excellent ornaments, trembled like a plantain tree in a
> strong wind, merely upon seeing Rāvaṇa, the king of the Rākṣasas. Covering her
> abdomen with her thighs and her breasts with her arms, the wide-eyed and
> beautiful-complexioned lady sat down weeping. The ten-headed Rāvaṇa saw Vaidehī,
> who was being guarded by groups of Rākṣasīs. She was in a pitiful state,
> afflicted by sorrow, tossing about like a boat in the middle of the ocean.
---

5.
asaṁvr̥tāyām āsīnāṁ dharaṇyāṁ saṁśitavratām |
chinnāṁ prapatitāṁ bhūmau śākhām iva vanaspateḥ ||

6.
malamaṇḍanadigdhāṅgīṁ maṇḍanārhām amaṇḍitām |
mr̥ṇālī paṅkadigdhēva vibhāti na vibhāti ca ||

7.
samīpaṁ rājasiṁhasya rāmasya viditātmanaḥ |
saṅkalpahayasaṁyuktair yāntīm iva manorathaiḥ ||

8.
śuṣyantīṁ rudatīm ekāṁ dhyānaśokaparāyaṇām |
duḥkhasyāntam apaśyantīṁ rāmāṁ rāmam anuvratām ||

9.
veṣṭamānām athāviṣṭāṁ pannagendravadhūm iva |
dhūpyamānāṁ graheṇeva rohiṇīṁ dhūmaketunā ||

> **Meaning 5-9:** Sitting on the bare earth, observing strict vows, she was like
> a severed branch of a forest tree fallen on the ground. Although her limbs were
> covered in dirt, she continued to shine with her natural radiance. Though that
> auspicious lady was worthy of ornaments, her brilliance did not diminish even
> when unadorned; she appeared like a lotus stem smeared in mud—seemingly devoid
> of luster, yet her natural brilliance was still emanating. She appeared as if
> she were traveling to the presence of the world-renowned Śrī Rāmacandra, the
> best among kings, by yoking the horses of her resolutions to the chariots of
> her desires. She was drying up with grief, weeping, alone, deeply absorbed in
> thought and sorrow, not seeing the end of her misery, completely devoted to
> Śrī Rāma. She was writhing and possessed like the wife of the king of serpents,
> and appeared like the star Rohiṇī enveloped by the comet Dhūmaketu.
---

10.
vr̥ttaśīle kule jātām ācāravati dhārmike |
punaḥ saṁskāram āpannāṁ jātam iva ca duṣkule ||

11.
abhūtēnāpavādēna kīrtiṁ nipatitām iva |
āmnāyānām ayōgēna vidyāṁ praśithilām iva ||

12.
sannām iva mahākīrtiṁ śraddhām iva vimānitām |
prajñām iva parikṣīṇām āśāṁ pratihatām iva ||

13.
āyatīm iva vidhvastām ājñāṁ pratihatām iva |
dīptām iva diśaṁ kāle pūjām apahr̥tām iva ||

14.
padminīm iva vidhvastāṁ hataśūrāṁ camūm iva |
prabhām iva tapodhvastām upakṣīṇām ivāpagām ||

15.
vedīm iva parāmr̥ṣṭāṁ śāntām agniśikhām iva |
paurṇamāsīm iva niśāṁ rāhugrastendumaṇḍalām ||

> **Meaning 10-15:** Though born in a family of excellent conduct and character,
> and married into a righteous family, she now appeared as if she were married into
> a wicked family and subjected to wrong influences. She looked like fame brought
> down by false accusations; like knowledge that has become loose due to lack of
> study; like great fame diminished; like respect dishonored; like intellect
> faded; like hope thwarted. She resembled the future ruined; a command obstructed;
> a blazing direction during a calamity; worship taken away. She was like a lotus
> pond destroyed; an army with its heroes killed; radiance obscured by darkness;
> a river dried up; a sacrificial altar defiled; a flame of fire extinguished;
> and like the full moon night when the moon's orb is swallowed by Rāhu.
---

16.
utkr̥ṣṭaparṇakamalāṁ vitrāsitavihaṅgamām |
hastihastaparāmr̥ṣṭām ākulāṁ padminīm iva ||

17.
patiśokāturāṁ śuṣkāṁ nadīṁ visrāvitām iva |
parayā mr̥jayā hīnāṁ kr̥ṣṇapakṣe niśām iva ||

18.
sukumārīṁ sujātāṅgīṁ ratnagarbhagr̥hocitām |
tapyamānām ivoṣṇena mr̥ṇālīm aciroddhr̥tām ||

19.
gr̥hītām ālitāṁ stambhe yūthapena vinākr̥tām |
niḥśvasantīṁ suduḥkhārtāṁ gajarājavadhūm iva ||

20.
ekayā dīrghayā veṇyā śobhamānām ayatnataḥ |
nīlayā nīradāpāye vanarājyā mahīm iva ||

> **Meaning 16-20:** She appeared like a lotus pond whose leaves and lotuses have
> been torn out, whose birds have been frightened away, and which has been
> agitated and trampled by an elephant's trunk. Afflicted by grief for her
> husband, she was like a dried-up river whose waters have been drained;
> completely devoid of bodily care, she resembled a night in the dark fortnight.
> Very delicate, with beautifully formed limbs, accustomed to living in a palace
> filled with gems, she appeared like a freshly uprooted lotus stem scorched by
> the heat of the sun. Like a female elephant captured, bound to a pillar, separated from the
> leader of her herd, she was sighing heavily and stricken with intense grief. She
> shone effortlessly with a single long braid, like the earth with a dark line of
> forests at the end of the rainy season.
---

21.
upavāsena śokena dhyānena ca bhayena ca |
parikṣīṇāṁ kr̥śāṁ dīnām alpāhārāṁ tapodhanām ||

22.
āyācamānāṁ duḥkhārtāṁ prāñjaliṁ devatām iva |
bhāvena raghumukhyasya daśagrīvaparābhavam ||

23.
samīkṣamāṇāṁ rudatīm aninditāṁ supakṣmatāmrāyataśuklalocanām |
anuvratāṁ rāmam atīva maithilīṁ pralobhayām āsa vadhāya rāvaṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 21-23:** Severely weakened, emaciated, and in a pitiful state due to fasting,
> sorrow, anxiety, and fear, subsisting only on air, she was observing the rules
> of penance and vows. The sorrow-stricken lady, folding her hands in reverence,
> appeared as if she were praying to her family deity with a full heart for the
> defeat of the ten-headed Rāvaṇa by the power of the chief of the Raghus. With
> beautiful eyelashes, and wide eyes that were reddish at the corners and white
> in the middle, she was looking in all directions for a rescuer while weeping.
> Considering Śrī Rāma as her everything, the flawless Maithilī was devotedly
> practicing the dharma of a chaste wife. Rāvaṇa, whose death was approaching,
> began to entice her for his own destruction.
---
