=== Sarga 50 ===

---
1.
tasya tad vacanaṁ śrutvā vānarasya mahātmanaḥ |
ājñāpayad vadhaṁ tasya rāvaṇaḥ krodhamūrchitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 1:** Hearing the words of the great Vānara, Rāvaṇa, overcome with
> rage, ordered that Hanumān be put to death.

---
2.
vadhe tasya samājñapte rāvaṇena durātmanā |
niveditavato dautyaṁ nānumene vibhīṣaṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 2:** When the wicked Rāvaṇa gave the order to kill the envoy who had
> delivered his message, Vibhīṣaṇa did not agree with it.

---
3.
taṁ rakṣaḥ adhipatiṁ kruddhaṁ tacca kāryam upasthitam |
viditvā cintayām āsa kāryaṁ kāryavidhau sthitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 3:** Seeing the Rākṣasa king so furious, and seeing the matter at
> hand, Vibhīṣaṇa, who was skilled in judging what should be done, thought it
> over carefully.

---
4.
niścitārthastataḥ sāmnāpūjya śatrujidagrajam |
uvāca hitam atyarthaṁ vākyaṁ vākyaviśāradaḥ ||

> **Meaning 4:** Having made up his mind, he first honored the conqueror of his
> enemies, his elder brother, with gentle words. Then Vibhīṣaṇa, a master of
> speech, spoke these deeply beneficial words.

---
5.
kṣamasva rōṣaṁ tyaja rākṣasēṁdra
prasīda madvākyamidaṁ śr̥ṇuṣva |
vadhaṁ na kurvaṁti parāvarajñāḥ
dūtasya saṁtō vasudhādhipēṁdrāḥ ||

6.
rājadharmaviruddhaṁ ca lokavr̥tteśca garhitam |
tava cāsadr̥śaṁ vīra kaperasya pramāpaṇam ||

> **Meaning 5-6:** Vibhīṣaṇa said: "Forgive your anger and let it go, king of
> the Rākṣasas. Be gracious and listen to my words. Wise kings of the earth who
> understand what is higher and lower never kill an envoy. Killing this Vānara
> goes against the duty of a king, is condemned by the ways of the world, and is
> unworthy of you, hero."

---
7.
dharmajñaśca kr̥tajñaśca rājadharmaviśāradaḥ |
parāvarajñō bhūtānāṁ tvamēva paramārthavit ||

8.
gr̥hyaṁtē yadi rōṣēṇa tvādr̥śōpi vipaścitaḥ |
tata śśāstravipaścittvaṁ śrama ēva hi kēvalam ||

9.
tasmātprasīda śatrughna rākṣasēṁdra durāsada |
yuktāyuktaṁ viniścitya dūtadaṁḍō vidhīyatām ||

10.
vibhīṣaṇavacaḥ śrutvā rāvaṇō rākṣasēśvaraḥ |
rōṣēṇa mahatāviṣṭō vākyamuttaramabravīt ||

11.
na pāpānāṁ vadhē pāpaṁ vidyatē śatrusūdana |
tasmādēnaṁ vadhiṣyāmi vānaraṁ pāpacāriṇam ||

12.
adharmamūlaṁ bahurōṣayuktaṁ
anāryajuṣṭaṁ vacanaṁ niśamya |
uvāca vākyaṁ paramārthatattvaṁ
vibhīṣaṇō buddhimatāṁ variṣṭhaḥ ||

13.
prasīda laṁkēśvara rākṣasēṁdra
dharmārthayuktaṁ vacanaṁ śr̥ṇuṣva |
dūtānavadhyā ssamayēṣu rājan
sarvēṣu sarvatra vadaṁti saṁtaḥ ||

14.
asaṁśayaṁ śatrurayaṁ pravr̥ddhaḥ kr̥taṁ hy anenāpriyam aprameyam |
na dūtavadhyāṁ pravadanti santaḥ dūtasya dr̥ṣṭā bahavo hi daṇḍāḥ ||

> **Meaning 7-14:** "You know dharma, you remember what others have done for
> you, you are skilled in a king's duty, and you understand all beings, high and
> low. You alone know the highest truth. If even a wise man like you is gripped
> by anger, then all your study of the śāstras was nothing but wasted effort. So
> be gracious, destroyer of enemies, king of the Rākṣasas, hard to approach.
> Weigh what is right and wrong, and decide on a punishment fit for an envoy."
> Hearing Vibhīṣaṇa's words, Rāvaṇa, lord of the Rākṣasas, filled with great
> anger, gave this reply: "There is no sin in killing sinners, Vibhīṣaṇa. I will kill this sinful Vānara." Hearing such a speech, rooted in
> adharma, full of rage and fit only for the ignoble, Vibhīṣaṇa, the best of the
> wise, spoke words of the highest truth: "Be gracious, lord of Laṅkā, king of
> the Rākṣasas, and listen to words grounded in dharma and wisdom. In every
> treaty and every place, the wise declare that envoys must not be killed, king.
> He is surely a grown enemy, and he has done us immeasurable harm. Still, the
> wise do not call for an envoy's death, since many punishments for an envoy are
> known."

---
15.
vairūpyām aṅgeṣu kaśābhighātaḥ mauṇḍyaṁ tathā lakṣmaṇasaṁnipātaḥ |
etān hi dūte pravadanti daṇḍān vadhastu dūtasya na naḥ śrutaḥ api ||

> **Meaning 15:** Deforming the body, whipping, shaving the head, and branding
> with a mark are the punishments prescribed for an envoy. We have never heard
> of killing an envoy.

---
16.
kathaṁ ca dharmārthavinītabuddhiḥ parāvarapratyayaniścitārthaḥ |
bhavadvidhaḥ kopavaśe hi tiṣṭhet kopaṁ niyacchanti hi sattvavantaḥ ||

> **Meaning 16:** How can someone like you, whose mind is trained in dharma and
> wisdom and who is sure of what is higher and lower, fall under the power of
> anger? Those with true strength keep their anger in check.

---
17.
na dharmavāde na ca lokavr̥tte na śāstrabuddhigrahaṇeṣu vāpi |
vidyeta kaścit tava vīratulyaḥ tvaṁ hy uttamaḥ sarvasurāsurāṇām ||

> **Meaning 17:** In speaking of dharma, in worldly conduct, or in grasping the
> teachings of the śāstras, there is no one equal to you, hero. You are the best
> of all devas and asuras.

---
18.
parākramotsāhamanasvināṁ ca surāsurāṇāmapi durjayena |
tvayā’prameyena surendrasaṃghā jitāśca yuddhe śvasakranarendrāḥ ||

> **Meaning 18:** You are hard even for devas and asuras to defeat, and you are
> immeasurable in strength. You have won battles over the hosts of the devas,
> and over Indra and other kings, who are proud of their courage and spirit.

---
19.
itthaṁ vidhasya itthaṁ vidhasya amaradaitya śatroḥ śūrasya vīrasya tavājitasya |
kurvanti vīrā manasāpyalīkaṁ prāṇairvimuktā na tu bhoḥ purā te ||

---
20.
na cāpy asya kaperghāte kaṁ cit paśyāmy ahaṁ guṇam |
teṣv ayaṁ pātyatāṁ daṇḍaḥ yairayaṁ preṣitaḥ kapiḥ ||

> **Meaning 19-20:** Heroes who are dead to their own lives would not even think
> of wronging a warrior like you, the enemy of devas and daityas, brave, heroic
> and unconquered. I also see no gain at all in killing this Vānara. Let the
> punishment fall instead on those who sent this Kapi.

---
21.
sādhurvā yadi vāsādhuḥ paraireṣa samarpitaḥ |
bruvan parārthaṁ paravān na dūto vadham arhati ||

> **Meaning 21:** Whether he is good or bad, he was sent by others. He speaks
> only for someone else and depends on another, so an envoy does not deserve to
> be killed.

---
22.
api cāsmin hate rājan nānyaṁ paśyāmi khecaram |
iha yaḥ punarāgacchet paraṁ pāraṁ mahodadhiḥ ||

> **Meaning 22:** Besides, if he is killed, king, I see no other traveler of the
> sky who could come back here from the far shore of the great ocean.

---
23.
tasmān nāsya vadhe yatnaḥ kāryaḥ parapuraṁjaya |
bhavān sendreṣu deveṣu yatnam āsthātum arhati ||

> **Meaning 23:** So, conqueror of enemy cities, don't make an effort to kill
> him. It is better for you to put your effort into dealing with Indra and the
> devas.

---
24.
asmin vinaṣṭe na hi dūtam anyaṁ paśyāmi yastau nararājaputrau |
yuddhāya yuddhapriyadurvinītāv udyojayed dīrghapathāvaruddhau ||

> **Meaning 24:** If he is killed, I see no other envoy who could stir up those
> two princes, who love battle and are headstrong, and who are cut off by such a
> long journey, to come and fight.

---
25.
parākramotsāhamanasvināṁ ca surāsurāṇām api durjayena |
tvayā manonandana nairr̥tānāṁ yuddhāyatirnāśayituṁ na yuktā ||

> **Meaning 25:** You are hard to defeat even for devas and asuras, and you are
> the joy of the Nairṛtas' hearts. It is not right to destroy the very chance of
> battle for the raksasas who are proud of their courage and spirit.

---
26.
hitāśca śūrāśca samāhitāśca kuleṣu jātāśca mahāguṇeṣu |
manasvinaḥ śastrabhr̥tāṁ variṣṭhāḥ koṭyagraśaste subhr̥tāśca yodhāḥ ||

> **Meaning 26:** Your warriors are loyal, brave and alert, born into families
> of great virtue. They are proud, the best of weapon-bearers, and you maintain
> them well, by the crore.

---
27.
tad ekadeśena balasya tāvat ke cit tavādeśakr̥taḥ apayāntu |
tau rājaputrau vinigr̥hya mūḍhau pareṣu te bhāvayituṁ prabhāvam ||

> **Meaning 27:** So let some of them, at your command, go out with just a part
> of your army, capture those two foolish princes, and show your power over your
> enemies.

---
28.
niśācarāṇāmadhipōnujasya
vibhīṣaṇa syōttamavākya miṣṭam |
jagrāha buddhyā suralōkaśatruḥ
mahābalō rākṣasarājamukhyaḥ ||

> **Meaning 28:** That mighty lord of the night-wanderers, enemy of the devas and
> foremost of Rākṣasa kings, accepted in his mind the excellent and welcome
> counsel of his younger brother Vibhīṣaṇa.
