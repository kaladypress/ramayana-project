=== Sarga 18 ===

---

1.
tathā viprekṣamāṇasya vanam puṣpitapādapam |
vicinvataś ca vaidehīṅ kiñ cic cheṣā niśābhavat ||

2.
ṣaḍaṅgavedaviduṣāṅ kratupravarayājinām |
śuśrāva brahmaghoṣāṁś ca virātre brahmarakṣasām ||

3.
atha maṅgalavāditraiḥ śabdaiḥ śrotramanoharaiḥ |
prābodhyata mahābāhur daśagrīvo mahābalaḥ ||

4.
vibudhya tu yathākālam rākṣasendraḥ pratāvapān |
srastamālyāmbaradharo vaidehīm anvacintayat ||

5.
bhr̥śan niyuktas tasyāñ ca madanena madotkaṭaḥ |
na sa tam rākṣasaḥ kāmaṁ śaśākātmani gūhitum ||



> **Meaning 1-5:** As Hanumān was thus observing the forest filled with
> blossoming trees and searching for Vaidehī, the night came to an end.
> Towards the end of the night, he heard the chanting of the Vedas by the
> Brahma-rākṣasas, who were well-versed in the six auxiliary disciplines of
> the Veda and had performed excellent sacrifices. Then, the mighty and
> exceedingly strong ten-headed one was awakened by the auspicious sounds of
> musical instruments that were pleasing to the ears. Waking up at the appropriate time, the powerful king of Rākṣasas adjusted his scattered garlands and slipping garments, as Sītādevī constantly lingered in his thoughts. Being deeply attached to her and
> severely intoxicated with lust, that Rākṣasa could not conceal the desire
> within his heart.
---
6.
sa sarvābharaṇair yukto bibhrac chriyam anuttamām |
tān nagair vividhair juṣṭāṁ sarvapuṣpaphalopagaiḥ ||

7.
vr̥tām puṣkariṇībhiś ca nānāpuṣpopaśobhitām |
sadāmadaiś ca vihagair vicitrām paramādbhutām ||

8.
īhāmr̥gaiś ca vividhaiś vr̥tān dr̥ṣṭimanoharaiḥ |
vīthīḥ samprekṣamāṇaś ca maṇikāñcanatoraṇāḥ ||

9.
nānāmr̥gagaṇākīrṇām phalaiḥ prapatitair vr̥tām |
aśokavanikām eva prāviśat santatadrumām ||

10.
aṅganāśatamātran tu taṁ vrajantam anuvrajat |
mahendram iva paulastyan devagandharvayoṣitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 6-10:** Adorned with all his ornaments and bearing unsurpassed
> splendor, he entered that Aśoka grove. It was filled with various trees
> bearing all kinds of flowers and fruits, surrounded by lotus ponds, and
> beautified by diverse blossoms. It was astonishingly wonderful, made
> delightful by birds perpetually intoxicated with joy, and filled with
> various wolves that captivated the eyes. As he observed the
> pathways adorned with archways of gems and gold, crowded with herds of
> diverse deer, and covered with fallen fruits, he entered that grove
> densely packed with trees. Following him as he walked were a hundred
> beautiful women, just as celestial maidens and Gandharva women follow the
> great Indra.
---

11.
dīpikāḥ kāñcanīḥ kāś cij jagr̥hus tatra yoṣitaḥ |
bālavyajanahastāś ca tālavr̥ntāni cāparāḥ ||

12.
kāñcanair api bhr̥ṅgārair jahruḥ salilam agrataḥ |
maṇḍalāgrān asīṁś caiva gr̥hyānyāḥ pr̥ṣṭhato yayuḥ ||

13.
kā cid ratnamayīm pātrīm pūrṇām pānasya bhāminī |
dakṣiṇā dakṣiṇenaiva tadā jagrāha pāṇinā ||

14.
rājahaṁsapratīkāśañ chatram pūrṇaśaśiprabham |
sauvarṇadaṇḍam aparā gr̥hītvā pr̥ṣṭhato yayau ||

15.
nidrāmadaparītākṣyo rāvaṇasyottamastriyaḥ |
anujagmuḥ patiṁ vīraṅ ghanaṁ vidyullatā iva ||

> **Meaning 11-15:** Among those women, some carried golden lamps, while
> others held chowries and palm-leaf fans in their hands. Some carried water
> in golden pitchers, walking ahead of him, while others walked behind
> holding circular swords. One beautiful lady held a jewel-studded vessel
> full of wine in her right hand. Another walked behind him holding an
> umbrella with a golden handle, which resembled a royal swan and shone like
> the full moon. With their eyes filled with sleep and intoxication, the
> excellent women of Rāvaṇa followed their heroic husband like flashes of
> lightning following a dark cloud.
---

16.
vyāviddhahārakeyūrāḥ samāmr̥ditavarṇakāḥ |
samāgalitakeśāntāḥ sasvedavadanāstathā ||

17.
ghūrṇantyo madaśeṣeṇa nidrayā ca śubhānanāḥ |
svedakliṣṭāṅgakusumāḥ sumālyākulamūrdhajāḥ ||

18.
prayāntan nairr̥tapatin nāryo madiralocanāḥ |
bahumānācca kāmācca priyā bhāryāstamanvayuḥ ||

19.
sa ca kāmaparādhīnaḥ patistāsām mahābalaḥ |
sītāsaktamanā mando mandāñcitagatirbabhau ||

20.
tataḥ kāñcīninādañ ca nūpurāṇāñ ca niḥsvanam |
śuśrāva paramastrīṇāṁ sa kapir mārutātmajaḥ ||

> **Meaning 16-20:** With their pearl necklaces and armlets tossed about,
> the sandalwood paste on their bodies rubbed off, their hair disheveled,
> and their faces covered in sweat, they followed him. Those beautiful
> women, swaying from the remnants of intoxication and sleep, with the
> flowers on their bodies crushed by sweat and their hair entangled in
> beautiful garlands, accompanied him. Those women with intoxicating eyes,
> driven by great respect and desire, followed their husband, the king of
> the Nairr̥tas, as his beloved wives. That mighty husband of theirs,
> entirely subjugated by lust, walked with a slow and graceful gait, his
> mind singularly fixed on Sītā. Then, that son of Vāyu, the Vānara, heard
> the tinkling sound of the waistbands and the chiming of the anklets of
> those excellent women.
---

21.
tañ cāpratimakarmāṇam acintyabalapauruṣam |
dvāradeśam anuprāptan dadarśa hanumān kapiḥ ||

22.
dīpikābhir anekābhiḥ samantād avabhāsitam |
gandhatailāvasiktābhir dhriyamāṇābhir agrataḥ ||

23.
kāmadarpamadair yuktañ jihmatāmrāyatekṣaṇam |
samakṣam iva kandarpam apaviddha śarāsanam ||

24.
mathitāmr̥taphenābham arajo vastram uttamam |
salīlam anukarṣantaṁ vimuktaṁ saktam aṅgade ||

25.
tam patraviṭape līnaḥ patrapuṣpaghanāvr̥taḥ |
samīpam upasaṅkrāntan nidhyātum upacakrame ||

> **Meaning 21-25:** Hanumān, the Vānara, saw him who had accomplished
> incomparable deeds and possessed unimaginable strength and valor, arriving
> at the gateway. Illuminated on all sides by numerous lamps fueled by
> fragrant oils and carried before him, Rāvaṇa appeared filled with the
> pride and intoxication of lust, his eyes red and slightly slanting. He
> looked like Manmatha himself without his bow. His excellent, spotless
> upper garment, which resembled the froth of churned nectar, had slipped
> and caught on his armlet, and he was playfully pulling at it. Hanumān,
> hidden among the leaves and branches, completely covered by dense foliage
> and flowers, began to carefully observe him as he approached.
---

26.
avekṣamāṇaś ca tato dadarśa kapikuñjaraḥ |
rūpayauvanasampannā rāvaṇasya varastriyaḥ ||

27.
tābhiḥ parivr̥to rājā surūpābhir mahāyaśāḥ |
tanmr̥gadvijasaṅghuṣṭam praviṣṭaḥ pramadāvanam ||

28.
kṣībo vicitrābharaṇaḥ śaṅkukarṇo mahābalaḥ |
tena viśravasaḥ putraḥ sa dr̥ṣṭo rākṣasādhipaḥ ||

29.
vr̥taḥ paramanārībhis tārābhir iva candramāḥ |
tan dadarśa mahātejās tejovantam mahākapiḥ ||

30.
rāvaṇo ’yam mahābāhur iti sañcintya vānaraḥ |
avapluto mahātejā hanūmān mārutātmajaḥ ||

31.
sa tathāpy ugratejāḥ san nirdhūtas tasya tejasā |
patraguhyāntare sakto hanūmān saṁvr̥to ’bhavat ||

32.
sa tām asitakeśāntāṁ suśroṇīṁ saṁhatastanīm |
didr̥kṣur asitāpāṅgīm upāvartata rāvaṇaḥ ||

> **Meaning 26-32:** Looking down from there, that bull among Vānaras saw
> the excellent women of Rāvaṇa, endowed with youth and beauty. Surrounded
> by those beautiful women, the highly renowned king entered that pleasure
> grove, which was resonant with the cries of animals and birds. The son of
> Viśravā, the king of Rākṣasas, who was intoxicated, adorned with diverse
> ornaments, possessing ears like spikes, and endowed with immense strength,
> was seen by him. Surrounded by those excellent women like the moon
> encircled by stars, the great Vānara beheld that highly radiant one.
> Thinking, 'This is the same mighty-armed Rāvaṇa whom I saw earlier
> sleeping in his grand palace in Laṅkā,' the highly radiant Vānara,
> Hanumān, the son of Vāyu, climbed down a little bit. Although possessing
> immense brilliance himself, Hanumān, as if unable to bear Rāvaṇa's
> radiance, withdrew and remained concealed, hiding amidst the dense foliage
> so that no one could see him, in order to observe his actions. Desirous of
> seeing Sītā, who
> possessed beautiful dark hair, lovely hips, firm breasts, and beautiful
> eyes, that Rāvaṇa approached her.
