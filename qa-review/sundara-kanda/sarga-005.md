=== Sarga 5 ===

---
1.
tataḥ sa madhyaṁ gatam aṁśumantaṁ
jyotsnāvitānaṁ mahad udvamantam |
dadarśa dhīmān divi bhānumantaṁ
goṣṭhe vr̥ṣaṁ mattam iva bhramantam ||

2.
lokasya pāpāni vināśayantaṁ
mahodadhiṁ cāpi samedhayantam |
bhūtāni sarvāṇi virājayantaṁ
dadarśa śītāṁśum athābhiyāntam ||

3.
yā bhāti lakṣmīr bhuvi mandarasthā
tathā pradoṣeṣu ca sāgarasthā |
tathaiva toyeṣu ca puṣkarasthā
rarāja sā cāruniśākarasthā ||

> **Meaning 1-3:** Then, the wise Hanumān saw
> the moon, radiant with rays, spreading a vast canopy of moonlight, stationed
> in the middle of the sky. The radiant moon appeared like a proud bull
> wandering in a cow-pen. He was dispelling the sins of the world, swelling the
> great ocean, and illuminating all living beings. Just as Lakṣmī (the goddess
> of fortune) shines on Mount Mandara, in the ocean during the evening, and on
> lotuses in the water, she similarly shone gracefully upon the beautiful
> moonlight.
---

4.
haṁso yathā rājatapañjurasthaḥ
siṁho yathā mandarakandarasthaḥ |
vīro yathā garvitakuñjarasthaḥ
candro ’pi babhrāja tathāmbarasthaḥ ||

5.
sthitaḥ kakudmān iva tīkṣṇaśr̥ṅgaḥ
mahācalaḥ śveta ivoccaśr̥ṅgaḥ |
hastīva jāmbūnadabaddhaśr̥ṅgaḥ
vibhāti candraḥ paripūrṇaśr̥ṅgaḥ ||

6.
vinaṣṭaśītāṁbutuṣārapaṁkō
mahāgrahagrāha vinaṣṭapaṁkaḥ |
prakāśalakṣmyāśraya nirmalāṁkō
rarāja caṁdrō bhagavān śaśāṁkaḥ || 

7.
śilātalaṁ prāpya yathā mr̥gēṁdrō
mahāraṇaṁ prāpya yathā gajēṁdraḥ |
rājyaṁ samāsādya yathā narēṁdraḥ
tathā prakāśō virarāja caṁdraḥ || 

8.
prakāśacaṁdrōdaya naṣṭadōṣaḥ
pravr̥ttarakṣaḥ piśitāśadōṣaḥ |
rāmābhirāmērita cittadōṣaḥ
svargaprakāśō bhagavān pradōṣaḥ || 

> **Meaning 4-8:** Just as a swan inside a
> silver cage, a lion in a cave of Mount Mandara, or a proud warrior mounted on
> an elephant, the moon shone brightly in the sky. The perfectly full Moon,
> shining majestically in the sky, was likened to three majestic sights: a
> sharp-horned bull, a great white mountain with a high peak, and a magnificent
> elephant with its tusks adorned with gold. The divine moon, its spots cleansed
> of the mud-like cold dews, shone immaculately, freed from the grasp of the
> great eclipse, acting as a refuge of radiant beauty. Like a lion reaching a
> rocky plateau, a king elephant entering a great forest, or newly coronated and
> seated on his throne, the brilliant moon shone majestically. The luminous
> twilight (Pradosha), marked by the moon's ascent, brought joy to people by
> banishing the darkness. Simultaneously, night-active demons (Nishacharas)
> indulged in meat-based delicacies. While men and women were peacefully
> enjoying their time together, the Moon, as a source of universal joy, also
> provided heavenly happiness to Hanuman during that evening hour.
---

9.
taṁtrīsvanāḥ karṇasukhāḥ pravr̥ttāḥ 
svapaṁti nāryaḥ patibhissuvr̥ttāḥ |
naktaṁcarāścāpi tathā pravr̥ttāḥ 
vihartumatyadbhutaraudravr̥ttāḥ || 

10.
mattapramattāni samākulāni
rathāśvabhadrāsana saṁkulāni |
vīraśriyā cāpi samākulāni
dadarśa dhīmān sa kapiḥ kulāni || 

11.
parasparaṁ cādhikamākṣipaṁti
bhujāṁśca pīnānadhinikṣipaṁti |
mattapralāpānadhivikṣipaṁti
mattāni cānyōnya madhikṣipaṁti ||

12.
rakṣāṁsi vakṣāṁsi ca vikṣipaṁti
gātrāṇi kāṁtāsu ca vikṣipaṁti |
rūpāṇi citrāṇi ca vikṣipaṁti
dr̥ḍhāni cāpāni ca vikṣipaṁti || 

13.
dadarśa kāṁtāśca samālabhaṁtyaḥ
tathāparāstatra puna ssvapaṁtyaḥ |
surūpavaktrāśca tathā hasaṁtyaḥ
kruddhāḥ parāścāpi viniśśvasaṁtyaḥ || 

> **Meaning 9-13:** In the city, sweet sounds of
> stringed instruments pleasing to the ears were playing, and virtuous women
> were sleeping beside their husbands. Meanwhile, night-roaming Rākṣasas engaged
> in incredibly dreadful and fierce activities were also wandering about.
> Hanuman saw the houses of Lanka, which were bustling with those who were
> intoxicated and excited. Some were filled with chariots, horses, and splendid
> palanquins. Others featured elephants decorated in ambari and were grand with
> signs of rich wealth. He saw Rākṣasas boasting excessively to one another,
> throwing their stout arms around, indulging in drunken babble, and hurling
> insults at each other. The Rākṣasas beat their chests and embraced their
> beloveds; they displayed diverse and marvelous forms and wielded stout bows.
> Inside those palaces, some women were anointing themselves with sandalwood and
> other fragrant pastes, some women were going back to deep sleep, some
> beautiful women were smiling, and some were sighing.
---

14.
mahāgajaiścāpi tathā nadadbhiḥ
supūjitaiścāpi tathā susadbhiḥ |
rarāja vīraiśca viniśśvasadbhiḥ
hradō bhujaṁgairiva niśśvasadbhiḥ ||

15.
buddhipradhānān rucirābhidhānān
saṁśraddhadhānān jagataḥ pradhānān |
nānāvidhānān rucirābhidhānān
dadarśa tasyāṁ puri yātudhānān ||

16.
nanaṁda dr̥ṣṭvā sa ca tān surūpān
nānāguṇānātmaguṇānurūpān |
vidyōtamānān sa tadānurūpān
dadarśa kāṁścicca punarvirūpān ||

> **Meaning 14-16:** The city shone with roaring
> great elephants, highly respected and virtuous individuals, and hissing
> warriors resembling deeply sighing serpents in a lake. In that city, Hanumān
> saw Yātudhānas who were preeminent in intellect, bearing beautiful names, full
> of faith, and considered leaders of the world. Seeing those handsome Rākṣasas
> of various qualities matching their inner nature, he rejoiced; yet, he also
> saw some who were extremely deformed and terrifying.
---

17.
tatō varārhā ssuviśuddhabhāvāḥ
tēṣāṁ striyastatra mahānubhāvāḥ |
priyēṣu pānēṣu ca saktabhāvāḥ
dadarśa tārā iva suprabhāvāḥ ||

18.
śriyā jvalaṁtīstrapayōpagūḍhāḥ
niśīthakālē ramaṇōpagūḍhāḥ |
dadarśa kāścit pramadōpagūḍhāḥ
yathā vihaṁgāḥ kusumōpagūḍhāḥ ||

19.
anyāḥ punarharmya talōpaviṣṭāḥ
tatra priyāṁkēṣu sukhōpaviṣṭāḥ |
bhartuḥ priyā dharmaparā niviṣṭāḥ
dadarśa dhīmān madanābhiviṣṭāḥ ||

20.
aprāvr̥tāḥ kāṁcanarājivarṇāḥ
kāścit parārthyā stapanīyavarṇāḥ |
punaśca kāścicchaśa lakṣmavarṇāḥ
kāṁtaprahīṇā rucirāṁgavarṇāḥ ||

> **Meaning 17-20:** He then saw women of noble
> birth and pure character, possessing great dignity. Addicted to sweet drinks,
> these highly radiant women shone like stars. Some, glowing with beauty but
> veiled in modesty, were embraced by their lovers in the middle of the night,
> resembling birds taking shelter in blossoms. Other beautiful and virtuous
> women were seated comfortably on the terraces of their mansions, resting on
> the laps of their husbands. Hanumān saw some women uncovered, possessing
> complexions like golden streaks, others resembling the color of molten gold,
> some having the complexion of the moon, and others endowed with beautiful
> bodies bereft of their lovers.
---

21.
tataḥ priyān prāpya manō’bhirāmān
suprītiyuktāḥ prasamīkṣya rāmāḥ |
gr̥hēṣu hr̥ṣṭāḥ paramābhirāmāḥ
haripravīraḥ sa dadarśa rāmāḥ ||

22.
caṁdraprakāśāśca hi vaktramālāḥ
vakrākṣipakṣmāśca sunētramālāḥ |
vibhūṣaṇānāṁ ca dadarśa mālāḥ
śatahradānāmiva cārumālāḥ ||

> **Meaning 21-22:** The foremost among the
> Vānaras saw those extremely beautiful women, filled with immense joy upon
> reuniting with their mind-captivating lovers, rejoicing in their homes. He
> observed their faces glowing like the moon, their beautiful eyes adorned with
> curved eyelashes, and the rows of their ornaments shining brightly like
> flashes of lightning.
---

23.
na tvēva sītāṁ paramābhijātāṁ
pathi sthitē rājakulē prajātām |
latāṁ praphullāmiva sādhujātāṁ
dadarśa tanvīṁ manasābhijātām ||

24.
sanātanē vartmani sanniviṣṭāṁ
rāmēkṣaṇāṁ tāṁ madanābhiviṣṭām |
bharturmana śśrīmadanupraviṣṭāṁ
strībhyō varābhyaśca sadā viśiṣṭām ||

25.
uṣṇārditāṁ sānusr̥tāsrakaṁṭhīṁ
purā varārhōttama niṣkakaṁṭhīm |
sujātapakṣmāmabhiraktakaṁṭhīm
vanē pranr̥ttāmiva nīlakaṁṭhīm ||

26.
avyaktarēkhāmiva caṁdrarēkhāṁ
pāṁsupradigdhāmiva hēmarēkhām |
kṣataprarūḍhāmiva bāṇarēkhāṁ
vāyuprabhinnāmiva mēgharēkhām ||

27.
sītāmapaśyanmanujēśvarasya
rāmasya patnīṁ vadatāṁ varasya |
babhūva duḥkhābhihataścirasya
plavaṁgamō maṁda ivācirasya ||

> **Meaning 23-27:** However, he did not find
> the most beautiful and gentle Sītā— she who follows the path of dharma, who
> originated from a royal dynasty, who possessed a beauty as perfect as a
> blooming flower, and who was not born from a womb (ayonijā). He did not find
> her—who never crosses the path of righteousness, who is in the pain of
> separation from Śrī Rāma, who is always waiting for his glance, who stays
> firmly within Rāma’s heart, and who is the best among all women. He could not
> find her—who is in deep anguish over her separation from Rāma, weeping
> uncontrollably with a throat choked by sorrow; she who once wore precious
> necklaces, possesses beautiful eyelids and a melodious voice, and resembles a
> peahen dancing beautifully in the forests. He did not find that Sītā—who now
> appears like a faint crescent of the moon, an unpolished string of gold, a
> thin scar left after the healing of an arrow wound, or a thin cloud scattered
> by heavy winds. Having searched for a long time without finding Sītā—the wife
> of Śrī Rāma, the most eloquent of speakers—Hanumān was struck by a deep and
> enduring sorrow, looking dull and dejected for a moment.
---
