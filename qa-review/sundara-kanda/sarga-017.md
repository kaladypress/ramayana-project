=== Sarga 17 ===

---

1.
tataḥ kumudaṣaṇḍābho nirmalan nirmalaḥ svayam |
prajagāma nabhaś candro haṁso nīlam ivodakam ||

2.
sācivyam iva kurvan sa prabhayā nirmalaprabhaḥ |
candramā raśmibhiḥ śītaiḥ siṣeve pavanātmajam ||

3.
sa dadarśa tataḥ sītām pūrṇacandranibhānanām |
śokabhārair iva nyastām bhārair nāvam ivāmbhasi ||

4.
didr̥kṣamāṇo vaidehīṁ hanūmān mārutātmajaḥ |
sa dadarśāvidūrasthā rākṣasīr ghoradarśanāḥ ||

> **Meaning 1-4:** Then, the spotless moon, resembling a cluster of white
> lotuses, coursed through the sky to west, like a swan gliding in blue waters.
> Offering his assistance as if with his radiance, the bright moon served
> the son of Vāyu with his cool rays. Then he beheld Sītā, whose face
> resembled the full moon, sinking under the weight of her sorrow like a
> heavily laden boat sinking in water. Desiring to see Vaidehī, Hanumān, the
> son of Vāyu, saw terrifying Rākṣasīs stationed not far away.
---

5.
ekākṣīm ekakarṇāñ ca karṇaprāvaraṇān tathā |
akarṇāṁ śaṅkukarṇāñ ca mastakocchvāsanāsikām ||

6.
atikāyottamāṅgīñ ca tanudīrghaśirodharām |
dhvastakeśīn tathākeśīṅ keśakambaladhāriṇīm ||

7.
lambakarṇalalāṭāñ ca lambodarapayodharām |
lambauṣṭhīñ cibukauṣṭhīñ ca lambāsyāṁ lambajānukām ||

8.
hrasvān dīrghāñ ca kubjāñ ca vikaṭāṁ vāmanān tathā |
karālām bhugnavastrāñ ca piṅgākṣīṁ vikr̥tānanām ||

9.
vikr̥tāḥ piṅgalāḥ kālīḥ krodhanāḥ kalahapriyāḥ |
kālāyasamahāśūlakūṭamudgaradhāriṇīḥ ||

10.
varāhamr̥gaśārdūlamahiṣājaśivā mukhāḥ |
gajoṣṭrahayapādāś ca nikhātaśiraso ’parāḥ ||

11.
ekahastaikapādāś ca kharakarṇyaśvakarṇikāḥ |
gokarṇīr hastikarṇīś ca harikarṇīs tathāparāḥ ||

12.
anāsā atināsāś ca tiryan nāsā vināsikāḥ |
gajasannibhanāsāś ca lalāṭocchvāsanāsikāḥ ||

13.
hastipādā mahāpādā gopādāḥ pādacūlikāḥ |
atimātraśirogrīvā atimātrakucodarīḥ ||

14.
atimātrāsya netrāś ca dīrghajihvānakhās tathā |
ajāmukhīr hastimukhīr gomukhīḥ sūkarīmukhīḥ ||

15.
hayoṣṭrakharavaktrāś ca rākṣasīr ghoradarśanāḥ |
śūlamudgarahastāś ca krodhanāḥ kalahapriyāḥ ||

16.
karālā dhūmrakeśīś ca rakṣasīr vikr̥tānanāḥ |
pibantīḥ satatam pānaṁ sadā māṁsasurāpriyāḥ ||

17.
māṁsaśoṇitadigdhāṅgīr māṁsaśoṇitabhojanāḥ |
tā dadarśa kapiśreṣṭho romaharṣaṇadarśanāḥ ||

> **Meaning 5-17:** Some had one eye, some had one ear, some used their ears
> as coverings, some were without ears, some had ears like spikes, and some
> had noses that snorted heavily. Some had gigantic bodies and prominent
> heads, some had long, slender necks, some had disheveled hair, some had no
> hair, and some wore blankets of hair. Some had pendulous ears and
> foreheads, some had hanging bellies and breasts, some had protruding lips,
> some had lips on their chins, some had hanging faces, and some had hanging
> knees. Some were short, some tall, some hunchbacked, some distorted, some
> dwarfs, some with terrifying features, some with twisted garments, some
> with yellow eyes, and some with deformed faces. They were deformed, tawny,
> dark, wrathful, fond of quarreling, and carried huge darts, clubs, and
> mallets made of iron. Some had faces of boars, deer, tigers, buffaloes,
> goats, and jackals; others had the feet of elephants, camels, and horses,
> while some had sunken heads. Some had a single hand or a single foot, some
> had ears like donkeys or horses, and others had ears like cows, elephants,
> or monkeys. Some were noseless, some had oversized noses, some had crooked
> noses, some had noses like elephants, and some had noses extending to
> their foreheads. Some had the feet of elephants, huge feet, feet like
> cows, or tufts of hair on their feet; some had disproportionately large
> heads and necks, and some had massive breasts and bellies. Some had overly
> large mouths and eyes, long tongues and nails; some had the faces of
> goats, elephants, cows, and pigs. Some had faces of horses, camels, and
> donkeys; these Rākṣasīs of terrifying appearance, holding spears and
> mallets in their hands, were wrathful and fond of quarreling. These
> terrible Rākṣasīs with smoke-colored hair and deformed faces were
> constantly drinking liquor and always fond of meat and wine. The foremost
> of the Vānaras beheld those Rākṣasīs, whose bodies were smeared with meat
> and blood, whose food consisted of meat and blood, and whose very sight
> caused one's hair to stand on end.
---

18.
skandhavantam upāsīnāḥ parivārya vanaspatim |
tasyādhastāc ca tān devīm rājaputrīm aninditām ||

19.
lakṣayām āsa lakṣmīvān hanūmāñ janakātmajām |
niṣprabhāṁ śokasantaptām malasaṅkulamūrdhajām ||

20.
kṣīṇapuṇyāñ cyutām bhūmau tārān nipatitām iva |
cāritrya vyapadeśāḍhyām bhartr̥darśanadurgatām ||

21.
bhūṣaṇair uttamair hīnām bhartr̥vātsalyabhūṣitām |
rākṣasādhipasaṁruddhām bandhubhiś ca vinākr̥tām ||

22.
viyūthāṁ siṁhasaṁruddhām baddhāṅ gajavadhūm iva |
candralekhām payodānte śāradābhrair ivāvr̥tām ||

23.
kliṣṭarūpām asaṁsparśād ayuktām iva vallakīm |
sītām bhartr̥hite yuktām ayuktām rakṣasāṁ vaśe ||

24.
aśokavanikāmadhye śokasāgaram āplutām |
tābhiḥ parivr̥tān tatra sagrahām iva rohiṇīm ||

> **Meaning 18-24:** Sitting around and surrounding a large tree, they were
> guarding the flawless princess, the divine lady seated beneath it. The
> glorious Hanumān observed the daughter of Janaka, devoid of radiance,
> tormented by sorrow, with her hair tangled and covered in dirt. She looked
> like a fallen star whose merit had been exhausted and dropped to the
> earth; rich in character and reputation, she was miserable due to being
> unable to see her husband. Devoid of excellent ornaments but adorned with
> devotion to her husband, she was confined by the king of Rākṣasas and
> separated from her kin. She resembled an elephant captured and bound by a
> lion, separated from her herd, or like the crescent moon at the end of the
> rainy season covered by autumn clouds. With a faded appearance due to lack
> of care, she looked like an untuned lute; devoted to the welfare of her
> husband, Sītā was unfittingly held in the power of the Rākṣasas. Sitting
> in the midst of the Aśoka grove, submerged in an ocean of sorrow, she was
> surrounded by them like the star Rohiṇī seized by planets.
---

25.
dadarśa hanumān devīṁ latām akusumām iva |
sā malena ca digdhāṅgī vapuṣā cāpy alaṅkr̥tā |
mr̥ṇālī paṅkadigdheva vibhāti ca na bhāti ca ||

26.
malinena tu vastreṇa parikliṣṭena bhāminīm |
saṁvr̥tām mr̥gaśāvākṣīn dadarśa hanumān kapiḥ ||

27.
tān devīn dīnavadanām adīnām bhartr̥tejasā |
rakṣitāṁ svena śīlena sītām asitalocanām ||

28.
tān dr̥ṣṭvā hanumān sītām mr̥gaśāvanibhekṣaṇām |
mr̥gakanyām iva trastāṁ vīkṣamāṇāṁ samantataḥ ||

29.
dahantīm iva niḥśvāsair vr̥kṣān pallavadhāriṇaḥ |
saṅghātam iva śokānān duḥkhasyormim ivotthitām ||

30.
tāṅ kṣāmāṁ suvibhaktāṅgīṁ vinābharaṇaśobhinīm |
praharṣam atulaṁ lebhe mārutiḥ prekṣya maithilīm ||

31.
harṣajāni ca so ’śrūṇi tān dr̥ṣṭvā madirekṣaṇām |
mumoca hanumāṁs tatra namaś cakre ca rāghavam ||

32.
namaskr̥tvā ca rāmāya lakṣmaṇāya ca vīryavān |
sītādarśanasaṁhr̥ṣṭo hanūmān saṁvr̥to ’bhavat ||

> **Meaning 25-32:** Hanumān saw the divine lady resembling a creeper
> stripped of its flowers; her body was soiled with dirt, yet she was
> adorned by her natural beauty. She appeared like a lotus stem smeared with
> mud, which shines and yet does not shine. Clad in a soiled and worn-out
> garment, Hanumān saw the beautiful lady with eyes like a fawn, completely
> surrounded. Sītā, with dark eyes, whose face was miserable yet
> undiminished due to her husband's glory, was protected by her own noble
> character. Seeing Sītā with eyes resembling those of a fawn, looking
> around in terror like a young doe, sighing as if scorching the trees
> bearing fresh leaves, like an embodiment of sorrow or a rising wave of
> grief; seeing that slender lady with beautifully proportioned limbs, who
> looked beautiful even without ornaments, Māruti attained incomparable joy
> upon beholding the princess of Mithilā. Shedding tears of joy upon seeing
> that lady with intoxicating eyes, Hanumān offered his namaskarams to
> Rāghava there. Having bowed to Śrī Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, the mighty Hanumān,
> thrilled at the sight of Sītā, remained concealed.
---
