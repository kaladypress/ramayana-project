=== Sarga 14 ===

---

1.
sa muhūrtam iva dhyatvā manasā cādhigamya tām |
avapluto mahātejāḥ prākāraṁ tasya veśmanaḥ ||

2.
sa tu saṁhr̥ṣṭasarvāṅgaḥ prākārastho mahākapiḥ |
puṣpitāgrān vasantādau dadarśa vividhān drumān ||

3.
sālān aśokān bhavyāṁś ca campakāṁś ca supuṣpitān |
uddālakān nāgavr̥kṣāṁś cūtān kapimukhān api ||

4.
athāmravaṇasañchannāṁ latāśatasamāvr̥tām |
jyāmukta iva nārācaḥ pupluve vr̥kṣavāṭikām ||

5.
sa praviṣya vicitrāṁ tāṁ vihagair abhināditām |
rājataiḥ kāñcanaiś caiva pādapaiḥ sarvatovr̥tām ||

6.
vihagair mr̥gasaṅghaiś ca vicitrāṁ citrakānanām |
uditādityasaṅkāśāṁ dadarśa hanumān kapiḥ ||


> **Meaning 1-5:** Having meditated for a moment and determined his course
> of action in his mind, the highly radiant one leaped onto the boundary
> wall of Ravana's Ashoka grove. Standing on the boundary wall, the great Kapi, with
> his body thrilling with joy, beheld various trees in full bloom at the
> onset of spring. He saw Sāla, Aśoka, Bhavya, Campaka, Uddālaka, Nāga,
> Cūta, and Kapimukha trees in full blossom. Then, like an arrow released
> from a bowstring, he leaped into the grove of trees, which was concealed
> by a mango orchard and surrounded by hundreds of creepers. Having entered
> that wonderful grove, which was resonant with the songs of birds and
> surrounded on all sides by silver and golden trees.Hanumān, the Kapi, beheld that wonderful forest,
> which was filled with birds and flocks of deer, and shone like the rising
> sun.
---


7.
vr̥tāṁ nānāvidhair vr̥kṣaiḥ puṣpopagaphalopagaiḥ |
kokilair bhr̥ṅgarājaiś ca mattair nityaniṣevitām ||

8.
prahr̥ṣṭamanuje kale mr̥gapakṣisamākule |
mattabarhiṇasaṅghuṣṭāṁ nānādvijagaṇāyutām ||

9.
mārgamāṇo varārohāṁ rājaputrīm aninditām |
sukhaprasuptān vihagān bodhayām āsa vānaraḥ ||

10.
utpatadbhir dvijagaṇaiḥ pakṣaiḥ sālāḥ samāhatāḥ |
anekavarṇā vividhā mumucuḥ puṣpavr̥ṣṭayaḥ ||

11.
puṣpāvakīrṇaḥ śuśubhe hanumān mārutātmajaḥ |
aśokavanikāmadhye yathā puṣpamayo giriḥ ||

> **Meaning 7-11:** It was filled with various kinds of trees bearing flowers and fruits,
> and was constantly frequented by cuckoos and intoxicated Bhṛṅgarāja birds.
> At that joyous time, the forest was teeming with birds and beasts, echoing
> with the cries of intoxicated peacocks, and filled with various flocks of
> birds. Searching for the flawless princess, an embodiment of beauty, the Vānara
> woke up the birds that were sleeping comfortably. As the flocks of birds
> flew up, their wings struck the trees, and the various trees showered a
> rain of multicolored flowers. Covered with those flowers, Hanumān, the son
> of Vāyu, shone in the midst of the Aśoka grove like a mountain made of
> flowers.
---

12.
diśaḥ sarvābhidāvantaṁ vr̥kṣaṣaṇḍagataṁ kapim |
dr̥ṣṭvā sarvāṇi bhūtāni vasanta iti menire ||

13.
vr̥kṣebhyaḥ patitaiḥ puṣpair avakīrṇā pr̥thagvidhaiḥ |
rarāja vasudhā tatra pramadeva vibhūṣitā ||

14.
tarasvinā te taravas tarasābhiprakampitāḥ |
kusumāni vicitrāṇi sasr̥juḥ kapinā tadā ||

15.
nirdhūtapatraśikharāḥ śīrṇapuṣpaphaladrumāḥ |
nikṣiptavastrābharaṇā dhūrtā iva parājitāḥ ||

16.
hanūmatā vegavatā kampitās te nagottamāḥ |
puṣpaparṇaphalāny āśu mumucuḥ puṣpaśālinaḥ ||

17.
vihaṅgasaṅghair hīnās te skandhamātrāśrayā drumāḥ |
babhūvur agamāḥ sarve māruteneva nirdhutāḥ ||

18.
vidhūtakeśī yuvatir yathā mr̥ditavarṇikā |
niṣpītaśubhadantauṣṭhī nakhair dantaiś ca vikṣatā ||

19.
tathā lāṅgūlahastaiś ca caraṇābhyāṁ ca marditā |
babhūvāśokavanikā prabhagnavarapādapā ||

> **Meaning 12-19:** Seeing the Kapi darting in all directions amidst the
> cluster of trees, all beings thought him to be the personification of
> Spring. Covered with various kinds of flowers fallen from the trees, the
> earth there shone like a beautifully adorned woman. Shaken violently by
> the swift Vānara, those trees then released wonderful blossoms. With their
> leaves and treetops shaken off, and their flowers and fruits shattered,
> the trees looked like defeated gamblers stripped of their garments and
> ornaments. Shaken by the swift Hanumān, those excellent trees, which were
> once resplendent with flowers, quickly shed their blossoms, leaves, and
> fruits. Devoid of the flocks of birds and reduced to mere trunks, all
> those trees became motionless, as if battered by a storm. Trampled by
> Māruti's tail, hands, and feet, the excellent trees in that forest were
> severely shattered. Then, that Aśoka grove appeared like a young woman
> who, exhausted by the amorous pastimes of her lover, has disheveled hair
> and faded body makeup.
---

20.
mahālatānāṁ dāmāni vyadhamat tarasā kapiḥ |
yathā prāvr̥ṣi vindhyasya meghajālāni mārutaḥ ||

21.
sa tatra maṇibhūmīś ca rājatīś ca manoramāḥ |
tathā kāñcanabhūmīś ca vicaran dadr̥śe kapiḥ ||

22.
vāpīś ca vividhākārāḥ pūrṇāḥ paramavāriṇā |
mahārhair maṇisopānair upapannās tatas tataḥ ||

23.
muktāpravālasikatā sphaṭikāntarakuṭṭimāḥ |
kāñcanais tarubhiś citrais tīrajair upaśobhitāḥ ||

24.
phullapadmotpalavanāś cakravākopakūjitāḥ |
natyūharutasaṅghuṣṭā haṁsasārasanāditāḥ ||

25.
dīrghābhir drumayuktābhiḥ saridbhiś ca samantataḥ |
amr̥topamatoyābhiḥ śivābhir upasaṁskr̥tāḥ ||

26.
latāśatair avatatāḥ santānakasamāvr̥tāḥ |
nānāgulmāvr̥tavanāḥ karavīrakr̥tāntarāḥ ||

> **Meaning 20-26:** The Kapi forcefully tore down the garlands of massive
> creepers, just as the wind scatters the clusters of clouds over the
> Vindhya mountains during the monsoon. Roaming there, the Kapi beheld
> enchanting grounds paved with gems, silver, and gold. He saw ponds of
> various shapes, filled with excellent water, and adorned here and there
> with highly valuable jeweled steps. The ponds had sand made of pearls and
> corals, floors inlaid with crystal, and were beautifully decorated with
> wonderful golden trees growing on their banks. They were filled with
> clusters of blooming lotuses and water lilies, echoing with the calls of
> Cakravāka birds, resounding with the cries of Natyūhas, and resonant with
> the sounds of swans and Sārasa cranes. Those ponds were surrounded on all
> sides by long streams lined with trees, whose waters were like nectar, and
> which were beautifully constructed with auspicious elements. They were
> covered with hundreds of creepers, surrounded by Santānaka trees, enclosed
> by thickets of various bushes, and interspersed with Karavīra plants.
---

27.
tato ’mbudharasaṅkāśaṁ pravr̥ddhaśikharaṁ girim |
vicitrakūṭaṁ kūṭaiś ca sarvataḥ parivāritam ||

28.
śilāgr̥hair avatataṁ nānāvr̥kṣaiḥ samāvr̥tam |
dadarśa kapiśārdūlo ramyaṁ jagati parvatam ||

29.
dadarśa ca nagāt tasmān nadīṁ nipatitāṁ kapiḥ |
aṅkād iva samutpatya priyasya patitāṁ priyām ||

30.
jale nipatitāgraiś ca pādapair upaśobhitām |
vāryamāṇām iva kruddhāṁ pramadāṁ priyabandhubhiḥ ||

31.
punar āvr̥ttatoyāṁ ca dadarśa sa mahākapiḥ |
prasannām iva kāntasya kāntāṁ punar upasthitām ||

32.
tasyādūrāt sa padminyo nānādvijagaṇāyutāḥ |
dadarśa kapiśārdūlo hanumān mārutātmajaḥ ||

33.
kr̥trimāṁ dīrghikāṁ cāpi pūrṇāṁ śītena vāriṇā |
maṇipravarasopānāṁ muktāsikataśobhitām ||

> **Meaning 27-33:** Then, the foremost of the Kapis beheld a delightful
> mountain in that landscape, which resembled a cloud, had lofty peaks,
> wonderful summits, and was surrounded on all sides by smaller peaks. It
> was covered with stone caves and surrounded by various trees. The Kapi saw
> a river tumbling down from that mountain, appearing like a beloved who,
> feigning anger in love, swiftly leaps from the lap of her lover. With the
> branches of the trees on the banks bending down to touch the water, it
> seemed as if dear relatives were blocking the flow and restraining that
> angry lady with their hands. Just as that lady, having been convinced by
> her dear friends, becomes pleased and turns back to reach her lover, the
> excellent Kapi again saw the river's water turning back towards the
> mountain. Not far from there, Hanumān, the son of Vāyu and the foremost of
> the Kapis, beheld lotus ponds teeming with various flocks of birds. He
> also saw an artificial, elongated pond filled with cool water, boasting
> steps made of excellent gems, and adorned with pearl-like sand.
---

34.
vividhair mr̥gasaṅghaiś ca vicitrāṁ citrakānanām |
prāsādaiḥ sumahadbhiś ca nirmitair viśvakarmaṇā ||

35.
kānanaiḥ kr̥trimaiś cāpi sarvataḥ samalaṅkr̥tām |
ye ke cit pādapās tatra puṣpopagaphalopagāḥ ||

36.
sacchatrāḥ savitardīkāḥ sarve sauvarṇavedikāḥ |
latāpratānair bahubhiḥ parṇaiś ca bahubhir vr̥tām ||

37.
kāñcanīṁ śiṁśupām ekāṁ dadarśa sa mahākapiḥ |
vṛtāṁ hemamayībhistu vedikābhiḥ samantataḥ ||

38.
so ’paśyad bhūmibhāgāṁś ca gartaprasravaṇāni ca |
suvarṇavr̥kṣān aparān dadarśa śikhisannibhān ||

39.
teṣāṁ drumāṇāṁ prabhayā meror iva mahākapiḥ |
amanyata tadā vīraḥ kāñcano ’smīti vānaraḥ ||

> **Meaning 34-39:** He saw a wonderful, variegated forest filled with
> diverse herds of deer, featuring immense palaces built by Viśvakarmā, and
> beautifully adorned on all sides with artificial woods. Whatever trees
> were there, bearing flowers and fruits, all of them had protective
> umbrellas, platforms, and golden altars. The great Kapi beheld a single
> golden Śiṁśapā tree, enveloped by numerous spreading creepers and abundant
> leaves, and surrounded on all sides by platforms made of gold. He observed
> the grounds, the hollows, and the springs, and saw other golden trees
> shining like fire. Due to the radiant glow of those trees, the heroic
> Vānara, the great Kapi, considered himself to be made of gold, like Mount
> Meru.
---

40.
tāṁ kāñcanais tarugaṇair mārutena ca vījitām |
kiṅkiṇīśatanirghoṣāṁ dr̥ṣṭvā vismayam āgamat ||

41.
supuṣpitāgrāṁ rucirāṁ taruṇāṅkurapallavām |
tām āruhya mahāvegaḥ śiṁśapāṁ parṇasaṁvr̥tām ||

42.
ito drakṣyāmi vaidehīṁ rāma darśanalālasām |
itaś cetaś ca duḥkhārtāṁ sampatantīṁ yadr̥cchayā ||

43.
aśokavanikā ceyaṁ dr̥ḍhaṁ ramyā durātmanaḥ |
campakaiś candanaiś cāpi bakulaiś ca vibhūṣitā ||

44.
iyaṁ ca nalinī ramyā dvijasaṅghaniṣevitā |
imāṁ sā rāmamahiṣī nūnam eṣyati jānakī ||

45.
sā rāma rāmamahiṣī rāghavasya priyā sadā |
vanasañcārakuśalā nūnam eṣyati jānakī ||

46.
atha vā mr̥gaśāvākṣī vanasyāsya vicakṣaṇā |
vanam eṣyati sā ceha rāmacintānukarśitā ||

> **Meaning 40-46:** Seeing that golden Śiṁśapā tree fanned by the wind and
> echoing with the sound of hundreds of small bells, he was filled with
> amazement. Ascending that beautiful Śiṁśapā tree, which had blossoming
> tips, tender shoots, and was thickly covered with leaves, the highly swift
> Hanumān thought: "From here, I shall see the Vaidehī who longs for the
> sight of Rāma, as she wanders here and there at random, tormented by
> sorrow. This Aśoka grove of the wicked Ravana is exceedingly beautiful,
> adorned with Campaka, Candana, and Bakula trees. This lotus pond is
> delightful and frequented by flocks of birds; the queen of Rāma, Jānakī,
> will surely come here. That Jānakī, the queen of Rāma, who is adept at
> wandering in the forest, will surely come here. Or perhaps, that lady with
> eyes like a fawn's, who is familiar with this forest, will come here,
> emaciated by her thoughts of Rāma.
---

47.
rāmaśokābhisantaptā sā devī vāmalocanā |
vanavāsaratā nityam eṣyate vanacāriṇī ||

48.
vanecarāṇāṁ satataṁ nūnaṁ spr̥hayate purā |
rāmasya dayitā bhāryā janakasya sutā satī ||

49.
sandhyākālamanāḥ śyāmā dhruvam eṣyati jānakī |
nadīṁ cemāṁ śivajalāṁ sandhyārthe varavarṇinī ||

50.
tasyāś cāpy anurūpeyam aśokavanikā śubhā |
śubhā yā pārthivendrasya patnī rāmasya sammitā ||

51.
yadi jivati sā devī tārādhipanibhānanā |
āgamiṣyati sāvaśyam imāṁ śivajalāṁ nadīm ||

52.
evaṁ tu matvā hanumān mahātmā pratīkṣamāṇo manujendrapatnīm |
avekṣamāṇaś ca dadarśa sarvaṁ supuṣpite parṇaghane nilīnaḥ ||

> **Meaning 47-52:** "Tormented by the grief of separation from Rāma, that
> divine lady with beautiful eyes, who has always loved living in the
> forest, will come wandering in this wood. Surely, the devoted wife of Rāma
> and the daughter of Janaka has always cherished the company of
> forest-dwellers. With her mind set on the evening twilight hour, the
> beautiful-complexioned Jānakī will certainly come to this river of
> auspicious waters for her Sandhyā rites (bathing and meditation). This
> highly auspicious Aśoka grove is indeed fitting for her, who is the
> auspicious wife of the king of kings and the equal of Rāma. If that divine
> lady with a face like the moon is alive, she will inevitably come to this
> river of auspicious waters." Thinking thus, the great-souled Hanumān,
> waiting for the wife of the king of men, concealed himself within the
> dense, blossoming leaves and observed everything.
---
