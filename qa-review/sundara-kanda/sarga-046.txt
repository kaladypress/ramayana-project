=== Sarga 46 ===

---
1.
hatān mantrisutān buddhvā vānareṇa mahātmanā |
rāvaṇaḥ saṁvr̥tākāraḥ cakāra matim uttamām ||

> **Meaning 1:** When Rāvaṇa learned that the great-souled Vānara had killed his
> ministers' sons, he hid his feelings and made a firm decision.

---
2.
sa virūpākṣayūpākṣau durdharaṁ caiva rākṣasaṁ |
praghasaṁ bhāsakarṇaṁ ca pañcasenāgranāyakān ||

> **Meaning 2:** He called five commanders of his army: Virūpākṣa, Yūpākṣa, the
> Rākṣasa Durdhara, Praghasa, and Bhāsakarṇa.

---
3.
saṁdideśa daśagrīvaḥ vīrān nayaviśāradān |
hanūmadgrahaṇe vyagrān vāyuvegasamān yudhi ||

> **Meaning 3:** Daśagrīva gave orders to these heroes, skilled in strategy,
> eager to capture Hanumān, and as swift as the wind in battle.

---
4.
yāta senāgragāḥ sarve mahābalaparigrahāḥ |
savājirathamātaṅgāḥ sa kapiḥ śāsyatām iti ||

> **Meaning 4:** He said, "Go, all of you, army leaders with a great force
> behind you, with horses, chariots and elephants. Bring that Kapi under
> control."

---
5.
yat taiśca khalu bhāvyaṁ syāt tam āsādya vanālayam |
karma cāpi samādheyaṁ deśakālavirodhitam ||

> **Meaning 5:** He added, "When you reach him in his forest home, think
> carefully about what is needed. Whatever you do must also suit the place and
> the time."

---
6.
na hyahaṁ taṁ kapiṁ manye karmaṇā pratitarkayan |
sarvathā tan mahad bhūtaṁ mahābalaparigraham ||

7.
vānaraḥ ayam iti jñātvā na hi śudhyati me manaḥ |
naivāhaṁ taṁ kapiṁ manye yatheyaṁ prastutā kathā ||


8.
bhaved indreṇa vā sr̥ṣṭam asmadarthaṁ tapobalāt |
sa nāgayakṣagandharvā devāsuramaharṣayaḥ ||

> **Meaning 6-8:** Rāvaṇa said, "Judging by what he has done, I don't think he's
> an ordinary Kapi. He must be some great being with a great power behind him. I
> can't convince myself that he's just a Vānara. I truly don't believe he's a
> Kapi, given how this story has unfolded. Indra may have created him, using the
> power of his austerities, to work against us. Or it may be the Nāgas, Yakṣas,
> Gandharvas, devatās, Asuras and great sages ..."

---
9. 
yuṣmābhiḥ sahitaiḥ sarvaiḥ mayā saha vinirjitāḥ |
tairavaśyaṁ vidhātavyaṁ vyalīkaṁ kiṁ cid eva naḥ ||

> **Meaning 9:** "... all of whom you and I, together, have defeated. Surely they
> must have devised some act of hostility against us."

---

10.
yātā senāgragāḥ sarve mahābalaparigrahāḥ |
savājirathamātaṅgāḥ sa kapiśśāsyatāmiti ||

11.
tad eva nātra saṁdehaḥ prasahya parigr̥hyatām |
nāvamanyo bhavadbhiśca hariḥ krūraparākramaḥ ||

12.
dr̥ṣṭā hi harayaḥ śīghrā mayā vipulavikramāḥ |
vālī ca saha sugrīvaḥ jāmbavāṁśca mahābalaḥ ||

13.
nīlaḥ senāpatiścaiva ye cānye dvividādayaḥ |
naiva teṣāṁ gatirbhīmā na tejo na parākramaḥ ||

14.
na matirna balotsāhaḥ na rūpaparikalpanam |
mahat sattvam idaṁ jñeyaṁ kapirūpaṁ vyavasthitam ||

15.
prayatnaṁ mahad āsthāya kriyatām asya nigrahaḥ |
kāmaṁ lokāstrayaḥ sendrāḥ sasurāsuramānavāḥ ||

16.
bhavatām agrataḥ sthātuṁ na paryāptā raṇājire |
tathāpi tu nayajñena jayam ākāṅkṣatā raṇe |
ātmā rakṣyaḥ prayatnena yuddhasiddhirhi cañcalā ||

> **Meaning 9-16:** "all of them were defeated by me, together with all of you.
> Surely they must have done something to harm us. So go, all of you army
> leaders, with your mighty forces, horses, chariots and elephants, and bring
> that Kapi under control. There is no doubt about it. Seize him by force. Yet
> don't look down on him, for this Hari is fierce in his valor. I have seen
> swift Vānaras of great strength: Vālī, Sugrīva, the mighty Jāmbavān, the
> commander Nīla, Dvivida and others. None of them had speed like this, or such
> fierce energy or valor. Nor did they have this intelligence, strength or
> drive, or the ability to change their form. This must be understood as a being
> of great power, standing here in the form of a Kapi. Make a great effort and
> subdue him. The three worlds, with Indra, the devatās, Asuras and humans,
> cannot stand before you on the battlefield. Even so, a man who is wise in
> strategy and wants victory should protect himself with care, because success
> in battle is unstable."

---
17.
te svāmivacanaṁ sarve pratigr̥hya mahaujasaḥ |
samutpeturmahāvegā hutāśasamatejasaḥ ||

> **Meaning 17:** Taking their master's command, those mighty warriors leapt out
> with great speed, blazing like fire.

---
18.
rathaiśca mattairnāgaiśca vājibhiśca mahājavaiḥ |
śastraiśca vividhaistīkṣṇaiḥ sarvaiścopacitā balaiḥ ||

> **Meaning 18:** They set out with chariots, maddened elephants, swift horses,
> and many sharp weapons, supported by their full armies.

---
19.
tatastaṁ dadr̥śurvīrā dīpyamānaṁ mahākapim |
raśmimantam ivodyantaṁ svatejoraśmimālinam ||

> **Meaning 19:** Then the heroes saw the great Kapi, glowing like the rising
> sun, shining with the rays of his own radiance.

---
20.
toraṇasthaṁ mahāvegaṁ mahāsattvaṁ mahābalam |
mahāmatiṁ mahotsāhaṁ mahākāyaṁ mahābalam ||

> **Meaning 20:** He stood on the gateway, of great speed, great strength, great
> intelligence, great enthusiasm and a huge body.

---
21.
taṁ samīkṣyaiva te sarve dikṣu sarvāsv avasthitāḥ |
taistaiḥ praharaṇairbhīmaiḥ abhipetustatastataḥ ||

> **Meaning 21:** As soon as they saw him, they spread out in every direction
> and attacked him from all sides with their fearsome weapons.

---
22.
tasya pañcāyasāstīkṣṇāḥ sitāḥ pītamukhāḥ śarāḥ |
śirasty utpalapatrābhā durdhareṇa nipātitāḥ ||

> **Meaning 22:** Durdhara struck his head with five sharp iron arrows. They
> were pale, yellow-tipped, and looked like lotus petals.

---
23.
sa taiḥ pañcabhirāviddhaḥ śaraiḥ śirasi vānaraḥ |
utpapāta nadan vyomni diśo daśa vinādayan ||

> **Meaning 23:** Hit on the head by those five arrows, the Vānara leapt into
> the sky, roaring, making all ten directions echo.

---
24.
tatastu durdharo vīraḥ sarathaḥ sajjakārmukaḥ |
kirañ śaraśatairnaikaiḥ abhipede mahābalaḥ ||

> **Meaning 24:** Then the mighty hero Durdhara, in his chariot with his bow
> ready, charged at him, pouring out hundreds of arrows.

---
25.
sa kapirvārayām āsa taṁ vyomni śaravarṣiṇam |
vr̥ṣṭimantaṁ payodānte payodam iva mārutaḥ ||

> **Meaning 25:** The Kapi held off that rain of arrows in the sky, as the wind
> blocks a rain cloud at the end of the monsoon.

---
26.
ardyamānastatastena durdhareṇānilātmajaḥ |
cakāra ninadaṁ bhūyaḥ vyavardhata ca vegavān ||

> **Meaning 26:** Pressed hard by Durdhara, the son of the Wind roared again
> and, full of speed, grew even more powerful.

---
27.
sa dūraṁ sahasotpatya durdharasya rathe hariḥ |
nipapāta mahāvegaḥ vidyudrāśirgirāv iva ||

> **Meaning 27:** Leaping up suddenly from far away, the swift Hari dropped onto
> Durdhara's chariot like a mass of lightning falling on a mountain.

---
28.
tatastaṁ mathitāṣṭāśvaṁ rathaṁ bhagnākṣakūvaram |
vihāya nyapatad bhūmau durdharastyaktajīvitaḥ ||

> **Meaning 28:** The chariot's eight horses were crushed, and its axle and
> shaft were broken. Durdhara jumped down onto the ground, dead.

---
29.
taṁ virūpākṣayūpākṣau dr̥ṣṭvā nipatitaṁ bhuvi |
saṁjātaroṣau durdharṣāv utpetaturariṁdamau ||

> **Meaning 29:** Seeing him lying on the ground, the fierce enemy-destroyers
> Virūpākṣa and Yūpākṣa grew furious and leapt up.

---
30.
sa tābhyāṁ sahasotpatya viṣṭhito vimale ambare |
mudgarābhyāṁ mahābāhuḥ vakṣasy abhihataḥ kapiḥ ||

> **Meaning 30:** The long-armed Kapi hovered in the clear sky as the two
> suddenly rose and struck him on the chest with maces.

---
31.
tayorvegavatorvegaṁ vinihatya mahābalaḥ |
nipapāta punarbhūmau suparṇasamavikramaḥ ||

> **Meaning 31:** The mighty one, equal to Garuḍa in power, stopped the force of
> those two fast attackers and came down to the ground again.

---
32.
sa sālavr̥kṣam āsādya samutpāṭya ca vānaraḥ |
tāv ubhau rākṣasau vīrau jaghāna pavanātmajaḥ ||

> **Meaning 32:** Then the son of the Wind uprooted a sāla tree and killed those
> two Rākṣasa heroes with it.

---
33.
tatastāṁstrīn hatāñ jñātvā vānareṇa tarasvinā |
abhipede mahāvegaḥ prasahya praghaso harim ||

> **Meaning 33:** Seeing those three killed by the swift Vānara, Praghasa rushed
> in fiercely and attacked the Hari with great speed.

---
34.
bhāsakarṇaśca saṁkruddhaḥ śūlam ādāya vīryavān |
ekataḥ kapiśārdūlaṁ yaśasvinam avasthitau ||

> **Meaning 34:** The mighty Bhāsakarṇa, in a rage, took up a spear. The two
> stood together on one side, facing the glorious tiger among Kapis.

---
35.
paṭṭiśena śitāgreṇa praghasaḥ pratyapothayat |
bhāsakarṇaśca śūlena rākṣasaḥ kapisattamam ||

> **Meaning 35:** Praghasa struck the best of Kapis with a sharp-pointed
> paṭṭiśa, and the Rākṣasa Bhāsakarṇa struck him with his spear.

---
36.
sa tābhyāṁ vikṣatairgātraiḥ asr̥gdigdhatanūruhaḥ |
abhavad vānaraḥ kruddhaḥ bālasūryasamaprabhaḥ ||

> **Meaning 36:** His body was cut by those two weapons and the hairs on his
> body were smeared with blood. The Vānara grew furious and shone like the young
> sun.

---
37.
samutpāṭya gireḥ śr̥ṅgaṁ samr̥gavyālapādapam |
jaghāna hanumān vīraḥ rākṣasau kapikuñjaraḥ ||
giriśṛṅgasuniṣpiṣṭau tilaśastau babhūvatuḥ
> **Meaning 37:** Hanumān, the mighty elephant among Kapis, tore off a mountain
> peak along with its animals, snakes and trees, and killed the two Rākṣasas with it.
> They were ground to bits like sesame seeds under the peak.

---
38.
tatasteṣv avasanneṣu senāpatiṣu pañcasu |
balaṁ tad avaśeṣaṁ tu nāśayām āsa vānaraḥ ||

> **Meaning 38:** When the five commanders were gone, the Vānara destroyed the
> rest of the army.

---
39.
aśvairaśvān gajairnāgān yodhairyodhān rathai rathān |
sa kapirnāśayām āsa sahasrākṣa ivāsurān ||

> **Meaning 39:** He destroyed horses with horses, elephants with elephants,
> warriors with warriors and chariots with chariots, like Indra destroying the
> Asuras.

---
40.
hatairnāgaiśca turagaiḥ bhagnākṣaiśca mahārathaiḥ |
hataiśca rākṣasairbhūmī ruddhamārgā samantataḥ ||

> **Meaning 40:** With elephants, horses and great chariots with broken axles
> lying dead, and with slain Rākṣasas everywhere, the earth was covered and
> every road blocked.

---
41.
tataḥ kapistān dhvajinīpatīn raṇe nihatya vīrān sabalān savāhanān |
tad eva vīraḥ parigr̥hya toraṇaṁ kr̥takṣaṇaḥ kāla iva prajākṣaye ||

> **Meaning 41:** After killing those army commanders and their heroes in
> battle, along with their forces and vehicles, the hero Hanumān took hold of
> the gateway again, like Kāla waiting at the destruction of all beings.

---
