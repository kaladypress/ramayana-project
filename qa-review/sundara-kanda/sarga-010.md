=== Sarga 10 ===

---

1.
tatra divyōpamaṁ mukhyaṁ sphāṭikaṁ ratnabhūṣitam |
avēkṣamāṇō hanumān dadarśa śayanāsanam ||

2.
dāṁtakāṁcanacitrāṁgaiḥ vaiḍūryaiśca varāsanaiḥ |
mahārhāstaraṇōpētaiḥ upapannaṁ mahādhanaiḥ ||

3.
tasyacaikatamē dēśē sōgrya’mālā vibhūṣitam |
dadarśa pāṁḍuracchatraṁ tārādhipatisannibham ||

4.
jātarūpaparikṣiptaṁ citrabhānusamaprabham |
aśōkamālāvitataṁ dadarśa paramāsanam ||

5.
vālavyajanahastābhiḥ vījyamānaṁ samaṁtataḥ |
gaṁdhaiśca vividhairjuṣṭaṁ varadhūpēna dhūpitam ||

6.
paramāstaraṇāstīrṇaṁ āvikājinasaṁvr̥tam |
dāmabhirvaramālyānāṁ samaṁtādupaśōbhitam ||

> **Meaning 1-6:** Looking around, Hanumān saw a supreme, divine-looking bed
> made of crystal and adorned with gems. That excellent bed was decorated with
> ivory, gold, and Vaidūrya gems, and spread with highly valuable carpets. In
> one part of it, he saw a white umbrella, adorned with excellent garlands,
> resembling the lord of the stars (the moon). The supreme bed was plated with
> gold, shining like the sun, and draped with garlands of Aśoka flowers. It was
> being fanned from all sides by women holding fly-whisks. It was enriched with
> various fragrances and perfumed with excellent incense. Spread with the finest
> coverings and covered with sheep-skin, it was beautifully decorated all around
> with strings of excellent garlands.
---


7.
tasmin jīmūtasaṁkāśaṁ pradīptōttamakuṁḍalam |
lōhitākṣaṁ mahābāhuṁ mahārajatavāsasam ||

8.
lōhitēnānuliptāṁgaṁ caṁdanēna sugaṁdhinā |
saṁdhyāraktamivākāśē tōyadaṁ sataṭidgaṇam ||

9.
vr̥tamābharaṇairdivyaiḥ surūpaṁ kāmarūpiṇam |
savr̥kṣavanagulmāḍhyaṁ prasuptamiva maṁdaram ||

10.
krīḍitvōparataṁ rātrau varābharaṇabhūṣitam |
priyaṁ rākṣasakanyānāṁ rākṣasānāṁ sukhāvaham ||

11.
pītvāpyuparataṁ cāpi dadarśa sa mahākapiḥ |
bhāsvarē śayanē vīraṁ prasuptaṁ rākṣasādhipam ||

12.
niśśvasaṁtaṁ yathā nāgaṁ rāvaṇaṁ vānararṣabhaḥ |
āsādya paramōdvignaḥ sōpāsarpat subhītavat ||

13.
athārōhaṇamāsādya vēdikāṁtaramāśritaḥ |
suptaṁ rākṣasaśārdūlaṁ prēkṣatē sma mahākapiḥ ||

> **Meaning 7-13:** On that brilliantly shining bed, the Mahākapi saw the heroic
> Rāvaṇa sleeping after his nighttime amorous sports and drinking. He resembled
> a dark cloud with a cluster of lightning, possessing red eyes, long arms, and
> wearing garments woven with golden threads. With his limbs smeared with red,
> fragrant sandalwood paste, he shone like a dark cloud in the evening sky
> illuminated by twilight. Adorned with dazzling divine ornaments, handsome, and
> capable of assuming any form at will, he rested motionless, looking exactly as
> if Mount Mandara itself, filled with trees, forests, and bushes, was sleeping.
> Wearing priceless ornaments, the beloved of the Rākṣasa maidens and the bringer
> of joy to the Rākṣasas was in a deep sleep. Approaching Rāvaṇa, who was
> snoring heavily like an elephant, the foremost among the Vānaras became highly
> agitated. Thinking, "Fie! This wicked man abducted another man's wife. It is
> proper to stay away from him," Hanumān retreated as if greatly
> frightened. Then, ascending another raised platform via the stairs, the
> Mahākapi gazed at the sleeping tiger among Rākṣasas.
---


14.
śuśubhē rākṣasēṁdrasya svapata śśayanōttamam |
gaṁdhahastini saṁviṣṭē yathā prasravaṇaṁ mahat ||

15.
kāṁcanāṁgadasannaddhau ca dadarśa sa mahātmanaḥ |
vikṣiptau rākṣasēṁdrasya bhujāviṁdradhvajōpamau ||

16.
airāvataviṣāṇāgraiḥ āpīḍanakr̥tavraṇau |
vajrōllikhitapīnāṁsau viṣṇucakraparikṣatau ||

17.
pīnau samasujātāṁsau saṁgatau balasaṁyutau |
sulakṣaṇanakhāṁguṣṭau svaṁgulītalalakṣitau ||

18.
saṁhatau parighākārau vr̥ttau karikarōpamau |
vikṣiptau śayanē śubhrē paṁcaśīrṣāvivōragau ||

19.
śaśakṣatajakalpēna suśītēna sugaṁdhinā |
caṁdanēna parārdhyēna svanuliptau svalaṁkr̥tau ||

20.
uttamastrī vimr̥ditau gaṁdhōttamaniṣēvitau |
yakṣapannaga gaṁdharva dēvadānavarāviṇau ||

21.
dadarśa sa kapistasya bāhū śayanasaṁsthitau |
maṁdarasyāṁtarē suptau mahāhī ruṣitāviva ||

22.
tābhyāṁ sa paripūrṇābhyāṁ bhujābhyāṁ rākṣasēśvaraḥ |
śuśubhē’calasaṁkāśaḥ śr̥ṁgābhyāmiva maṁdaraḥ ||

> **Meaning 14-22:** The excellent bed of the sleeping lord of the Rākṣasas
> shone like a great mountain stream that a Gandhahastin (a majestic elephant
> in rut) had entered. (Just as the strong scent of a Gandhahastin frightens
> away all other male elephants from the stream, leaving only the female
> elephants behind, Rāvaṇa was surrounded solely by women, with no other man
> daring to be near). The Mahātman saw the two arms of the lord of the Rākṣasas,
> bound with golden armlets and thrown apart. They bore scars from the piercing
> strikes of the tusks of Airāvata, their fleshy shoulders were scratched by the
> Vajra, and they were marked by the discus of Viṣṇu. Those strong, well-proportioned
> arms were robust, firmly jointed, and possessed well-formed nails, thumbs,
> and palms. Compact, shaped like iron maces, round, and resembling the trunks
> of elephants, they lay thrown apart on the white bed
> like five-headed serpents. Anointed with excellent, cool, and fragrant
> sandalwood paste resembling the blood of a hare, and well-adorned, those arms
> had been massaged by excellent women and enjoyed the best of fragrances. They
> were capable of making Yakṣas, Pannagas, Gandharvas, Devas, and Dānavas cry
> out in pain. The Kapi saw his arms resting on the bed like two angry great
> serpents sleeping deep inside Mount Mandara. With those two massive arms, the
> lord of the Rākṣasas, who resembled a mountain, shone like Mount Mandara with
> its two peaks.
---


23.
cūtapunnāga surabhiḥ vakulōttamasaṁyutaḥ |
mr̥ṣṭānnarasa saṁyuktaḥ pānagaṁdhapurassaraḥ ||

24.
tasya rākṣasasiṁhasya niścakrāma mahāmukhāt |
śayānasya viniśśvāsaḥ pūrayanniva tadgr̥ham ||

25.
muktāmaṇivicitrēṇa kāṁcanēna virājitam |
makuṭēnāpavr̥ttēna kuṁḍalōjvalitānanam ||

26.
raktacaṁdanadigdhēna tathā hārēṇa śōbhinā |
pīnāyataviśālēna vakṣasābhivirājitam ||

27.
pāṁḍarēṇāpaviddhēna kṣaumēṇa kṣatajēkṣaṇam |
mahārhēṇa susaṁvītaṁ pītēnōttamavāsasā ||

28.
māṣarāśipratīkāśaṁ niśśvasaṁtaṁ bhujaṁgavat |
gāṁgē mahati tōyāṁtē prasuptamiva kuṁjaram ||

29.
caturbhiḥ kāṁcanairdīpaiḥ dīpyamānacaturdiśam |
prakāśīkr̥ta sarvāṁgaṁ mēghaṁ vidyudgaṇairiva ||

> **Meaning 23-29:** The breath of that lion among Rākṣasas, fragrant with mango
> and Punnāga flowers, combined with excellent Vakula flowers, mixed with the
> essence of delicious food, and preceded by the scent of wine, emerged from his
> face, as if filling that entire chamber. He was shining with his crown,
> which was beautifully set with pearls and gems and adorned with gold, though
> it had slipped to the side, and his face was illuminated by his earrings. He
> looked splendid with his broad, expansive, and massive chest, smeared with red
> sandalwood paste and adorned with a necklace. Dressed in a highly valuable
> yellow upper garment and wrapped in a white silk cloth that had slipped, he
> had blood-red eyes. Breathing heavily like a serpent and resembling a heap of
> black gram, laying on white bed, he looked like a great elephant sleeping in the
> vast waters of the Gaṅgā. Illuminated on all four sides by four golden lamps,
> with all his limbs made visible, he resembled a dark cloud accompanied by flashes
> of lightning.
---


30.
pādamūlagatāścāpi dadarśa sumahātmanaḥ
patnīssa priyabhāryasya tasya rakṣaḥpatērgr̥hē ||

31.
śaśiprakāśavadanāḥ cārukuṁḍalabhūṣitāḥ |
amlānamālyābharaṇā dadarśa hariyūthapaḥ ||

32.
nr̥ttavāditrakuśalāḥ rākṣasēṁdrabhujāṁkagāḥ |
varābharaṇadhāriṇyaḥ niṣaṇṇā dadr̥śē hariḥ ||

33.
vajravaiḍūrya garbhāṇi śravaṇāṁtēṣu yōṣitām |
dadarśa tāpanīyāni kuṁḍalānyaṁgadāni ca ||

34.
tāsāṁ caṁdrōpamairvaktraiḥ śubhairlalitakuṁḍalaiḥ |
virarāja vimānaṁ tat nabhastārāgaṇairiva ||

> **Meaning 30-34:** At the foot of the bed, the leader of the Vānaras also saw
> the wives of that great-souled lord of the Rākṣasas, who dearly loved his
> wives, inside his palace. He saw them wearing unfading garlands and ornaments,
> with faces as radiant as the moon, and adorned with beautiful earrings. The
> Hari saw those women, skilled in dance and instrumental music, resting in the
> embrace of the lord of the Rākṣasas' arms, wearing excellent ornaments. He saw
> the golden earrings and armlets, inlaid with diamonds and Vaidūrya gems, at
> the ends of the ears of those women. With their auspicious faces resembling
> the moon and their beautiful earrings, that Vimāna (bed in the shape of Vimāna) shone like the sky filled
> with clusters of stars.
---


35.
madavyāyāmakhinnāstāḥ rākṣasēṁdrasya yōṣitaḥ |
tēṣu tēṣvavakāśēṣu prasuptā stanumadhyamāḥ ||

36.
aṁgahāraistathaivānyā kōmalairnr̥ttaśālinī |
vinyastaśubhasarvāṁgī prasuptā varavarṇinī ||

37.
kācidvīṇāṁ pariṣvajya prasuptā saṁprakāśatē |
mahānadīprakīrṇēva nalinī pōtamāśritā ||

38.
anyā kakṣagatēnaiva maḍḍukēnāsitēkṣaṇā |
prasuptā bhāminī bhāti bālaputrēva vatsalā ||

39.
paṭahaṁ cārusarvāṁgī pīḍya śētē śubhastanī |
cirasya ramaṇaṁ labdhvā pariṣvajyēva bhāminī ||

40.
kācidvaṁśaṁ pariṣvajya suptā kamalalōcanā |
rahaḥ priyatamaṁ gr̥hya sakāmēva ca kāminī ||

41.
vipaṁcīṁ parigr̥hyānyā niyatā nr̥ttaśālinī |
nidrāvaśamanuprāptā sahakāṁtēva bhāminī ||

42.
anyā kanakasaṁkāśaiḥ mr̥dupīnairmanōramaiḥ
mr̥daṁgaṁ paripīḍyāṁgaiḥ prasuptā mattalōcanā ||

43.
bhujapārśvāṁtarasthēna kakṣagēṇa kr̥śōdarī |
paṇavēva sahāniṁdyā suptā madakr̥tāṁśramā ||

44.
ḍiṁḍimaṁ parigr̥hyānyā tathaivāsakta ḍiṁḍimā |
prasuptā taruṇaṁ vatsaṁ upagūhyēva bhāminī ||

45.
kācidāḍaṁbaraṁ nārī bhujasaṁyōgapīḍitam |
kr̥tvā kamalapatrākṣī prasuptā madamōhitā ||

46.
kalaśīmapavidhyānyā prasuptā bhāti bhāminī |
vasaṁtē puṣpaśabalā mālēva parimārjitā ||

47.
pāṇibhyāṁ ca kucau kācit suvarṇakalaśōpamau |
upagūhyābalā suptā nidrābalaparājitā ||

48.
anyā kamalapatrākṣī pūrṇēṁdusadr̥śānanā |
anyāmāliṁgya suśrōṇīṁ prasuptā madavihvalā ||

49.
ātōdyāni vicitrāṇi pariṣvajya varastriyaḥ |
nipīḍya ca kucaissuptāḥ kāminyaḥ kāmukāniva ||

> **Meaning 35-49:** Exhausted from intoxication and amorous exertions, those
> slender-waisted wives of the lord of the Rākṣasas slept in various places. One
> beautiful woman, skilled in dance, slept with her delicate limbs positioned in
> a dance posture, her auspicious body resting gracefully. Another slept
> embracing a Vīṇā, shining like a lotus plant caught in a great river and
> clinging to a boat. Another dark-eyed beautiful woman slept with a Maḍḍuka
> drum in her armpit, looking like a loving mother holding her infant son.
> Another woman with beautiful limbs and auspicious breasts slept tightly
> embracing a Paṭaha drum, like a woman who has found her lover after a long
> time. Another lotus-eyed woman slept embracing a flute, like a passionate
> woman secretly holding her dearest lover. Another beautiful woman, skilled in
> dance, completely under the sway of sleep, slept holding a Vipañcī (Veena with
> seven strings), like a woman with her lover. Another woman with intoxicating
> eyes slept tightly embracing a Mṛdaṅga drum with her soft, plump, and attractive
> golden limbs. A slender-waisted woman slept out of exhaustion from intoxication,
> holding a Paṇava drum tightly between her arms and sides, like a flawless companion.
> Another beautiful woman slept embracing a Ḍiṇḍima drum, completely attached to
> it, like a woman holding her young child. A lotus-eyed woman, intoxicated by
> wine, slept tightly pressing an Āḍambara drum between her arms. Another
> beautiful woman slept having knocked over a Kalaśī (water pot), shining like a
> colorful flower garland swept away in the spring. Another helpless woman,
> overcome by the power of sleep, slept tightly holding her two breasts, which
> resembled golden pots, with her hands. Another lotus-eyed woman with a face
> like the full moon, exhausted from intoxication, slept embracing another woman
> with beautiful hips. Those excellent women, behaving like passionate lovers,
> slept tightly embracing and pressing against the various musical instruments.
---


50.
tāsāmēkāṁtavinyastē śayānāṁ śayanē śubhē |
dadarśa rūpasaṁpannāṁ aparāṁ sa kapiḥ striyam ||

51.
muktāmaṇisamāyuktaiḥ bhūṣaṇai ssuvibhūṣitām |
vibhūṣayaṁtīmiva tat svaśriyā bhavanōttamam ||

52.
gaurīṁ kanakavarṇāṁgīṁ iṣṭāmaṁtaḥ purēśvarīm |
kapirmaṁḍōdarīṁ tatra śayānāṁ cārurūpiṇīm ||

sa tāṁ dr̥ṣṭvā mahābāhuḥ bhūṣitāṁ mārutātmajaḥ |
53.
tarkayāmāsa sītēti rūpayauvanasaṁpadā |
harṣēṇa mahatā yuktō nanaṁda hariyūthapaḥ ||

āsphōṭayāmāsa cucuṁba pucchaṁ
nanaṁda cikrīḍa jagau jagāma |
54.
staṁbhānarōhannipapāta bhūmau
nidarśayan svāṁ prakr̥tiṁ kapīnām ||

> **Meaning 50-54:** Resting in a secluded part of that auspicious bed, the Kapi
> saw another woman endowed with immense beauty. She was beautifully adorned
> with ornaments set with pearls and gems, seemingly illuminating that excellent
> palace with her own radiance. The Kapi saw the beautiful-looking Mandodarī,
> with a fair golden complexion, the beloved queen of the inner apartments,
> sleeping there. Seeing that highly adorned woman, the great-armed son of Vāyu
> reasoned, based on her wealth of beauty and youth, that she must be Sītā.
> Filled with immense joy, the leader of the Vānaras rejoiced. Showing his
> natural monkey behavior, he slapped his arms, kissed his tail, rejoiced,
> played around, sang, paced about, climbed the pillars, and jumped down to the
> ground.
