1: Hearing Viśvāmitra's words, the great king went numb for a moment; when he came to his senses, he spoke these words.
2: My lotus-eyed Rāma is not yet sixteen years old. I do not see him as fit to fight against the Rākṣasas.
3: I am the master and protector of this full akṣauhiṇī army. Surrounded by it, I myself will go and fight those night-wanderers.
4: These brave and mighty warriors of mine are skilled in weapons. They are fit to fight the hordes of Rākṣasas. You should not take Rāma.
5: I myself will go with bow in hand and guard the front line of battle. As long as I have breath in me, I will keep fighting those night-wanderers.
6: Your sacred observance will be well protected and free of obstacles. I will go there myself. You should not take Rāma.
7: He is only a boy, he hasn't completed his training, and he doesn't know his own strength or his enemy's. He has no real power in weapons and no skill in warfare.
8: He is no match for the Rākṣasas, for they always fight with deceit. Separated from Rāma, I wouldn't have the will to live even for a moment, great sage. You should not take Rāma.
9: But if you really want to take Rāghava, sage of firm vows, then take him together with me and the four divisions of my army.
10: Sixty thousand years have passed since I was born, Kauśika, and I had this son only after great hardship. You should not take Rāma.
11: Among my four sons, my greatest affection is for him — the eldest, who is foremost in dharma. You should not take Rāma.
12: How strong are these Rākṣasas? Whose sons are they, and who are they? How huge are they, great sage, and who protects them?
13: And how can Rāma, or my forces, or I myself counter these Rākṣasas who fight by trickery, Brahmin?
14: Tell me everything, revered sage. How am I to stand against them in battle? These Rākṣasas are wicked and swollen with pride in their strength.
15: Hearing his words, Viśvāmitra replied.
16: There is a Rākṣasa named Rāvaṇa, born in the line of Pulastya. He received a boon from Brahmā and now torments the three worlds terribly.
17: Immensely strong and valiant, and surrounded by countless Rākṣasas, Rāvaṇa is famed as the mighty king of the Rākṣasas.
18: He is the own brother of Vaiśravaṇa and the son of the sage Viśravas. Even though that mighty Rākṣasa does not personally come to obstruct my yajña,
19: instigated by him, two very powerful Rākṣasas — Mārīca and Subāhu — are the ones who will disrupt the yajña.
20: Hearing this from the sage, the king replied: I am not able to stand against that wicked one in battle.
21: Knower of dharma, please show mercy to my little son. For an unfortunate man like me, you are both guru and devatā.
22: Even the devatās, Dānavas, Gandharvas, Yakṣas, birds, and Nāgas cannot withstand Rāvaṇa. What can humans do against him in battle?
23: In battle, that Rākṣasa strips even the mighty of their strength. So I cannot fight him or his forces, best of sages, whether with my army or with my sons beside me.
24: Brahmin, under no circumstances can I part with my young son — he is just a boy, radiant like the immortals, and knows nothing of warfare.
25: And those two sons of Sunda and Upasunda, who are like Death itself in battle, are the ones who obstruct your yajña. I cannot possibly send my dear son to face them.
26: Mārīca and Subāhu are brave, powerful, and well trained. Together with my friends and allies, I will fight either one of them myself.
27: At this anxious pleading of the king, a great anger entered the son of Kuśika, the best of Brahmins. The great sage flared up like a sacrificial fire when ghee is poured into it.
