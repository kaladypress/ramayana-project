1: Hearing those words of the fierce Rākṣasa, Sītā, distressed and speaking in a faint, trembling voice, slowly replied:
2: Tormented by grief, weeping and trembling, that devoted, ascetic lady of graceful bearing thought only of her husband.
3: Placing a blade of grass between them, the pure-smiling lady spoke: "Turn your mind away from me, and turn it instead to your own wives!
4: You have no right to covet me, any more than a sinner has a right to attain spiritual perfection; as a woman devoted to a single husband, I will never commit such a vile and contemptible act!
5: Born into an illustrious lineage and wedded into a sacred family, I could never do this." Having spoken thus to Rāvaṇa, renowned Vaidehī,
6: turned her back upon the Rākṣasa and spoke again: "Being a chaste woman and the wife of another, I can never be a fit wife for you.
7: Reflect carefully on dharma, and strictly observe the vows of the virtuous! Night-ranger, just as your wives must be protected, so too must the wives of others; make yourself the measure of others, and take pleasure only in your own wives.
8: A man discontent with his own wives, fickle, and enslaved by his senses—such a depraved fool is dragged to ruin by coveting the wives of others.
9: Are there no righteous men here in Laṅkā, or do you simply refuse to follow them? For your intellect is completely perverse and devoid of all moral conduct.
10: Guided by delusion, you pay no heed to the wise counsel spoken for your own good, hastening the destruction of the Rākṣasas.
11: When ruled by an undisciplined king who delights in lawlessness, prosperous nations and cities crumble to ruin.
12: So too this Laṅkā, packed with hoards of jewels, having you for its master, will soon be annihilated because of your crime alone.
13: When a short-sighted and wicked person is struck down by the consequence of his own misdeeds, Rāvaṇa, all living creatures rejoice at his destruction!
14: The very people you have oppressed will look upon your corpse and exclaim with joy: 'By good fortune, this tyrant has met his ruin!'
15: I cannot be seduced by power or tempted by wealth; I am inseparable from Rāghava, as light is from the sun.
16: Having rested my head upon the honored arm of the Sovereign of the World, how could I ever rest upon the arm of any other?
17: I am the rightful wife of that master of the earth alone, just as sacred knowledge belongs exclusively to an enlightened, vow-observing brāhmaṇa.
18: Do the right thing, Rāvaṇa: reunite sorrow-stricken me with Rāma, just as a wild female elephant in the forest is reunited with the leader of the herd.
19: If you wish to preserve your position and avoid a terrifying death, that foremost among men, Rāma, is the only one you should seek to befriend.
20: For he is renowned as a knower of righteousness and compassionate to those who seek refuge; forge friendship with him, if you have any desire to live!
21: Seek his pardon, for he protects those who surrender; with a humble and pure heart, you must hand me back to him.
22: Only by returning me to the foremost of the Raghus will you find safety; if you act otherwise, Rāvaṇa, you will meet certain death.
23: A hurled thunderbolt might miss you, and Antaka, the god of death, might spare you for a time; but once enraged, that protector of the world, Rāghava, will never spare a creature like you!
24: Soon you will hear the deafening roar of Rāma's bowstring, booming like the crash of a thunderbolt loosed by Indra.
25: Smooth-jointed arrows inscribed with the names of Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, hissing with fiery heads like venomous serpents, will rain down upon this place without delay.
26: Striking down the Rākṣasas on every side throughout this city, those falling shafts feathered with heron plumes will leave not a single foot of ground untouched.
27: That great Garuḍa who is Rāma will swoop down in his fury and tear apart the giant serpents who are the Rākṣasa lords, just as Vainateya rips up real venomous snakes!
28: My husband, the conqueror of foes, will quickly rescue me from you, just as Viṣṇu reclaimed the radiant Goddess Śrī from the Asuras with three strides.
29: When Janasthāna was laid waste and the army of Rākṣasas wiped out, you, cowardly Rākṣasa, committed this despicable deed because you were powerless to face them in the open!
30: Vile wretch! Sneaking into the empty hermitage when those two lion-like brothers were out of sight, you stole me away!
31: Catching merely the scent of Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, you would never have dared stand before them—no more than a mongrel dog can stand before two tigers!
32: In combat against them, your hold on the yoke of battle would be broken instantly, just as the single-armed demon Vr̥tra was crushed by the two arms of Indra.
33: My master Rāma, alongside Saumitri, will swiftly drain your life-breaths with his arrows, as the blazing sun evaporates a shallow pool of water.
34: Whether you flee to the mountain of Kubera, hide in his palace, or take refuge in the assembly hall of King Varuṇa, you will never escape the son of Daśaratha, any more than a doomed tree can escape a thunderbolt!"
