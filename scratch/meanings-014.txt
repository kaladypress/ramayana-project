1: Having reflected for a moment and determined her presence in his mind, the radiant hero leapt down onto the boundary wall of that residence.
2: Standing on the rampart, his body thrilling with joy, the great Kapi saw diverse trees in full blossom at the arrival of spring.
3: He saw blooming śāla, aśoka, bhavya, campaka, uddālaka, nāgavr̥kṣa, mango, and kapimukha trees.
4: Then, like an iron arrow loosed from a bowstring, he leapt into the tree garden, which was shaded by mango groves and wrapped in hundreds of flowering creepers.
5: Entering that wondrous orchard, resonant with the songs of birds and filled on all sides with trees crafted of silver and gold,
6: the Kapi Hanumān saw that marvelous forest teeming with birds and deer, glowing like the rising sun.
7: It was crowded with diverse trees laden with flowers and fruits, constantly frequented by cuckoos and joyful bhṛṅgarāja birds.
8: In that season that gladdens human hearts, the garden teemed with birds and beasts, echoing with the calls of peacocks in rut and filled with flocks of various birds.
9: As he searched for the blameless princess of graceful form, the Vānara woke the birds that were sleeping peacefully.
10: As flocks of birds took flight, their wings brushed against the śāla trees, which released a shower of multicolored blossoms.
11: Showered with blossoms in the middle of the Aśoka grove, Hanumān, the son of Māruta, shone like a mountain of flowers.
12: Seeing the Kapi darting in all directions through the groves of trees, all creatures took him to be the spirit of Spring himself.
13: Carpeted with varied blossoms fallen from the trees, the earth there shone like an adorned woman.
14: Shaken violently by the swift Kapi, those trees shed their multicolored flowers in abundance.
15: With their treetops stripped of leaves, their flowers and fruits shaken down, the trees looked like defeated gamblers stripped of their clothes and ornaments.
16: Shaken by the swift Hanumān, those noble flowering trees quickly shed their blossoms, leaves, and fruits.
17: Abandoned by flocks of birds and reduced to bare trunks, all those trees stood still as if shattered by a hurricane.
18: Like a young woman with disheveled hair, her cosmetic paste rubbed off, her lips bitten and kissed, marked by scratches and teeth,
19: so the Aśoka grove, with its choice trees broken and battered by Hanumān's tail, hands, and feet, lay disheveled and ravaged.
20: The Kapi tore through garlands of massive creepers with speed, as the monsoon wind scatters banks of clouds over the Vindhya mountains.
21: Roaming there, the Kapi saw enchanting grounds paved with gems, silver, and gold.
22: He saw ponds of various shapes filled with pristine water, fitted here and there with valuable jeweled stairways.
23: Their sands were made of pearls and coral, their bed-floors inlaid with crystal, and they were adorned on their banks with marvelous golden trees.
24: They were filled with beds of blooming lotuses and water lilies, echoing with the calls of cakravākas, resounding with the cries of water-fowls, and musical with the sounds of swans and sārasa cranes.
25: They were fed on all sides by long tree-lined rivulets carrying nectar-like, wholesome water.
26: They were draped with hundreds of creepers, shaded by santānaka trees, covered with various thickets, and bordered by karavīra bushes.
27: Then the tiger among Kapis saw a charming pleasure mountain resembling a rain cloud, with towering summits and flanked on all sides by smaller peaks,
28: fitted with stone pavilions and surrounded by diverse trees — an enchanting hill standing in that garden.
29: From that hill, the Kapi saw a river tumbling down like an affectionate woman slipping from her lover's lap.
30: With the branches of trees on its banks dipping into the water, it looked like an angry woman held back by loving kinsfolk.
31: Then the great Kapi saw the river's water winding back in a curve, resembling a mollified lady returning to her beloved.
32: Not far from there, the tiger among Kapis, Hanumān, son of Māruta, saw lotus ponds teeming with diverse flocks of water birds.
33: He also saw an artificial pleasure lake filled with cool water, boasting staircases of precious gems and sands of pearls.
34: It was embellished with various herds of deer, diverse colorful woods, and great pavilions built by Viśvakarmā.
35: Adorned on all sides with artificial groves, whatever trees stood there bearing flowers and fruits
36: were fitted with canopies, viewing platforms, and golden daises.
37: The great Kapi saw a single golden śiṁśapā tree, enveloped in dense spreading creepers and leaves, surrounded on all sides by platforms of gold.
38: He observed the grounds, hollows, and watercourses, and saw other golden trees blazing like fire.
39: Bathed in the radiant glow of those trees, the heroic Vānara, the great Kapi, felt as if he too had turned to gold, like Mount Meru.
40: Seeing that golden tree fanned by the breeze, tinkling with the chiming of hundreds of tiny bells, he was struck with wonder.
41: Climbing into that beautiful śiṁśapā tree, laden with blossoms, tender sprouts, and dense foliage, the swift Hanumān thought:
42: "From here I will see Vaidehī, who longs to see Rāma, as she wanders here and there in her grief.
43: This Aśoka grove of the wicked Rāvaṇa is exceedingly enchanting, adorned with campaka, sandalwood, and bakula trees.
44: And this delightful lotus pond is frequented by flocks of birds; Jānakī, the queen of Rāma, will surely come here.
45: Ever dear to Rāghava, adept at walking in forest paths, that queen of Rāma will certainly visit this place.
46: Or that fawn-eyed lady, knowing the ways of this wood, will come here to wander, worn thin by thoughts of Rāma.
47: Tormented by grief for Rāma, that beautiful-eyed devī, who has always loved living in the wild, will come walking in this grove.
48: For in the past, Rāma's beloved wife, the virtuous daughter of Janaka, always cherished the gentle forest creatures.
49: Mindful of the evening twilight hour, that fair lady Jānakī will surely come to this auspicious stream to perform her sandhyā rites.
50: This lovely Aśoka grove is truly worthy of her — the radiant wife of the king of kings and the worthy counterpart of Rāma.
51: If that moon-faced devī is alive, she will inevitably come to this river of pure, auspicious waters."
52: Thinking thus and waiting for the wife of the king of men, the great-souled Hanumān concealed himself within the dense foliage of that blossoming tree and kept watch over everything.
