1: When a full year had passed and the sacred horse had returned, the king's yajña commenced on the northern bank of the Sarayū.
2: With Ṛśyaśṛṅga as the presiding priest, the foremost Brāhmaṇas carried out the rites of the Aśvamedha, the great yajña of this noble king.
3: The priests, masters of the Vedas, performed the rites as ordained in the scriptures, proceeding through each stage in proper order and with due propriety, exactly as the śāstras direct.
4: Following the scriptures, the Brāhmaṇas performed the Pravargya and the Upasad rites. They also carried out, exactly as prescribed, all the additional rites the śāstras ordain.
5: Then, after worshipping the presiding devatās of the rites, all those eminent sages, with great delight, performed the rites as prescribed, beginning with the morning offerings (Prātaḥsavana).
6: The offering to Indra was made as prescribed, and the pure Soma was extracted. Then the midday rites (Mādhyandina Savana) proceeded in their proper sequence.
7: In the same way, those foremost Brāhmaṇas, consulting the śāstras, performed the third rites (Tṛtīya Savana) for this noble king.
8: In the proceedings of the yajña, no oblation was left unoffered, nor was any offering incorrectly substituted. There was not a single flaw in the procedure. Every ritual was executed strictly according to the mantras and completed without hindrance.
9: Throughout the days of the yajña, no one was seen tired or hungry. Not a single Brāhmaṇa was unlearned, nor did any Brāhmaṇa have fewer than a hundred students and followers.
10: Brāhmaṇas feasted every day, as did people of all varṇas; ascetics dined there, and so too did sannyāsīs (renunciates).
11: The elderly, the sick, women, and children ate day and night, yet Daśaratha never felt satisfied that he had fed them enough.
12: "Serve food! Serve food! And distribute clothes of every kind!" Urged on by the ministers, the attendants did so again and again.
13: Day after day, many heaps of cooked rice, prepared according to the prescribed ritual rules, could be seen there, rising like mountains.
14: Men and crowds of women who had come from many lands to witness that noble king's yajña were well provided with food and drink.
15: The food, prepared according to ritual rules, was truly delicious. Daśaratha heard the foremost Brāhmaṇas say, "Ah, we are fully satisfied! May all be well with you!"
16: Finely adorned men served food to the Brāhmaṇas, while others wearing gleaming jeweled earrings attended to their needs.
17: In the intervals between rites, wise and eloquent Brāhmaṇas engaged in many reasoned debates, each trying to outdo the other.
18: Every day, the skilled Brāhmaṇas performed all the rites there, exactly as directed by the scriptures.
19: Among the king's assembly priests (Sadasyas), there was no Brāhmaṇa who did not know the six Vedāṅgas, who had not kept his vows, who was not widely learned, or who was not skilled in debate.
20: When the time came to raise the yūpas (sacred posts) for that yajña, six posts of bilva wood, six of khadira, and another six of palāśa beside the bilva posts, were all set in place.
21: Two more posts were set up there, one of śleṣmātaka wood and one of devadāru; and the interval between each post was the span of two outstretched arms.
22: All these posts were made by men who knew the śāstras and were skilled in the rites of yajña. To add to the splendor of the yajña, they were adorned with gold.
23: There were twenty-one posts in all, each twenty-one cubits (aratnis) high, and every one of them was decorated with cloth.
24: Skilled craftsmen crafted them strong and durable, setting them in place as prescribed. Every post was eight-sided and smooth, perfectly straight without any curve, knot, or hollow.
25: Draped in cloth and adorned with flowers and fragrances, they shone like the luminous stars of the Saptarṣis in the sky.
26: Bricks were made properly and to exact measure. Brāhmaṇas skilled in the geometry of altar-building (śulba) built up the fire altar there.
27: For Daśaratha, that lion among kings, skilled Brāhmaṇas built the fire altar in the shape of Garuḍa with golden wings—three times the standard size, constructed in eighteen courses of bricks.
28: Animals were tied to the posts there, each dedicated to its own devatā, along with serpents and birds, as the scriptures direct.
29: At the place of immolation (Śāmitra), the priests tied the horse and aquatic creatures such as tortoises to the posts, strictly according to the scriptures.
30: Three hundred animals were tied to the posts there, together with King Daśaratha's returned horse—the foremost jewel among horses.
31: Kausalyā worshipped that horse, circumambulating it on all sides, and with supreme joy, made the ceremonial marks with the three ritual blades.
32: Desiring to fulfill sacred dharma, Kausalyā, the chief queen, stayed one night with that sacrificed horse, with a calm and composed mind.
33: The Hotā, Adhvaryu, and Udgātā then brought Kausalyā, Sumitrā, and Kaikeyī into ritual contact with the horse.
34: Then the priest, perfect in his learning and with his senses under control, drew out the omentum (vapā) of that horse and cooked it as the scriptures prescribe.
35: At the appointed time and strictly according to ritual ordinances, King Daśaratha inhaled the fragrant smoke of the omentum, dispelling his sins.
36: All sixteen officiating priests together offered all the remaining limbs of the horse into the sacred fire, strictly adhering to scriptural procedure.
37: In other yajñas the oblation is placed on branches of the plakṣa tree. In the Aśvamedha alone, the portion is offered on cane (vetasa).
38: The Brāhmaṇas, following the Kalpasūtra, reckon the Aśvamedha as a three-day rite. Its first day was arranged as the Catuṣṭoma.
39: The second day is counted as the Ukthya, and the one after it as the Atirātra. Many other rites were also performed there, as the śāstras direct:
40: the Jyotiṣṭoma and the Āyus, the Atirātra, the Abhijit, the Viśvajit, and the Aptoryāma—six yajñas in all.
41: King Daśaratha, who brought prosperity to his lineage, gave the eastern lands to the Hotā, the western to the Adhvaryu, and the southern lands to the Brahmā.
42: And likewise the northern lands to the Udgātā—this was the dakṣiṇā ordained long ago by Brahmā for the great Aśvamedha yajña.
43: Having completed the yajña strictly according to sacred ordinances, King Daśaratha, that foremost of men who made the sacrifice flourish, gave the entire earth to the officiating priests.
44: But the priests replied to the king, who was now cleansed of all sin, "You alone are worthy of ruling and protecting this entire earth!
45: We have no need for land, O King, nor can we govern it, for we are ever devoted to Vedic study.
46: Please grant us some compensation in exchange instead—whether gems, gold, cows, or whatever wealth you wish to offer. Give us that, foremost of men, for we have no need for the earth."
47: When the Brāhmaṇas who had mastered the Vedas spoke thus to the king,
48: the king gave them one million cows, a hundred million gold coins, and four times that amount in silver.
49: Then all the priests together handed over that wealth to the sage Ṛśyaśṛṅga and to the wise Vasiṣṭha.
50: Those foremost sages then distributed that wealth equitably among all the priests. Deeply pleased and overjoyed, they all expressed their gratitude and blessings to the king.
51: Then, with a focused mind, the king gave ten million pieces of Jāmbūnada gold to the Brāhmaṇas who had come to witness the yajña (prasarpakas).
52: Then, after all the treasury wealth had been given away, a poor Brāhmaṇa came seeking alms; Daśaratha, the joy of the Raghu dynasty, gave him the precious ornament from his own hand.
53: When the Brāhmaṇas had been honored according to sacred rites and were well pleased, King Daśaratha, ever devoted to them, bowed in deep reverence before them, his whole being thrilled with joy.
54: The Brāhmaṇas then pronounced many kinds of blessings on that generous hero among men as he lay prostrate on the ground.
55: Then the king was glad at heart, having completed that unsurpassed yajña, which destroys sin and leads to heaven, and which even the greatest kings find hard to achieve.
56: King Daśaratha said to Ṛśyaśṛṅga, "O sage of noble vows, please now perform the rite that will expand my lineage."
57: "So be it," replied that foremost of Brāhmaṇas to the king. "Mahārāja, four sons will be born to you, who will carry your lineage forward."
58: Hearing those sweet words, King Daśaratha bowed to him in deep reverence. Filled with supreme joy, the noble monarch addressed Ṛśyaśṛṅga once more.
