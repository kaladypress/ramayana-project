1: "Long ago, while Śiva was absorbed in tapas, the devatās and the hosts of ṛṣis, seeking a commander for their armies, approached Brahmā.
2: "Bowing low before the revered Pitāmaha, all the suras—with Indra and Agni at their head—spoke these earnest words:
3: "'Revered god, the commander you once granted us has taken up supreme tapas and is now practicing austerity together with Umā.
4: "'Please ordain whatever must be done next for the good of the worlds, knower of cosmic order—you alone are our supreme refuge.'
5: "Hearing the devatās' plea, Brahmā, grandsire of all worlds, comforted them with gentle words and replied:
6: "'What the mountain's daughter declared—that you will not have children with your wives—her unimpeded word will surely come true, without a doubt.
7: "'Yet here is Gaṅgā, who courses through the heavens. In her, Agni will bring forth a son who will be the commander of the devatās and vanquisher of their foes.
8: "'The eldest daughter of the mountain king will cherish that son, and Umā too will gladly honor him—of this there is no doubt.'
9: "Hearing his words, Raghunandana, all the suras felt their hopes fulfilled, bowed low, and paid homage to Brahmā.
10: "All the devatās then went to Mount Kailāsa, rich with minerals, Rāma, and entrusted Agni with the task of bringing forth a son:
11: "'Accomplish this mission for the devatās, god Agni—pour your mighty tejas into Gaṅgā, the mountain's daughter.'
12: "Pledging to do so for the devatās, Agni went to Gaṅgā and said, 'Receive this sacred seed, goddess—this is the wish of the devatās.'
13: "Hearing his words, she took on a divine form; and seeing her majesty, Agni poured his tejas forth on every side.
14: "Agni bathed the goddess on every side, Raghunandana, until all the currents of Gaṅgā were completely filled with his fire.
15: "Scorched by that fire and overwhelmed in spirit, Gaṅgā cried out to Agni, priest of all the devatās, 'I cannot bear your surging tejas, god!'
16: "Then Agni, the sacred fire of all the devatās, told Gaṅgā, 'Place this embryo here at the foothills of Himavat.'
17: "Hearing Agni's words, the radiant Gaṅgā released that dazzlingly bright embryo from her currents, blameless prince.
18: "What poured forth from her shone with the brilliance of molten jāmbūnada gold—
19: "reaching the earth, it turned into pure, auspicious gold and silver, while from its fierce intensity came copper and iron.
20: "Its dross became tin and lead, and upon touching the earth, all of it multiplied into every kind of metal and mineral.
21: "The instant the embryo was laid down, the entire forest along the mountainside was bathed in its glow and turned completely to gold.
22: "From that day on, Rāghava, gold—radiant as fire, tiger among men—has been known as jātarūpa, and every blade of grass, tree, creeper, and bush there turned to gold.
23: "Once the boy was born, Indra and the hosts of Maruts appointed the Kṛttikās to nurse him with their milk.
24: "Making a solemn pact among themselves, they nursed the newborn, declaring with one mind, 'He is the son of us all.'
25: "Then all the devatās named him Kārtikeya and declared, 'This son will be famous across all three worlds, without a doubt.'
26: "Hearing those words, they bathed the child—who had poured forth from the stream of the womb—shining with supreme beauty like a blazing fire.
27: "Because he had poured forth from the stream of the womb, the devatās also called that illustrious, fire-like Kārtikeya by the name Skanda, Kākutstha.
28: "Matchless milk then welled up in the six Kṛttikās, and taking six faces, he drank from all six of them at once.
29: "After drinking that milk, in a single day the mighty youth—still tender in form—defeated the armies of the Daityas by his own prowess.
30: "Then the hosts of suras, led by Agni, gathered together and consecrated that spotlessly radiant hero as commander of the celestial armies.
31: "This, Rāma, is the full story of Gaṅgā and the birth of Kumāra that I have told you—an account that is both blessed and purifying.
32: "Any person on earth who is devoted to Kārtikeya, Kākutstha, will enjoy a long life with sons and grandsons, and in the end attain the realm of Skanda."
