1: Hearing those words of the wise Viśvāmitra, the deeply radiant great ascetic Śatānanda felt his hair stand on end with joy.
2: Gautama's eldest son, his splendor kindled by tapas, was filled with the highest wonder simply at the sight of Rāma.
3: Looking upon the two princes seated comfortably at ease, Śatānanda spoke to Viśvāmitra, the foremost of munis:
4: "Foremost of munis, did you show my illustrious mother—who had practiced tapas for so long—to the prince?
5: "Deeply radiant sage, did my illustrious mother offer hospitality with forest produce to Rāma, who is worthy of honor from all living beings?
6: "Deeply radiant sage, did you tell Rāma what happened long ago—the wrong committed against my mother by the devatā?
7: "Kauśika, foremost of munis—may all be well with you—was my mother reunited with my father after beholding Rāma?
8: "Son of Kuśika, did my father honor Rāma, and has this deeply radiant prince come here after receiving the homage of that great-souled sage?
9: "Son of Kuśika, did my father with a serene mind accept the reverent greeting of Rāma when he arrived there?"
10: Hearing his words, the great muni Viśvāmitra, skilled in speech, replied to the eloquent Śatānanda:
11: "Foremost of munis, nothing was left undone; whatever needed to be done, I did, and the sage's wife was reunited with the muni just as Reṇukā was with Bhārgava."
12: Hearing the words of the wise Viśvāmitra, the deeply radiant Śatānanda spoke to Rāma:
13: "Rāghava, foremost of men, welcome to you! By good fortune you have arrived here, led by the unconquered maharṣi Viśvāmitra.
14: "The deeply radiant Viśvāmitra is a brahmarṣi of boundless splendor whose deeds through tapas are beyond comprehension; know him to be your supreme refuge.
15: "Rāma, there is no one on earth more fortunate than you, for you have as your protector this son of Kuśika, who has performed tremendous tapas.
16: "Listen as I recount the power and the life story of the great-souled Kauśika; hear it from me as I tell you.
17: "For a long time he was a righteous king, a tamer of his foes, learned in dharma, accomplished in every branch of knowledge, and devoted to the welfare of his people.
18: "From Prajāpati came a king named Kuśa, and Kuśa's son was the mighty and deeply righteous Kuśanābha.
19: "Kuśanābha's son was renowned as Gādhi, and Gādhi's son is this deeply radiant great muni Viśvāmitra.
20: "The deeply radiant King Viśvāmitra ruled the earth and governed the kingdom for many thousands of years.
21: "Once, having marshaled his army, the deeply radiant king set out across the earth surrounded by a full akṣauhiṇī.
22: "Moving gradually through cities, realms, rivers, mountains, and āśramas, the king eventually arrived at Vasiṣṭha's hermitage.
23: "Vasiṣṭha's āśrama-grounds were filled with trees bearing all kinds of flowers and fruits, teeming with herds of various beasts, and frequented by Siddhas and Cāraṇas.
24: "Adorned by devatās, dānavas, Gandharvas, and Kinnaras, it abounded with gentle deer and was thronged with flocks of birds.
25: "It was filled with hosts of brahmarṣis and frequented by hosts of devarṣis—great-souled sages perfected in the practice of tapas and blazing like fire.
26: "Ever thronged with glorious, great-souled sages who resembled Brahmā himself—some subsisting on water, some on air, and others on fallen leaves,
27: "and still others living on fruits and roots, self-controlled, free of anger, masters of their senses—along with r̥ṣis and Vālakhilyas devoted to japa and homa,
28: "Foremost of the victorious, the mighty Viśvāmitra beheld Vasiṣṭha's āśrama-grounds, which looked like a second Brahmaloka.
