1: In that city of Ayodhyā lived King Daśaratha — well versed in the Vedas, far-sighted, immensely radiant, gathering all virtues in himself, and deeply loved by both the townspeople and the countryfolk.
2: He was a supreme chariot-warrior of the Ikṣvāku line, a performer of sacrifices, devoted to dharma, master of himself, a royal sage resembling a great rishi, and renowned throughout the three worlds.
3: Mighty and having subdued his foes, blessed with loyal allies and master of his senses, he rivaled Indra and Kubera in wealth and accumulated treasures.
4: Just as the glorious Manu had protected the world, so King Daśaratha, living in that city, protected the world.
5: Steadfast in truth and pursuing the three ends of life — dharma, artha, and kāma — he protected that foremost city just as Indra protects Amarāvatī.
6: In that noble city, the people were joyous, righteous, and highly learned; contented with their own wealth, they were free from greed and spoke only truth.
7: In that supreme city, there was no householder who had a meager store, none who lacked fulfillment, and none who was without cattle, horses, wealth, and grain.
8: Nowhere in Ayodhyā could one find a person who was lustful, miserly, cruel, unlettered, or skeptical of the divine.
9: All the men and women were devoted to dharma, deeply self-restrained, joyous in character and conduct, and pure as great rishis.
10: No one was to be found without earrings, a headdress, or garlands of flowers, none with meager comforts, and none whose body was unwashed, unanointed with fragrant pastes, or devoid of sweet perfume.
11: No one was seen eating impure food or failing to give in charity; no one lacked armlets, gold necklaces, or bracelets, and no one lacked self-control.
12: Among the brāhmaṇas of Ayodhyā, there was none who failed to maintain the sacred fire, none who did not perform sacrifices, none who gave less than thousands in charity, and none of mixed lineage.
13: Always dedicated to their own duties, the brāhmaṇas had conquered their senses; they practiced charity, Vedic study, and were restrained in accepting gifts.
14: At that time, no one was an atheist, no one was untruthful, no one lacked deep learning, no one was envious, no one was powerless, and no one was ignorant.
15: In Ayodhyā, no man or woman could be found who was wretched, mentally distressed, afflicted by grief, devoid of beauty and grace, or disloyal to the king.
16: Across all four orders of society, the people worshiped the devatās, honored guests, lived long lives, and remained steadfast in righteousness and truth.
17: The kṣatriyas were guided by the brāhmaṇas, the vaiśyas cooperated with the kṣatriyas, and the śūdras, dedicated to their own duties, served the three other classes.
18: That city was safeguarded by that king of the Ikṣvāku line, just as it had been guarded in ancient days by the wise Manu, first ruler of mankind.
19: It was filled with warriors like blazing fire — highly skilled, fierce and unyielding, fully trained in the martial arts, like lions in mountain caves.
20: The city was filled with superior horses born in Kāmboja, Bāhlīka, Vanāyu, and the river regions, all swift as the steeds of Indra.
21: It abounded in mountain-like elephants born in the Vindhya ranges and the Himalayas, immensely powerful and exuding rut.
22: That city was filled with noble elephants descended from the lineages of Añjana and Vāmana, comprising the prime breeds: Bhadra-Mandra, Bhadra-Mṛga, and Mṛga-Mandra.
23: Always filled with mountain-like elephants in rut, that city shone true to its name, unconquerable for two yojanas around.
24: Adorned with wondrous residences, secured by stout gates and locking-bars, auspicious and bustling with thousands of citizens, that truly unassailable city of Ayodhyā was ruled by the king, who equaled Indra himself.
