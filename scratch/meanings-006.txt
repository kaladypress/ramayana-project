1: Moving with swift agility, able to assume any form at will, the Kapi roamed at his pleasure through the lanes and mansions of Laṅkā.
2: Then that glorious hero reached the palace of the Rākṣasa king, encircled by a radiant rampart glowing with the brilliance of the sun.
3: It was guarded by terrifying Rākṣasas just as a vast forest is protected by ferocious lions; looking upon that palace, the Kapikuñjara shone with wonder.
4: It was enclosed by marvelous archways overlaid with silver and decorated with gold, fitted with exquisite courtyards and splendid gateways.
5: It was attended by brave, untiring commanders mounted on noble elephants, and by invincible chariot horses harnessed to swift war-cars.
6: The palace grounds were constantly traversed by rumbling, ornate chariots armored with lion and tiger skins, embellished with ivory, gold, and silver.
7: It was strewn with numerous jewels, furnished with priceless seats and vessels, and served as the vast abode and assembly ground of great chariot warriors.
8: It was filled on all sides with thousands of captivating, magnificent animals and birds of every variety.
9: Securely guarded by disciplined border-wardens and stalwart Rākṣasas, it was filled in every quarter with choice, peerless women.
10: Resounding with the laughter of delighted, jewel-like women and the chiming of their finest ornaments, that palace of the Rākṣasa king roared like the ocean swell.
11: Endowed with royal majesty and scented with the finest sandalwood, it was crowded with eminent nobles just as a mighty forest is filled with lions.
12: It echoed with the boom of kettle-drums and mr̥daṅgas and the sounding of conches; it was a sanctuary where daily oblations were offered, sacred sacrifices were performed on festive days, and worship was constantly maintained by the Rākṣasas.
13: Vast and deep as the ocean, resounding like the ocean's roar, that grand mansion of the mighty king was hung with tapestries of precious gems.
14: Strewn with great jewels, gleaming with architectural grandeur, and crowded with elephants, horses, and chariots, that residence was observed by the great Kapi.
15: Deeming it the very crown jewel of Laṅkā, the great Kapi Hanumān moved cautiously through the precincts close to Rāvaṇa's inner palace.
16: The fearless Vānara wandered from house to house, inspecting the gardens and mansions of the Rākṣasas.
17: Leaping down with tremendous speed, the valiant hero entered the mansion of Prahasta, and from there bounded to the house of Mahāpārśva.
18: Then the great Kapi leapt to the cloud-like mansion of Kumbhakarṇa, and then to the abode of Vibhīṣaṇa.
19: He visited the houses of Mahodara, Virūpākṣa, Vidyujjihva, and Vidyunmālin.
20: The swift great Kapi bounded to the house of Vajradaṁṣṭra, and to those of Śuka and the wise Sāraṇa.
21: Then the Hariyūthapa went to the palace of Indrajit, and to the residences of Jambumālī and Sumālī.
22: The great Kapi bounded next to the mansions of Raśmiketu, Sūryaśatru, and Vajrakāya.
23: The son of Māruta searched the homes of Dhūmrākṣa, Saṁpāti, Vidyudrūpa, Bhīma, Ghana, and Vighana.
24: He inspected the dwellings of Śukanābha, Vakra, Śaṭha, Vikaṭa, Hrasvakarṇa, Daṁṣṭra, and the Rākṣasa Romaśa.
25: He searched the homes of Yuddhonmatta, Matta, Dhvajagrīva, Nādī, Vidyujjihva, Indrajihva, and Hastimukha.
26: Proceeding methodically one by one, Hanumān, the son of Māruta, examined the residences of Karāla, Piśāca, and Śoṇitākṣa.
27: In those luxurious palaces, the renowned great Kapi beheld the immense wealth and splendor of those opulent Rākṣasa chiefs.
28: Having passed by the mansions of all those chiefs on every side, the glorious hero finally arrived at the supreme residence of the Rākṣasa king.
29: Moving about, that foremost of Haris, the tiger among Haris, saw deformed-eyed Rākṣasīs stationed around Rāvaṇa's sleeping chambers.
30: In the palace of that sovereign of Rākṣasas, he saw diverse platoons of sentries armed with spears, iron clubs, javelins, and lances.
31: He saw colossal Rākṣasas ready with various weapons, and exceptionally swift steeds of red, piebald, and pure white hues.
32: He saw noble, handsome war-elephants, trained to crush enemy tusker formations, masterfully schooled in battle tactics and rivaling Airāvata in combat.
33: In that palace he saw those destroyers of hostile armies dripping temporal rut like raining clouds and flowing mountain springs.
34: Roaring like rumbling thunderclouds and unassailable in battle, thousands of troops and war contingents were stationed there, adorned with refined gold.
35: Draped in unbroken meshes of gold and glowing like the morning sun, those battalions and transports stood within the residence of King Rāvaṇa.
36: The Kapi, son of Māruta, saw palanquins of varied designs, enchanting creeper-bowers, and exquisite picture galleries.
37: Within the residence of King Rāvaṇa, he saw pleasure pavilions, artificial wooden hills, charming sporting chambers, and daytime retreats.
38: That foremost mansion, vast as a plateau of Mount Mandara, was crowded with decorative peacock roosts.
39: Bristling with flagstaffs and filled with endless heaps of jewels, it held networks of hidden treasures on every side.
40: Executed with flawless craftsmanship, it rivaled the palace of Kubera himself; through the radiance of its gems and the majesty of Rāvaṇa,
41: that mansion blazed like the sun with its rays; and the Hariyūthapa saw beds, seats, and gleaming vessels fashioned entirely of pure gold.
42: Moist with sweet wines and intoxicating liquors, crowded with jeweled goblets, it was spacious and delightful like the celestial palace of Kubera.
43: It resonated with the chiming of anklets, the tinkling of jeweled waistbands, and the booming rhythms of mr̥daṅga drums.
44: Flanked by clusters of palaces, crowded with hundreds of jewel-like women, and laid out with well-ordered chambers, Hanumān stepped into that magnificent mansion.
