1: In that city of Ayodhyā lived Daśaratha, a knower of the Vedas who embraced all people, far-sighted, of great radiance, and beloved by the people of the city and the countryside.
2: He was the Atiratha among the Ikṣvākus — a peerless chariot warrior who performed yajñas, loved dharma, and had mastered himself. Like a maharṣi, he was a rājarṣi (royal sage), renowned in all three worlds.
3: He was mighty, a destroyer of his enemies, rich in friends and allies, and a master of his senses. In wealth and treasure, he was the equal of Śakra (Indra) and Vaiśravaṇa (Kubera).
4: Just as the radiant Manu once protected the world, so King Daśaratha protected the world while living in Ayodhyā.
5: He was true to his word and followed the trivarga — dharma, artha and kāma. Under his care that city was protected like Amarāvatī under Indra, the greatest of the devatās.
6: In that finest of cities, the people were happy and learned. They had righteous hearts, were content with their own wealth, free of greed, and spoke only the truth.
7: In that excellent city there was no householder with meager savings, none whose goals had gone unmet, and none without cows, horses, wealth and grain.
8: Nowhere in Ayodhyā could one find a man ruled by lust, a miser, or a cruel person. There was no one uneducated and no nāstika, a denier of dharma and the Vedas.
9: All the men and women were of dharmic character and well restrained. They were cheerful, and through their good nature and conduct they were as spotless as maharṣis.
10: No one lacked earrings, a crown or a garland, and no one lived with few comforts. No one was unwashed, without sandalwood paste on the body, or without fragrance.
11: No one ate poorly or was stingy with gifts, and no one went without armlets, a necklace (niṣka), or ornaments on the hands. No one lacked self-mastery either.
12: In Ayodhyā there was no brāhmaṇa who had not established the sacred fire (āhitāgni), who did not perform yajñas, or who did not give thousands in charity. And there was no mixing of the varṇas.
13: The brāhmaṇas were always devoted to their own duties and had mastered their senses. They loved giving and study, and were restrained in accepting gifts.
14: At that time, no one was faithless toward the Vedas, untruthful, unlearned, envious, incapable, or ignorant.
15: In that city, there was no one who did not know the six Vedāṅgas, none who lived without sacred vows, and none who failed to give gifts by the thousands. Not a single person was destitute, troubled in mind, or afflicted with grief.
16: Nowhere in Ayodhyā could any man or woman be seen who was lacking in grace and prosperity, devoid of beauty, or wanting in devotion to the king.
17: Across all four varṇas, beginning with the brāhmaṇas, the people worshipped the devatās and honored guests; all were grateful, generous, brave, and endowed with great prowess.
18: In that foremost of cities, all the people were blessed with long life, devoted to dharma and truth, and lived happily in the company of their wives, sons, and grandsons.
19: The kṣatriyas looked to the brāhmaṇas as their guide, while the vaiśyas followed the lead of the kṣatriyas. Devoted to their own duty, the śūdras served the other three varṇas in mutual harmony.
20: That city was thoroughly protected by the sovereign of the Ikṣvākus, just as it had been safeguarded in ancient times by the wise Manu, ruler of mankind.
21: The city was filled with warriors as fierce as fire — skilled, intolerant of insult, accomplished in every martial science, and formidable as lions in their mountain caves.
22: It was filled with the finest steeds — bred in Kāmboja, Bāhlīka, and Vanāyu, and along the sacred rivers — swift and spirited as the green coursers of Indra.
23: The city also teemed with mighty, mountain-like elephants, born in the Vindhya ranges and the snow-clad Himālayas, ferocious in rut and endowed with immense strength.
24: It was populated by great elephants descended from the noble celestial lines of the four Diggajas — Airāvata, Mahāpadma, Añjana, and Vāmana.
25: That city was perpetually filled with mountain-like elephants ever in rut — belonging to the three pure breeds: Bhadra, Mandra, and Mṛga; those combining all three; and the mixed breeds: Bhadra-Mandra, Bhadra-Mṛga, and Mṛga-Mandra.
26: Extending for two more yojanas all around, that city shone forth, truly worthy of its name Ayodhyā (the Unconquerable) — dwelling within which King Daśaratha protected the entire world.
27: Having subdued all his enemies, the great and glorious King Daśaratha ruled over that magnificent city, shining in their midst just as the moon presides over the stars.
28: With its sturdy gateways and firm crossbars, adorned with wondrous mansions and teeming with thousands of citizens, that auspicious city of Ayodhyā — truly unassailable as its name proclaimed — was ruled by that lord of the earth, an equal to Indra himself.
