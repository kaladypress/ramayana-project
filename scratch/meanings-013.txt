1: Leaping swiftly from the aerial palace onto the rampart, the Hari troop leader Hanumān flashed like lightning darting through a dark cloud.
2: Having thoroughly searched Rāvaṇa's residences without finding Jānakī Sītā, the Kapi spoke these words to himself:
3: "Serving Rāma's cause, I have searched almost all of Laṅkā, yet I do not see Vaidehī Sītā, radiant in every way.
4: Ponds, tanks, lakes, streams, rivers, marshy forest tracts, and inaccessible mountains — I have scoured this entire land, yet I do not see Jānakī.
5: The king of vultures, Sampāti, stated that Sītā was here in Rāvaṇa's dwelling, yet I cannot find her.
6: Could Sītā Vaidehī, Maithilī, daughter of Janaka, ever helplessly submit to the wicked Rāvaṇa? Never!
7: As the demon flew up swiftly carrying Sītā away, terrified of Rāma's arrows, she may have slipped and fallen mid-flight.
8: Or perhaps, as she was carried along the skyway frequented by the Siddhas, looking down upon the boundless ocean, that noble lady's heart gave out in fright.
9: Or by the tremendous speed of Rāvaṇa's flight and the crushing grip of his arms, that noble, wide-eyed lady may have breathed her last.
10: Or while he was soaring high over the sea, struggling to break free, the daughter of Janaka may have plunged into the ocean depths.
11: Or has this vile wretch Rāvaṇa devoured the helpless, suffering Sītā because she guarded her virtue without any protector near?
12: Or could that innocent, dark-eyed lady have been eaten by the wicked-minded wives of the Rākṣasa king?
13: Perhaps that poor lady passed away, keeping her mind fixed on Rāma's face, which resembles the full moon and has eyes like lotus petals.
14: Crying piteously, 'O Rāma! O Lakṣmaṇa! O Ayodhyā!' Vaidehī Maithilī may have given up her life in grief.
15: Or she is hidden away in Rāvaṇa's palace, weeping softly and plaintively like a maina trapped in a cage.
16: Born into the noble house of Janaka, wife of Rāma, how could that slender-waisted lady with eyes like lotus petals ever yield to Rāvaṇa?
17: If the daughter of Janaka is lost, vanished, or dead, it is impossible for me to report this to Rāma, who loves his wife so dearly.
18: Telling him brings disaster, yet not reporting brings disaster too. What on earth should I do? The dilemma seems insurmountable!
19: 'In such a critical situation, what is timely and right to do?' Hanumān pondered deeply once again.
20: 'If I go back from here to the city of the Vānara king without seeing Sītā, what will my mission have achieved?
21: My leap across the ocean will have been useless, as will my entry into Laṅkā and my reconnaissance of the Rākṣasas.
22: What will Sugrīva say, or the assembled Haris, or the two sons of Daśaratha when they arrive at Kiṣkindhā?
23: If I return and break this terrible news to Kākutstha — 'I did not find Sītā' — he will give up his life on the spot.
24: Hearing those harsh, terrible, cruel, and piercing words concerning Sītā, burning his very senses, he will not survive.
25: Seeing him in agony and resolved on death, the wise Lakṣmaṇa, so deeply attached to his brother, will not survive either.
26: Hearing that both brothers have perished, Bharata too will die; and seeing Bharata dead, Śatrughna will not live.
27: Seeing their sons dead, the mothers — Kausalyā, Sumitrā, and Kaikeyī — will surely cease to live.
28: Sugrīva, the king of the Plavagas, grateful and true to his vow, seeing Rāma gone, will then abandon his own life.
29: Brokenhearted, stricken with sorrow, joyless and miserable, Rumā will end her life in grief for her husband.
30: Already worn thin by grief over Vāli, Tārā too will not survive the death of King Sugrīva.
31: With the death of his mother and father, and the catastrophe befalling Sugrīva, how could Prince Aṅgada cling to life?
32: Overcome with grief for their king, the forest-dwellers will beat their heads with their palms and fists.
33: Cherished by the glorious king of Kapis with comforting words, gifts, and respect, the Vānaras will give up their lives.
34: Never again will those Kapikuñjaras gather to sport in the forests, on the mountains, or in caves.
35: Stricken by their master's tragedy, along with their wives, children, and ministers, they will gather and fling themselves from mountain peaks into rocky chasms.
36: The Vānaras will take poison, hang themselves, enter blazing fire, starve themselves to death, or fall on their own weapons.
37: If I return, I foresee terrible lamentation, the extinction of the Ikṣvāku line, and the annihilation of all the forest-dwellers!
38: Therefore, I will not return to the city of Kiṣkindhā from here; for without Maithilī, I cannot bear to face Sugrīva.
39: If I stay here and do not return, those two righteous chariot-warriors and the resolute Vānaras will sustain their lives in hope.
40: Living on whatever falls into my hands or mouth, disciplined, dwelling at the foot of trees, I will become a hermit without seeing Janaka's daughter.
41: Or in this coastal land rich in roots, fruits, and water, I will build a funeral pyre and enter the blazing fire.
42: Or as I sit in steady contemplation seeking to dissolve my physical form, crows and wild beasts can devour my body.
43: Sages have sanctioned this way of departure too, I believe: if I do not find Jānakī, I will walk straight into the sea.
44: The long-standing garland of my fame, deeply rooted, glorious, and renowned, is shattered because I cannot find Sītā.
45: Or I will become an ascetic, living strictly at the foot of trees; I will never return from here without finding that dark-eyed lady.
46: If I return from here without discovering Sītā, Aṅgada and all the Vānaras together will perish.
47: Yet death brings many evils, while as long as a person lives, he may attain good fortune. Therefore, I will hold on to my life; if one remains alive, reunion is certain!'
48: Weighing such manifold sorrow in his mind again and again, the Kapikuñjara could see no far shore to his grief.
49: 'Or I will kill the mighty, ten-necked Rāvaṇa! Granted that Sītā was abducted, this will be fitting retribution!
50: Or seizing him and carrying him across the ocean, I will deliver him to Rāma like a sacrificial beast to the god Paśupati!'
51: Anxious at not having found Sītā, enveloped in brooding sorrow, the Vānara reflected:
52: 'As long as I do not find Sītā, the glorious wife of Rāma, I will scour this city of Laṅkā again and again.
53: If I bring Rāma here solely on Sampāti's word, and Rāghava does not see his wife, his wrath would consume all the Vānaras.
54: I will stay right here, controlling my diet and subduing my senses, so that all those men and Vānaras do not perish on my account.
55: Look, here is the vast Aśoka grove with its huge trees! I will enter it, for I have not searched it yet.
56: Bowing to the Vasus, Rudras, Ādityas, the Aśvins, and the Maruts, I will go forward to multiply the grief of the Rākṣasas.
57: Defeating the Rākṣasas, I will restore the devī, the joy of the Ikṣvāku line, to the ascetic Rāma, like the attainment of spiritual perfection.'
58: Having meditated for a moment, his senses freed from the knots of anxious doubt, the mighty-armed Hanumān, son of Māruta, rose to his feet.
59: "Salutations to Rāma and to Lakṣmaṇa, and to Devī Sītā, the daughter of Janaka! Salutations to Rudra, Indra, Yama, and Anila! Salutations to the moon, the sun, and the host of the Maruts!"
60: Bowing to them and to Sugrīva, the son of Māruta looked in every direction toward the Aśoka grove.
61: Entering that lovely Aśoka grove in his mind first, the Vānara, son of Māruta, considered his next steps:
62: "This Aśoka grove, dense with trees and landscaped with every elegance, must surely be heavily guarded by many Rākṣasas.
63: Guards stationed here must certainly be watching over the trees; even the divine Wind, the soul of all, breathes softly so as not to agitate them.
64: For Rāma's purpose and to elude Rāvaṇa, I have shrunk my form; the devas along with the hosts of r̥ṣis will grant me success here.
65: May the self-born Lord Brahmā, the devas, Agni, Vāyu, and the thunderbolt-wielding Indra grant me success!
66: May Varuṇa with the noose in hand, the moon and the sun, the great-souled Aśvins, and all the Maruts grant me success!
67: May all living beings, the sovereign of all beings, and whatever other divinities dwell unseen along my path grant me success!
68: When will I see that noble lady's face — with its prominent nose, gleaming white teeth, unblemished beauty, pure smile, and lotus-petal eyes, radiant as the serene full moon?
69: How will that frail, suffering lady, overpowered by force by this vile, sinful, cruel-deeded wretch disguised in terrible splendor, appear before my eyes today?"
