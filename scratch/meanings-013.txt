1: When spring arrived again, a full year had passed, and the valiant king proceeded to perform the Aśvamēdha yajña for the sake of obtaining sons.
2: Bowing to Vasiṣṭha and honoring him with due respect, he spoke humbly to that foremost Brāhmaṇa, seeking the birth of sons:
3: "Foremost among sages, learned one, please conduct the yajña for us as prescribed in the scriptures, and arrange things so that no obstacle arises in any of its rites.
4: You are my affectionate well-wisher, my supreme and venerable guru; the burden of this yajña now undertaken must indeed be borne by you."
5: That best of Brāhmaṇas replied to the king, "So be it; I will carry out everything that you have proposed."
6: Then Vasiṣṭha addressed the elderly Brāhmaṇas experienced in rites of the yajña, as well as the senior, deeply righteous masters of Vāstu and architecture,
7: the work supervisors, artisans, carpenters, well-diggers, accountants, craftsmen, and likewise actors and dancers,
8: as well as trustworthy men skilled in the technical sciences, and scholars deeply learned in sacred lore: "By the king's command, all of you begin the preparations for the yajña.
9: Have many thousands of bricks brought quickly, and construct pavilions for the visiting kings, richly endowed with every comfort.
10: Build hundreds of fine dwellings for the Brāhmaṇas, sturdily constructed to withstand wind and weather, and well stocked with abundant foods and drinks.
11: Likewise, spacious quarters must be prepared for the citizens of the capital, stocked with plenty of food and furnished with every comfort.
12: Likewise, fine and wholesome food must be served to the people of the countryside with due respect and hospitality, and never casually or with indifference.
13: so that people of all four varṇas receive due honor and proper reception; no one must be treated with disdain, even under the sway of desire or anger.
14: Those workers and craftsmen who are attentively dedicated to the tasks of the yajña must especially be honored, according to their respective skill and merit.
15: They should all be generously provided with wealth and food, so that everything is arranged properly and no detail is overlooked.
16: 'Carry out your work with hearts full of affection and goodwill.' Then all of them came together and said to Vasiṣṭha:
17: 'We will do exactly as you have instructed; nothing whatsoever will be neglected.'
18: Then Vasiṣṭha summoned Sumantra and said: 'Invite the righteous rulers across the earth,
19: and respectfully gather people of all lands by the thousands—Brāhmaṇas, Kṣatriyas, Vaiśyas, and Śūdras.
20: Janaka, the ruler of Mithilā, is heroic and truly valiant, deeply versed in all the scriptures and established in the Vedas.
21: Go in person to invite and receive that illustrious king with great respect. Knowing the longstanding bond between our families, I mention him to you before all others.
22: Likewise, go in person to invite the king of Kāśī—he is affectionate, ever pleasant in speech, virtuous in conduct, and radiant like a deva.
23: Invite here also the aged, deeply righteous king of Kekaya, the father-in-law of this lion among kings, King Daśaratha, together with his son.
24: With great honor, invite Lomapāda, the renowned and noble ruler of Aṅga, the dear companion of this lion among kings.
25: Invite the eastern rulers, the kings of Sindhu, Sauvīra, and Saurāṣṭra, and all the monarchs of the south.
26: And all other kings across the earth who are friendly to us, invite them swiftly, along with their entourages and kinsmen.'
27: Hearing Vasiṣṭha's command, Sumantra promptly dispatched trustworthy messengers to invite those kings.
28: Then, by the sage's order, the righteous Sumantra himself set out in haste to personally escort the foremost rulers.
29: All the work supervisors then reported to the wise Vasiṣṭha everything that had been prepared for the yajña.
30: Pleased with their report, that foremost of Brāhmaṇas instructed them all again: "Nothing must ever be given to anyone with disrespect, nor even offhandedly in jest. Whatever is given with contempt brings harm to the giver—of this there is no doubt."
31: Then, after several days and nights, the monarchs arrived, bringing many valuable offerings for King Daśaratha.
32: Then Vasiṣṭha, thoroughly delighted, addressed the king:
33: "Mahārāja, the kings have arrived at your invitation, and I have honored all these eminent rulers according to their rank.
34: Everything required for the yajña has been prepared by attentive workers; now, O king, come forth to the yajña grounds nearby to begin the rite.
35: You should see the yajña grounds, O king, fully equipped with every provision and built swiftly, exactly as envisioned."
36: Then, following the counsel of both Vasiṣṭha and Ṛśyaśṛṅga, the ruler of the earth set out from his palace for the yajña grounds on an auspicious day under a propitious constellation.
37: Then all the foremost Brāhmaṇas, headed by Vasiṣṭha, placed Ṛśyaśṛṅga in front and commenced the rituals of the yajña.
38: All entered the yajña enclosure according to scriptural ordinances, and the glorious king, together with his queens, undertook the sacred vow of consecration.
