1: Having spent the rest of the night on the bank of the Śoṇa with the maharṣis, Viśvāmitra spoke as the night gave way to a bright dawn:
2: "The night has dawned brightly, Rāma, and the morning twilight is beginning. Get up, get up—blessings upon you—and make ready for our journey."
3: Hearing his words, Rāma performed his morning rites, got ready to depart, and said:
4: "This Śoṇa has clear, pure water, shallow fords, and lovely sandy banks. Which path should we take to cross it, Brāhmaṇa?"
5: Addressed in this way by Rāma, Viśvāmitra replied, "I will show you the path—this is the way the maharṣis travel."
6: After the wise Viśvāmitra spoke these words, the ṛṣis set out, taking in the sight of many different forests along the way.
7: After traveling a long distance, by midday they came upon the Jāhnavī, foremost among rivers, frequented by munis.
8: At the sight of her sacred waters, alive with swans and sārasa cranes, all the munis and the Rāghavas were filled with joy.
9: They set up their camp along her bank, bathed according to proper custom, and offered tarpaṇa to the pitṛs and devatās.
10: After offering oblations in the agnihotra fires and partaking of the sacred haviḥ—sweet as amṛta—they sat down on the pure bank of the Jāhnavī with contented hearts.
11: Then, as everyone sat gathered around the noble-souled Viśvāmitra on all sides, Rāma spoke to him:
12: "Revered sage, I want to hear about the river Gaṅgā, who flows through three paths. How did she traverse the three worlds to reach the ocean, sovereign of all rivers and streams?"
13: Prompted by Rāma's request, the great muni Viśvāmitra began to tell the story of Gaṅgā's birth and her descent.
14: "There is a king of mountains named Himavān, a vast treasury of minerals. He had two daughters, Rāma, whose beauty had no equal on earth.
15: "Their graceful mother, Rāma, was the daughter of Meru—the enchanting Menā, beloved wife of Himavān.
16: "To her was born this Gaṅgā, Himavān's eldest daughter, and his second daughter was named Umā, Rāghava.
17: "Later, all the suras, wanting to fulfill a great purpose for the devatās, asked the mountain king for his eldest daughter, the three-pathed river Gaṅgā.
18: "In keeping with dharma, Himavān gave away his world-purifying daughter—Gaṅgā, who moves wherever she wills—out of a desire for the good of all three worlds.
19: "Accepting her for the sake of the three worlds, those benefactors of all three realms took Gaṅgā and departed, deeply fulfilled in heart.
20: "As for the mountain's other daughter, Raghunandana, rich in spiritual power, she took up a fierce vow and practiced intense tapas.
21: "Endowed with that fierce tapas, Umā—revered by all the worlds—was given by the great mountain king to the matchless Rudra.
22: "These are the two daughters of the mountain king, revered by all the worlds: Gaṅgā, foremost among rivers, and the goddess Umā, Rāghava.
23: "This is the sacred account I have told you of how the three-pathed river first took her course into the heavens, my dear Rāma, foremost among the wise and virtuous."
