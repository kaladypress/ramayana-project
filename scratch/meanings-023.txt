1: When the night ended and dawn broke, the great sage Viśvāmitra spoke to Kākutstha (Rāma), who was lying on his bed of leaves.
2: "Rāma, noble son of Kausalyā, the morning twilight has begun. Rise, tiger among men — it is time to perform the daily morning rites."
3: Hearing those noble words of the sage, the two heroic princes bathed, offered arghya to Sūrya, and chanted the supreme Gāyatrī japa.
4: Having completed their morning rites, the two valiant princes joyfully bowed to Viśvāmitra, rich in austerity, and stood before him ready to set out.
5: As they traveled on, those two valiant princes saw the divine river Gaṅgā, which flows through the three worlds, at its auspicious confluence with the Sarayū.
6: There stood a sacred hermitage of sages of fierce spiritual power, who had practiced the highest austerities for many thousands of years.
7: Seeing that sacred hermitage, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa were deeply pleased and said to the great-souled Viśvāmitra:
8: "Whose sacred hermitage is this, and who lives here? Venerable sage, we want to hear about it, for our curiosity is great."
9: Hearing their question, that foremost of sages smiled and said, "Listen, Rāma, to whom this hermitage once belonged.
10: "Kandarpa, whom the wise call Kāma, once had a physical form. Here, Sthāṇu (Śiva) was absorbed in disciplined austerity—
11: "newly wed, that chief of the devatās, walking with the hosts of Maruts. The foolish Kāma dared to disrupt his austerity, and was met with a fierce roar by the great-souled Śiva.
12: "Rāma, when foolish Kāma was burned by Śiva's terrible eye, every limb of his body was completely consumed.
13: "His body was destroyed there as the great-souled Śiva burned him to ashes; in his anger, the supreme devatā made Kāma bodiless.
14: "From that time on, Rāghava, he has been known as Anaṅga (the bodiless one), and that prosperous region where he shed his body became known as the land of Aṅga.
15: "This is his sacred hermitage, and these sages here were once his disciples. They are devoted to dharma, brave prince, and there is no sin in them.
16: "Handsome Rāma, let us spend tonight here between the two sacred rivers; tomorrow we will cross over.
17: "Best of men, let us all bathe, perform our japa, and offer oblations into the sacred fire, and then enter this sacred hermitage."
18: Through the divine sight born of their austerity, the sages knew they were conversing there and came forward in great joy to meet them.
19: Offering arghya, pādya, and hospitality first to Viśvāmitra, the son of Kuśika, they then extended the same welcome to Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa.
20: After Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa, and Viśvāmitra had received their hospitality, those composed sages entertained them with stories and duly performed their sandhyā prayers.
21: Brought inside by the devout sages living there, Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa, and Viśvāmitra stayed very comfortably with them at Kāma's hermitage.
22: With delightful stories, the righteous Kauśika (Viśvāmitra), foremost among sages, entertained the two charming princes, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa.
