1: Then the heroic forest-dwellers, led by Aṅgada, together with the great Kapi Hanumān, accepted Jāmbavān's advice.
2: Then all those bulls among the Plavagas, delighted, with Vāyuputra in the lead, left the summit of Mahendra and leapt into the sky.
3: Huge in body and immense in strength, like Meru and Mandara, like great elephants in rut, they seemed to cover the whole sky.
4: All beings honored Hanumān — self-possessed, mighty, and tremendously swift — and the Vānaras kept their eyes fixed on him, as if carrying him along with their gaze.
5: They were set on bringing Rāghava's purpose to fulfillment and winning great glory for their master. Their aim was achieved, and they were lifted high by the success of their mission.
6: All of them were eager to share the good news, all of them were eager for battle, and all of them, high-minded as they were, were determined to help Rāma strike back.
7: Leaping and soaring through the sky, those forest-dwellers reached a forest like Nandana, full of trees and creepers.
8: It was the forest called Madhuvana, protected by Sugrīva. No being could trespass on it, and it delighted the heart of every being.
9: The mighty Kapi Dadhimukha, maternal uncle of the great-souled Sugrīva, chief of the Kapis, always guarded it.
10: When the Vānaras reached that great forest, the place dearest to the heart of the king of the Vānaras, they became wildly excited.
11: Then the joyful Vānaras, tawny as honey, saw the great Madhuvana and begged the prince for honey.
12: The prince first took the consent of the elders led by Jāmbavān, and then gave them permission to eat the honey.
13: Given leave by the wise prince, Vāli's son, the Haris went to the trees that were swarming with bees.
14: Others ate roots and fruits as well. All of them were thrilled and became wildly drunk.
15: Now that they had permission, all the forest-dwellers were overjoyed, and in their delight they danced about here and there.
16: Some sang, some bowed, some danced, some laughed. Some fell down, some wandered about, some leapt, some babbled.
17: Some leaned on one another, some went at one another, some chattered with one another, some played with one another.
18: Some rushed from tree to tree, some dropped from the treetops to the ground. Some, bursting with speed, shot up from the ground to the tops of great trees.
19: One came laughing up to another who was singing; another came weeping up to one who was laughing. Another came shoving one who was weeping, and another came roaring at one who was shoving.
20: That Kapi army was in total uproar, their behavior wild and reckless from drinking honey. There was no one there who was not drunk, and no one who was not fully satisfied.
21: Then the Kapi named Dadhimukha saw the forest being devoured and the trees stripped of their leaves and flowers. In anger he tried to stop those Kapis.
22: Though those swaggering Vānaras threatened him, the guardian of the forest — an aged Hari-hero of fierce energy — once again made up his mind to protect the forest from them.
23: To some he spoke harshly and boldly; others he struck with his palms without holding back. He got into fights with some, and with others he tried gentle persuasion.
24: He tried to hold them back with words and with force, but in their drunkenness they surrounded him. Losing all fear, they came at him together, knocked him down, and dragged him around, with no thought for how wrong it was.
25: Scratching him with their nails, biting him with their teeth, and pounding him with their palms and feet, all the Kapis in their drunkenness nearly finished that Kapi off — and they stripped the great forest bare.
