1: Then, after many days had passed, the delightful spring season arrived, and the king felt the wish to perform the sacrifice.
2: Bowing his head, he won over that brahmin, who shone with a devatā-like radiance, and requested him to officiate at the sacrifice for the sake of continuing his line.
3: Well honored, Ṛśyaśṛṅga said, "So be it," and told the king, "Let the materials for the sacrifice be gathered, and let the horse be set free."
4: Then the king spoke to Sumantra, the best of ministers: "Sumantra, quickly bring the ṛtvijas (priests) who are masters of the Veda."
5: Then Sumantra, swift in his stride, went at once and brought all those brahmins who had mastered the Vedas.
6: He brought Suyajña, Vāmadeva, Jābāli, and Kāśyapa, the family priest Vasiṣṭha, and the other foremost brahmins.
7: Then the righteous King Daśaratha honored them and spoke these gentle words, full of dharma and purpose.
8: "I long for sons, and without them I find no happiness. For that reason I have decided to perform the Aśvamedha, the horse sacrifice."
9: "So I wish to perform the sacrifice by the rites seen in the scriptures. Through the power of the sage's son (Ṛśyaśṛṅga), I will also gain my desires."
10: Then all the brahmins, led by Vasiṣṭha, welcomed the words that had come from the king's mouth, saying, "Well said!"
11: Led by Ṛśyaśṛṅga, they replied to the king: "Let the materials be gathered, and let the horse be set free."
12: "You will surely have four sons of immeasurable valor, since this righteous resolve for sons has come to you."
13: Hearing the words of the brahmins, the king was delighted, and with joy he said these auspicious words to his ministers.
14: "On the word of my teachers, let the materials be gathered for me quickly. Let the horse be released, guarded by capable men and accompanied by a teacher."
15: "Let the sacrificial ground be laid out on the northern bank of the Sarayū. Let the propitiatory rites be performed in full, as the ritual manual prescribes and in the proper manner."
16: "Any king can perform this sacrifice, but there must be no serious fault in this best of sacrifices."
17: "Learned brahmarākṣasas look for any flaw in it. A sacrifice that lacks proper procedure destroys its performer at once."
18: "So make arrangements so that this sacrifice is completed for me according to the rules, and let those skilled in the tasks here carry them out."
19: Then all the ministers agreed, saying, "So be it," and carried out the king's words exactly as instructed.
20: Then the brahmins praised the bull among kings, who knew dharma. After receiving his leave, they all went back the way they had come.
21: After the brahmins had gone and he had dismissed the ministers, the radiant king entered his own palace.
