1: Then, at the break of a clear dawn, the two subduers of enemies (Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa) completed their morning rites and, placing Viśvāmitra ahead of them, came to the bank of the Gaṅgā.
2: All those great-souled sages of strict vows brought up a fine boat and said to Viśvāmitra:
3: "Please step into the boat with the princes leading the way. Travel your path safely without mishap, and let no time be lost."
4: Saying "So be it" and honoring those sages, Viśvāmitra crossed the ocean-bound river together with the two princes.
5: Reaching the middle of the river, Rāma heard a loud roar swelled by the rushing waters, and sought to know what caused that sound.
6: Then, in midstream, Rāma asked that foremost of sages, "What is this tumultuous roar of clashing waters?"
7: Hearing Rāghava's curious question, the righteous sage explained the true cause of that sound:
8: "Rāma, tiger among men, on Mount Kailāsa, Brahmā created a lake by the resolve of his mind (manas) — and for that reason it is called Lake Mānasa.
9: "From that lake (saras) flows the river that embraces Ayodhyā — called Sarayū because it issues from that lake, and sacred because it sprang from Brahmā's lake.
10: "This matchless roar comes from the Sarayū rushing into the Jāhnavī (Gaṅgā), born from the churning of their waters. Rāma, offer your reverence to this confluence with a composed mind."
11: Those two deeply righteous princes bowed in reverence to both rivers, and, reaching the southern bank, walked on with swift, light steps.
12: Seeing a terrifying, untamed forest, Rāma, the Ikṣvāku prince, asked that foremost of sages:
13: "How impassable this forest is! It rings with swarms of crickets, and is overrun with fierce beasts of prey and birds with harsh, frightening cries.
14: "It is filled with all kinds of birds screeching with dreadful sounds, and marked by lions, tigers, wild boars, and elephants.
15: "Crowded with dhava, aśvakarṇa, kakubha, bilva, tinduka, pāṭala, and badarī trees — what is this dreadful forest?"
16: The radiant great sage Viśvāmitra replied to him, "Listen, dear Kākutstha, to whom this dreadful forest belongs.
17: "Best of men, there once were two prosperous lands here — Malada and Karūṣa — built by the design of the devatās themselves.
18: "Long ago, Rāma, when the thousand-eyed Indra slew Vṛtra, the sin of brahmahatyā (killing a brāhmaṇa) entered him, leaving him overwhelmed with impurity and hunger.
19: "Then the devatās and the sages rich in austerity bathed Indra with pitchers of Gaṅgā water consecrated with mantras, and washed away his impurity.
20: "Having freed Mahendra's body of its impurity (mala) and filth (kārūṣa) by casting it here on this land, they were filled with joy.
21: "When Indra became free of impurity and filth, and pure once more, the mighty one was deeply pleased and granted the region an incomparable boon:
22: "'These two prosperous lands, having borne the impurity of my body, will gain fame throughout the world as Malada and Karūṣa.'
23: "Seeing the honor thus bestowed on the land by the wise Śakra (Indra), the devatās praised him, saying, 'Well done, well done!'
24: "Rāma, subduer of enemies, for a long time these two prosperous lands, Malada and Karūṣa, flourished happily with abundant wealth and grain.
25: "After some time, however, there appeared a yakṣī able to take any form at will, possessing the strength of a thousand elephants.
26: "May all be well with you, Rāma — her name is Tāṭakā, the wife of the powerful Sunda; her son is the rākṣasa Mārīca, who equals Śakra in prowess.
27: "With round, massive arms, a huge head, a vast mouth and body, that giant rākṣasa of terrifying form constantly terrorizes the people.
28: "Rāghava, this wicked Tāṭakā continually ravages these two lands, Malada and Karūṣa.
29: "She lives just over half a yojana from here, blocking the road — and we must pass right through Tāṭakā's forest.
30: "Relying on the strength of your arms, slay this wicked creature; at my command, rid this land of its thorn once more.
31: "No one dares to enter this region as it is now, Rāma, devastated by that dreadful, unbearable yakṣiṇī.
32: "I have told you everything — how this dreadful forest was completely laid waste by the yakṣī, and her destruction still continues even today."
