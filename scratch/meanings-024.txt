1: Approaching Sītā, those harsh-natured Rākṣasīs with deformed faces spoke cruel and unpleasant words to her:
2: "Listen, Sītā! Why won't you agree to live in the royal palace, which enchants all creatures and is furnished with the most luxurious beds?
3: Being a human woman, you think so highly of being the wife of a mere mortal! Turn your mind away from Rāma; you will never be his again!
4-5: Go to Rāvaṇa, the ruler of Rākṣasas who enjoys the riches of all three worlds, take him as your husband, and enjoy yourself as you please! Why do you, a human woman, still pine for that human Rāma, who has lost his kingdom, failed in his aims, and wanders in despair, blameless lady?"
6: Hearing the taunts of the Rākṣasīs, lotus-eyed Sītā, her eyes filled with tears, replied:
7: "What you have all gathered here to say is despised by the entire world. Such vile words will never find a place in my heart!
8: A human woman can never be the wife of a Rākṣasa. You may eat me if you wish, but I will never do your bidding!
9: Whether he is wretched or stripped of his kingdom, he who is my husband is my highest master; I am eternally devoted to him, just as Suvarcalā is to the sun.
10-14: Just as noble Śacī attends upon Indra, Arundhatī upon Vasiṣṭha, Rohiṇī upon the moon, Lopāmudrā upon Agastya, Sukanyā upon Cyavana, Sāvitrī upon Satyavān, Śrīmatī upon Kapila, Madayantī upon Saudāsa, Keśinī upon Sagara, and Damayantī, daughter of Bhīma, upon her husband Nala—so am I entirely devoted to my husband Rāma, the foremost of the Ikṣvākus." Hearing Sītā's words, the Rākṣasīs boiled over with rage and, urged on by Rāvaṇa's orders, hurled vicious threats at her, while the Kapi Hanumān, hiding motionless and silent in the śiṁśapā tree, listened to them menacing Sītā.
15: Furious, they surrounded the trembling princess from all sides and fiercely licked their red, drooping lips.
16: Seizing their sharp battle-axes in violent rage, they screamed: "She does not deserve to have Rāvaṇa, the king of Rākṣasas, as her husband!"
17: Threatened by those dreadful Rākṣasīs, that fair-faced lady wiped away her tears and shrank back toward the śiṁśapā tree.
18: Reaching the śiṁśapā tree surrounded by the Rākṣasīs, wide-eyed Sītā stood there, completely overwhelmed by grief.
19: Those terrifying Rākṣasīs hemmed her in on every side, menacing her as she stood emaciated, sorrow-faced, and wrapped in soiled garments.
20: Then a hideous Rākṣasī named Vinatā, furious in look, terrifying, and with a sunken belly, snarled at her:
21: "Sītā, you have shown more than enough love for your husband; pushing anything too far only brings disaster, good woman!
22: I'm satisfied that you've done your duty as a human woman; may good come to you. Now listen to the sensible advice I'm giving you, Maithilī!
23: Accept Rāvaṇa, the king of all Rākṣasas, as your husband—he is valiant, handsome, and rivals Indra himself!
24: He is generous, open-handed, and speaks kindly to everyone; leave that wretched human Rāma behind and take refuge with Rāvaṇa!
25: Decked in divine cosmetics and celestial jewels, Vaidehī, become the sovereign queen of all the worlds from this very day!
26: Be to him as the goddess Svāhā is to Agni, or Śacī to Indra, lovely lady! What use to you is Rāma, a miserable creature whose life is practically over?
27: If you don't listen to what I'm telling you, we will all devour you this very moment!"
28: Then another Rākṣasī named Vikaṭā, with sagging breasts, shook her fist and roared furiously at Sītā:
29: "Stupid woman! Out of pity and soft-heartedness, Maithilī, we have put up with far too many of your insulting words!
30: You refuse to accept our sound advice given at the right time. You've been brought across an ocean that no one else can ever cross!
31: You are trapped inside Rāvaṇa's formidable palace, Maithilī, locked within his house and closely guarded by us!
32: Not even Indra himself could rescue you now! So listen to what I'm telling you for your own good, Maithilī!
33: Stop crying! Cast off this pointless grief. Embrace joy and pleasure, and give up this constant misery!
34: Sītā, enjoy yourself with the king of Rākṣasas as you please! You know very well, timid girl, that a woman's youth is fleeting!
35: Take your pleasure before it slips away! In lovely gardens and mountain groves,
36: roam freely with the king of Rākṣasas, dark-eyed beauty! Seven thousand women will bow to your command, gorgeous lady!
37: Accept Rāvaṇa, the ruler of all Rākṣasas, as your husband! If you don't do exactly as I say, Maithilī, I'll rip your heart out and eat it!"
38: Then a Rākṣasī named Caṇḍodarī, of dread appearance, brandished a huge spear and snarled:
39: "Seeing this doe-eyed woman with breasts trembling from fright, dragged here by Rāvaṇa, an immense craving has overcome me!
40: I want to feast on her liver, her spleen, her heart and its arteries, her intestines, and her head!"
41: Then a Rākṣasī named Praghasā declared:
42: "Why are we just sitting around? Let's wring this cruel woman's neck! Then we'll report to the king that the human woman is dead!
43: There's no doubt about it—he'll tell us, 'Eat her!' Then another Rākṣasī named Ajāmukhī chimed in:
44: "Chop her up into equal bites, and let's divide her among ourselves; I don't want any arguing! Bring out plenty of drinks and fresh garlands right now!
45: Then a Rākṣasī named Śūrpaṇakhā said:
46: "I like what Ajāmukhī says! Bring out the sorrow-banishing liquor right away; once we have the human meat, let's go dance at Nikumbhilā!"
47: Threatened in this dreadful manner by those terrifying Rākṣasīs, Sītā, who looked like the daughter of a god, lost all her composure and wept aloud.
