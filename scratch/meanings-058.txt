1: Then, on the peak of Mount Mahendra, the mighty Haris, with Hanumān at their head, experienced immense joy.
2: When the great-souled Vānaras were all seated happily, Jāmbavān, filled with delight, turned to the joyful great Kapi.
3: Jāmbavān asked the son of Anila about how the mission had unfolded: "How did you find the devī, and how is she living there?
4: And how does the cruel Daśānana treat her? Great Kapi, tell us the truth about all of this.
5: How did you speak with the devī, and what did she reply? Once we hear the facts, we will consider our next course of action.
6: Tell us clearly what we should say when we go there, and what we should keep to ourselves."
7: Prompted by him, his hair standing on end with delight, Hanumān bowed his head in reverence to Devī Sītā and replied:
8: "As you all saw with your own eyes, I leapt into the sky from the summit of Mahendra, intent on reaching the southern shore of the ocean.
9: As I was flying, an obstacle seemed to appear in my path — I saw a wondrous, enchanting golden mountain peak.
10: It stood blocking my path, and I took the mountain for an obstacle. I approached that wondrous, golden jewel of a mountain,
11: and decided in my mind that I had to shatter it. So I struck that great mountain with my tail.
12: Its sun-like peak broke into a thousand pieces. Understanding my resolve, the great mountain said:
13: Calling me 'Son' in a sweet voice that cheered my heart, he said, 'Know me to be your uncle, a dear friend of Mātariśvan.
14: I am known as Maināka, living in the great ocean. In ancient times, my son, the greatest mountains had wings and flew across the earth as they pleased, causing havoc everywhere.
15: Hearing of the mountains' conduct, the glorious Mahendra, the subduer of Pāka, sheared off their wings by the thousands with his vajra.
16: But your great-souled father Māruta rescued me then, my dear child, and flung me into the great ocean.
17: Subduer of foes, I must help Rāma, for Rāma is foremost among upholders of dharma and equals Mahendra in valor.'
18: Having heard these words from the great-souled Maināka,
19: I told the mountain my mission, and my mind was eager to press on. The great-souled Maināka then gave me leave to go.
20: That great mountain vanished, withdrawing his human form into his mountain body within the vast ocean.
21: Summoning my greatest speed, I set out on the rest of the journey and flew swiftly along the path for a long time.
22: Then in the middle of the sea I saw Devī Surasā, mother of the Nāgas. That devatā spoke to me:
23: 'Foremost of Haris, the devatās have appointed you as my food, so I will eat you. You have been granted to me after a long time.'
24: Addressed thus by Surasā, I stood with joined palms, bowed, and with a pale face spoke these words:
25: 'The glorious Rāma, son of Daśaratha and scorcher of foes, entered the Daṇḍaka forest with his brother Lakṣmaṇa and with Sītā.
26: His wife Sītā was abducted by the wicked Rāvaṇa. By Rāma's command, I am going to her as a messenger.
27: You live in this realm, so you ought to assist Rāma. Or else, let me first see Maithilī and Rāma of untiring deeds,
28: and then I will enter your mouth — I give you my solemn promise.' When I said this to her, Surasā, who could assume any form at will,
29: replied, 'No one passes me by; this is the boon granted to me.' Addressed thus by Surasā, I expanded myself to ten yojanas across.
30: In an instant I grew half as wide again, and she opened her mouth to match my size.
31: Seeing her mouth gaping wide, I shrank my body, and at that very moment became as small as a thumb.
32: Darting swiftly into her mouth, I came right out the next instant. Then Devī Surasā resumed her true form and spoke to me again:
33: 'Go happily on your way, gentle one, best among Haris, to fulfill your mission! Reunite Vaidehī with the great-souled Rāghava!
34: Be blessed, mighty-armed Vānara, I am pleased with you.' Then all beings praised me, calling out, 'Well done! Well done!'
35: Then I soared into the wide sky like Garuḍa. But suddenly my shadow was seized, though I could see nothing catching me.
36: With my speed checked, I looked in all ten directions, but could see nothing that was holding back my flight.
37: Then the thought struck me: 'What kind of obstacle has arisen in my path, where no form can be seen?'
38: As I wondered in distress, I cast my eyes downward, and there I saw a terrifying Rākṣasī lying in the water.
39: Laughing with a roar like thunder, that terrifying creature spoke these crude words to me as I hovered there calmly:
40: 'Where are you going, huge one? You are the food I crave in my hunger! Satisfy my body, which has gone without food for so long!'
41: I accepted her challenge with 'So be it,' and made my body swell larger than the span of her mouth.
42: Her dreadful, gaping mouth widened to swallow me, but she did not realize what I was doing or notice my transformation.
43: Then in the blink of an eye I contracted my huge form, tore out her heart, and shot back up into the sky.
44: Her arms flung wide, that terrifying mountain of a Rākṣasī fell into the salt sea once I had ripped out her heart.
45: Then I heard the sky-dwelling Siddhas and Cāraṇas proclaim: 'Hanumān has swiftly slain the terrifying Rākṣasī Siṁhikā!'
46: Having killed her, mindful of my urgent task, I traveled a great distance and saw the southern shore of the ocean, adorned with hills, where that city of Laṅkā lies.
47: When the sun set, I entered the city, the stronghold of the Rākṣasas, unseen by the fiercely powerful demons.
48: As I was entering, a woman rose up before me like a cloud at the dissolution of the world, letting out a burst of mocking laughter.
49: She wanted to kill me, her hair blazing like fire.
50: Striking that terrifying creature with my left fist, I subdued her. As I entered at dusk, she said to me in fear:
51: 'Hero, I am the city of Laṅkā, conquered by your valor. Because of this, you will vanquish every single one of the Rākṣasas.'
52: I searched for Janaka's daughter throughout the night, even entering Rāvaṇa's inner quarters, but I could not find the slender-waisted lady.
53: Unable to find Sītā in Rāvaṇa's palace, I sank into an ocean of sorrow and could see no far shore.
54: In my grief I noticed a splendid palace garden, surrounded by an imposing golden wall.
55: Leaping over the wall, I saw a grove with many trees, and in the middle of the Aśoka garden stood a great śiṁśapā tree.
56: Climbing into it, I looked down upon a grove of golden plantains, and not far from the śiṁśapā tree I saw a fair lady.
57: She was youthful and dark-complexioned, with lotus eyes, her face emaciated from fasting, wearing a single garment, her hair dulled with dust.
58-59: Her form was worn with sorrow and grief, yet she remained devoted to her husband's welfare, surrounded by misshapen, cruel Rākṣasīs who feed on flesh and blood, like a deer trapped among tigresses. There in the midst of the Rākṣasīs, she was constantly threatened.
60: Wearing her hair in a single braid, heartbroken, completely absorbed in thoughts of her husband, sleeping on the bare ground, pale and withered like a lotus pond at the onset of winter frost,
61: spurning Rāvaṇa and resolved to die, that fawn-eyed lady was at last discovered by me.
62: Seeing Rāma's blameless wife in such a state, I stayed right there in the śiṁśapā tree, watching her.
63: Then from Rāvaṇa's dwelling I heard a loud commotion, deeply resounding, mingled with the tinkling of girdles and anklets.
64: Very anxious, I contracted my form and hid among the dense foliage of the śiṁśapā tree like a bird.
65: Then the mighty Rāvaṇa and his wives arrived at the spot where Sītā was sitting.
66: Seeing the king of the Rākṣasas, Sītā, graceful in form, shrank her thighs together and clasped her full breasts with her arms.
67: Terrified and deeply distressed, she glanced here and there, seeing no protection anywhere, trembling in her misery.
68: Daśagrīva said to that deeply sorrowing Sītā, bowing his head low: 'Accept me and think well of me!
69: Proud woman, if out of pride you do not accept me, then in two months' time, Sītā, I will drink your blood.'
70: Hearing these words from the wicked Rāvaṇa, Sītā was furious and spoke these noble words:
71: 'Vile Rākṣasa, I am the wife of Rāma of boundless splendor, sovereign of the Ikṣvāku line, and daughter-in-law of Daśaratha!
72: How did your tongue not fall out when speaking such unspeakable words? What valor do you have, ignoble wretch, who in my husband's absence
73: abducted me and fled here unseen by that great-souled hero? You are not Rāma's equal — you are not even fit to be his slave! Rāghava is worthy of worship, truthful in speech, and renowned in battle.'
74: Addressed so harshly by Jānakī, Daśānana instantly flared up in rage like a fire on a funeral pyre.
75: Rolling his cruel eyes, he raised his right fist to strike Maithilī down, while the women raised a cry of horror.
76: Springing forward from among the women, that wicked king's wife,
77: the noble lady named Mandodarī, held him back. Tormented by passion as he was, she addressed him in a soothing voice:
78: 'You equal Mahendra in valor, what do you want with Sītā? Enjoy yourself with me today — Jānakī is not superior to me.
79: Enjoy yourself here with the daughters of the devatās, Gandharvas, and Yakṣas, master! What will you do with Sītā?'
80: Then, gathered together, those women raised up that mighty night-stalker and quickly led him back to his palace.
81: When Daśagrīva had gone, the grotesque-faced Rākṣasīs began menacing Sītā with cruel and terrible words.
82: Jānakī treated their talk like a blade of grass, and their threats against Sītā proved completely futile.
83: Exhausted from their useless roaring, those flesh-eating Rākṣasīs went to Rāvaṇa and reported Sītā's firm resolve.
84: Then all of them, hopeless and giving up their efforts, lay around her on all sides and fell into deep sleep.
85: While they slept, Sītā, devoted to her husband's good, lamented piteously in her misery and wept in deep sorrow.
86: Rising from among them, Trijaṭā spoke these words: 'Eat your own selves quickly! Sītā, the virtuous daughter of Janaka and daughter-in-law of Daśaratha, will not perish!
87: For today I saw a dreadful dream that made my hair stand on end — foretelling the destruction of the Rākṣasas and the victory of her husband.
88-89: She alone is capable of protecting this band of Rākṣasīs from Rāghava. Let us implore Vaidehī, for that seems best to me. When such a dream is seen about a grieving woman, she is freed from all her sorrows and attains the highest joy.
90: When pleased by our prostration, Maithilī, daughter of Janaka, is well able to protect the Rākṣasīs from great danger.'
91: Then that modest young woman, thrilled by the prospect of her husband's victory, said: 'If this comes true, I will certainly be your shelter.'
92: Seeing Sītā in such a dreadful plight, I pondered where I sat, and my mind found no peace.
93: I thought of a way to speak with Jānakī, and then I recited the lineage of the Ikṣvāku dynasty.
94: Hearing those words in praise of the royal sages, the devī, her eyes veiled with tears, spoke to me:
95: 'Who are you, bull among Vānaras, by whose agency and how have you come here?
96: What bond of affection do you have with Rāma? Please tell me.' Hearing these words from her, I too spoke:
97: 'Devī, your husband Rāma has an ally of fearsome valor — the mighty and courageous king of Vānaras named Sugrīva.
98: Know me to be his servant, Hanumān, who has come here. I was dispatched to you by your husband, Rāma of untiring deeds.
99: Glorious lady, that tiger among men, the illustrious son of Daśaratha, personally gave me this signet ring for you as a token of recognition.
100: Tell me your wish, devī — what should I do? Shall I carry you back to the side of Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, or what next?'
101: Hearing this and understanding everything, Sītā, joy of Janaka, said: 'Let Rāghava destroy Rāvaṇa and take me back himself.'
102: Bowing my head to that noble, blameless devī, I asked for a token of recognition that would bring joy to Rāghava's heart.
103: Then Sītā said to me, 'Take this excellent jewel, by which the mighty-armed Rāma will think highly of you.'
104: Saying this, the graceful lady handed me the splendid jewel in deep distress and charged me with a message in words.
105: Then I bowed respectfully to the princess, circumambulated her, and prepared in my mind to return here.
106: Reflecting in her mind, she spoke once more: 'Hanumān, you must report my situation to Rāghava
107: in such a way that both heroic brothers, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, together with Sugrīva, will come here without delay.'
108: 'If it happens otherwise, my life will last only two months. Kākutstha will not see me, and I will die like an orphan.'
109: Hearing these piteous words, anger seized me, and I saw what remaining task lay immediately ahead.
110: Then my body grew like a mountain. Eager for battle, I began to destroy that grove.
111: Awakening, the grotesque-faced Rākṣasīs saw the grove shattered, its beasts and birds fleeing in panic.
112: Seeing me in that grove, they gathered from all around, hurried away, and reported to Rāvaṇa:
113: 'King of immense strength, a wicked Vānara, ignorant of your might, has laid waste your inaccessible grove!
114: King of kings, order the immediate execution of that evil-minded creature who has offended you, so that he is destroyed!'
115: Hearing this, the king of Rākṣasas dispatched the fiercely invincible Rākṣasas called Kiṅkaras, who do his bidding.
116: Eighty thousand of them, armed with tridents and clubs, were wiped out by me in that grove using an iron beam.
117: Those few survivors who escaped fled swiftly and told Rāvaṇa that their entire force had been slain by me.
118: Then the idea came to me to attack the sanctuary palace. Killing a hundred Rākṣasas stationed there with a pillar, in my fury I wrecked that crowning jewel of Laṅkā.
119: Next he dispatched Prahasta's son Jambumālī, accompanied by many hideous and terrifying Rākṣasas.
120: With that dreadful iron beam, I struck down that powerful, battle-skilled Rākṣasa along with all his followers.
121: Hearing of this, the king of Rākṣasas sent the mighty sons of his ministers,
122: supported by large infantry forces. With that very same beam, I dispatched them all to the abode of Yama.
123: Hearing that the agile sons of his ministers had fallen in battle, Rāvaṇa dispatched five heroic army commanders.
124: I wiped out every one of them along with their troops. Then Daśagrīva sent his mighty son Akṣa into battle with a host of Rākṣasas.
125: That young prince, son of Mandodarī, skilled in the art of war,
126: suddenly leapt into the sky with shield and sword; I caught him by the feet, whirled him around a hundred times, and dashed him to pieces.
127: Hearing that Akṣa had been broken and destroyed, Daśānana in his fury ordered his second son, the mighty and battle-frenzied Indrajit.
128: I shattered his entire force and sapped the strength of that bull among Rākṣasas in battle, which brought me great satisfaction.
129: For that mighty-armed, powerful warrior had come with supreme confidence,
130: dispatched by Rāvaṇa along with fierce, intoxicated warriors. But realizing I was invincible and seeing his own army crushed,
131: he swiftly bound me with the Brahmāstra, and then the Rākṣasas tied me fast with ropes.
132: Seizing me, they dragged me into Rāvaṇa's presence. Seeing me, the wicked Rāvaṇa questioned me,
133: asking why I had come to Laṅkā and why I had slaughtered the Rākṣasas. I told him plainly that everything had been done for Sītā's sake.
134: 'I came to your palace, ruler, longing to see her. I am the Vānara Hanumān, the bodily son of Māruta.
135: Know me as Rāma's envoy and minister to Sugrīva. I have come before you on Rāma's embassy.
136: Listen to the counsel I deliver to you: the king of Haris addresses these measured words to you, king of Rākṣasas.
137: The glorious Sugrīva sends you greetings of welfare — words consistent with dharma, prosperity, and desire, wholesome like nourishing food.
138: "While I was living on Mount R̥ṣyamūka with its dense trees, the battle-tested Rāghava formed a friendship with me.
139: He told me, 'King, a Rākṣasa has stolen my wife. You must make a pact to help me in this.'
140: And I said to him concerning the slaying of Vāli, 'You must make a pact to help me in this.'
141: So the master Rāghava, along with Lakṣmaṇa, made a pact of friendship with Sugrīva, who had been stripped of his kingdom by Vāli, before the sacred fire as witness.
142: Slaying Vāli in combat with a single arrow, he made Sugrīva the great king of the Vānaras and ruler of the leaping hosts.
143: We must assist him here with all our heart. In accordance with dharma, he has sent me to you.
144: Bring Sītā out at once and restore her to Rāghava, before the heroic Haris crush your army!
145: Who has not known the might of the Vānaras of old, who go even to the devatās when invited?"'
146: When I told him that the king of Vānaras said this to him, he glared at me in fury, as if burning me with his eyes.
147: Not knowing my real power, that cruel and wicked Rākṣasa Rāvaṇa ordered my execution.
148: Then his wise brother named Vibhīṣaṇa pleaded with the king of Rākṣasas on my behalf:
149: 'Not so, tiger among Rākṣasas! Abandon this resolve. You are taking a path forbidden by statecraft.
150: The slaying of an envoy is nowhere sanctioned in political treatises, Rākṣasa. An envoy must speak the truth and say what is beneficial.
151: Even for a grave offense, warrior of peerless valor, mutilation is prescribed for an envoy, but never execution according to the treatises.'
152: Advised thus by Vibhīṣaṇa, Rāvaṇa commanded the Rākṣasas: 'Let his tail be set on fire today!'
153: Hearing his command, they wrapped my tail all over with hemp bark and cotton cloths.
154: Those fierce Rākṣasas, fully prepared, set fire to my tail and beat me with wooden fists.
155: Though bound tight by many ropes and restrained by the Rākṣasas, I felt no distress, for I wanted to inspect the city by daylight.
156: Then those heroic Rākṣasas paraded me along the royal highway and brought me to the city gate, bound and wrapped in flames.
157: Then I shrank my huge form, slipped free of those bonds, and stood restored to my natural size.
158: Grabbing an iron beam, I struck down those Rākṣasas and swiftly leapt up onto the city gate.
159: With my burning tail, calm and unhurried, I torched the city with its towers and gateways, like the fire at the end of an era consuming all creatures.
160: Then fear struck me: 'Jānakī must surely have perished, for not a spot in Laṅkā can be seen unburnt — the entire city has been reduced to ashes!
161: In burning Laṅkā, I have burned Sītā without doubt; I have ruined Rāma's great purpose!'
162: As I was overwhelmed by grief and anxiety, I heard the auspicious voices of the Cāraṇas
163: speaking in wonder that Jānakī had not perished in the flames. Hearing those wondrous words, the realization dawned on me that Jānakī was unharmed, and auspicious omens confirmed it.
164: Even while my tail blazed, the fire had not burned me.
165: My heart had felt cheerful, and the breezes smelled fragrant. From these verified signs and compelling reasons,
166: and from the infallible words of the sages, my heart was overjoyed. I saw Vaidehī once more, and she gave me leave to depart.
167: Then I ascended Mount Ariṣṭa there and began my return leap, longing to see you all.
168: Traveling along that path frequented by the wind, moon, sun, Siddhas, and Gandharvas, I have reached you here.
169: By the power of Rāghava and the spiritual strength of all of you, I have accomplished everything for the service of Sugrīva.
170: I have related all this to you exactly as it happened. Now let whatever remains to be done here be carried out."
