1: Then the wise Kapi saw the radiant moon climbed to mid-sky, spreading a vast canopy of moonlight, roaming across the heavens like an impassioned bull wandering in its pen.
2: He watched the cool-rayed moon gliding on, dispelling the darkness and sorrows of the world, swelling the great ocean with its tides, and illuminating all living beings.
3: The same splendor that shines on Mount Mandara on earth, that glows upon the sea at dusk, and that rests upon the lotus in the waters, now blazed forth upon that lovely moon.
4: Like a swan resting in a cage of silver, like a lion in a cave on Mount Mandara, or like a proud hero mounted on a spirited elephant, the moon shone high in the sky.
5: With its horns full and sharp, the moon shone like a horned bull, like a towering white mountain with lofty peaks, or like an elephant with tusks capped in gold.
6: With the darkness dispelled by the bright moonrise, the night was alive with fierce flesh-eating Rākṣasas. Stirs of passion touched hearts at the sight of lovely women, and that divine night sparkled with the splendor of heaven.
7: Sweet strains of lute strings, pleasing to the ear, echoed softly as faithful wives slept beside their husbands. Meanwhile, night-stalkers of astonishing and terrifying deeds set out to revel in the night.
8: The wise Kapi observed noble households bustling with people intoxicated and carefree, packed with chariots, horses, and splendid thrones, overflowing with the glory of warriors.
9: The demons jostled and challenged one another, flinging their muscular arms about, babbling drunken talk, and hurling insults at each other in their intoxication.
10: He saw Rākṣasas puffing out their chests, draping their bodies over their lovers, while some women talked intimately with their partners and others lay asleep.
11: With trumpeting great elephants, well-honored noble warriors, and heroes breathing deeply in their might, the city shone like a deep lake filled with hissing serpents.
12: In that city he saw Yātudhānas of sharp intellect, bearing pleasing names, steadfast in loyalty, leaders among their people, possessing diverse forms and honorable titles.
13: He was amazed to see many of handsome appearance, endowed with various virtues matching their character, resplendent and noble, while seeing others of hideous and grotesque form.
14: Then he saw their noble women, worthy of the best luxuries, pure in emotion, devoted to their lovers and fond of sweet wine, gleaming with radiant beauty like stars.
15: Blazing with splendor and veiled in modest charm, some were embraced by their lovers in the middle of the night, while others were lost in passionate delight, like birds nestled in blooming blossoms.
16: The wise Kapi saw still other women sitting on rooftop terraces, resting happily in the laps of their beloveds — devoted to dharma, dear to their husbands, and overcome by deep affection.
17: Some were uncovered, glowing with the color of gold stripes; some of the finest beauty had complexions of molten gold; while others, forsaken by their lovers, were pale as the moon's mark despite their lovely forms.
18: The heroic Hari saw beautiful women in their homes, delighted to have found the lovers of their hearts, gazing with deep affection upon their charming partners.
19: He saw rows of moon-bright faces with curved eyelashes and beautiful eyes, and radiant garlands of jewelry shining like streaks of lightning.
20: But he did not see Sītā — born of a royal lineage rooted in righteousness, delicate like a flowering creeper of noble stock, exquisite in mind and spirit.
21: He did not find her who was established on the eternal path of virtue, her gaze ever fixed on Rāma, consumed by love for him, dwelling constantly in her husband's noble heart, superior to all the finest women.
22: He did not see her, tormented by sorrow with tears streaming down her throat, who once wore a precious golden necklace; her eyelashes were graceful and her voice was melodious, like a peahen dancing in the forest.
23: She was like the crescent moon obscured by clouds, like a streak of gold covered with dust, like an arrow-wound starting to scar, or like a line of clouds scattered by the wind.
24: Failing to see Sītā, the wife of Rāma, the ruler of men and foremost of eloquent speakers, the Plavaṅgama was stricken with sorrow, his spirit weighed down in despondency.
