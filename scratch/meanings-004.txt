1: After Rāma had regained his kingdom, the venerable and self-controlled sage Vālmīki composed his entire life story in rich and varied verse.
2: The sage composed twenty-four thousand verses, arranged in five hundred sargas, across six kāṇḍas as well as the Uttara Kāṇḍa.
3: Having composed the work, including future events and the Uttara Kāṇḍa, the wise master thought to himself, "Who indeed could recite and sing this poem?"
4: As the great-souled sage was reflecting on this, Kuśa and Lava, dressed in hermits' attire, came forward and touched his feet in reverence.
5: He looked upon the two brothers, Kuśa and Lava — princes living in his hermitage who knew dharma, possessed great renown, and were blessed with melodious voices.
6: Seeing that they were brilliant and thoroughly grounded in the Vedas, the master taught them the poem to illuminate and reinforce Vedic wisdom.
7: The sage of steadfast vows composed this entire epic, known as the Rāmāyaṇa, which is also titled "The Great Story of Sītā" and "The Slaying of Paulastya (Rāvaṇa)."
8: The work was sweet whether recited or sung, set to the three tempos, structured in the seven melodic modes, and tuned to the rhythm of stringed instruments.
9: The two sang this epic poem, which was rich in all the poetic rasas: the romantic, compassionate, comic, furious, fearful, heroic, and the rest.
10: Masters of the science of music, skilled in modulations and pitch registers, the melodious brothers appeared like two Gandharvas in visible form.
11: Endowed with beauty and auspicious marks, speaking in sweet tones, they seemed like two living reflections cast straight from Rāma's own form.
12: Those two blameless princes committed the entire poem to memory — this supreme narrative rooted in dharma, making every word readily obedient to their speech.
13: In gatherings of rishis, brāhmaṇas, and righteous people, those two knowers of truth sang with deep concentration exactly as they had been taught.
14: One day, those two noble, fortunate youths, endowed with every auspicious mark, stood before an assembly of seated, purified rishis and sang this epic poem.
15: Hearing it, all the sages were filled with great wonder; with eyes brimming with tears, they exclaimed to them, "Splendid! Splendid!"
16: Delighted in their hearts, those dharma-loving sages showered praise on Kuśa and Lava, who were so worthy of acclaim, as they sang.
17: "Ah, what sweetness in the singing, and especially in the verses! Though these events took place long ago, they seem to unfold right before our eyes!"
18: Entering deeply into the inner emotion of the poem, the two sang together in unison, their performance sweet, captivating, and rich in melodic perfection.
19: Praised in this way by the great rishis celebrated for their austerities, the two sang with even greater sweetness and charm.
20: One delighted sage stood up and gave them a water jar, while another illustrious hermit gladly presented them with bark garments.
21: Another sage gave a black antelope skin, and another gave a sacred thread; someone gave a water-pot, and another great sage gave a girdle of muñjā grass.
22: Another sage gave a seat made of audumbara wood, while some uttered words of blessing, wishing them well; another then gave a cushion of grass, and yet another sage gave a loincloth.
23: Another sage, overjoyed, gave them an axe; another gave an ochre robe, and yet another sage gave bark cloth.
24: One joyful sage gave a cord for tying their matted hair, another gave a rope for tying firewood, a certain rishi gave a sacrificial vessel, and another gave a bundle of firewood.
25: Other great rishis there happily blessed them with long life, and in this manner all those truth-speaking sages bestowed boons upon them.
26: "Wondrous is this narrative proclaimed by the sage! It will be the supreme foundation for poets, brought to completion in perfect order."
27: Masters of every musical style, the two sang this song that bestows long life and vitality, and is delightful to all ears.
28: As the two singers were being praised everywhere, Bharata's elder brother, Rāma, noticed them one day singing along the streets and royal avenues.
29: Inviting the two brothers, Kuśa and Lava, into his palace, Rāma, destroyer of foes, honored those two who were so worthy of respect.
30: That scorcher of foes sat upon an exquisite golden throne, surrounded by his ministers and brothers seated near him.
31: Seeing the two handsome youths holding their vīṇās, Rāma said to Lakṣmaṇa, Bharata, and Śatrughna:
32: "Listen to this narrative sung so beautifully in melodious voices by these two youths of divine radiance, rich in profound meaning and expressive words."
33: And they sang in a sweet, impassioned manner, with well-inflected and prolonged tones, perfectly aligned with the rhythm of the strings and conveying clear meaning.
34: Delighting every part of their listeners' being, their minds, and their hearts, that music, so soothing to the ears, shone brilliantly in the royal assembly.
35: "Though living as hermits of great austerity, these two youths, Kuśa and Lava, bear all the royal marks of kings. Listen closely to this glorious story they recite, which brings great well-being even to me."
36: Prompted by Rāma's words, the two sang in the classical style with flawless technique; and Rāma, seated amid his assembly, gradually became completely captivated in his heart.
