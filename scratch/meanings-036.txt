1: Once the muni had finished speaking, both heroes, Rāghava and Lakṣmaṇa, praised his story and said to the foremost of munis:
2: "What you have told us, Brāhmaṇa, is supreme and rooted in dharma. Please tell us more about the mountain king's eldest daughter.
3: "You know the full story of her divine and earthly origins. For what reason did that purifier of the worlds come to sanctify the three paths?
4: "How did Gaṅgā, foremost among rivers, become famed as Tripathagā, and through what deeds is she celebrated across the three worlds, knower of dharma?"
5: When Kākutstha asked this, Viśvāmitra, rich in tapas, recounted the entire story in full before the assembled ṛṣis:
6: "Long ago, Rāma, after his marriage, the dark-throated Śiva, master of great tapas, looked upon the goddess with longing and united with her.
7: "A hundred divine years passed for the dark-throated god, yet no son was born to them, Rāma, scorcher of foes.
8: "Then the devatās, led by Brahmā, grew deeply alarmed and asked themselves, 'If a child is born from this union, who could possibly withstand its power?'
9: "All the suras went to him, bowed low, and pleaded: 'God of gods, Mahādeva, ever devoted to the world's welfare, accept the homage of the suras and be gracious to us.
10: "'The worlds will not be able to bear your fiery tejas, foremost of the suras. Turn instead to supreme tapas and practice austerity together with the goddess.
11: "'For the good of all three worlds, hold your tejas within yourself. Protect all these realms—do not bring them to ruin!'
12: "Hearing the devatās' plea, Maheśvara, sovereign of all worlds, replied, 'So be it,' and then asked them:
13: "'I will hold back my tejas within myself, along with Umā. Let the devatās and the earth be at peace.
14: "'Still, this unsurpassed tejas of mine has already been stirred from its seat—tell me, noblest of the suras, who will receive and hold it?'
15: "Addressed in this way, the devatās answered the bull-bannered god, 'The earth will receive and hold this tejas that has been stirred.'
16: "Hearing this, the sovereign of the suras released it upon the earth, and that fiery tejas spread across the whole world with all its mountains and forests.
17: "Then the devatās turned to Agni and said, 'Enter this mighty tejas of Rudra, accompanied by Vāyu.'
18: "Pervaded in turn by Agni, that tejas formed the white Śveta mountain and a divine grove of śara reeds blazing like fire and the sun—where the supremely radiant Kārtikeya, born of Agni, came into being.
19: "Then the devatās and the hosts of ṛṣis worshipped Umā and Śiva with deep reverence and joyful hearts.
20: "At that moment, Rāma, the mountain's daughter turned to the devatās, and—filled with indignation, her eyes red with anger—she pronounced a curse on them all:
21: "'Because you interfered when I was united with my husband longing for a son, none of you will ever be able to father children with your own wives.
22: "'From this day on, may your wives remain childless.' Having cursed all the suras in this way, she cursed the earth as well:
23: "'Earth, you will take on many forms and become the wife of many kings!
24: "'Darkened by my anger, foolish earth, you will never know the joy of a son of your own, since you refused to let me have mine.'
25: "Seeing all the suras standing there abashed, the sovereign of the suras prepared to depart toward the western quarter guarded by Varuṇa.
26: "Going there, Maheśvara settled into tapas together with the goddess on the northern slope of a peak rising from the Himavat range.
27: "This, Rāma, is the full account of the mountain's daughter that I have told you. Now listen with Lakṣmaṇa as I tell you of Gaṅgā's descent."
