1: The great Ikṣvāku king Daśaratha had ministers who were distinguished by their virtues. They understood counsel, could read a person's gestures and expressions, and were always devoted to the king's welfare and wishes.
2: That heroic king had eight renowned ministers. They were pure in conduct and loyal, and they always attended to the duties of the crown.
3: They were Dhr̥ṣṭi, Jayanta, Vijaya, Siddhārtha, Arthasādhaka, Aśoka and Mantrapāla, and Sumantra was the eighth.
4: Two excellent sages, Vasiṣṭha and Vāmadeva, were his honored priests, and there were other counselors as well.
5: They were learned and well trained, modest, skilled and self-controlled. They were prosperous and noble-minded, masters of the śāstras, and firm in valor.
6: They were famous and attentive, and they carried out instructions exactly as given. They had gained splendor, patience and fame, and they always smiled warmly before speaking.
7: Not even out of anger, desire or self-interest would they say anything false. Nothing was unknown to them, whether it concerned their own people or others, whether it was being done, already done, or even only planned, because they kept watch through spies.
8: They were skilled in practical affairs and tested in friendship. When the time came, they would impose punishment even on their own sons.
9: They were diligent in filling the treasury and in maintaining the army. They would not harm even an enemy if he was innocent.
10: They were brave and always energetic, and they followed the science of kingship. They constantly protected the honest people who lived in the realm.
11: Without harming brāhmaṇas or kṣatriyas, they filled the treasury. They weighed a person's strength and weakness first, and then punished severely when needed.
12: They were honest, single-minded, and all-knowing in what they did.
13: There was no liar anywhere, in the city or in the kingdom. No one there was wicked, and no man took pleasure in another man's wife.
14: The whole kingdom and its excellent capital were peaceful. All the people were well dressed and well groomed, and all of good character.
15: For the good of the king they stayed alert, with policy as their guiding eye. They had won their guru's approval for their virtues and were famous for their courage.
16: They were known even in foreign lands, because their judgments were sound everywhere. They understood the truth about making peace and waging war, and they were naturally rich in good qualities.
17: They were able to keep counsel secret and sharp in subtle matters of reasoning. They knew the finer points of the science of statecraft and always spoke pleasantly.
18: With such ministers, rich in virtue and ability, the faultless King Daśaratha ruled the earth.
19: He watched over his people through spies and delighted them by following dharma. He protected his subjects and avoided adharma.
20: Famous in the three worlds, generous and true to his word, that tiger among men ruled this earth.
21: He found no enemy who was superior or even equal to himself. He had friends, his neighboring chiefs bowed to him, and his might had eliminated every threat. That king ruled the world as Indra, the ruler of the devatās, rules the heavens.
22: Surrounded by those ministers, who were devoted to good counsel, loyal, skilled and capable, King Daśaratha shone with splendor, like the sun rising with its radiant rays.
