1: Then those two princes, tamers of their foes, who understood place and time and knew how to speak fittingly at the right moment, said to Kauśika:
2: "Venerable sage, we wish to hear at what exact time we must guard against those two night-stalkers, so that we do not miss that moment."
3: Pleased to hear the two descendants of Kakutstha speak like this, eager for battle, all the sages praised the two princes:
4: "From today onward, Rāghavas, stand guard for six nights; this sage has entered his sacrificial vow and will now observe silence."
5: Hearing those words, the two renowned princes went without sleep for six days and nights, guarding the penance grove.
6: Those two heroic, supreme archers, tamers of foes, kept alert vigil and protected the foremost sage Viśvāmitra.
7: When that time had passed and the sixth day arrived, Rāma said to Saumitri, "Be vigilant and stay focused!"
8: Even as Rāma, eager for battle, was speaking, the sacrificial altar blazed up amidst the preceptors and priests.
9: With darbha grass, camasa cups, sruk ladles, and heaps of firewood and flowers, the altar blazed brightly alongside Viśvāmitra and his officiating priests.
10: As the sacrifice proceeded duly with Vedic mantras, a tremendous, terrifying roar suddenly broke out in the sky.
11: Just as monsoon clouds surge forward and cover the sky, the two rākṣasas came rushing in, unleashing their powers of illusion.
12: Mārīca and Subāhu, along with their followers—terrifying to look at—swooped down and poured torrents of blood over the altar.
13: Seeing the altar drenched by that flood of blood, Rāma sprang forward at once and spotted them in the sky.
14: Seeing the two of them diving down suddenly, lotus-eyed Rāma looked toward Lakṣmaṇa and said:
15: "Watch, Lakṣmaṇa! Like clouds scattered by the wind, these wicked, flesh-eating rākṣasas will be blown away by the Mānavāstra—
16: "I will make sure of it, without a doubt, though I have no wish to kill creatures like these." Saying this, swift Rāma fitted an arrow to his bow.
17: Filled with fierce anger, Rāghava released the noble, dazzling Mānavāstra straight at Mārīca's chest.
18: Struck squarely by that supreme Mānavāstra, Mārīca was hurled a full hundred yojanas away into the surging ocean.
19: Seeing Mārīca flung far away, reeling and unconscious under the force of the cooling Śīteṣu arrow, Rāma said to Lakṣmaṇa:
20: "Look, Lakṣmaṇa, at the cooling Mānavāstra, bound by dharma—it knocks him senseless and carries him off without taking his life.
21: "As for these others—ruthless, evil-doing rākṣasas steeped in sin, destroyers of sacrifices and drinkers of blood—I will slay them!"
22: Taking up the mighty Āgneyāstra, Raghunandana shot it into Subāhu's chest; pierced through, Subāhu fell dead to the ground.
23: Then, taking up the Vāyavyāstra, the illustrious and noble Rāghava struck down the remaining rākṣasas, bringing immense joy to the sages.
24: Having slain all those sacrifice-wrecking rākṣasas, Raghunandana was honored there by the ṛṣis just as Indra was of old after his victory.
25: When the sacrifice was complete, the great sage Viśvāmitra saw that every direction was free of calamity and said to Kākutstha:
26: "My purpose is fulfilled, mighty-armed Rāma! You have honored your guru's word, and you have made the name Siddhāśrama truly meaningful, illustrious one."
