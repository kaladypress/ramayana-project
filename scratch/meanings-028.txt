1: Having received those astras, the pure-hearted Kākutstha, his face beaming with joy, spoke to Viśvāmitra as they walked along:
2: "Venerable sage, now that I have received these astras, I am invincible even to the devatās. Yet, foremost of sages, I wish to learn the mantras for recalling them."
3: When Kākutstha spoke thus, the great sage Viśvāmitra—steadfast, pure, and faithful to his vows—imparted the recall mantras:
4: "Satyavant, Satyakīrti, Dhr̥ṣṭa, and Rabhasa; Pratihāratara, Parāṅmukha, and Avāṅmukha;
5: "these two—Lakṣya and Alakṣya; Dr̥ḍhanābha and Sunābhaka; Daśākṣa and Śatavaktra; Daśaśīrṣa and Śatodara;
6: "Padmanābha and Mahānābha; Dundunābha and Svanābhaka; Jyotiṣa and Śakuna; and both Nairāśya and Vimala;
7: "Yaugandhara and Vinidra; Daityapramadhana; Śucibāhu and Mahābāhu; Niṣkali and Viruci; Sārcirmālī and Dhr̥tirmālī; Vr̥ttimān and Rucira;
8: "Pitrya and Saumanasa; Vidhūta and Makara; Karavīrakara, and Dhana and Dhānya, Rāghava;
9: "Kāmarūpa and Kāmaruci; Moha and Āvaraṇa; Jr̥mbhaka, Sarpanābha, and Panthāna and Varaṇa.
10: "Receive these radiant, shape-shifting sons of Bhr̥śāśva, Rāma—may blessings be upon you, Rāghava, for you are truly worthy of them."
11: "So be it!" replied Kākutstha with an overjoyed heart, and he received that collection of mantras; and those astras too appeared before Rāma.
12: Embodied in divine, radiant forms that brought delight to see—some glowing like live embers, some like smoke, and others shining like the sun and moon—they stood bowed with palms joined in reverence.
13: With joined palms and sweet speech, they said to Rāma, "Tiger among men, here we are—command us, what can we do for you?"
14: Raghunandana told them, "Go wherever you wish; come to my mind and assist me whenever the time for action arrives."
15: Then, taking leave of Rāma and walking around him clockwise in reverence, they said to Kākutstha, "So be it," and departed the way they had come.
16: Then, at the command of that knower of Brahman, the illustrious and joyful Rāma imparted all those supreme astras and their recall mantras to Lakṣmaṇa as well.
17: Having mastered them, Rāghava walked on with the great sage Viśvāmitra and spoke these sweet, gentle words:
18: "What is that cloud-like grove of trees shining not far from the mountain? I am filled with great curiosity!
19: "It is lovely to see, teeming with deer, deeply enchanting, and adorned with many kinds of sweet-singing birds.
20: "Foremost of sages, we have emerged from that hair-raising wilderness, and I can tell by the peacefulness of this place! Venerable sage, tell me everything—whose āśrama is this?
21: "Where is that place where those wicked, evil-natured destroyers of brāhmaṇas come to obstruct the sacrifice of a great sage like you? Venerable one, where is that sacrificial ground of yours—
22: "where I must protect the rite, brāhmaṇa, and slay the rākṣasas? Foremost of sages, I wish to hear all of this."
