1: "Hearing those words of the wise Kuśanābha, the hundred daughters touched his feet with their heads and spoke:
2: "'King, the all-pervading Vāyu wanted to force himself on us; taking an evil path, he has no regard for dharma.
3: "'We told him, "Blessings upon you—we have a father and are not free to choose for ourselves. Ask our father for us, to see if he will give us to you."
4: "'Refusing to listen, that wicked-minded Vāyu struck us all down violently even as we were speaking.'
5: "Hearing their words, the supremely righteous and radiant king replied to his hundred matchless daughters:
6: "'You have shown true forbearance, my daughters, and done a great and noble thing. By standing united, you have protected the honor of our family.
7: "'Forbearance is the true ornament of both women and men. What you endured was hard to bear, especially at the hands of one of the devatās, yet every single one of you, my daughters, showed the same extraordinary restraint.
8: "'Forbearance is charity, forbearance is yajña, forbearance is truth, my daughters. Forbearance is renown, forbearance is dharma—the entire world rests upon forbearance.'
9: "After sending his daughters away, Kākutstha, that king of divine valor, wise in statecraft, consulted with his ministers about arranging their marriage to a worthy match at the right place and time.
10: "During that very time, a great sage named Cūlī—strictly celibate and pure in conduct—was practicing intense austerity devoted to Brahmā.
11: "While the ṛṣi was practicing his austerities there, a gandharvī named Somadā—the daughter of Ūrmilā, blessings upon you—served him faithfully.
12: "Humble and completely devoted to serving him, that virtuous woman stayed there for a long time, and her preceptor grew deeply pleased with her.
13: "In due course, Raghunandana, he said to her, 'I am thoroughly pleased with you—blessings upon you! What favor can I do for you?'
14: "Seeing that the sage was pleased, the sweet-voiced gandharvī—overjoyed and eloquent—spoke to that master of speech:
15: "'You are filled with the radiance of Brahmā, united with the supreme spirit, and rich in austerity. I wish for a righteous son endowed with that same spiritual power.
16: "'I have no husband, blessings upon you, and I belong to no man. Since I have approached you in pure devotion, please grant me a son through your spiritual power.'
17: "Pleased with her, the brahmarṣi granted her a matchless son—Cūlī's mind-born son, who became renowned as Brahmadatta.
18: "That King Brahmadatta ruled in the city of Kāmpilyā in supreme splendor, just as Indra, king of the devatās, rules heaven.
19: "Then, Kākutstha, the deeply righteous King Kuśanābha decided to give his hundred daughters in marriage to Brahmadatta.
20: "Inviting Brahmadatta to his court, the radiant monarch gave him all hundred daughters with a deeply gladdened heart.
21: "Then in due order, Raghunandana, King Brahmadatta took each of their hands in marriage, shining like Indra, king of the devatās.
22: "The instant he touched their hands, their hunchbacked deformities vanished and their suffering ended; all hundred daughters shone once more with supreme grace and beauty.
23: "Seeing them freed from Vāyu's affliction, King Kuśanābha was overjoyed and felt wave after wave of delight.
24: "Once the wedding was complete, King Kuśanābha sent King Brahmadatta on his way, accompanied by his brides and his company of preceptors.
25: "Somadā too was delighted by a match so worthy of her son. The gandharvī warmly welcomed her daughters-in-law as was fitting, affectionately embracing each of the brides and praising Kuśanābha."
