1: Then the spotless moon, glowing like a bed of white water lilies, traversed the clear sky like a swan gliding across deep blue water.
2: As if lending assistance with his pure radiance, the bright moon served the son of Pavana with his cool beams.
3: Then he saw Sītā, her face like the full moon, weighed down by the burden of sorrow like a heavily laden boat sinking in deep waters.
4: As he gazed intent on seeing Vaidehī, Hanumān, the son of Māruta, saw terrifying Rākṣasīs stationed close by.
5: Some had only one eye, some a single ear, some used their ears as blankets to cover themselves; some had no ears at all, some had spike-like ears, and some had nostrils that breathed out through the top of their heads.
6: Some had colossal heads set on massive bodies, while others had long, slender necks; some had disheveled matted hair, some were bald, and some wore shaggy cloaks made of hair.
7: Some had drooping ears and sagging foreheads, pendulous bellies and sagging breasts; some had hanging lips, some had lips resting on their chins, some had elongated faces, and some had sagging knees.
8: Some were short, some extraordinarily tall, some hunchbacked, some misshapen, and some were dwarfs; some were dreadful in twisted attire, with yellowish eyes and monstrous faces.
9: Grotesque, tawny, pitch-black, easily enraged, and fond of quarrels, they brandished massive spears, iron spikes, and mallets forged of black iron.
10: Some had the faces of wild boars, deer, tigers, buffaloes, goats, or jackals; others had the feet of elephants, camels, or horses, and some had heads sunken deep into their chests.
11: Some had a single hand and a single foot; some had ears like donkeys, horses, cows, elephants, or Haris.
12: Some were noseless, some had enormous noses, some had crooked noses or nostrils turned sideways; some had trunk-like noses like elephants, and some had nostrils breathing out from their foreheads.
13: Some had the feet of elephants, giant feet, cattle hooves, or hairy tufts upon their feet; some had monstrous heads and necks, and enormous breasts and bellies.
14: Some had cavernous mouths and bulging eyes, long projecting tongues and claws; some had faces like goats, elephants, cows, or sows.
15: Others had the faces of horses, camels, and donkeys—these dreadful Rākṣasīs held spears and mallets in hand, fierce-tempered and delighting in strife.
16: Fierce, with smoke-colored hair and deformed features, these female demons were constantly drinking liquor, ever craving meat and strong spirits.
17: Their bodies were smeared with raw flesh and blood, their food was meat and blood; the Kapīśreṣṭha looked upon these hideous creatures whose very sight made the hair stand on end.
18: They sat in a circle guarding a massive, thick-trunked tree, beneath which sat that divine, faultless princess.
19: The glorious Hanumān observed Janaka's daughter, faded in luster, scorched by grief, her hair unwashed and matted.
20: She looked like a fallen star dropped to the earth when its store of merit was spent; rich in virtue and renowned character, she was destitute only in being denied the sight of her husband.
21: Stripped of fine ornaments, her only adornment was her unwavering love for her husband; imprisoned by the king of Rākṣasas, she was severed from all her kin.
22: Like a female elephant separated from her herd and held captive by a lion, or like the crescent moon veiled by autumn clouds when the rainy season ends,
23: neglected and worn from lack of care like an untuned vīṇā, Sītā, devoted solely to her husband's welfare, sat unsuitably trapped under the power of Rākṣasas.
24: Submerged in an ocean of grief in the heart of the Aśoka grove, she was surrounded by those monsters like the star Rohiṇī besieged by malefic planets.
25: Hanumān beheld that devī resembling a flowering creeper stripped of its blossoms; her form was soiled with dirt yet graced by natural elegance, like a mud-smeared lotus stalk that shines and yet seems dimmed.
26: Wrapped in a single soiled and frayed garment, that noble lady with fawn-like eyes was observed by the Kapi Hanumān.
27: Though her face was woeful, that devī remained undefeated through her husband's spiritual luster; protected by her own stainless character, dark-eyed Sītā sat firm.
28: Seeing Sītā with eyes like a young fawn, glancing fearfully all around like a startled doe,
29: scorching the leafy trees with her hot sighs, seeming like an embodiment of sorrows or a rising crest of grief,
30: slender, exquisitely formed, radiating natural beauty even without ornaments, Māruti experienced boundless joy upon beholding Maithilī.
31: Tears of joy streamed from Hanumān's eyes as he beheld that enchanting-eyed princess, and in that moment he offered his reverent salutations to Rāghava.
32: Having bowed in mind to Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, the valiant Hanumān, ecstatic at having seen Sītā, remained quietly hidden within the tree.
