1: When Viṣṇu had resolved to become the son of that great-souled king, Daśaratha, the revered Brahmā spoke to all the devatās:
2: "Viṣṇu is heroic, true to his pledge, and ever seeks our welfare. Bring forth mighty companions for him, able to take any form at will.
3: Create heroes skilled in magic, as swift as the wind, wise in statecraft, full of intelligence, and equal to Viṣṇu in valor.
4: Create beings who cannot be overcome, who know the right means to every end, whose bodies are divine, and who are gifted in the use of every weapon, as if they had drunk the nectar of immortality.
5: Among the foremost Apsarasas, in the forms of Gandharvīs, among the daughters of Yakṣas and Pannagas, among Ṛkṣa women and Vidyādharīs, among Kinnarīs and female Vānaras, create sons in the form of Haris, sons equal to you in valor.
6: I created Jāmbavān, the bull among bears, long ago. He was born suddenly from my mouth as I yawned."
7: Having heard this command from the blessed Brahmā, they accepted it and fathered sons in the form of Vānaras.
8: The great-souled ṛṣis, Siddhas, Vidyādharas, Uragas, and Cāraṇas also fathered brave sons who roamed the forest.
9: Indra fathered Vālin, the Vānara king, who shone like Mahendra himself. Tapana, the Sun, best of those who give heat, fathered Sugrīva.
10: Bṛhaspati fathered the great Kapi named Tāra, the wisest and most excellent among all the chief Vānaras.
11: The glorious Vānara Gandhamādana was the son of Dhanada, Kubera. Viśvakarmā fathered the great Kapi named Nala.
12: The glorious Nīla was the son of Pāvaka, Agni, and shone with a brilliance like fire. In splendor, fame, and valor he surpassed the other Vānaras.
13: The two Aśvins, rich in beauty and admired for it, themselves fathered Mainda and Dvivida.
14: Varuṇa fathered the Vānara named Suṣeṇa. The mighty Parjanya fathered Śarabha.
15: The glorious Vānara named Hanumān was the true son of Māruta. His body was as hard as a thunderbolt, and he was as swift as Garuḍa, son of Vinatā. Among all the chief Vānaras he was wise and strong.
16: Many thousands of them were created, all ready to destroy Daśagrīva.
17: They were heroes of immeasurable strength and courage, able to take any form. They were huge and mighty, like mountains of elephants. Bears, Vānaras, and langurs were all born in an instant.
18: Whatever form, dress, and valor each devatā had, the sons born to each were exactly the same, one by one.
19: Some were born to langur females, and some, slightly greater in valor, were born to bear females and to Kinnarīs. Vānaras were born in this way.
20: The glorious devatās, maharṣis, Gandharvas, Tārkṣyas, and Yakṣas, along with Nāgas, Kimpuruṣas, Siddhas, Vidyādharas, and Uragas,
21: many of them, delighted, fathered thousands of Vānaras there. All were huge in body and all of them roamed the forest.
22: They were born then to the foremost Apsarasas, to Vidyādharīs, to Nāga women, and to Gandharvīs.
23: They had strength and the power to take any form, and they roamed wherever they pleased. In pride and in strength they were like lions and tigers.
24: All of them fought with rocks, and all fought with trees. Claws and fangs were their weapons, and all of them knew every kind of weapon.
25: They could shake the greatest mountains and uproot the firmest trees. With their speed they could churn the ocean, the king of rivers.
26: They could tear the earth open with their feet and leap across the great sea. They could enter the sky and even seize the clouds.
27: They could seize wild elephants in rut as they wandered the forest. Roaring, they could bring birds down from the sky with their cry.
28: Of such Haris, able to take any form, hundreds of hundreds of thousands were born, as leaders of great-souled troops.
29: Among the foremost troops of Haris, those Hariyūthapas became the best of troop leaders, and they in turn fathered brave Haris.
30: Some went to the Ṛkṣavān mountain in their thousands. Others settled on many different mountains and in the forests.
31: All those Hariyūthapas served the two brothers, Sugrīva, the son of the Sun, and Vālin, the son of Indra.
32: They served Nala, Nīla, Hanumān, and the other Hariyūthapas. All of them had strength like Tārkṣya's and were skilled in battle. As they roamed, they struck down lions, tigers, and great serpents.
35: Mighty-armed Vālin, of vast valor, protected all of them, bears, langurs, and Vānaras, with the strength of his arms.
36: By those heroes, in their many shapes and with their many kinds of marks and features, this earth with its mountains, forests, and oceans was filled.
37: Surrounded by those mighty leaders of Vānara troops, who looked like masses of clouds and peaks of mountains and whose bodies were fearsome, the earth was covered, all for the sake of helping Rāma.
