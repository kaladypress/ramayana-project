1: "When that god of gods had departed, Rāma, Bhagīratha stood pressing the earth with just the tip of his big toe and worshiped Śiva for a full year.
2: "With his arms raised, unsupported and without shelter, subsisting on air alone, he stood motionless like a tree-stump day and night, Rāma, subduer of foes.
3: "Then, when a full year was complete, Paśupati—the consort of Umā, revered by all the worlds—spoke these words to the king:
4: "'I am pleased with you, foremost among men, and I will fulfill your wish—I will hold the daughter of the mountain-king upon my head.'
5: "Then Gaṅgā, eldest daughter of Himavān and revered by all the worlds, assumed a colossal form and an irresistible force, and plunged from the sky, Rāma, onto the auspicious head of Śiva.
6: "And that goddess Gaṅgā, supremely hard to contain, thought to herself, 'I will sweep Śaṅkara along in my current and plunge straight into Pātāla.'
7: "Perceiving her arrogance, the divine Hara grew angry, and the three-eyed god resolved to make her vanish completely.
8: "Sacred as she was, she fell upon the sacred head of Rudra—into the deep, Himavān-like labyrinth of his coiled jaṭās, Rāma.
9: "Despite all her efforts, she could not reach the earth at all—she found no way out from within that tangle of jaṭās.
10: "The goddess wandered round and round inside his jaṭās for many years. Seeing her held there, Bhagīratha once again undertook supreme tapas.
11: "Deeply pleased by his tapas, Raghunandana, Hara then released Gaṅgā toward the sacred lake Bindusaras.
12: "As she was released, seven streams emerged from her: Hlādinī, Pāvanī, and Nalinī—
13: "those three auspicious, pure-watered streams of Gaṅgā flowed eastward; while Sucakṣu, Sītā, and the mighty river Sindhu—
14: "these three pure-watered streams flowed westward, while the seventh of them followed behind Bhagīratha's chariot.
15: "Mounted upon his divine chariot, the radiant rājarṣi Bhagīratha rode ahead, and Gaṅgā followed closely in his path.
16: "Descending from the sky onto Śaṅkara's head and from there onto the earth, her waters surged forward with a thunderous roar.
17: "With shoals of fish, tortoises, and river-dolphins tumbling down and already landed in the waters, the earth shone with wonder.
18: "Then devarṣis, Gandharvas, Yakṣas, and hosts of Siddhas gathered there and watched as she descended from the sky to the earth.
19: "Riding city-sized vimānas, swift horses, and noble elephants—and hovering eagerly in midair—the devatās assembled there.
20: "Eager to witness that supreme, most wondrous descent of Gaṅgā in all the world, hosts of devatās of boundless radiance converged upon the scene.
21: "With the thronging hosts of suras and the brilliance of their ornaments, the cloudless sky blazed as though lit by a hundred suns.
22: "With darting river-dolphins, serpents, and shimmering fish, the sky looked as though streaked with flashes of lightning.
23: "As pale-white plumes of spray scattered in a thousand directions, the sky appeared filled with autumn clouds and flocks of soaring swans.
24: "In one place she rushed swiftly forward, in another she wound in curves or spread wide; here she plunged down, there she surged upward, and elsewhere she flowed gently on.
25: "In places, wave clashed against wave, leaping high into the air again and again before crashing back onto the earth.
26: "Having poured down from Śaṅkara's head onto the earth below, those pure, stainless waters shone with radiant beauty.
27: "There, the r̥ṣis, Gandharvas, and inhabitants of the earth touched those waters, revering them as sanctified by contact with Śiva's form.
28: "And those who had fallen from the heavens to the earth under a curse bathed in those waters and were freed from every stain.
29: "Their sins washed away by that water and their merit restored, they rose back into the sky and returned to their own worlds.
30: "Overjoyed by those resplendent waters, the whole world rejoiced; bathing in Gaṅgā, all beings were freed from weariness and distress.
31: "Mounted upon his divine chariot, the radiant rājarṣi Bhagīratha drove on ahead, while Gaṅgā followed close behind him.
32: "All the devatās with the hosts of r̥ṣis, as well as Daityas, Dānavas, Rākṣasas, foremost Gandharvas and Yakṣas, Kinnaras, and Mahoragas—
33: "serpents and Apsarases, Rāma, and all creatures of the water—joyfully followed Gaṅgā as she trailed Bhagīratha's chariot.
34: "Wherever King Bhagīratha led, there went the illustrious Gaṅgā—foremost of all rivers and destroyer of every sin.
35: "Along the way, Gaṅgā flooded the yajña-enclosure of the noble-souled Jahnu, a sage of wondrous deeds who was then performing a yajña.
36: "Seeing her arrogance, Jahnu grew angry, Rāghava, and in an astonishing feat he drank up all the waters of Gaṅgā.
37: "Utterly amazed, the devatās, Gandharvas, and r̥ṣis paid homage to the noble-souled Jahnu, foremost among men.
38: "And they offered Gaṅgā to the great-souled sage as his daughter; pleased by this, the radiant, mighty sage released her through his ears.
39: "That is why Gaṅgā is known as Jahnu's daughter and called Jāhnavī. Once more, Gaṅgā flowed onward behind Bhagīratha's chariot.
40: "Reaching the ocean at last, that foremost of rivers entered Rasātala to bring Bhagīratha's mission to completion.
41: "Having guided Gaṅgā there with such tireless effort, the rājarṣi Bhagīratha saw his ancestors reduced to ashes and was nearly overcome with grief.
42: "Then the supreme waters of Gaṅgā flooded those mounds of ashes, and, purified of all sin, those ancestors attained svarga, Raghūttama.
