1: After killing the Kiṅkaras, Hanumān stopped to think: "I've wrecked the grove, but I haven't destroyed the Caitya palace.
2-3: So I'll tear down this palace too." With that thought, Hanumān, best of the Haris, decided to show his strength. He leapt onto the Caitya palace, which rose high like the peak of Meru, and climbed up it — Hanumān, son of Māruta.
4: Having climbed the palace, which looked like a mountain, Hanumān, leader of the Hari troops, shone with immense splendor, like a second sun risen in the sky.
5: Hanumān, unstoppable, overpowered the towering Caitya palace. Blazing with radiance, he stood there like the Pāriyātra mountain.
6: Then Hanumān, son of Māruta, grew to a huge size. He slapped his arms boldly and filled all of Laṅkā with the sound.
7: That loud, ear-splitting slap made birds fall from the sky. Then he shouted these words in a booming voice:
8: "Victory to Rāma, the master of weapons, and to mighty Lakṣmaṇa! Victory to King Sugrīva, who is protected by Rāghava!
9: I am the servant of Rāma, king of Kosala, whose deeds are never tired or tainted. I am Hanumān, son of Māruta, destroyer of enemy armies.
10: Even a thousand Rāvaṇas could not match me in battle, as I strike with rocks and with thousands of trees.
11: I will crush the city of Laṅkā, bow to Maithilī, and leave with my purpose fulfilled, while all the Rākṣasas look on."
12: Having said this from atop the palace, Hanumān, bull among the Plavaṅgamas, roared in a terrifying voice that struck fear into the Rākṣasas.
13-14: At that great roar, a hundred guards of the Caitya palace rushed out. They carried many kinds of weapons: spears, swords and axes. With fierce eyes they hurled them and surrounded Māruti, striking with colorful maces and iron clubs studded with gold.
15: They struck the best of the Vānaras with arrows that blazed like the sun. They circled him like a huge, swirling whirlpool in the waters of the Gaṅgā.
16: The mass of Rākṣasas, closing in around Hariśreṣṭha, looked magnificent. Then Vātātmaja flew into a rage and took on a terrifying form.
17-18: Hanumān, son of Māruta, tore a huge pillar decorated with gold out of that palace in one forceful pull. Mighty Hanumān whirled it around, a hundred edges flashing. Fire broke out, and the palace began to burn.
19: Seeing the palace burning, Hanumān, leader of the Hari troops, killed those hundred Rākṣasas, as Indra kills Asuras with his thunderbolt. Then, radiant in the sky, he spoke these words:
20-21: "Thousands of great and powerful Vānara chiefs like me have been sent out, living under Sugrīva's command. We and many other Vānaras are roaming the whole earth.
22: Some have the strength of ten elephants, some ten times more. Some match a thousand elephants in valor. Some are as strong as a flood, and some as strong as the wind. Others among those Hari leaders have strength beyond measure.
24: Surrounded by Haris like these, armed with teeth and claws, in hundreds, thousands, lakhs, crores and tens of thousands,
25: Sugrīva will come, and he will be the end of every one of you. This city of Laṅkā will not remain, nor will you, nor Rāvaṇa — because the great ruler of the Ikṣvākus has declared this feud."
