1: When Hanumān, Mārutātmaja, had told this whole story, he went on to speak further.
2: "Sītā's virtue is intact. Because of that, Rāghava's effort and Sugrīva's urgency have borne fruit, and so has my great leap across the ocean.
3: "You bulls among the Plavagas, noble Sītā's character is worthy of her. By the power of her tapas she could hold up the worlds, or, if she became angry, she could burn them to ashes.
4: "In every way, Rāvaṇa, the king of the Rākṣasas, must have built up enormous tapas. Otherwise her tapas would have destroyed him when he touched her body.
5: "Even a tongue of flame, touched by the hand, could not do what Janaka's daughter could do once her mind was clouded by anger.
6: "The task has now come this far, and I have reported it to you. After taking leave of all the great Vānaras led by Jāmbavān, it would be right for us to go and see those two princes, and to bring Vaidehī with us.
7: "Even alone, I am enough to destroy, by sheer force, that city of Laṅkā with its hordes of Rākṣasas, and the mighty Rāvaṇa too.
8: "How much more so when I have heroes like you at my side, Plavagas who are brave, strong, self-disciplined, skilled in weapons, and eager for victory!
9: "In battle I will kill Rāvaṇa along with his army, his generals, his sons, and his brothers.
10: "Even if Śakrajit uses the Brāhma, Aindra, Raudra, Vāyavya, and Vāruṇa astras, which are hard even to look at in battle,
11: "I will cut them down and I will kill the Rākṣasas. If you give me permission, my strength will hold him back.
12: "An unbroken, unmatched shower of rocks hurled by me could kill even the devatās in battle. What chance would those night-roamers have?
13: "The ocean might overflow its shore, and even Mount Mandara might move, but no enemy army could make Jāmbavān tremble in battle.
14: "The heroic Kapi, Vāli's son Aṅgada, is enough by himself to wipe out all the hosts of Rākṣasas, and all the Rākṣasas who came before them too.
15: "The tremendous force of Panasa and of the great Nīla could shatter even Mount Mandara. What, then, of Rākṣasas in battle?
16: "Among the devatās, Asuras, Yakṣas, Gandharvas, Nāgas, and birds, name anyone who could stand up to Mainda or Dvivida in battle!
17: "These two, the noblest of Plavagas and the greatly blessed sons of the Aśvins — I see no one on the battlefield who could fight them.
18: "Proud of a boon from Pitāmaha, these two finest of all the Vānaras are full of supreme confidence. They have even drunk amṛta.
19: "Long ago, to honor the Aśvins, Brahmā, the grandfather of all the worlds, granted these two an unequaled boon: no one can kill them.
20: "Intoxicated by pride in that boon, these two heroic Plavaṅgamas crushed the great army of the devatās and drank their amṛta.
21: "These two alone, once enraged, could destroy Laṅkā with its horses, chariots, and elephants. All the other Vānaras could simply stand by.
22: "In fact, I have already struck Laṅkā down. I burned it and reduced it to ashes. And on the royal roads, everywhere, I announced my name:
23: "'Victory to Rāma of immense strength, and to the mighty Lakṣmaṇa! Victory to King Sugrīva, who is protected by Rāghava!
24: "'I am the servant of the king of Kosala, born of Pavana, and my name is Hanumān!' This is what I proclaimed everywhere.
25: "In the middle of the Aśoka grove of the evil Rāvaṇa, at the foot of a Śiṁśapā tree, the virtuous lady sits in a pitiful state.
26: "Surrounded by Rākṣasīs and worn away by grief and anguish, she looks like a crescent moon dimmed by a ring of clouds.
27: "Vaidehī pays no attention to Rāvaṇa, who is drunk on his own power. Jānakī, the graceful one, is devoted to her husband and stands firm.
28: "The noble Vaidehī loves Rāma with her whole being. Her mind rests on Rāma alone, just as Paulomī's rests on Purandara.
29: "Sītā wears a single garment and is covered in dust. Her body is wasted by grief and anguish, and her heart is set on her husband's welfare. I saw her among the Rākṣasīs, who threatened her again and again.
30: "I saw that young woman in the grove, surrounded by hideous Rākṣasīs. She wears her hair in a single braid, she is miserable, and she thinks only of her husband.
31: "She sleeps on the ground. Her body has lost its color, like a lotus pond at the onset of winter. She has turned away from Rāvaṇa completely and has made up her mind to die.
32: "Somehow I managed to win the trust of the doe-eyed one. Then I spoke with her and explained everything to her.
33: "When she heard about the friendship between Rāma and Sugrīva, she was overjoyed. Her conduct is disciplined, and her devotion to her husband is supreme.
34: "That noble lady does not kill Daśagrīva herself, though she could. Rāma will only be the instrument of his death.
35: "She is slender by nature, and separation from Rāma has worn her down even further. She has grown thin, like the learning of a student who studies on the pratipad day, when study is forbidden.
36: "This is how the greatly blessed Sītā is living, completely given over to grief. Whatever needs to be done about this, let us arrange all of it."
