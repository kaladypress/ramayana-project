1: Having narrated that story to Rāma in sweet, measured words, Viśvāmitra spoke to him again, saying:
2: "Long ago, there was a brave king, ruler of Ayodhyā, named Sagara. Though devoted to dharma and longing for children, he had no offspring.
3: "Rāma, the daughter of the king of Vidarbha, Keśinī by name, was Sagara's senior queen—deeply virtuous and truthful.
4: "The daughter of Ariṣṭanemi, unmatched on earth in beauty, was Sagara's second queen, known as Sumati.
5: "Together with both his queens, the king went to the Himavān and practiced severe tapas on the mountain peak Bhr̥guprasravaṇa.
6: "Then, when a full hundred years had passed, the muni Bhr̥gu—foremost among the truthful—pleased by their tapas, granted Sagara a boon:
7: "'You will gain great blessings of offspring, sinless one, and you will win unmatched renown in the world, foremost among men.
8: "'One of your queens, dear king, will give birth to a son who will carry on your line; the other will give birth to sixty thousand sons.'
9: "As he spoke, the two queens bowed humbly before that tiger among men and, deeply delighted, joined their palms in añjali and asked:
10: "'Which of us will have the single son, Brāhmaṇa, and which will bear the many? We wish to hear this, Brāhmaṇa—may your words come true.'
11: "Hearing their words, the supremely righteous Bhr̥gu spoke this noble reply: 'Let your own choice decide it here.
12: "'Either one son who will carry on the line, or many sons of great strength, renown, and boundless energy—which of you chooses which boon?'
13: "Hearing the muni's words, Raghunandana, Keśinī chose the one son who would carry on the line, Rāma, there in the king's presence.
14: "Then Sumati, sister of Suparṇa, chose the sixty thousand sons of boundless energy and renown.
15: "Having circumambulated the r̥ṣi and bowed low with his head to the ground, the king returned to his own city with his wives, Raghunandana.
16: "Then, in due course, the senior queen Keśinī gave birth to Sagara's son, known by the name Asamañja.
17: "As for Sumati, tiger among men, she gave birth to a gourd-like embryo; when the gourd split open, sixty thousand sons emerged from it.
18: "Nurses tended and raised them in jars filled with ghee, and in the fullness of time, all of them grew into youth.
19: "Thus, after a long time, Sagara's sixty thousand sons stood in the prime of beauty and youth.
20: "Meanwhile, Sagara's eldest son, best of men, would seize little children and hurl them into the waters of the Sarayū, Raghunandana, laughing aloud as he watched them drown.
21: "Bent on harming the citizens, he was banished from the city by his father.
22: "That Asamañja had a valiant son named Aṁśumān, esteemed by all the world and kind-spoken to everyone.
23: "Then, after a long time, best of men, a firm resolve arose in Sagara's mind: 'I will perform a great sacrifice.'
24: "Having made this decision, the king—well versed in the Vedas—together with his assembly of officiating priests, began the preparations for the sacred sacrifice."
