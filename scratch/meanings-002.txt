1: Having crossed the unassailable ocean, the immensely powerful hero stood composed and looked upon Laṅkā perched on the peak of Mount Trikūṭa.
2: As the valiant one paused there, showered by a rain of blossoms shed by the trees, he shone as if formed entirely of flowers.
3: Though the glorious Kapi of supreme prowess had traversed a hundred yojanas, he was not even out of breath, nor did he feel any fatigue.
4: "I could easily cross many hundreds of yojanas, so what is this ocean whose span measures a mere hundred yojanas?"
5: Foremost among the mighty and greatest of those who leap, endowed with tremendous speed, he pressed on toward Laṅkā after crossing the vast ocean.
6: He passed through dark green meadows, fragrant woods, rocky foothills, and hill ranges.
7: The radiant Hanumān, bull among the Plavagas, strode across tree-covered hills and flowering forest groves.
8: Standing upon that mountain, the son of Pavana looked upon the forests, the pleasure gardens, and the city of Laṅkā perched on the mountain peak.
9: He saw Sarala, Karṇikāra, and Kharjūra trees in full bloom, along with Priyāla, Muculinda, Kuṭaja, and Ketaka trees.
10: He saw fragrant Priyaṅgu, Nīpa, and Saptacchada trees, as well as Asana, Kovidāra, and blossoming Karavīra trees.
11: The trees were heavy with loads of blossoms or covered with tender buds, crowded with birds, their crests swaying gently in the wind.
12: He saw stepped pools covered with lotuses and blue water-lilies, alive with swans and Kāraṇḍavas, alongside lovely pleasure grounds and various reservoirs of water.
13: The Kapikuñjara surveyed the charming gardens filled with diverse trees bearing flowers and fruits in all seasons.
14: Approaching Laṅkā, ruled by Rāvaṇa, the glorious hero saw it encircled by defensive moats adorned with white lotuses and blue water-lilies.
15: Because of his abduction of Sītā, Rāvaṇa had fortified it securely, with fierce Rākṣasa archers constantly patrolling on every side.
16: The beautiful great city was enclosed by a golden rampart and filled with sky-high mansions resembling heavenly bodies and pale autumn clouds.
17: It was lined with broad, gleaming white avenues, crowded with hundreds of watchtowers, and draped with rows of flags and banners.
18: With its splendid golden gateways carved with rows of flowering creepers, Hanumān saw Laṅkā looking like the celestial city of the devatās in heaven.
19: Perched on the mountain crest with its gleaming white mansions, Laṅkā appeared to the glorious Kapi like a city suspended in the sky.
20: Built by Viśvakarmā and ruled by the king of the Rākṣasas, the city seemed to Hanumān as if floating in mid-air.
21: Its earthworks and ramparts were like hips, the vast surrounding waters like a new garment, its spiked catapults and tridents like locks of hair, and its watchtowers like ear-ornaments.
22: Laṅkā seemed fashioned purely out of thought by Viśvakarmā; having reached its northern gate, the Vānara paused in contemplation.
23: Rising like the peak of Mount Kailāsa, scraping the firmament and soaring into the sky with its towering, magnificent palaces,
24: teeming with fierce Rākṣasas like Bhogavatī with venomous serpents, it was unimaginably grand, flawlessly built, beautifully planned, and once inhabited by Kubera.
25: It was guarded by numerous fierce and valiant fanged Rākṣasas bearing spears and battleaxes, like a cave guarded by deadly vipers.
26: Surveying its massive fortifications, the vast ocean, and the dread enemy Rāvaṇa, the Vānara thought to himself:
27: "Even if the Haris manage to reach here, their coming will be in vain, for Laṅkā cannot be conquered in battle even by the devatās.
28: Even if the mighty-armed Rāghava reaches this perilous, fortress-like city ruled by Rāvaṇa, what can he achieve?
29: Among these Rākṣasas there is no scope for gentle persuasion, no room for bribery, no possibility of sowing division, and no chance of victory through open war.
30: Only four of the great Vānaras can make the crossing here: Vāli's son Aṅgada, Nīla, our wise king Sugrīva, and myself.
31: First let me find out whether Vaidehī is still alive or not; once I have seen Janaka's daughter, I will consider what to do."
32: Standing upon that mountain peak, the Kapikuñjara, devoted to Rāma's welfare, reflected for a while:
33: "In this present form, it is impossible for me to enter the city of the Rākṣasas, guarded by cruel and mighty demons.
34: These Rākṣasas possess fierce energy, immense prowess, and great physical strength; I must slip past all of them while searching for Jānakī.
35: To accomplish this great mission, the right moment has come for me to enter the city of Laṅkā by night in an unnoticeable form."
36: Looking upon that city, unassailable even to gods and demons, Hanumān sighed repeatedly and thought:
37: "By what means can I see Maithilī, Janaka's daughter, without being seen by the wicked Rāvaṇa, king of the Rākṣasas?
38: How can the mission of the wise Rāma remain safe from ruin? How can I meet Janaka's daughter alone in seclusion?
39: Even well-conceived plans come to grief when undertaken out of season and place, or when handled by an indecisive envoy, just as darkness vanishes at sunrise.
40: Even a firm intellect falters in discerning between gain and loss when envoys merely fancy themselves clever; such messengers ruin their own missions.
41: How can this mission remain unharmed? How can I avoid making a blunder? And how can my leap across the ocean not go to waste?
42: If the Rākṣasas catch sight of me, this mission of the wise Rāma, who seeks Rāvaṇa's destruction, will end in failure.
43: It is impossible to remain anywhere here unnoticed by the Rākṣasas, even in the disguise of a Rākṣasa — let alone in any other form!
44: I think even the wind cannot move here undetected, for nothing escapes the notice of these formidable Rākṣasas.
45: If I stay here in my natural form, I will meet destruction, and my master's objective will be lost.
46: Therefore, shrinking my form to a tiny size under cover of night, I will enter Laṅkā for the success of Rāghava's mission.
47: Slipping by night into this impenetrable city of Rāvaṇa, I will search through every mansion until I see Janaka's daughter."
48: Having resolved on this, the heroic Kapi Hanumān, eager to see Vaidehī, waited for sunset.
49: When the sun had set and night fell, Māruti shrank his body to the size of a cat, presenting an astonishing sight.
50: At dusk, the valiant Hanumān leaped up swiftly and entered the charming city with its well-proportioned, broad avenues.
51: With its rows of mansions supported by pillars of gold and silver and adorned with golden lattice windows, it looked like a city of the Gandharvas.
52: He saw that great city filled with seven- and eight-storied mansions whose floors were inlaid with crystal and embellished with gold.
53: The mansions of the Rākṣasas shone resplendent with floors inlaid with cat's-eye gems and decorated with networks of pearls.
54: The ornate golden gateway arches of the Rākṣasas illuminated the splendidly adorned city of Laṅkā on all sides.
55: Seeing Laṅkā, unfathomable and wondrous in form, the Mahākapi felt both dejection and wonder, yet remained eager to find Vaidehī.
56: He saw the glorious city ringed with towering white palaces, fitted with costly golden lattice-work arches, guarded by the mighty arms of Rāvaṇa, and held by night-roaming demons of terrifying strength.
57: As if offering him help, the moon rose with thousands of rays, spreading a canopy of moonlight over the earth and shining brightly in the midst of the star-strewn sky.
58: The heroic Kapipravīra saw the radiant moon ascending, gleaming like a white conch shell and pale as milk or lotus stalks, swimming through the heavens like a swan upon a lake.
