1: Having heard the whole story — rooted in dharma and full of dharma — from Nārada, the sage whose soul was devoted to dharma set out to search more clearly for everything that had happened in the life of the wise Rāma.
2: The sage sipped water in the proper way, sat down with folded palms on darbha grass laid with its tips pointing east, and through the power of his dharma and yogic vision sought out the course of those events.
3: He saw it all: Rāma's birth, his great valor, his kindness toward everyone, how dear he was to the world, his patience, his gentleness, and his truthful nature.
4: He saw the many other wonderful stories from the time Rāma spent with Viśvāmitra, Jānakī's marriage, and the breaking of the great bow.
5: He saw the dispute between Rāma and Rāma (Paraśurāma), the virtues of Dāśarathi, the preparations for Rāma's coronation, and Kaikeyī's wicked intent.
6: He saw the coronation blocked, Rāma sent into exile, the king's grief and lament, and his passing to the other world.
7: He saw the sorrow of the people, Rāma sending the citizens back, his conversation with the Niṣāda chief (Guha), and the return of the charioteer (Sumantra).
8: He saw the crossing of the Gaṅgā, the meeting with Bharadvāja, and, with Bharadvāja's permission, the arrival at Citrakūṭa.
9: He saw the building of the dwelling there, Bharata's arrival, his pleading with Rāma to return, and the water rites offered for their father.
10: He saw the coronation of the sandals, Bharata's life at Nandigrāma, Rāma's journey into the Daṇḍaka forest, and his meeting with Sutīkṣṇa.
11: He saw Sītā's time with Anasūyā and the gift of the fragrant body-paste (aṅgarāga), the conversation with Śūrpaṇakhā, and her disfigurement.
12: He saw the killing of Khara and Triśiras, Rāvaṇa rising into action, the killing of Mārīca, and the abduction of Vaidehī.
13: He saw Rāghava's lament, the death of the king of vultures (Jaṭāyu), the encounter with Kabandha, and the sight of Pampā.
14: He saw the meeting with Śabarī, the meeting with Hanumān, and the lament of the great-souled Rāghava at Pampā.
15: He saw the journey to Ṛṣyamūka, the meeting with Sugrīva, the building of trust, their friendship, and the fight between Vāli and Sugrīva.
16: He saw Vāli struck down, Sugrīva placed on the throne, Tārā's lament, and the stay through the nights of the rainy season.
17: He saw the anger of Rāghava, that lion among men, the gathering of the forces, the sending out of search parties in every direction, and the description of the earth.
18: He saw the giving of the ring, the discovery of the Ṛkṣa's cave, the Vānaras' vow to fast to death, and the meeting with Sampāti.
19: He saw the climb up the mountain, the leap across the ocean, the entry into Laṅkā by night, and Hanumān's anxious thinking there, all alone.
20: He saw Hanumān go into the drinking hall, see the women's quarters, go to the Aśoka grove, and find Sītā.
21: He saw the giving of the token of recognition, Sītā's words, the threats of the Rākṣasīs, and Trijaṭā's dream.
22: He saw Sītā giving the jewel, the breaking of the trees, the Rākṣasīs fleeing, and the slaughter of the Kiṅkaras.
23: He saw the capture of Vāyusūnu (Hanumān), his roaring as Laṅkā burned, his leap back across the ocean, and the raid on the honey.
24: He saw Rāghava being comforted, the handing over of the jewel, the encounter with the Ocean, and the building of Nala's bridge.
25: He saw the crossing of the ocean, the siege of Laṅkā by night, the alliance with Vibhīṣaṇa, and his disclosure of the way to kill Rāvaṇa.
26: He saw the death of Kumbhakarṇa, the killing of Meghanāda, the destruction of Rāvaṇa, and the recovery of Sītā in the enemy's city.
27: He saw Vibhīṣaṇa's coronation, the sight of the Puṣpaka, the journey to Ayodhyā, and the reunion with Bharata.
28: He saw the joyful celebration of Rāma's coronation, the sending home of all the armies, Rāma delighting his own kingdom, and the sending away of Vaidehī.
29: And whatever else of Rāma's life on earth was still to come — the revered sage Vālmīki set that down in the later part of the poem (the Uttara).
