1: Gathering his inner courage, the wise Hanumān, son of Māruta, stood poised upon the towering crest of Mount Lamba, which loomed like a dark, hanging rain cloud.
2: Under the shroud of night, that hero of immense strength, the Kapikuñjara, entered Laṅkā, the city guarded by Rāvaṇa, rich in delightful forests and sparkling water reservoirs.
3: It was adorned with palaces gleaming like autumn clouds, its deep murmur rivaled the roar of the ocean, and it was constantly caressed by the ocean breeze.
4: Well-defended by thriving legions of warriors, it rivaled Kubera's city Viṭapāvatī, embellished with graceful turrets, arched entries, and pale-white gateways.
5: Frequented and guarded by serpent-like warriors, it looked like the subterranean Bhogavatī, surrounded by lightning-filled clouds and touching the stellar pathways of the sky.
6: Echoing with the whistle of fierce winds, it resembled Indra's Amarāvatī, encircled completely by a massive rampart of molten gold.
7: Decked with fluttering banners chiming with nets of tiny bells, the rampart was approached swiftly by Hanumān, whose heart thrilled with joy.
8: Filled with wonder at heart, he surveyed the city on all sides, gazing at gates wrought of refined gold with daises carved of lapis lazuli.
9: The floors were paved with mosaics of gems, crystal, and pearls, while turrets of heated gold stood alongside walls gleaming with spotless white silver.
10: Its staircases of cat's-eye gems were strewn with glittering crystal dust, leading to magnificent quadrangles that seemed to leap upward into the sky.
11: Resounding with the calls of krauñca birds and peacocks and graced by royal swans, the city echoed in every quarter with musical instruments and the chime of jewelry.
12: Seeing Laṅkā, which rivaled Kubera's celestial capital Vasvokasārā and seemed to soar into the heavens, the Kapi Hanumān rejoiced.
13: Observing that splendid city of the Rākṣasa king, endowed with unsurpassed prosperity, the valiant hero reflected within himself:
14: "This city cannot be taken by assault by any ordinary force, defended as it is by Rāvaṇa's troops with their weapons perpetually raised!
15: This fortress might be accessible only to Kumuda, Aṅgada, the great Kapi Suṣeṇa, or the two brothers Mainda and Dvivida.
16: It could be entered only by the son of Vivasvān, the Hari Kuśaparvan, the bear chieftain Ketumāla, and myself."
17: Yet, reflecting upon the immense prowess of mighty-armed Rāghava and the valor of Lakṣmaṇa, the Kapi felt heartened and confident.
18: He looked upon that prosperous city as upon an adorned woman: dressed in robes of jewels, with storehouses for ear-ornaments and armories for her breasts.
19: The great Kapi saw that capital of the Rākṣasa king, where darkness was banished by bright torches and glowing mansions.
20: Venturing inside under cover of night, full of resolve, the son of Māruta stepped onto the royal highway strewn with pearls and flowers.
21: Filled with bursts of laughter, preceded by festive music, and lined with grand houses adorned with diamond lattices and motifs resembling lightning bolts, that lovely city shone like the sky veiled in clouds.
22: Laṅkā sparkled with the splendid mansions of the Rākṣasas, resembling white clouds, built in lotus, svastika, and vardhamāna designs, and richly decorated on all sides.
23: Roaming for the sake of Rāghava, that glorious hero devoted to the welfare of the king of Kapis observed the city arrayed with colorful garlands and ornaments, and rejoiced.
24: He heard sweet songs spanning the three registers sung by women in their revelry, sounding like singing Apsarās in heaven.
25: He heard the tinkling of waistbands, the chiming of anklets, and the footsteps on staircases in the palaces of the nobles, along with the arm-clapping and martial roars of warriors here and there.
26: He saw Yātudhānas engaged in sacred Vedic recitation, as well as fierce Rākṣasas roaring out praises in honor of Rāvaṇa.
27: He saw a massive army of Rākṣasas occupying the royal highway, and stationed at the central outpost, numerous sentries and scouts of the king.
28: He saw consecrated initiates—some with matted locks, some shaven-headed, some clad in deerskins and ox-hides—bearing handfuls of darbha grass as weapons and holding fire-vessels.
29: He saw guards armed with iron mallets and clubs, some with a single eye or multiple ears, and some with sagging, pendulous bellies.
30: He saw dreadful guards with twisted faces, misshapen bodies, and dwarfish statures, standing alongside archers, swordsmen, warriors armed with śataghnīs, pestles, and massive iron bars, all gleaming in ornate armor.
31: He saw warriors neither too stout nor too thin, neither excessively tall nor short—some grotesque, some shapeshifters, and others handsome and of radiant vitality.
32: The great Kapi saw warriors carrying javelins, uprooted trees, spears, thunderbolt-like weapons, slings, and nooses in their hands.
33: Wearing garlands, anointed with sandalwood paste, adorned with fine ornaments, those mighty warriors carried sharp spears and thunderbolt weapons.
34: Past them, the great Kapi observed the central garrison: a hundred thousand alert and disciplined troops stationed securely behind inner ramparts.
35: Resembling heaven itself, the palace resounded with celestial music, the neighing of spirited horses, and the ceaseless chiming of ornaments.
36: It was filled with war chariots, palanquins, flying vimānas, noble horses and elephants, including magnificent four-tusked elephants that looked like heaps of white clouds.
37: Passing through lovely gateways adorned with cheerful animals and birds, the Kapi slipped quietly into the guarded palace of the Rākṣasa king.
