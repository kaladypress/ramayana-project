1: Then Hanumān went to Jānakī, who was sitting at the foot of the Śiṁśapā tree. He bowed to her and said, "Thank heaven I see you here unharmed."
2: As he was about to leave, Sītā looked at him again and again and spoke to Hanumān, her words full of love for her husband.
3: "Dear one, sinless one, if you think it right, stay here for one day. Rest in some well-hidden place and leave tomorrow.
4: Vānara, unlucky as I am, having you near me might ease this boundless grief of mine, even if only for a moment.
5: Once you leave, Hariśārdūla, best of Vānaras, I can't be sure I'll even stay alive until you come back.
6: Hero, not seeing you again will torment me. I've gone from sorrow to deeper sorrow, and I'm already low in spirit and worn down by grief.
7: But, mighty hero, one great doubt stands before me about your allies, the Haris and Ṛkṣas.
8: How will those armies of Haris and Ṛkṣas, or those two sons of the best of men, ever cross this ocean that is so hard to cross?
9: Only three beings have the power to leap across this ocean: Vainateya, you, or Māruta, the wind.
10: So, with this task facing such a hard obstacle, what solution do you see? You're the one who knows how to get things done.
11: Of course, slayer of enemy heroes, you alone are enough to accomplish this task, and the strength that rises in you brings you glory.
12: But if Kākutstha, crusher of enemy armies, were to cover Laṅkā with his arrows and take me away himself, that would be worthy of him.
13: So make sure it happens in a way that matches the valor of that great-souled hero of battle."
14: Hearing her words, full of meaning, gentle, and well reasoned, Hanumān gave her his reply.
15: "Devī, Sugrīva, lord of the armies of Haris and Ṛkṣas and best among the leapers, is full of courage and has firmly resolved to act for your sake.
16: Vaidehī, Sugrīva, king of the Plavagas, will come here quickly, surrounded by thousands of crores of Vānaras.
17: And those two heroes, the best of men, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, will come here together to the city of Laṅkā and shatter it with their arrows.
18: Before long, Raghunandana will kill Rāvaṇa along with all his followers, and then, graceful one, he will take you back with him to his own city.
19: Take heart, and may all be well with you. Wait for the right time. Soon you will see Rāvaṇa killed by Rāma in battle.
20: Once the king of the Rākṣasas has been killed, along with his sons, ministers, and relatives, you will be reunited with Rāma, just as Rohiṇī is with the moon.
21: Kākutstha will come soon, surrounded by the foremost of the Haris and Ṛkṣas. He will defeat his enemies in battle and take away your grief."
22: Having comforted Vaidehī in this way, Hanumān, son of Māruta, made up his mind to leave and bowed to Vaidehī.
23: He had killed the leading Rākṣasas, made his own name known, comforted Vaidehī, and shown his supreme strength.
24: He had thrown the city into chaos, outwitted Rāvaṇa, displayed his fearsome power, and bowed to Vaidehī.
25: Now he decided to go back across the middle of the ocean. Then that Kapiśārdūla, eager to see his master,
26: climbed Ariṣṭa, the finest of mountains — he, the crusher of enemies. The mountain was covered with dark-blue stretches of forest, thick with tall Padmaka trees.
27: The clouds hanging between its peaks looked like an upper cloth draped over it. The bright rays of the sun seemed to be waking it gently, with affection.
28: Its streaks of minerals, like eyes, seemed to be opening. With the deep roar of its rushing waters, the mountain seemed to be reciting the Vedas.
29: With the clear sounds of its many springs, it seemed to be singing. With its towering Devadāru trees, it seemed to stand with its arms raised high.
30-31: With the thunder of its waterfalls, it seemed to be crying out on every side. With its dark autumn forests swaying, it seemed to tremble. With its Kīcaka bamboos whistling in the wind, it seemed to be humming. With its fearsome, deadly serpents, it seemed to be hissing in anger.
32: With its caves made deep and dim by mist, it seemed lost in meditation. With its foothills, like the feet of clouds, it seemed to be striding out in every direction.
33: With its cloud-wreathed peaks, it seemed to be yawning into the sky. It was beautiful with summits scattered everywhere and with many caves.
34: It was surrounded by Sāla, Tāla, and Aśvakarṇa trees and many bamboos, and adorned with wide canopies of flowering creepers.
35: It was crowded with herds of many kinds of animals, adorned with flowing streams of minerals, full of springs, and packed with heaps of rock.
36: Maharṣis, Yakṣas, Gandharvas, Kinnaras, and Uragas visited it. It was dense with creepers and trees, and lions filled its caves.
37: Tigers roamed it in packs, and its trees bore sweet roots and fruits. That was the mountain the immensely strong Plavagottama climbed, driven by joy and eager to see Rāma quickly.
38: As his feet pressed down on its lovely slopes, the rocks crumbled with a roar and were ground to powder.
39: Having climbed that king of mountains, the great Kapi grew in size, longing to reach the northern shore of the salt ocean from the southern one.
40: From the top of the mountain, the hero, son of Pavana, looked out at the fearsome ocean, home to fish and serpents.
41: Like the wind itself moving through the sky, Hariśārdūla, born of Māruta, set out from the south toward the north.
42: At that moment, the great mountain, pressed down by the Kapi,
43: roared along with the creatures living on it and sank into the earth, its peaks shaking and its trees crashing down.
44: Shaken by the force of his thighs, the flowering trees broke and fell to the ground, as if struck by Śakra's thunderbolt.
45: The terrifying roar of the mighty lions, crushed inside the hollows of the caves, was heard tearing through the sky.
46: Vidyādharīs suddenly flew up from the mountain, their clothes slipping and in disarray, their ornaments jumbled.
47: Huge, powerful serpents with fiery, flickering tongues and deadly venom writhed, their heads and necks crushed.
48: Kinnaras, Uragas, Gandharvas, Yakṣas, and Vidyādharas also abandoned that great, crushed mountain and took to the sky.
49: That splendid mountain, crushed by the mighty Hanumān, sank down into Rasātala along with its trees and tall peaks.
50: Ten yojanas wide and thirty yojanas high, the mountain was flattened level with the ground.
51: Wanting to leap across the fearsome salt ocean, whose shores were lashed by crashing waves, the Hari sprang up into the sky.
