1: Hanumān, the best of the Haris and a bull among Vānaras, said to them, "Vānaras, enjoy the honey without a care."
2: Hearing Hanumān's words, Aṅgada, the foremost of the Haris, replied in good spirits, "Let the Haris drink the honey.
3: Hanumān has accomplished his mission, so I must follow his word even if it means doing something improper — let alone something like this."
4: Hearing these words from Aṅgada's mouth, the bulls among Vānaras were thrilled. "Well said! Well said!" they cried, and they honored him.
5: After honoring Aṅgada, the bull among Vānaras, all the Vānaras rushed to Madhuvana like the current of a river.
6-7: Overjoyed, they forced their way past the guards of Madhuvana. Bold because they had been given free rein, and because they had seen and heard of Maithilī, they all drank the honey then and took the juicy fruits.
8: Then they all leapt up and ganged up on the forest guards who were blocking them in Madhuvana, and beat them by the hundreds.
9: Many of them grabbed honeycombs as big as a droṇa measure. Some used them to strike the guards together, while others ate them.
10: Some, honey-tawny in color, drank the honey and then threw the combs away. Some, wild with excitement, pelted one another with the leftover wax.
11: Others sat at the foot of the trees holding on to a branch. Completely worn out by intoxication, they spread leaves on the ground and lay down to sleep.
12: The Plavagas, drunk on honey, behaved like madmen in their glee. Some shoved one another, while others stumbled about.
13: Some let out loud roars, some warbled happily. Some of the Haris, drunk on honey, fell asleep on the ground.
14: Some did something and laughed while others did the opposite. Some did something and talked about it, while others thought up something else.
15: Even the honey-guards who were there, Dadhimukha's servants, were driven off by those fearsome Vānaras and fled in all directions.
16: They were dragged by their knees and tossed up into the sky, the path of the devatās. Deeply shaken, they went to Dadhimukha and said:
17: "Given free rein by Hanumān, they have wrecked Madhuvana by force. They dragged us by our knees and flung us up into the sky."
18: Then Dadhimukha, the Vānara guardian of the forest, was furious to hear that Madhuvana had been wrecked. He calmed those Haris down and said:
19: "Come, let's go. We will use force to stop these arrogant Vānaras who are eating the honey."
20: Hearing Dadhimukha's words, those heroic bulls among Vānaras went back to Madhuvana with him.
21: Dadhimukha, in their midst, picked up a huge tree and charged forward at full speed, and all those Plavaṅgamas charged with him.
22: The Vānaras seized rocks, trees, and boulders, and in their anger they advanced to where those Kapikuñjaras were.
23: Taking their master's command to heart, the heroes charged swiftly, armed with śāla trees, palmyra palms, and rocks.
24: Those heroic guards, in their thousands, attacked the Vānaras who were swollen with pride in their strength, whether perched in the trees or standing on the ground.
25: Then the bulls among Vānaras, led by Hanumān, saw that Dadhimukha was angry and rushed at him at full speed.
26: As that mighty, strong-armed Vānara came charging with a tree in hand, Aṅgada lost his temper and struck his own elder with both arms.
27: Blinded by drink, Aṅgada did not recognize him as his own elder kinsman. He quickly and violently pinned him down and pounded him against the ground.
28: His arms broken, his face turned away, shaken and soaked in blood, that heroic Kapikuñjara suddenly fainted for a moment.
29: The king's uncle quickly came to, and in a rage he drove back the honey-dazed Vānaras with a staff.
30: When that bull among Vānaras had somehow got free of the Vānaras, he went to a quiet spot and spoke to his servants who had gathered there:
31: "Let them stay here. We will go to where our master, the broad-necked Vānara Sugrīva, is staying with Rāma.
32: I will tell the king all about Aṅgada's offense. When he hears it, he will be furious and have these Vānaras killed.
33: This Madhuvana is dear to the great-souled Sugrīva. It is a divine forest, inherited from his father and grandfather, and even the devatās cannot easily enter it.
34: Sugrīva will punish all these honey-greedy Vānaras — their lives are as good as over — and have them killed along with their friends.
35: These wicked ones deserve to die, since they have defied the king's command. Our anger, born of outrage, will not go unanswered."
36: After saying this to the forest guards, the mighty Dadhimukha suddenly leapt up into the sky and set off with them.
37: In just the blink of an eye, that forest-dweller reached the place where the wise Vānara Sugrīva, son of the thousand-rayed Sun, was.
38: Seeing Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa, and Sugrīva, he dropped from the sky onto the level ground.
39: Having landed, the mighty Hari Dadhimukha, supreme chief of the guards, stood surrounded by all those guards.
40: With a sorrowful face and his palms joined above his head, he bowed his head down to Sugrīva's sacred feet.
