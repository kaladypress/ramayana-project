1: "Having reached the ocean with Gaṅgā following close behind him, that king entered the depths of the earth where his ancestors had been reduced to ashes.
2: "Then, Rāma, once those ashes were drenched by the waters of Gaṅgā, Brahmā, ruler of all the worlds, spoke these words to the king:
3: "'Tiger among men, the sixty thousand sons of the noble-souled Sagara have been delivered and have ascended to heaven like the devatās.
4: "'As long as the waters of the ocean remain in this world, king, so long will Sagara's sons stay in svarga like the devatās.
5: "'And this Gaṅgā will be regarded as your eldest daughter, and she will be celebrated throughout the world by a name derived from yours.
6: "'She will be known as the divine Gaṅgā, as Tripathagā, and as Bhāgīrathī—since she sanctifies the three paths as she flows, she is remembered as Tripathagā.
7: "'Ruler of men, perform the water-rites here for all your ancestors, king, and bring your vow to completion.
8: "'Even your illustrious forebear Sagara, foremost among the righteous, could not fulfill this cherished wish in his time, king.
9: "'Nor could Aṁśumān, dear child, whose radiance had no equal in the world, complete the vow though he strove to bring Gaṅgā down.
10: "'Even by that virtuous rājarṣi, equal in radiance to the maharṣis, whose tapas matched even mine, and who stood steadfast in kṣatriya-dharma—
11: "'your father Dilīpa of surpassing brilliance, greatly fortunate and sinless one—she still could not be brought down, though he too prayed to lead Gaṅgā here.
12: "'Foremost among men, you have carried that vow to completion, and you have won supreme, universally honored renown in the world.
13: "'And through this descent of Gaṅgā that you have accomplished, subduer of foes, you have become a great repository of dharma.
14: "'Bathe yourself in these ever-auspicious waters, best of men, tiger among men—become purified and enjoy the fruits of your merit.
15: "'Perform the water-rites for all your ancestors. May all be well with you—I will now return to my own realm, and you too may return home, king.'
16: "Having spoken thus, the illustrious sovereign of the devatās, grandsire of all the worlds, returned to devaloka just as he had come.
17: "The illustrious rājarṣi Bhagīratha then performed the sacred water-rites for Sagara's sons in due order and according to sacred custom—
18: "and having completed the offerings in purity, the king returned to his capital and, with his purpose fulfilled, Rāma, foremost among men, ruled over his kingdom.
19: "The people rejoiced to have their king back, Rāghava—freed from sorrow, thriving in prosperity, and rid of every distress.
20: "This, Rāma, is the full account of Gaṅgā that I have narrated to you. May blessings and well-being be yours—the hour for evening sandhyā is slipping away.
21: "Sacred, fame-bestowing, granting long life, svarga, and progeny—such is this narrative of Gaṅgā's descent that I have recounted.
22: "Both ancestors and devatās are delighted with the one who, with an attentive mind, listens to this auspicious story of Gaṅgā's descent—
23: "such a listener, Kākutstha, attains every desire; all sins are washed away, and both lifespan and renown grow ever greater."
