1: "When those sons of hers were slain, Rāma, Diti was overcome with deep grief and spoke these words to her husband, Kaśyapa, son of Marīci:
2: "'Revered one, my sons have been slain by your mighty sons. I desire a son who will slay Śakra, earned through long tapas.
3: "'I will practice tapas for this; please grant me a child—please permit me to have such a son, a slayer of Śakra.'
4: "Hearing those words of hers, the deeply radiant Kaśyapa, son of Marīci, replied to the heartbroken Diti:
5: "'So be it—may all be well with you. Keep yourself pure, rich in tapas; you will give birth to a son who will slay Śakra in battle.
6: "'If you remain pure until a full thousand years are complete, you will bear from me a son who will destroy the three worlds.'
7: "Having spoken thus, that sage of great radiance gently stroked her with his hand. After touching her in blessing, he said, 'May all be well,' and departed to practice tapas.
8: "Once he had departed, foremost of men, Diti was filled with supreme joy; reaching Kuśaplavana, she practiced exceedingly fierce tapas.
9: "While she was performing that tapas, the thousand-eyed Indra attended upon her, foremost of men, with the utmost care and excellence of conduct.
10: "The thousand-eyed Indra brought her fire, kuśa grass, firewood, water, as well as fruits and roots, and whatever else she desired.
11: "By massaging her body and relieving her fatigue, Śakra served Diti at all times.
12: "Then, when only ten years remained of the thousand years, Raghunandana, Diti was deeply pleased and spoke to the thousand-eyed Indra:
13: "'Foremost of the suras, when I entreated your noble-souled father, he granted me a boon for a son at the end of a thousand years.
14: "'Of my tapas, only ten years remain, best of the valorous. Blessings upon you—after that, you will see your brother.
15: "'For your sake, my son, I will win him over, eager for victory though he will be, and together with him, my son, you will enjoy sovereignty over the three worlds, free from all worry.'
16: "Having spoken thus to Śakra, when the sun reached midday, the lady Diti was overcome by sleep and lay down with her feet placed where her head belonged.
17: "Seeing her in that state of impurity, with her hair at her feet and her feet in the place of her head, Śakra laughed and rejoiced.
18: "Then Purandara entered the cavity of her body, Rāma, and with great self-mastery split the embryo into seven parts.
19: "As the embryo was split by the hundred-jointed vajra, it cried out loudly, Rāma, and at that sound Diti woke up.
20: "'Do not weep! Do not weep!' Śakra said to the embryo, yet even as it wept, the mighty Vāsava kept splitting it.
21: "'Do not kill him! Do not kill him!' Diti cried out, and out of respect for his mother's words, Śakra emerged from her body.
22: "Standing with palms joined in añjali and holding the vajra, Śakra said to Diti: 'Lady, you fell asleep in an impure state, with your hair at your feet.
23: "'Seizing that opportunity, lady, I split into seven parts the one who would have slain Śakra in battle. Please forgive me for this.'"
