1: Proceeding northeast with Saumitri, Rāma placed Viśvāmitra in front and arrived at the yajña enclosure.
2: Rāma, together with Lakṣmaṇa, said to the foremost of munis: "Truly grand is the abundance of the great-souled Janaka's yajña!
3: "Blessed sage, there are many thousands of brāhmaṇas here from various lands, all devoted to Vedic study.
4: "Revered sage, the encampments of the r̥ṣis are crowded with hundreds of carts; please choose a spot where we may stay."
5: Hearing Rāma's words, the great muni Viśvāmitra made their camp in a secluded spot well supplied with water.
6: Upon hearing that Viśvāmitra, the foremost of munis, had arrived, the king came forward placing his blameless purohita Śatānanda at the head.
7: The great-souled r̥tvik priests also quickly brought arghya and offered it to Viśvāmitra in accordance with dharma, accompanied by mantras.
8: Accepting that honor from the great-souled Janaka, Viśvāmitra inquired after the king's well-being and the smooth progress of the yajña.
9: After inquiring after the munis as well, along with the upādhyāyas and the purohita, he greeted them all with joy in the proper manner.
10: Then the king joined his palms in añjali and said to the foremost of munis: "Revered one, please take your seat together with these eminent munis."
11: Hearing Janaka's words, the great muni sat down, and the purohita, the r̥tvik priests, and the king along with his counselors also took their seats.
12: Seeing them all seated in proper order on every side, the king spoke to Viśvāmitra:
13: "Today the devatās have made the preparations for my yajña bear fruit; today, through being blessed with your darśana, I have already gained the reward of the yajña.
14: "Foremost of munis, I am blessed and deeply favored that you have come to my yajña hall accompanied by these munis.
15: "Great brahmarṣi Kauśika, the learned priests say that twelve days of the rite remain; after that, you will see the devatās arrive to claim their shares."
16: Having spoken thus to the foremost of munis with a joyful face, the devout king joined his palms in añjali and asked him earnestly once more:
17: "May all be well with you! These two young princes have the valor of devatās, the stride of elephants and lions, and the bearing of tigers and bulls—both true heroes.
18: "With large eyes like lotus petals, bearing swords, quivers, and bows, they resemble the Aśvins in beauty and have just reached the threshold of youth.
19: "Muni, how is it that these two, who look like immortals come down to earth from Devaloka of their own accord, have arrived here on foot? For what purpose have they come, and whose sons are they?
20: "Great muni, whose sons are these two heroes bearing fine weapons, gracing this land just as the moon and the sun adorn the sky?
21: "Matching one another in stature, expression, and bearing, these two heroes wear the side-locks of youth; I wish to hear the truth about them."
22: Hearing the words of the great-souled Janaka, Viśvāmitra introduced them as the two great-souled sons of Daśaratha.
23: He recounted their stay at Siddhāśrama and the slaying of the rākṣasas, their unhurried journey, and their visit to Viśālā,
24: as well as their meeting with Ahalyā, the reunion with Gautama, and their coming here to learn about and see the great bow.
25: After relating all of this to the great-souled Janaka, the deeply radiant great muni Viśvāmitra fell silent.
