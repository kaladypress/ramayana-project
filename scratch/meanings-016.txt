1: Then Nārāyaṇa, Viṣṇu, entreated by the foremost of the devas, spoke gently to them, though he already knew everything:
2: "Devas, what is the way to kill that king of the Rākṣasas? Tell me the means by which I can destroy that tormentor of the ṛṣis."
3: Asked this, all the devas replied to the imperishable Viṣṇu: "Take on a human body and kill Rāvaṇa in battle.
4: Subduer of enemies, he performed fierce tapas for a very long time. That pleased Brahmā, the creator of the worlds, who is worshipped by all the worlds.
5: Pleased, the mighty Brahmā gave that Rākṣasa a boon: he would have no fear from beings of every kind, except from humans.
6: Proud of the boon he received from the Grandfather, he is destroying the three worlds and even carrying off women.
7: When he asked for that boon, he looked down on humans and left them out. So, scorcher of foes, his death can come only from humans."
8: Hearing these words of the devas, the self-possessed Viṣṇu chose King Daśaratha to be his father.
9: Having made his decision and taken leave of the Grandfather, Viṣṇu vanished from sight, honored by the devas and the great ṛṣis.
10: At that time the radiant King Daśaratha, slayer of enemies, who had no son, was performing the Putrakāmeṣṭi yajña for sons, longing to have children.
11: Then, as the king was performing the yajña, a great being of unmatched splendor, tremendous power, and great strength rose up out of the sacred fire.
12: He was dark, dressed in red, with a red face and a voice like the boom of a dundubhi drum. The hair on his body, his beard, and the fine hair on his head were glossy, like a lion's mane.
13: He bore auspicious marks and was adorned with divine ornaments. He was as tall as a mountain peak, with the prowess of a proud tiger.
14: He shone like the sun and blazed like the flames of a fire. He carried a large vessel made of refined Jāmbūnada gold, with a lid edged in silver,
15: full of divine pāyasa. He held it in both arms, close to him, like a beloved wife, as though it were divine and auspicious itself.
16: Looking at King Daśaratha, he said: "King, know that I am a being sent by Prajāpati, and I have come here to you."
17: Then the king, with folded hands, replied: "Welcome, Bhagavān! What is my duty? What must I do?"
18: The divine being sent by Prajāpati spoke again: "O King, because the devas are pleased with your yajñas, you have received this today.
19: O King, tiger among men, accept this pāyasa. It bestows children, confers blessings, and enhances health.
20: Give it to your worthy wives and tell them to eat it. Through them you will have the sons for whom you are performing this yajña."
21: "So be it," said the delighted king. Bowing his head in reverence, he accepted the golden vessel, given by the devas and filled with divine food.
22: He bowed to that wonderful being, so pleasing to look at, and, filled with great joy, walked around him in reverence.
23: Then Daśaratha, having received the pāyasa made by the devas, was overjoyed, like a poor man who suddenly comes into wealth.
24: Then that wondrous, brilliantly shining being, having finished his task, vanished right there.
25: The king's inner palace glowed with rays of joy, the way the sky glows with the rays of the lovely autumn moon.
26: Entering the inner palace, he said to Queen Kausalyā and his other wives: "Accept this pāyasa. It will give you sons."
27: The king then gave half of the pāyasa to Kausalyā. Of the remaining half, he gave half to Sumitrā.
28: For the sake of having sons, he gave half of what remained to Kaikeyī; and the rest of that nectar-like pāyasa he gave—
29: after thinking it over, once again to Sumitrā. In this way, the king gave the pāyasa to each of his wives separately.
30: When the king's noble wives received this pāyasa, they all felt deeply honored, and their hearts were filled with joy.
31: Then those noble women, each having eaten her share of the king's excellent pāyasa, shone with a splendor like that of fire and the sun, and before long they conceived.
32: Seeing that his wives had conceived, the king felt his heart's wish fulfilled. He was as joyful as Viṣṇu in heaven, who is adored by Indra, the Siddhas, and the hosts of ṛṣis.
