1: Having praised Sītā, who is worthy of praise, and Rāma, delightful in his virtues, that foremost among Haris again became lost in thought.
2: Reflecting for a moment with eyes swimming in tears, the radiant Hanumān lamented over Sītā's plight:
3: "If even Sītā, who is revered by elders, beloved to her family, and respected by the disciplined Lakṣmaṇa, is stricken with sorrow, then Time is truly insurmountable!
4: Knowing the resolve of Rāma and the wise Lakṣmaṇa, this devī does not lose her composure entirely, just as the Gaṅgā holds steady even with the arrival of the rains.
5: Rāghava is truly worthy of Vaidehī, who matches him in character, age, conduct, lineage, and auspicious marks; and this dark-eyed lady is equally worthy of him."
6: Gazing upon her, who possessed the luster of freshly smelted gold and resembled Goddess Lakṣmī, cherished by all the world, he approached Rāma in his thoughts and said:
7: "It was for the sake of this wide-eyed lady that the mighty Vālī was slain, and Kabandha, whose prowess rivaled Rāvaṇa's, was brought down.
8: In the forest, the terribly valiant Rākṣasa Virādha was struck down in combat by Rāma's valor, just as the demon Śambara was slain by mighty Indra.
9: Fourteen thousand Rākṣasas of dread deeds were destroyed in Janasthāna by his arrows that blazed like tongues of fire.
10: Khara was slain in battle, Triśiras was struck down, and the formidable Dūṣaṇa was killed by the wise and self-mastered Rāma.
11: It was on her account that Sugrīva attained the kingship of the Vānaras, so hard to win, formerly guarded by Vālī and revered by the world.
12: It is for the sake of this wide-eyed lady that I crossed the glorious ocean, king of rivers and streams, and searched this entire city.
13: If Rāma were to turn the entire ocean-girdled earth or even the whole universe upside down for her sake, I believe it would be entirely justified!
14: If sovereignty over the three worlds were weighed against Sītā, the daughter of Janaka, the empire of all three worlds combined would not equal a single fraction of her worth.
15: This is she: the daughter of the noble and virtuous King Janaka of Mithilā, Sītā, unshakeable in her devotion to her husband.
16: She who arose cleaving through the soil when the field was furrowed by the plow's blade, dusted with the auspicious, lotus-pollen-like soil of the field.
17: This renowned princess is the eldest daughter-in-law of King Daśaratha, a hero of noble character who never turned back in battle.
18: This is the cherished wife of the righteous, grateful, and self-realized Rāma, now fallen under the power of Rākṣasīs!
19: Driven by the power of love for her husband, she renounced all royal comforts, disregarded every hardship, and entered the uninhabited forest.
20: Content with fruits and roots and devoted to attending upon her husband, she experienced the greatest joy in the forest, just as if she were living in a palace.
21: That lady of golden complexion, who always speaks with a sweet smile and never deserved misfortune, is now enduring this terrible agony.
22: Rāghava longs to see this woman of flawless conduct, who is now ravaged by Rāvaṇa, just as a thirsty traveler longs for a ruined water-station.
23: Upon regaining her, Rāghava will surely feel the same joy that a deposed king feels when he reclaims his realm.
24: Forsaking pleasures and comforts, cut off from her kin, she preserves her life only out of her deep yearning to be reunited with him.
25: She does not even notice these Rākṣasīs, nor these trees laden with flowers and fruits; with a singularly focused heart, she sees only Rāma.
26: A husband is truly a woman's greatest ornament, surpassing all other jewels; deprived of him, this lady, though worthy of radiant beauty, does not shine.
27: Rāma, that capable master, achieves an impossible feat by sustaining his life and refusing to sink under grief despite being separated from her.
28: Seeing this lady of dark tresses and lotus-petal eyes, who deserves only happiness yet suffers so bitterly, even my mind is shaken with grief.
29: She whose forbearance matches the earth and whose eyes are like lotuses, who was once protected by Rāghava and Lakṣmaṇa, is now watched over beneath a tree by hideous-eyed Rākṣasīs.
30: Like a lotus blighted by frost, its beauty withered, tormented by continuous calamities like a female cakravāka separated from her mate, the daughter of Janaka has fallen into a pitiful state.
31: These aśoka trees, their branch tips heavy with flowers, and the moon rising with thousands of cool beams as winter passes, only deepen her intense grief."
32: Reflecting upon all this, his intellect firmly resolved that this was indeed Sītā, that mighty and swift hero, the bull among Haris, remained perched within that tree.
