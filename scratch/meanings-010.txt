1: Looking all around, Hanumān saw a supreme, bed-chamber platform made of crystal and adorned with gems, resembling something divine.
2: It was furnished with costly and splendid seats adorned with carved ivory, gold, and cat's-eye gems, and covered with priceless carpets.
3: In one part of it, he saw a bright white umbrella decorated with fine garlands, shining like the full moon.
4: He saw a magnificent couch inlaid with pure gold, blazing with the brilliance of fire, draped with garlands of aśoka blossoms.
5: It was fanned on all sides by women holding yak-tail whisks, scented with diverse perfumes, and fragrant with the smoke of rich incense.
6: It was spread with luxurious coverings, draped with soft sheepskin and deer-pelt rugs, and adorned on all sides with strands of choice garlands.
7: Reclining upon it, dark as a rain cloud, wearing glowing earrings, red-eyed, mighty-armed, dressed in gold-embroidered robes,
8: his body anointed with fragrant red sandalwood, looking like a cloud reddened by twilight in the sky accompanied by flashes of lightning,
9: adorned with divine ornaments, handsome and capable of changing forms at will, appearing like Mount Mandara asleep with all its trees, forests, and thickets,
10: having finished his nocturnal revels, decked in fine ornaments, dear to the Rākṣasa maidens and the giver of joy to all Rākṣasas,
11: having drunk his fill and fallen asleep on that radiant couch, the heroic king of Rākṣasas was seen by the great Kapi.
12: Coming near Rāvaṇa as he hissed in his sleep like a venomous serpent, the bull among Vānaras felt deep apprehension and stepped back like one afraid.
13: Then, climbing the steps and taking shelter on another elevated dais, the great Kapi observed that sleeping tiger among Rākṣasas.
14: That splendid couch looked magnificent with the sleeping king of Rākṣasas upon it, like a vast plateau of Mount Prasravaṇa with a lordly scent-elephant resting upon it.
15: He saw the outstretched arms of that mighty king of Rākṣasas, bound with golden armlets and resembling the banner-poles of Indra.
16: They bore scars made by the thrusts of Airāvata's tusk-tips, their thick shoulders were furrowed by the thunderbolt of Indra, and marked by the blows of Viṣṇu's discus.
17: Well-proportioned and thick-set, symmetrical and powerful, with auspicious nails, thumbs, and fingers marked with clear lines,
18: massive as iron bars, rounded like the trunks of elephants, those arms lay flung across the white couch like two five-headed serpents.
19: They were richly anointed with the finest, cooling red sandalwood paste, crimson as hare's blood, and adorned with elegant ornaments.
20: Caressed by noble women, fragrant with choice perfumes, those arms had made Yakṣas, Pannagas, Gandharvas, devas, and Dānavas weep in terror.
21: The Kapi saw his arms resting on the bed like two great venomous serpents asleep in a gorge of Mount Mandara.
22: With those two powerful arms, the king of Rākṣasas looked magnificent like a mountain, resembling Mount Mandara with its two lofty peaks.
23: Fragrant with mango and punnāga blossoms, combined with the scent of choice bakula flowers, carrying the aroma of delicious food and the fragrance of fine wines,
24: the breath came forth from that sleeping lion of a Rākṣasa's wide mouth, seeming to fill the entire chamber.
25: His face was illuminated by gleaming earrings, while his golden crown, patterned with pearls and gems, was tilted slightly to one side.
26: He was splendid with his broad, thick, and expansive chest, anointed with red sandalwood and adorned with sparkling necklaces.
27: His red eyes were closed, his white silk upper cloth was tossed aside, and he was wrapped in an exquisite, costly yellow garment.
28: Dark as a heap of black beans, hissing like a serpent, he looked like a great elephant asleep by the waters of the sacred Gaṅgā.
29: With four golden lamps blazing in the four corners, his entire form was illuminated, looking like a dark cloud lit up by clusters of lightning flashes.
30: In that mansion of the king of Rākṣasas, who was devoted to his wives, the Kapi also saw his spouses resting at his feet.
31: The Hari troop leader saw women with faces bright as the moon, adorned with charming earrings and unfaded garlands and ornaments.
32: The Hari saw women skilled in dance and instrumental music, nestled near the arms and lap of the king of Rākṣasas, wearing splendid jewelry.
33: He saw golden earrings set with diamonds and cat's-eye gems dangling from their ears, and fine armlets on their arms.
34: With their moon-like faces and graceful, gleaming earrings, that chamber shone like the sky adorned with clusters of stars.
35: Exhausted from drinking and dancing, those slender-waisted wives of the Rākṣasa king lay fast asleep in various places.
36: Skilled in dance, one fair lady lay asleep with her graceful form relaxed, frozen in a gentle dance posture.
37: One woman lay clasping her vīṇā, glowing like a lotus clinging to a boat in a wide river.
38: Another dark-eyed beauty slept holding a maḍḍuka drum under her arm, looking like an affectionate mother holding her young child.
39: A radiant woman of faultless beauty slept pressing a paṭaha drum to her bosom, as though embracing a lover she had found after a long separation.
40: A lotus-eyed woman slept embracing her flute, looking like a passionate woman holding her beloved in secret.
41: Another dancer, clasping her vipañcī lute, had succumbed to sleep as though resting beside her lover.
42: Another woman with languid eyes slept pressing her mr̥daṅga drum against her soft, plump, golden form.
43: A slender-waisted, blameless woman, worn out by wine, slept holding a paṇava drum under her arm and beside her flank.
44: Another woman lay holding a ḍiṇḍima drum, clinging to it as though embracing a tender young calf.
45: Another lotus-eyed woman, overcome by wine, lay asleep clutching an āḍambara drum tightly in her arms.
46: Another lovely woman lay asleep with her pitcher knocked aside, looking like a colorful floral garland dropped in springtime.
47: Overcome by the power of sleep, another young woman slept with her hands clasping her bosom, which resembled golden jars.
48: Another lotus-eyed woman with a face like the full moon, tipsy with wine, slept embracing another graceful companion.
49: Clasping their varied musical instruments and pressing them to their chests, those beautiful women slept like passionate women holding their lovers.
50: On a splendid couch set apart from the rest, the Kapi saw another woman of exquisite beauty lying asleep.
51: Adorned with jewelry of pearls and gems, she seemed to illuminate that magnificent palace with her own natural radiance.
52: The Kapi saw the lovely Mandodarī lying there — fair, golden-complexioned, the beloved queen of the inner palace.
53: Seeing her adorned and endowed with such wealth of beauty and youth, the mighty-armed son of Māruta assumed she must be Sītā; and the Hari troop leader rejoiced, filled with immense joy.
54: He slapped his arms, kissed his tail, rejoiced, pranced about, sang, moved here and there, climbed pillars and dropped to the floor, displaying the true nature of Kapis.
