1: Urged by the king, Sumantra spoke these words: "Listen to how Ṛśyaśṛṅga was brought here, as it was told to me along with the ministers."
2: The priest, together with the ministers, said to Lomapāda, "We have thought of a plan that carries no risk."
3: "Ṛśyaśṛṅga lives in the forest and is devoted to austerity and Vedic study. He knows nothing of women, of sense pleasures, or of the happiness they bring."
4: "We will bring him to the city with pleasures of the senses that he will find appealing, the kind that unsettle the minds of men. Let it be decided quickly."
5: "Let courtesans, beautiful and richly adorned, go there. They will tempt him in many ways and bring him here with honor."
6: Hearing this, the king said, "So be it," to the priest. The priest and the ministers then did just that.
7: Hearing it, the foremost among the courtesans entered the great forest. Not far from the hermitage, they kept trying to catch sight of the ṛṣi's son, a fierce ascetic who always lived in the hermitage.
8: He was always content with his father and never stepped outside the hermitage.
9: From birth, the ascetic had never seen a woman, a man, or any other being born in a city or kingdom.
10: Then, one day, Vibhāṇḍaka's son happened to come to that spot, and there he saw those beautiful women.
11: Those women, dressed in dazzling clothes and singing in sweet voices, came up to the ṛṣi's son and all said to him:
12: "Who are you, and what do you do, brahmin? We want to know. You wander alone in this lonely, dreadful forest. Tell us."
13: He had never seen such forms before, and those women in the forest were enchanting. Out of affection, he felt moved to tell them about his father.
14: "My father is Vibhāṇḍaka, and I am his own son. My name is Ṛśyaśṛṅga, and I am known on earth by my deeds as well."
15: "Our hermitage is right here, nearby, fair-faced ones. I will honor all of you here, following the proper rites."
16: Hearing the ṛṣi's son, all of them were moved to agree. And so they all went with him to see his hermitage.
17: When they arrived, the ṛṣi's son honored them: "Here is arghya, here is pādya, and here are roots and fruits for you."
18: They accepted his hospitality, but all of them were restless and afraid of the ṛṣi, and they decided to leave quickly.
19: They said, "We have excellent fruits too, brahmin. Please take them. May you be well. Eat them without delay."
20: Then, all of them full of joy, embraced him and gave him modakas and many kinds of fine delicacies.
21: The radiant young ṛṣi took them for fruits, since as a permanent forest-dweller he had never tasted such things before.
22: Then they took leave of the brahmin, telling him they had a vow to keep. Afraid of his father, the women left under that pretext.
23: After all of them had gone, Kāśyapa's son, the brahmin, felt uneasy at heart and was consumed by sorrow.
24: The next day, the valiant young man came back to the same spot, where he had seen those lovely, richly adorned courtesans.
25: Seeing the brahmin approaching, they were delighted. They came up to him, and all of them said these words:
26: They said, "Come, gentle one, to our hermitage. There, this hospitality will be even more special for you."
27: Hearing their words, which touched his heart, he agreed to go. And the women then led him away.
28: As that great-souled brahmin was being brought there, the devatā suddenly sent rain, delighting the whole world.
29: The king humbly went out to meet the sage, who had arrived in his land with the rain, and bowed to the ground with his head.
30: With full attention, he offered him arghya according to custom, and begged the best of brahmins for his favor, so that the brahmin's anger would not arise.
31: He then took him into the inner quarters, gave him his daughter Śāntā in marriage according to the rites, and the king, with a calm mind, was filled with joy.
32: So Ṛśyaśṛṅga, of great radiance, lived there, honored with every comfort, together with his wife Śāntā.
