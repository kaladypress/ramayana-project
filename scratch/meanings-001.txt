1: Vālmīki, the ascetic, questioned Nārada — the foremost of sages, devoted to tapas and the study of the Vedas, and the finest of those skilled in speech.
2: "Who in this world today is truly virtuous and truly valiant? Who knows dharma, who is grateful, truthful in speech, and firm in his vows?
3: Who lives by good conduct, and who is devoted to the welfare of all beings? Who is learned, who is capable, and who alone is a delight to everyone who sees him?
4: Who is self-possessed, who has conquered anger, who is radiant and free of envy? And whom do even the devatās fear when his anger is roused in battle?
5: I want to hear this, because my curiosity is intense. Great sage, you are the one who can know such a man."
6: Nārada, who knows the three worlds, heard Vālmīki's words. Delighted, he said, "Listen," and began to speak.
7: "Sage, the qualities you have named are many, and rare to find together. I will reflect and tell you about the man who has them. Listen.
8: There is one born in the line of Ikṣvāku, known to the people as Rāma. He is self-controlled, of great valor, radiant, steadfast, and master of himself.
9: He is wise, skilled in statecraft, eloquent, and glorious — a destroyer of enemies. His shoulders are broad, his arms mighty, his neck shaped like a conch, and his jaw strong.
10: He is broad-chested and a great archer; his collarbones lie deep beneath strong muscle; he subdues his foes. His arms reach to his knees, his head and brow are finely shaped, and his stride is full of grace.
11: He is perfectly balanced and exquisitely formed, with a glowing complexion and great majesty. His chest is full, his eyes are wide, he is graced with fortune, and he bears every auspicious mark.
12: He knows dharma, keeps his word, and is devoted to the welfare of his people. He is renowned, rich in knowledge, pure, disciplined, and composed in mind.
13: He is the protector of the world of living beings and the guardian of dharma. He knows the true meaning of the Vedas and Vedāṅgas, and he is a master of Dhanurveda, the science of archery.
14: He understands the true purpose of all the śāstras. His memory is sharp and his insight quick. Loved by everyone, he is good, never small-minded, and discerning.
15: Good people always seek him out, just as rivers flow to the ocean. He is noble, treats everyone equally, and is always a delight to see.
16: Endowed with every virtue, he is the joy of Kausalyā. In depth he is like the ocean, and in steadiness like the Himavān.
17: In valor he is equal to Viṣṇu, and he is as lovely to see as the moon. In anger he is like the fire that ends the world, and in patience he is like the earth.
18: In generosity he equals Kubera, the giver of wealth, and in truthfulness he is like a second Dharma himself. Rāma was blessed with all these virtues, and his valor never failed —
19: — and he was the eldest son, rich in the highest qualities and dearly loved. Out of affection, King Daśaratha wished to install him as crown prince (yuvarāja).
20: Then Queen Kaikeyī, Daśaratha's wife, saw the preparations for the consecration. Since she had been granted boons long before, she now claimed them: Rāma's exile and Bharata's consecration.
21: The king was bound by his promise and by the noose of dharma. So Daśaratha sent his beloved son Rāma into exile.
22: The hero went to the forest, keeping the promise, obeying his father's command, and to please Kaikeyī.
23: As he left, his beloved brother Lakṣmaṇa — humble, and the joy of Sumitrā — followed him out of love.
24: Sītā too followed Rāma — the finest of women, a bride who bore every auspicious mark — just as Rohiṇī follows the moon.
25: The citizens and his father Daśaratha followed him a long way. At Śr̥ṅgavērapura, on the bank of the Gaṅgā, he sent the charioteer back.
26: Going from forest to forest and crossing rivers full of water, they reached Citrakūṭa, as Bharadvāja had directed.
27: There the three built a lovely dwelling and lived happily, enjoying the forest, like devas and Gandharvas.
28: After Rāma had gone to Citrakūṭa, King Daśaratha, broken with grief for his son, went to heaven, weeping for him.
29: After his death, the brāhmaṇas led by Vasiṣṭha urged Bharata to take the throne, but the mighty Bharata did not want the kingdom. The hero went to the forest to win Rāma's favor at his feet.
30: Rāma, Bharata's elder brother, gave him his sandals (pādukās) as a pledge for the kingdom and, urging him again and again, sent Bharata back.
31: Bharata, his wish unfulfilled, touched Rāma's feet and ruled the kingdom from Nandigrāma, longing for Rāma's return.
32: Rāma, foreseeing that the citizens and the people would come there again, entered the Daṇḍaka forest with a single-minded resolve.
33: After killing the Rākṣasa Virādha, he met Śarabhaṅga, Sutīkṣṇa, Agastya, and Agastya's brother.
34: At Agastya's word, and with great joy, he accepted Indra's bow, a sword, and two quivers whose arrows never run out.
35: While Rāma was living in the forest among the forest dwellers, all the r̥ṣis came to him asking him to destroy the Asuras and Rākṣasas.
36: While he lived there, Śūrpaṇakhā — a Rākṣasī of Janasthāna who could take any form she wished — was disfigured.
37: Then, roused by Śūrpaṇakhā's words, all the Rākṣasas rose up against him — Khara, Triśiras, and the Rākṣasa Dūṣaṇa.
38: Rāma killed them in battle, together with their followers. Fourteen thousand Rākṣasas were slain.
39: When Rāvaṇa heard of the slaughter of his kinsmen, he was overcome with rage, and he chose a Rākṣasa named Mārīca as his ally.
40: Mārīca tried many times to hold Rāvaṇa back: "Rāvaṇa, you cannot afford to make an enemy of someone that powerful."
41: But Rāvaṇa ignored his words. Driven by his fate, he went with Mārīca to Rāma's āśrama.
42: Using Mārīca, that master of illusion, he lured the two princes far away. Then he carried off Rāma's wife, after killing the vulture Jaṭāyu.
43: When Rāghava saw the vulture slain and heard that Maithilī had been taken, he was consumed with grief and wept, his senses in turmoil.
44: In that same grief he cremated the vulture Jaṭāyu. As he searched the forest for Sītā, he came upon a Rākṣasa
45: named Kabandha, deformed in shape and terrible to see. The mighty-armed Rāma killed him and cremated him, and Kabandha rose to heaven.
46: Kabandha told him, "Rāghava, go to Śabarī, the ascetic woman who lives by dharma and knows it deeply." So the radiant destroyer of enemies went to Śabarī.
47: Rāma, son of Daśaratha, was honored by Śabarī with due reverence. Then, on the shore of the Pampā, he met the Vānara Hanumān.
48: On Hanumān's advice he met Sugrīva. The mighty Rāma told Sugrīva everything that had happened.
49: Then the king of the Vānaras, in sorrow and out of friendship, told Rāma the whole story of his feud. There the Vānara also described Vāli's strength.
50: Rāma then vowed to kill Vāli. But Sugrīva kept doubting Rāghava's strength.
51: To give him confidence, Rāghava flicked the huge carcass of Dundubhi with his big toe, sending it a full ten yojanas.
52: Then, with a single mighty arrow, he pierced seven sāla trees, a mountain, and even Rasātala, the netherworld, which gave Sugrīva confidence.
53: His mind at ease and his trust won, the great Kapi went with Rāma to Kiṣkindhā, the cave city.
54: Then Sugrīva, the best of the Haris, golden-tawny in color, let out a roar. At that great roar, the king of the Haris, Vāli, came out.
55: Then, at Sugrīva's request, Rāghava killed Vāli in combat and installed Sugrīva himself in that kingdom.
56: Sugrīva, the bull among Vānaras, gathered all the Vānaras and sent them out in every direction, eager to find Janaka's daughter.
57: Then, on the word of the vulture Sampāti, the mighty Hanumān leapt across the salt ocean, a hundred yojanas wide.
58: Reaching Laṅkā, the city ruled by Rāvaṇa, he saw Sītā in the Aśoka grove, lost in thought.
59: He gave her the token of recognition, told her all the news, and comforted Vaidēhī. Then he smashed the gateway.
60: He killed five army commanders and seven sons of ministers, crushed the heroic Akṣa, and then allowed himself to be captured.
61: The hero knew that, by the boon of the Grandfather (Brahmā), he would be freed from the weapon. So, of his own will, he put up with the Rākṣasas who bound him.
62: Then the great Kapi burned the city of Laṅkā — all except Sītā, Maithilī — and came back to give Rāma the good news.
63: Approaching the great-souled Rāma, Hanumān of boundless spirit circled him reverently and reported exactly what had happened: "I have seen Sītā."
64: Then Rāma, with Sugrīva, went to the shore of the great ocean and churned up the sea with arrows blazing like the sun.
65: The Ocean, master of all rivers, revealed himself. At the Ocean's word, Rāma had Nala build a bridge.
66: Crossing by that bridge to Laṅkā and killing Rāvaṇa in battle, Rāma consecrated Vibhīṣaṇa as king of the Rākṣasas in Laṅkā.
67: That great deed of the great-souled Rāghava delighted the three worlds — all moving and unmoving beings, along with the devas and the r̥ṣis.
68: Worshipped by all the devatās, who were supremely pleased, Rāma, his task accomplished and his sorrow gone, rejoiced.
69: He received boons from the devatās and brought the fallen Vānaras back to life. Then he boarded the Puṣpaka and went to Nandigrāma.
70: At Nandigrāma the sinless Rāma, together with his brothers, cut off his matted hair. Having won back Sītā, he regained his kingdom.
71: The people are happy and joyful, content and prosperous, deeply righteous, free from suffering and disease, and free from the fear of famine.
72: Nowhere will men see the death of a son. Women will never be widowed, and they will always be devoted to their husbands.
73: There is no danger from storms, no creature drowns in the waters, and there is no danger from fire — just as it was in the Kr̥tayuga.
74: He will perform a hundred Aśvamēdha sacrifices and other sacrifices rich in gold offerings, and he will give countless cows — ten thousand crores — to the learned, according to the sacred rules.
75: Rāghava will establish royal dynasties a hundredfold, and he will set the four varṇas in this world each in its own dharma.
76: After ruling the kingdom for ten thousand years and ten hundred years more, Rāma will go to Brahmalōka.
77: This story of Rāma is sacred. It destroys sin, brings merit, and is equal to the Vedas. Whoever recites it is freed from all sins.
78: Whoever recites this Rāmāyaṇa, a story that grants long life, will be honored in heaven after death, together with his sons, grandsons, and followers.
79: A brāhmaṇa who recites it will gain mastery of speech; a kṣatriya will become a ruler of the land; a vaiśya will reap the profits of trade; and even a śūdra will attain greatness."
