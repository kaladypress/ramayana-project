1: Then, in response to the question of that immeasurable Rāma, the radiant Viśvāmitra began to explain:
2: "Here, mighty-armed Rāma, Viṣṇu, revered by the devatās, lived for many years and hundreds of yugas,
3: "practicing intense austerity and yoga. This was the ancient āśrama of the great-souled Vāmana, Rāma; it is known as Siddhāśrama because the great ascetic achieved perfection right here.
4: "During that very time, King Bali, the son of Virocana, conquered the hosts of devatās—including Indra and the Maruts—and ruled that kingdom, renowned across the three worlds.
5: "While Bali was performing a great sacrifice, the devatās, led by Agni, gathered together in person and spoke to Viṣṇu right here at this āśrama:
6: "'Viṣṇu, Bali, the son of Virocana, is performing a supreme sacrifice. Before his sacrificial vow is completed, accomplish what needs to be done for us!
7: "'To whatever suppliants approach him from everywhere, he grants whatever they ask, wherever and however they desire it.
8: "'Therefore, Viṣṇu, for the welfare of the devatās, resort to your power of māyā, assume the form of a dwarf (Vāmana), and bring about our highest good!'
9: "Meanwhile, Rāma, Kaśyapa, blazing with splendor like fire and shining with spiritual radiance, together with Aditi—
10: "the venerable sage, aided by the goddess, completed a divine vow lasting a thousand years and praised the boon-granting Madhusūdana:
11: "'You are the essence of austerity, a treasury of austerity, austerity embodied, and austerity itself! Through intense austerity, I see you, Puruṣottama.
12: "'In your body I see this entire universe, Supreme One. You are without beginning and beyond description; I have come to you for refuge.'
13: "Pleased, Hari said to the sinless Kaśyapa, 'Choose a boon—blessings be upon you! In my eyes, you are worthy of a boon.'
14: "Hearing his words, Kaśyapa, son of Marīci, said, 'This is what Aditi, the devatās, and I myself earnestly ask of you:
15: "'Giver of boons, faithful to your vows, please be pleased to grant this boon: sinless Supreme One, become the son of Aditi and me!
16: "'Slayer of asuras, become the younger brother of Śakra, and come to the aid of the grief-stricken devatās.
17: "'By your grace, this place will be known as Siddhāśrama. Once this task is accomplished, ruler of the devatās, arise from here, Venerable One!'
18: "Then the radiant Viṣṇu was born to Aditi, and assuming the form of Vāmana, he approached Bali, the son of Virocana.
19: "Begging for three paces of land and receiving the earth, he who is devoted to the welfare of all worlds strode across the worlds for their sake;
20: "restraining Bali by his power, that radiant one gave the earth back to Mahendra and restored the three worlds to Śakra's control.
21: "This fatigue-dispelling āśrama was graced by him in ancient times, and out of devotion to that Vāmana, I too live here and enjoy its peace.
22: "It is to this āśrama that those obstructing rākṣasas come; right here, tiger among men, those evildoers must be slain.
23: "Today we enter the peerless Siddhāśrama, Rāma. Dear boy, this āśrama is as much yours as it is mine."
24: As he entered the āśrama grounds, the great sage shone like the moon emerging from mist, joined by the Punarvasu constellation.
25: Seeing him, all the sages living in Siddhāśrama sprang up eagerly at once and offered reverence to Viśvāmitra.
26: They honored the wise Viśvāmitra as befitted his stature, and likewise offered traditional hospitality to the two princes.
27: After resting for a short while, the two princes, subduers of enemies and scions of Raghu, joined their palms in reverence and said to the tiger among sages:
28: "Foremost of sages, enter upon your sacrificial vow (dīkṣā) today itself—blessings be upon you! May this Siddhāśrama truly fulfill its name, and may your word prove true."
29: Thus addressed, the radiant great sage Viśvāmitra, self-restrained and with senses controlled, entered upon the dīkṣā.
30: The two princes spent that night there with calm, focused minds; rising at daybreak and performing their morning sandhyā worship,
31: purifying themselves with water and duly completing their silent prayers (japa), they bowed to Viśvāmitra, who was seated after offering the agnihotra oblation.
