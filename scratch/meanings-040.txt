1: "Hearing the devatās' words, the revered Brahmā replied to them—terrified as they were and bewildered by the power of destiny:
2: "'The wise Vāsudeva, to whom this entire earth belongs, has assumed the form of Kapila and unceasingly upholds the world.
3: "'This cleaving of the earth was ordained from eternity, and so too was the destruction of Sagara's short-lived sons.'
4: "Hearing Pitāmaha's words, conqueror of foes, the thirty-three devatās were overjoyed and returned the way they had come.
5: "Meanwhile, as Sagara's noble-souled sons tore open the earth, a roar like a thunderclap arose.
6: "After digging through the entire earth and circling it clockwise, all the sons of Sagara gathered together and told their father:
7: "'We have scoured the entire earth and slain its mighty beings—devatās, Dānavas, Rākṣasas, Piśācas, Uragas, and Kinnaras.
8: "'Yet we still cannot find the horse, nor the thief who stole it. What should we do now, blessings upon you? Please advise us on what to do next.'
9: "Hearing his sons' report, Raghunandana, Sagara—noblest of kings—replied in anger:
10: "'Dig again—blessings upon you—and break deep into the earth's core! Return only when you have found the horse-thief and accomplished your task.'
11: "Obeying the command of their noble-souled father Sagara, the sixty thousand sons plunged back down into Rasātala.
12: "As they dug there, they saw the quarter-elephant Virūpākṣa, huge as a mountain, upholding the earth.
13: "That great elephant Virūpākṣa bore the entire earth—with all its mountains and forests—upon his head, Raghunandana.
14: "Whenever that great elephant shifts his head out of weariness to rest at times, Kākutstha, an earthquake shakes the world.
15: "Walking clockwise around that great elephant, guardian of the quarter, and paying him due homage, Rāma, they dug onward through Rasātala.
16: "Having excavated the eastern quarter, they dug into the southern quarter next, and there too they saw a great elephant—
17: "the noble Mahāpadma, vast as a towering mountain, bearing the earth upon his head; and they were filled with deep wonder.
18: "Circling him clockwise, the sixty thousand sons of noble-souled Sagara then dug into the western quarter.
19: "In the western quarter as well, those mighty princes saw the quarter-elephant Saumanasa, huge as a mountain.
20: "Circling him clockwise and asking after his well-being, they dug onward and entered the northern quarter.
21: "In the north, foremost of the Raghus, they saw Bhadra—white as snow—upholding this earth with his auspicious form.
22: "Touching him with reverence and circling him clockwise, all sixty thousand sons dug deeper into the earth.
23: "Then, moving toward the famed northeastern quarter, all the sons of Sagara dug furiously into the earth.
24: "There, all those noble-souled princes, fierce in speed and immense in strength, saw Kapila—the eternal Vāsudeva.
25: "And seeing the horse grazing not far from that divine being, they were all filled with matchless joy, Raghunandana.
26: "Taking him to be the thief who had ruined their sacrifice, their eyes wild with rage,
27: "armed with spades and plows, and seizing trees and boulders of all kinds, they charged at him in fury, shouting, 'Stand your ground! Stop!'
28: "'You are the one who stole our sacrificial horse, you fool! Know that we, the sons of Sagara, have come for you!'
29: "Hearing their words, Raghunandana, Kapila was gripped by tremendous wrath and let out a thunderous huṅkāra.
30: "At once, Kākutstha, all the sons of Sagara were reduced to heaps of ash by that immeasurable, noble-souled Kapila.
