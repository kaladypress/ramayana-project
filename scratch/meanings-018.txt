1: As Hanumān was thus observing the grove of flowering trees and searching for Vaidehī, the night drew near its close.
2: Toward the break of dawn, he heard the chanting of the Vedas by the Brahma-rākṣasas, who were master scholars of the six auxiliary sciences of the Veda and seasoned performers of foremost sacrifices.
3: Then, accompanied by the melodious, auspicious sounds of musical instruments pleasing to the ear, the mighty-armed and powerful Daśagrīva was awakened.
4: Awakening at the proper hour, the radiant king of Rākṣasas, his garlands and robes slipping, turned his thoughts at once to Vaidehī.
5: Being passionately drawn to her and overwhelmed with the intoxication of desire, that Rākṣasa could not conceal the lust burning within his heart.
6: Adorned with all his jewels and radiating unsurpassed splendor, he set out for that grove graced by diverse trees bearing blossoms and fruits of every season.
7: It was surrounded by lotus ponds, beautified with colorful blossoms, and filled with birds perpetually exhilarated with joy—a place truly wondrous.
8: He walked along observing the pathways spanned by archways of gems and gold, stocked with delightful ornamental animal sculptures and teeming with captivating deer.
9: Crowded with varied herds of deer and carpeted with fallen fruits, that dense grove of continuous trees—the Aśoka grove—was entered by him.
10: As that scion of Pulastya strode forth, a retinue of about a hundred women followed him, just as celestial maidens and Gandharva women attend upon great Indra.
11: Among them, some women carried golden torches, while others held fly-whisks and palm-leaf fans in their hands.
12: Some walked in front carrying water in golden pitchers, while others walked behind bearing curved scimitars.
13: One capable woman carried with her right hand a jeweled goblet filled with wine.
14: Another walked behind holding a white parasol with a golden staff, resembling a royal swan and glowing like the full moon.
15: With eyes misty with sleep and intoxication, those choice wives of Rāvaṇa followed their heroic husband like flashes of lightning following a dark cloud.
16-20: With their pearl necklaces and armlets tossed about, the cosmetic sandal paste on their bodies rubbed away, their hair falling loose, and their faces beaded with sweat, those lovely women swayed under lingering intoxication and sleep. Their floral garlands were crushed with sweat and tangled in their tresses as those intoxicating-eyed wives, driven by deep affection and passion, followed their husband, the king of the Nairr̥tas. Wholly enslaved by lust, that mighty husband walked with a slow, swaying gait, his mind obsessively fixed on Sītā. Just then, that Kapi, the son of Māruta, heard the soft tinkling of jeweled waistbands and the chiming of anklets of those noble women.
21: The Kapi Hanumān saw that figure of matchless deeds and unimaginable strength and prowess arriving at the entrance.
22: He was illuminated on all sides by numerous torches fueled by fragrant oils and carried ahead of him.
23: Infused with desire, pride, and passion, his long reddish eyes glancing slantwise, he looked like the god of love himself, having laid aside his bow.
24: As he walked, he playfully pulled at his flawless upper garment, white as the churned froth of nectar, which had slipped down and caught on his armlet.
25: Hidden in the branching boughs and thickly screened by dense leaves and blossoms, Hanumān began to watch him closely as he drew near.
26: Looking carefully, the Kapikuñjara saw the choice women of Rāvaṇa, endowed with dazzling youth and beauty.
27: Surrounded by those lovely women, the renowned king entered that pleasure garden, which was resounding with the calls of beasts and birds.
28: Hanumān saw the son of Viśravas, the mighty king of Rākṣasas, intoxicated, adorned with diverse ornaments, and bearing pointed ears.
29: Surrounded by supreme women like the moon encircled by stars, that radiant monarch was watched by the glorious great Kapi.
30: Realizing, "This is indeed the mighty-armed Rāvaṇa," the radiant Vānara Hanumān, son of Māruta, shifted down slightly.
31: Though endowed with fierce luster himself, Hanumān drew back before Rāvaṇa's overwhelming presence and remained concealed deep within the thick foliage.
32: Yearning to behold that dark-tressed lady of graceful bearing, firm breasts, and dark-rimmed eyes, Rāvaṇa advanced toward her.
