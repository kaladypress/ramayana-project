1: When the great-souled king's Aśvamedha yagna was complete, the devatās accepted their shares of the offering and went back the way they had come.
2: His vows of initiation fulfilled, the king entered the city with his wives, his attendants, his army, and his mounts.
3: The king honored the rulers of the earth as each deserved. They bowed to the best of sages and left for their own lands, happy.
4: As those prosperous kings left the city for their homes, their bright armies shone with joy.
5: When the rulers of the earth had gone, the glorious King Daśaratha entered the city, with the best of brahmins walking before him.
6: Ṛśyaśṛṅga, well honored, left together with Śāntā. The wise king followed him along with his retinue.
7: Having sent them all off in this way, the king, his heart full, lived there happily, thinking about the birth of sons.
8: After the yagna ended, six seasons went by. Then, in the twelfth month, in Caitra, on the ninth tithi,
9: under the nakṣatra ruled by Aditi, with five planets in their exalted positions, and Cancer rising together with Jupiter and the Moon,
10: as the sun was rising, Kausalyā gave birth to Rāma, protector of the world, honored by all the worlds, and marked with every auspicious sign.
11: She bore the great and fortunate son, joy of the Ikṣvāku line, half of Viṣṇu. Kausalyā shone with that son of boundless radiance, as Aditi shone with her son, the best of the devatās, the wielder of the thunderbolt.
12: Bharata, true in valor, was born to Kaikeyī. He was a quarter of Viṣṇu himself, and he was endowed with every virtue.
13: Then Sumitrā gave birth to the two sons Lakṣmaṇa and Śatrughna. They were heroes, skilled in every weapon, and together they carried the other half of Viṣṇu.
14: Bharata, of serene mind, was born under Puṣya, with Pisces rising.
15: The two sons of Sumitrā were born under the Sārpa nakṣatra, with Cancer rising and the sun at its height. In this way the king's four great-souled sons were born, one after another.
16: They were virtuous and well matched to one another, and their radiance was like that of the Proṣṭhapada stars. The Gandharvas sang sweetly and the troops of Apsarasas danced.
17: The drums of the devatās sounded, and a shower of flowers fell from the sky.
18: A great festival filled Ayodhyā with crowds. The streets were packed with people and full of actors and dancers. They rang with singers and with players of instruments.
19: The king gave the gifts due to the sūtas, māgadhas, and bandins. To the brahmins he gave wealth and thousands of cattle.
20: When eleven days had passed, he held the naming ceremony. He named the eldest, the great-souled one, Rāma, and Kaikeyī's son Bharata.
21: Vasiṣṭha, delighted, named Sumitrā's sons Lakṣmaṇa and the other Śatrughna.
22: The king fed the brahmins and the people of the city and countryside. He gave the brahmins endless heaps of jewels, and he had all the rites beginning with the birth ceremonies performed for the boys.
23: Of them all, the eldest, Rāma, was like a banner. He delighted his father, and all beings valued him as they would the self-born Brahmā.
24: All of them knew the Vedas and were brave. All of them took joy in the good of the world. All were full of wisdom, and all had every virtue.
25: Even among them, the greatly radiant Rāma, true in valor, was dear to the whole world, spotless as the moon.
26: He was admired on elephant-back, on horseback, and in driving a chariot. He was devoted to the science of archery and to serving his father.
27: From childhood, Lakṣmaṇa, who increased the family's fortune, was deeply attached to his eldest brother Rāma, the delight of the world, and stayed so always.
28: He did everything that pleased Rāma, who was dearer to him than his own body. Lakṣmaṇa was full of grace, like Rāma's second life breath outside his body.
29: The best of men could not sleep without him. He would not eat even the finest food brought to him unless Lakṣmaṇa was there.
30: Whenever Rāghava rode out on horseback to hunt, Lakṣmaṇa followed close behind him, guarding him with his bow.
31: In the same way Śatrughna, the younger brother of Lakṣmaṇa, was always dearer than life to Bharata, and Bharata was just as dear to him.
32: With those four fortunate and beloved sons, Daśaratha was supremely happy, like Brahmā, the grandfather, among the devatās.
33: When all of them had grown wise and were full of virtue, modest and renowned, knowing everything and seeing far ahead,
34: all of them of such power and blazing splendor, their father Daśaratha was delighted, like Brahmā, ruler of the worlds.
35: Those tigers among men were devoted to studying the Vedas and to serving their father, and they excelled in archery.
36: Then the righteous King Daśaratha, together with his teachers and relatives, began to think about their marriages.
37: As the great-souled king thought this over among his ministers, the greatly radiant, great sage Viśvāmitra arrived.
38: Wishing to see the king, he said to the gatekeepers, "Quickly tell the king that Kauśika, son of Gādhi, has arrived."
39: Hearing his words, the gatekeepers, stirred by that command and flustered, all ran to the king's quarters.
40: They went to the royal palace and reported to King Ikṣvāku's descendant that the sage Viśvāmitra had come.
41: Hearing their words, the king, composed and delighted, went out to meet him with his priest, as Vāsava would go to meet Brahmā.
42: Seeing the ascetic of strict vows, blazing with radiance, the king's face lit up with joy, and he offered him arghya.
43: Viśvāmitra accepted the arghya offered by the king according to the rites prescribed in the śāstras. He asked the king about his well-being and his prosperity.
44: Kauśika, deeply devoted to dharma, asked about the well-being of the king's city, treasury, and realm, and of his relatives and friends.
45: "Have all your neighboring rulers bowed to you, and have your enemies been defeated? Are your rites, both divine and human, carried out well?"
46: The best of sages then met Vasiṣṭha and asked after his well-being, and he spoke to the other fortunate ṛṣis as was proper.
47: All of them, delighted, entered the king's palace. Honored by him, they sat down in their proper places.
48: Then the king, glad and most generous of heart, honored the great sage Viśvāmitra and spoke to him with joy.
49: "Your coming is like receiving nectar, like rain in a land without water, like the birth of a son to a childless man with a wife of his own rank.
50: It is like finding what was lost, and like the joy of a great celebration. That is how I feel about your arrival. Welcome, great sage.
51: What wish of yours can I fulfill? Why would I not be delighted? You are a worthy one to receive my service, brahmin. It is my good fortune that you have come, you who honor others.
52: Today my birth has borne fruit, and my life has become a good life, because I have seen the best of brahmins. This night has become a bright dawn for me.
53: Earlier you shone with the glory of ascetic power and were known as a royal sage. Now you have attained the rank of brahmarṣi, and I honor you in many ways.
54: Your visit has been a wonder for me, and a supremely purifying one. By seeing you, venerable one, I feel as if I have reached a holy place.
55: Tell me what you have come to ask. I wish to be blessed by serving your purpose, so that I may prosper.
56: Do not hesitate over the task, you of good vows. I will do it completely, for you are a devatā to me.
57: This great good fortune has come to me, brahmin, and the whole of the highest dharma has come from your visit."
58: Hearing these words, pleasing to the heart and to the ear, spoken humbly by a self-controlled king, the great sage, famed for his virtues and distinguished by them, felt supreme joy.
