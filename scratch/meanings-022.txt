1: Hearing Sītā's harsh words, the king of Rākṣasas spoke unpleasantly to that lady of pleasant appearance:
2: "The more a man tries to soothe and yield to a woman, the more she controls him; the more kindly he speaks, the more she insults him!
3: Only the passion that has arisen in me for you restrains my wrath, as a skilled charioteer reins in galloping horses upon reaching the road.
4: Cruel is the nature of desire in men: toward the very person to whom one's heart is bound, tenderness and love are born!
5: It is solely for this reason, fair-faced one, that I do not slay you, though you deserve death and humiliation for doting on a false hermit.
6: For every harsh word you fling at me, Maithilī, a terrible death is your rightful due."
7: Having spoken thus to Vaidehī, Rāvaṇa, the king of Rākṣasas, seething with anger and agitation, declared to Sītā:
8: "Two months still remain of the time limit I set for you; after that, lady of golden complexion, you must ascend my bed!
9: If after these two months you still refuse to accept me as your husband, my cooks will carve you up in the royal kitchen for my morning meal!"
10: Seeing Jānakī thus threatened by the king of Rākṣasas, the wide-eyed daughters of Devas and Gandharvas standing nearby were filled with sorrow.
11: Some with movements of their lips, others with their eyes and facial expressions, secretly comforted Sītā as she was terrified by that Rākṣasa.
12: Reassured by them, Sītā, proud in the purity of her conduct and virtue, addressed words for his own welfare to Rāvaṇa, the king of Rākṣasas:
13: "Surely there is no one here devoted to your true well-being, for no one restrains you from this contemptible crime!
14: Who else in all the three worlds except you would dare even in thought to desire me, the wife of the righteous Rāma, as sacred as Śacī to Indra?
15: Vile Rākṣasa, having uttered such sinful words to the wife of the immeasurably radiant Rāma, where can you flee to escape retribution?
16: As an enraged royal elephant and a hare together in the forest, so is Rāma like the majestic elephant, and you, base wretch, are like the helpless hare!
17: Are you not ashamed to insult that ruler of the Ikṣvākus, simply because you have not yet come within the range of his sight?
18: Why have those two cruel, deformed, dark-yellow eyes of yours not dropped to the earth for having cast their wicked gaze upon me?
19: How is it that your tongue does not split into pieces, you sinner, as you speak such filth to the wife of that righteous prince and daughter-in-law of Daśaratha?
20: Daśagrīva, only because I have received no command from Rāma and must preserve the spiritual power of my ascetic vows, I do not reduce you to ash right now with my fiery radiance!
21: I could never have been abducted from the side of the wise Rāma; this has all been ordained by destiny solely to bring about your destruction, there is no doubt!
22: You, claiming to be a hero, the brother of Kubera, backed by vast armies—why did you steal another man's wife by stealth after luring Rāma away?"
23: Hearing Sītā's words, Rāvaṇa, the king of Rākṣasas, rolled his cruel eyes and glared furiously at Jānakī.
24: Resembling a dark thundercloud, with massive arms and a thick neck, moving with the strength and stride of a lion, he flashed his tongue with fierce, blazing eyes.
25: Tall, with his swaying crown aloft, adorned with colorful garlands and unguents, wearing red garlands and scarlet robes, he was decked with armlets of refined gold.
26: Girded around his waist with a great golden sash, he resembled Mount Mandara wrapped with the serpent during the churning for nectar.
27: Adorned with earrings glowing like the morning sun, he looked like a mountain flanked by two blossoming aśoka trees covered with red shoots and flowers.
28-30: Resembling a wish-granting tree and looking like Spring incarnate, he was terrifying despite his lavish adornments, like an altar in a burning-ground. Glaring at Vaidehī with eyes bloodshot with rage and hissing like a serpent, Rāvaṇa spoke to Sītā: "Because you cling devotedly to one who is fallen from righteousness and stripped of all wealth, I will destroy you this very day, as the sun dissolves the evening twilight with its blazing power!"
32: Having thus threatened Maithilī, King Rāvaṇa, the terror of his enemies, issued his command to all those hideous-looking Rākṣasīs:
33: those with one eye, one ear, or ears like blankets, those with ears of cows or elephants, those with drooping ears, or no ears at all;
34: those with the feet of elephants, dogs, or cattle, or with tufted feet; those with a single eye, a single foot, broad feet, or no feet;
35: those with monstrously oversized heads and necks, swollen breasts and bellies, gaping mouths, bulging eyes, long tongues, or no tongues;
36: and those without noses, or with the faces of lions, cows, and sows, saying: "See to it that Sītā, Janaka's daughter, submits quickly to my will!
37: Gather together at once, all of you Rākṣasīs, and by harsh or gentle means, through conciliation, gifts, sowing dissension, or the threat of force, bring Vaidehī around to me."
38: Having repeatedly instructed them thus, the king of Rākṣasas, his heart seized by lust and fury, gave vent to his threats against Jānakī.
39: Then a Rākṣasī named Dhānyamālinī stepped forward swiftly, embraced the ten-headed Daśagrīva, and said:
40-43: "Enjoy yourself with me, great King! What do you want with this pale, wretched, mere mortal woman, sovereign of Rākṣasas? Surely, great King, the foremost of gods has not destined her to enjoy those supreme divine pleasures won by the might of your arms! The body of a man who longs for an unwilling woman is only consumed by fever; but when he loves a woman who desires him in return, that love is glorious!" Spoken to thus by the Rākṣasī, the mighty monarch was turned away from his wrath and led off.
44-45: Strode forth as if shaking the earth, the ten-headed one entered his palace, which blazed with the splendor of the radiant sun. Surrounding the ten-headed Daśagrīva, the captive daughters of the Devas, Gandharvas, and Nāgas followed him into that magnificent mansion.
46: Having thus threatened Maithilī—who stood trembling, yet unshakeably anchored in righteousness—Rāvaṇa, bewildered by passion, abandoned Sītā and returned to his own blazing abode.
