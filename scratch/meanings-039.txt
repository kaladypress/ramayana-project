1: After giving him the jewel, Sītā said to Hanumān: "Rāma knows this token well — truly."
2: "When he sees the jewel, heroic Rāma will remember three people: my mother, me, and King Daśaratha.
3: "Best of the Haris, now that you are spurred on to fresh effort, think about what must be done next in this undertaking.
4: "Best of the Haris, this whole mission rests on you. Think of the effort that will bring my sorrows to an end."
5: "As you think it through, may your effort bring my sorrow to an end." Promising "So be it," Māruti, of fearsome valor, bowed his head to Vaidehī and prepared to leave.
6: Knowing that the vānara, son of Māruta, was about to set out, the devī Maithilī spoke, her voice choked with tears:
7-8: "Hanumān, give my greetings to Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa together, and to Sugrīva with his ministers, and to all the elder vānaras. Best of the vānaras, convey my good wishes to them, as dharma requires.
9: "You must arrange things so that mighty-armed Rāghava rescues me from this flood of sorrow that hems me in.
10: "Hanumān, speak to glorious Rāma in such a way that he comes for me while I am still alive. By your words you will earn dharma.
11: "When he hears the words I have spoken, always full of encouragement, the resolve of Daśaratha's son to win me back will grow.
12: "As soon as he hears my message from you, heroic Rāghava will plan his campaign properly."
13: Hearing Sītā's words, Hanumān, son of Māruta, raised his joined palms to his head and replied:
14: "Kākutstha will come soon, surrounded by the finest of the Haris and Ṛkṣas. He will defeat your enemies in battle and drive away your grief.
15: "I see no one among humans, devas, or asuras who could stand before him when he looses his arrows.
16: "He could withstand even the sun, or Parjanya, or Yama, son of Vivasvān, in battle — especially for your sake.
17: "He is determined to rule the earth to the ocean's edge. Joy of Janaka, Rāma's victory depends on you."
18: Hearing his words — true, well reasoned, and well spoken — Jānakī valued them deeply and said this:
19: Then, as he was setting out, Sītā gazed at him again and again, and out of affection she spoke kindly to him, her words full of love for her husband:
20: "Hero, tamer of foes, if you think it wise, stay here one more day. Rest in some hidden spot and leave tomorrow.
21: "Mighty one, with you near me, unlucky as I am, I might find release from this great sorrow, if only for a moment.
22: "Once you have gone, tiger among the Haris, my very life will be in doubt until you return — of that there is no question.
23: "The grief of not seeing you would torment me even more, vānara, burning me when I am already struck by sorrow upon sorrow.
24: "And, hero, chief of the Haris, there is a great doubt that seems to stand before me, about your allies, the Haris and Ṛkṣas:
25: "How will those armies of Haris and Ṛkṣas, or those two sons of the best of men, ever cross this impassable ocean?
26: "Only three beings have the strength to leap across this ocean: Vainateya, you, and the Wind.
27: "So, hero, in this mission that is so hard to carry out, what solution do you see? You are the best of those who know how to get things done.
28: "Certainly, slayer of enemy heroes, you alone are able to accomplish this mission. The strength you would show would bring you glory.
29: "But if Rāma defeats Rāvaṇa in battle with his whole army and returns victorious to his city with me, that would bring me honor.
30: "If Kākutstha, crusher of enemy armies, covers Laṅkā with his arrows and takes me away, that would be worthy of him.
31: "So act in a way that brings about a feat worthy of that great-souled hero of battle."
32: Hearing those words — meaningful, affectionate, and well reasoned — Hanumān gave the rest of his reply:
33: "Devī, Sugrīva, master of the armies of Haris and Ṛkṣas, best of leapers, full of courage, has made up his mind for your sake.
34: "Vaidehī, that destroyer of rākṣasas will come soon, surrounded by thousands upon millions of vānaras.
35: "Under his command are Haris full of valor, courage, and great strength, who move as fast as thought.
36: "Their movement is never blocked — above, below, or sideways. Their energy is boundless, and they never falter, even in great tasks.
37: "Again and again those energetic ones, traveling the path of the wind, have circled the earth with its oceans and mountains.
38: "Among those forest-dwellers there are some greater than me, and some equal to me. No one in Sugrīva's company is inferior to me.
39: "If even I have come here, what about those mighty ones? It is not the best who are sent on errands — it is the others.
40: "So enough of this distress, devī. Let your grief go. The Hari troop leaders will reach Laṅkā in a single leap.
41: "Those two mighty lions among men will come to you riding on my back, like the sun and moon risen together.
42: "Those two heroes, the best of men, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa together, will reach the city of Laṅkā and blast it apart with their arrows.
43: "Rāghava, the joy of the Raghus, will kill Rāvaṇa and his followers and take you back to his own city, graceful lady.
44: "So take heart, and bless you. Wait for the right time. Soon you will see Rāma, blazing like fire.
45: "When the king of the rākṣasas has been killed along with his sons, ministers, and kin, you will be united with Rāma, like Rohiṇī with the moon.
46: "Soon, devī Maithilī, you will reach the far shore of your grief, and before long you will see Rāvaṇa killed by Rāma."
47: Having comforted Vaidehī in this way, Hanumān, son of Māruta, made up his mind to leave and spoke to Vaidehī again:
48: "Soon you will see Rāghava, slayer of enemies, master of himself, and Lakṣmaṇa, bow in hand, standing at the gate of Laṅkā.
49: "Soon you will see the vānaras gathered there — heroes whose weapons are their claws and teeth, valiant as lions and tigers, huge as the kings of elephants.
50: "Noble lady, you will see troop after troop of leading Kapis, like mountains and clouds, roaring on the slopes of Laṅkā and Malaya.
51: "But Rāma, struck to the core by Manmatha's terrible arrow, finds no peace, like an elephant mauled by a lion.
52: "Don't cry, devī. Don't let grief trouble your heart. Like Śacī with Śakra, you have a husband to protect you.
53: "Who is greater than Rāma? Who is equal to Saumitri? Those two brothers, like fire and wind, are your refuge.
54: "Devī, you will not stay long in this terrible place, crowded with hordes of rākṣasas. Your beloved will come before long. Bear it only until I reach him."
