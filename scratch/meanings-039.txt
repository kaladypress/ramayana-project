1: Having heard Viśvāmitra's words at the end of the story, Raghunandana, deeply pleased, spoke to the muni, who shone like a blazing fire:
2: "Blessings upon you, Brāhmaṇa—I want to hear this story in full detail: how did my ancestor perform that great sacrifice?"
3: With a gentle smile, Viśvāmitra replied to Kākutstha, "Listen, Rāma, to the full story of the noble-souled Sagara.
4: "The foremost of mountains, Himavān—known as Śaṅkara's father-in-law—and Mount Vindhya stand facing each other, gazing at one another from afar.
5: "Between those two mountains, Puruṣottama, that sacrifice was begun—for that region, tiger among men, is celebrated as ideal for sacred rites.
6: "Aṁśumān—a steadfast bowman and mighty mahāratha, Kākutstha—guarded the wandering sacrificial horse, my dear Rāma, following Sagara's wishes.
7: "On a sacred parvan day during the sacrifice, Vāsava took on the form of a Rākṣasa and stole away the sacrificial horse.
8: "As the noble-souled king's horse was being carried off, Kākutstha, all the officiating priests spoke to the royal sacrificer:
9: "'Right on this sacred parvan day, the sacrificial horse is being swiftly carried away! Slay the thief, Kākutstha, and bring the horse back at once.
10: "'This causes a breach in the sacrifice, bringing misfortune upon us all. Act at once, king, so that the sacred rite may remain unbroken.'
11: "Hearing the priests' words in that sacrificial assembly, the king spoke to his sixty thousand sons:
12: "'My sons, foremost among men, I see no trace of where the Rākṣasas have gone—even though this great sacrifice is attended by illustrious sages purified by mantras.
13: "'Go then, search far and wide, my sons—blessings upon you! Traverse the entire earth, garlanded by the oceans.
14: "'Spread out so that each of you covers a full yojana in width, my sons, and dig down into the earth until you find the horse!
15: "'Search for the thief who stole the horse, by my command. Meanwhile, consecrated for the rite, I will remain right here with my grandson and the assembly of priests—blessings upon you—until the horse is recovered.'
16: "Upon hearing this, the immensely powerful princes, glad at heart and bound by their father's command, went forth across the earth, Rāma.
17: "Each of them dug into a stretch of earth a yojana long and a yojana wide, tiger among men, cleaving the ground with arms as hard as the vajra.
18: "Torn apart by pikes like thunderbolts and fearsome plows, the earth cried out aloud, Raghunandana.
19: "A terrible outcry arose from the Nāgas, Asuras, Rākṣasas, and all the subterranean beings being slaughtered there, Rāghava.
20: "For sixty thousand yojanas, Raghunandana, those mighty warriors tore through the earth all the way down to the depths of Rasātala.
21: "In this way, the king's sons ranged everywhere across Jambūdvīpa—thick with mountains—digging it up on every side, tiger among kings.
22: "Then the devatās, together with the Gandharvas, Asuras, and Pannagas—all deeply alarmed—went to Brahmā.
23: "With downcast faces and gripped by terror, they bowed before the noble-souled Brahmā and spoke:
24: "'Revered one, the entire earth is being dug up by Sagara's sons, and countless noble creatures of the waters are being slaughtered!
25: "'Crying out, "Here is the destroyer of our sacrifice—this is the one who stole our horse!" Sagara's sons are killing every creature in their path!'"
