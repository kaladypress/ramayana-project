1: Having fulfilled their purpose, the two heroes Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa spent that night there joyfully, with deeply delighted hearts.
2: At daybreak, after performing their morning rites, the two brothers went together to Viśvāmitra and the other ṛṣis.
3: Bowing respectfully to that foremost sage, who shone like a blazing fire, the two sweet-spoken princes addressed him in gentle and noble words:
4: "Here we stand before you as your servants, Muniśārdūla. Command us as you wish—what order would you like us to carry out?"
5: When the two had spoken, all the maharṣis, with Viśvāmitra at their head, said to Rāma:
6: "Foremost among men, King Janaka of Mithilā is going to perform a most righteous yajña, and we are heading there.
7: "You too will come with us, Naraśārdūla, and there you will get to see a wondrous jewel of a bow.
8: "Long ago, foremost among men, the devatās presented that formidable, supremely radiant bow of immeasurable power in the assembly at a yajña.
9: "Neither devatās, nor gandharvas, nor asuras, nor rākṣasas, nor humans have ever been able to string it by any means.
10: "Kings and mighty princes eager to test the bow's power have all failed to string it.
11: "There, Kākutstha, you will see that bow belonging to the great-souled ruler of Mithilā, as well as a yajña wondrous to behold.
12: "Indeed, Naraśārdūla, that Maithila king had asked all the devatās for that supreme, finely crafted bow as the fruit of his yajña.
13: "Kept as a sacred object of worship in that king's palace, Rāghava, it is honored with all kinds of fragrant pastes and aguru-scented incense."
14: Having said this, the foremost sage set out with the company of ṛṣis and the Kākutstha brothers, first taking leave of the forest devatās:
15: "May all be well with you! Having fulfilled my vow, I am leaving Siddhāśrama for the northern bank of the Jāhnavī, toward the Himavān mountain."
16: Then, after circumambulating the matchless Siddhāśrama, he set out toward the north.
17: As that foremost sage walked on, a train of about a hundred carts belonging to his followers, the Vedic scholars, traveled behind him.
18: The deer and flocks of birds living in Siddhāśrama also followed the great-souled sage Viśvāmitra, until he and the company of ṛṣis gently turned them back.
19: After traveling a long distance, as the sun sank low, the composed company of sages made camp on the bank of the Śoṇā river.
20: Once the sun had set, those sages of boundless vigor bathed, offered oblations to the sacred fire, and sat down with Viśvāmitra at their head.
21: Rāma too, along with Saumitri, paid respects to the sages and sat down before the wise Viśvāmitra.
22: Filled with curiosity, the radiant Rāma then asked the great sage Viśvāmitra, that Muniśārdūla:
23: "Venerable sage, what land is this, graced with such lush forests? I would like to hear about it—blessings upon you, please tell me its true story."
24: Prompted by Rāma's request, the steadfast great ascetic recounted the full history of that region in the midst of the ṛṣis.
