1: "When her embryo had been split into seven parts, Diti, though deeply grieved, spoke gently to the unconquerable thousand-eyed Indra:
2: "'Through my own fault this pregnancy has been undone in seven parts; there is no fault of yours in this, ruler of the devatās, slayer of Bala.
3: "'Yet in this misfortune of my womb, I wish you would grant me a favor: let these seven become the guardians of the seven realms of the Maruts.
4: "'Let these seven sons of mine move through the heavens as the seven hosts of the wind, renowned as the Mārutas, my sons of divine form.
5: "'Let one move through Brahmaloka and another through Indraloka, while the third, of great renown, moves through the sky, famed as Vāyu.
6: "'And let the remaining four, foremost of the suras, roam the four directions under your command—may all be well with you—as devatās, these sons of mine, renowned as the Mārutas by the very name born of your words.'
7: "Hearing those words of hers, the thousand-eyed Purandara, slayer of Bala, joined his palms in añjali and spoke to Diti:
8: "'All of this will come to pass just as you have said, without a doubt. May all be well with you—your sons will roam freely as devatās.'
9: "Having reached this agreement in that tapas-grove, mother and son both departed to heaven, Rāma, with their aims fulfilled—so we have heard.
10: "This is that very region, Kākutstha, where Mahendra stayed long ago, and where he attended in this way upon Diti as she perfected her tapas.
11: "Now, tiger among men, Ikṣvāku had a supremely righteous son born of Alambuṣā, famed by the name Viśāla.
12: "And it was by him that this city named Viśālā was founded right here.
13: "Viśāla's son, Rāma, was the mighty Hemacandra, and after Hemacandra came his son, famed as Sucandra.
14: "Sucandra's son, Rāma, was renowned as Dhūmrāśva, and Dhūmrāśva's son was Sr̥ñjaya.
15: "Sr̥ñjaya's son was the illustrious, valorous Sahadeva, and Sahadeva's son was the supremely righteous Kuśāśva.
16: "Kuśāśva's son was the deeply radiant, valorous Somadatta, and Somadatta's son was famed as Kākutstha.
17: "His deeply radiant son, invincible and resembling an immortal, is named Sumati, and he now lives in and rules this city.
18: "By the grace of Ikṣvāku, all the kings of Viśālā are long-lived, noble-souled, valorous, and deeply righteous.
19: "Tonight we will stay comfortably here, Rāma; tomorrow morning, foremost of men, you will go to see Janaka."
20: Meanwhile, hearing that Viśvāmitra had arrived, the deeply radiant and illustrious Sumati, foremost among noble men, came out to welcome him.
21: Offering him the highest honor together with his teachers and kinsmen, he joined his palms in añjali, inquired after his well-being, and spoke to Viśvāmitra:
22: "I am blessed, I am favored, muni, that you have come to my kingdom and granted me your darśana—there is no one more fortunate than I."
