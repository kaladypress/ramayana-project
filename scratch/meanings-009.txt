1: In the center of that supreme palace complex, Hanumān, the son of Māruta, saw an immense and spacious mansion of extraordinary beauty.
2: Half a yojana wide and a full yojana long, that residence of the king of Rākṣasas was filled with numerous palace towers.
3: Searching everywhere for the wide-eyed Vaidehī Sītā, Hanumān, the destroyer of foes, explored in every direction.
4: Looking all around that magnificent Rākṣasa dwelling, the illustrious Hanumān reached the inner palace of the king of Rākṣasas.
5: It was surrounded by elephants with four tusks and others with three, spacious and uncrowded, guarded by warriors with weapons raised.
6: That dwelling was filled with Rāvaṇa's Rākṣasī wives and with princesses abducted by force through his valor.
7: It resembled the ocean filled with crocodiles and sea monsters, swarming with giant fish and timiṅgilas, agitated by fierce winds and inhabited by sea serpents.
8: Whatever splendor resides with Vaiśravaṇa, and whatever splendor belongs to Indra of tawny steeds, all of it dwelt permanently and without fail in Rāvaṇa's abode.
9: The prosperity found in the mansions of King Kubera, of Yama, and of Varuṇa was matched or even surpassed by the opulence here in this Rākṣasa palace.
10: In the center of that palace complex, the son of Pavana saw another exquisitely built mansion, adorned with numerous turrets and arched gateways.
11: It was the divine aerial chariot named Puṣpaka, adorned with all kinds of gems, crafted in heaven by Viśvakarmā for Brahmā.
12: Kubera had obtained it from the Grandfather through severe austerity, and the king of Rākṣasas had seized it by defeating Kubera with his might.
13: Supported by well-crafted pillars carved with figures of mythical beasts and wrought of pure gold, it seemed ablaze with splendor.
14: It was adorned on all sides with graceful roof pavilions resembling Mount Meru and Mandara, appearing to scrape the very sky.
15: Resplendent like fire and the sun, masterfully created by Viśvakarmā, it was fitted with golden stairways and lovely, elevated platforms.
16: It had latticed windows of gold and crystal, and raised daises studded with sapphires, large azure gems, and precious jewels.
17-20: Its floors shone with variegated coral, priceless jewels, and flawless pearls. Anointed with fragrant red sandalwood of molten-gold hue, it glowed like the morning sun. Adorned with various magnificent domed pavilions, the great Kapi ascended that divine Puṣpaka Vimāna. Standing there, he inhaled the rich, heavenly fragrance of foods, drinks, and roasted delicacies, thick as if the wind itself had taken solid form.
21: That aroma seemed to beckon the mighty hero like a dear kinsman calling out, "Come this way," drawing him toward the chamber where Rāvaṇa was.
22: Moving in that direction, he saw an immense and splendid hall, dear to Rāvaṇa's heart like a beloved and noble lady.
23: It had staircases inlaid with jewels, latticework of gold, floors paved with crystal, and decorative reliefs inlaid with ivory.
24: Adorned with pearls, corals, silver, and gold, it was supported and embellished by countless jewel-encrusted pillars.
25: With its tall, straight, and evenly set pillars standing like lofty wings on all sides, the hall seemed ready to take flight into the heavens.
26: It was carpeted with an immense rug patterned with maps of the earth, spreading out like the vast earth itself with all its kingdoms and settlements.
27: Resounding with the calls of pet birds, perfumed with celestial scents, furnished with the finest couches, it was frequented by the king of Rākṣasas.
28: Veiled in the fragrant smoke of burning agaru incense, pure and white as a swan, yet variegated with offerings of flowers, it glowed with mottled brilliance.
29: It delighted the heart, enhanced the radiance of one's complexion, dispelled all sorrow, and seemed to generate divine fortune itself.
30: Maintained by Rāvaṇa, that hall nurtured all five senses with the finest of fivefold sensory pleasures, like an affectionate mother.
31: "Is this heaven? Is this the realm of the devatās? Can this be Indra's city? Or is this supreme divine accomplishment?" thought the son of Māruta.
32: He saw golden lamps burning motionless, as if deep in thought, resembling gamblers who had lost everything to sharper gamblers at dice.
33: Between the blaze of the lamps, the personal aura of Rāvaṇa, and the brilliance of jewels, he felt the whole hall was blazing with light.
34: Then he saw thousands of beautiful women resting on the carpet, wearing colorful garments and garlands, adorned in diverse styles of dress.
35: As midnight passed, overcome by wine and exhaustion after reveling in the night, they had ceased their play and fallen into deep sleep.
36: That sleeping assembly, with all ornaments fallen silent, looked like a vast lotus pool where swans and bees had fallen quiet.
37: The son of Māruta saw the faces of those beautiful women, with their eyes closed, their teeth covered by their lips, smelling sweet as lotuses.
38: Their faces looked like lotuses that had bloomed at day's close and then folded their petals again for the night.
39: "Surely intoxicated bees seek out these lotus-faces again and again, mistaking them for blooming water lilies,"
40: reasoned the illustrious great Kapi, for he judged those faces to match water-born lotuses in every pleasant quality.
41: That hall shone resplendent with those women, like a clear autumn sky adorned with constellations of stars.
42: Surrounded by them, the king of Rākṣasas shone like the glorious moon encircled by stars.
43: Then the Hari thought: "These must be all the stars that fall from the sky when their merit is exhausted, gathered together here in one place."
44: The radiance, color, and serene grace of those women shone clearly like the bright beams of great stars.
45: With heavy, thick garlands displaced and fine jewelry scattered during their drinking and revelry, their senses had been stolen by sleep.
46: Some noble women had their forehead tilakas smudged, some had their anklets slipped out of place, and some had their necklaces fallen to their sides.
47: Some had their faces covered by pearl necklaces, some had their clothes slipped off, and some had their girdle strings broken, looking like young mares exhausted after a long ride.
48: Others wearing beautiful earrings had their garlands crushed and broken, looking like blooming creepers trampled by a lordly elephant in a vast forest.
49: Bright necklaces shining like moonbeams lay upon the breasts of some women, looking like swans sleeping between their curves.
50: The cat's-eye chains on others looked like dark-winged kādamba birds, while the golden cords on some resembled cakravāka birds.
51: Resembling rivers filled with swans and ducks and adorned with cakravākas, those women shone with their hips looking like sandy riverbanks.
52: With their tiny girdle bells resembling ripples, their gold ornaments like large lotuses, their amorous sentiments like whirlpools, they appeared like slumbering rivers.
53: The imprints left by jewelry upon their soft forms and breasts looked just like lovely ornaments themselves.
54: The hems of the fine garments of some women, stirred by the breath from their mouths, fluttered repeatedly over their faces.
55: Fluttering over the faces of those wives of golden complexion, those lovely multicolored cloth hems shone like raised banners.
56: The radiant earrings of some beautiful women swayed gently in the soft breeze of their breath.
57: The breath from their mouths, naturally sweet and carrying the aroma of sugar-wine, gently wafted over Rāvaṇa.
58: Some of Rāvaṇa's wives, mistaking their co-wives' faces for Rāvaṇa's, kissed and smelled their lips again and again in their sleep.
59: So deeply devoted were their minds to Rāvaṇa that, lost in unconscious sleep, they acted lovingly toward their rivals.
60: Restful women lay there using their arms adorned with bracelets as pillows, nestled in their lovely garments.
61: One woman slept on another's chest, another rested on a companion's arm, one lay in another's lap, and another held a friend's arms.
62: Supported by one another's thighs, sides, waists, and backs, their bodies were entwined under the spell of wine and affection.
63: Strung together by the thread of their intertwined arms, that garland of women shone like a floral wreath threaded on a string and settled with intoxicated bees.
64: Like a grove of creepers in the month of Mādhava, blossoming and swaying in the breeze, with flowers clustered and tangled with one another,
65: their stems entwined and bees hovering between them, that forest of Rāvaṇa's women appeared like a blooming woodland stirred by the wind.
66: Even on close inspection, it was impossible to distinguish between the ornaments, features, garments, and garlands of those sleeping women.
67: While Rāvaṇa slept peacefully, the burning golden lamps seemed to gaze with unblinking eyes upon those women of diverse beauty.
68: There were daughters of royal sages, pitr̥s, Daityas, Gandharvas, and Rākṣasas, all brought under the power of his desire.
69: All those women had been carried off by Rāvaṇa in his lust for conquest; some, overcome by passion, had come to him of their own desire.
70: Not a single woman there had been taken by force without being won over by his valor and accomplishments; none loved another man, and none had belonged to another before — except that noble daughter of Janaka.
71: There was no wife of his who was of low birth, deficient in beauty, devoid of grace, unskilled in serving him, lacking in spirit, or undesirable to her lover.
72: The thought arose in the noble mind of the Hari chief: "If Rāghava's lawful wife were safe and happy like these wives of the Rākṣasa king, all would be well with him."
73: Then, sorrowful at heart, he reflected: "Sītā is surely superior to all of them in virtue; yet this powerful king of Laṅkā has committed a vile and ignoble crime against her."
