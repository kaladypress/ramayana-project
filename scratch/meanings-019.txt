1: Hearing those vast and wondrous words of the king, the radiant Viśvāmitra was thrilled, his hair standing on end, and he replied.
2: Mahārāja, what you have said is worthy of you, and you will find nothing like it anywhere else on earth. You were born in a great lineage, and Vasiṣṭha is your guide.
3: Now hear the matter that is in my heart, and decide to do what I ask. Mahārāja, stay true to your word.
4: Best of men, I am keeping a sacred observance to reach its fulfilment. But two Rākṣasas who can take any form are obstructing it.
5: Many times my vow has been carried out, and each time it was about to be completed, these two Rākṣasas, Mārīca and Subāhu, powerful and well trained, came to ruin it.
6: When my vow was thrown into disorder like this, they poured a flood of flesh and blood over that altar.
7: I had worked hard and lost heart, so I left that place. King, I cannot bring myself to let my anger loose, because the observance is of such a kind that no curse can be released during it.
8: Mahārāja, give me your own son Rāma, whose valor never fails.
9: You should give me your eldest, the brave boy with the crow-wing locks. He is capable, and I will keep him safe with my own divine power.
10: I will surely grant him great and many-sided good in destroying those Rākṣasas who cause the harm. There is no doubt of it.
11: Through this he will gain fame in all three worlds. And once those two come face to face with Rāma, they cannot hold their ground in any way.
12: No man other than Rāghava has the strength to kill them. Those two sinners, swollen with pride in their strength, have fallen under the noose of Death.
13: Mahārāja, they are no match for the great-souled Rāma. And you should not let affection for your son hold you back.
14: I promise you, consider those two Rākṣasas already dead. I know the great-souled Rāma, whose valor is true. So does the radiant Vasiṣṭha, and so do these ascetics who live in tapas.
15: Great king, if you want lasting merit and the highest fame on earth, you should give Rāma to me.
16: Kākutstha, if your ministers, with Vasiṣṭha at their head, give their permission, then send Rāma with me.
17: You should give me your son, the lotus-eyed Rāma, willingly and without attachment, for the ten nights of the yajña.
18: Rāghava, do whatever you need to so that the time of my yajña does not slip past. May all go well with you, and do not let your mind sink into grief.
19: After speaking these words, full of dharma and good purpose, the radiant and righteous great sage Viśvāmitra fell silent.
20: Hearing Viśvāmitra's auspicious words, the king was overcome with deep sorrow and sank down, gripped by fear.
21: Hearing the sage's words, which tore at his heart and mind, the king was seized by great fear. Deeply shaken, he stirred from his seat.
