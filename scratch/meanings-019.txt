1: At that very time, the blameless princess Vaidehī saw Rāvaṇa, the king of Rākṣasas, who was endowed with youth and radiant form and adorned with the finest ornaments.
2: Merely upon seeing him, that lady of beautiful waist trembled like a plantain tree shaken in a violent gale.
3: Covering her belly with her thighs and shielding her breasts with her arms, that wide-eyed lady of fair complexion sat weeping bitterly.
4: The ten-headed Daśagrīva beheld Vaidehī guarded by hordes of Rākṣasīs, forlorn and overwhelmed by grief, like a shattered ship sinking in the ocean.
5: Seated upon the bare, unsheltered earth and resolute in her sacred vows, she looked like a severed branch of a mighty tree fallen to the ground.
6: Her form was smeared with dirt instead of cosmetics; worthy of noble adornments yet unadorned, she seemed like a mud-stained lotus stalk that shines with its own grace yet appears dimmed.
7: She seemed to journey directly to the presence of the wise and self-realized Rāma, the lion among kings, drawn by the chariot of her yearning heart yoked to the swift horses of her resolve.
8: Wasting away in sorrow, weeping all alone, immersed in mournful contemplation, she saw no end to her suffering, completely dedicated to Rāma.
9: Writhing in distress like a captive serpent bride, she appeared like the constellation Rohiṇī afflicted by the baleful comet Ketu.
10: Born into an illustrious lineage of flawless conduct, righteousness, and pure tradition, she now seemed, through this cruel turn of fate, like someone subjected to a base upbringing in an unworthy clan.
11: She looked like high renown brought down by baseless slander, or like sacred learning fallen into decay through neglect of study.
12: She seemed like great fame diminished, like deep faith insulted and scorned, like a keen intellect weakened, or like cherished hope dashed.
13: She looked like a promising future destroyed, like a sovereign command thwarted, like a quarter of the sky blazing in omen during calamity, or like an offering stolen before worship.
14: She appeared like a lotus pool devastated, like an army whose champions have been slain in battle, like dazzling radiance obscured by thick darkness, or like a river dried up to a trickle.
15: She looked like a sacrificial altar desecrated, like a flame of sacred fire extinguished, or like the night of the full moon when the lunar orb is eclipsed by Rāhu.
16: She resembled a lotus lake whose leaves and blooms have been violently torn out, its water birds terrified and scattered, ravaged and churned by a wild elephant's trunk.
17: Anguished with grief for her husband, she was like a drained river sucked dry of its water; wholly neglected and unwashed, she was like a moonless night in the dark fortnight.
18: Delicate, of exquisitely formed features, accustomed to residing in palace chambers adorned with jewels, she seemed like a freshly uprooted lotus fiber scorched by the blazing heat.
19: Separated from the leader of her herd, captured and chained to a post, she was sighing heavily in deep distress like the captive mate of an elephant king.
20: Graced effortlessly with a single long dark braid, she looked like the earth fringed by a dark line of forests at the departure of monsoon clouds.
21: Emaciated and worn thin by fasting, grief, anxious thoughts, and terror, partaking of little food, that lady of ascetic discipline sat in misery.
22: Stricken with sorrow, she prayed with folded hands as if imploring the devatās with all her heart for the total defeat of Daśagrīva by the foremost of the Raghus.
23: As that blameless Maithilī wept, looking about with wide eyes graced by lovely lashes, clear white and reddish at the corners, and devoted exclusively to Rāma, Rāvaṇa began to entice her, courting his own destruction.
