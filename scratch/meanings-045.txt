1: Having heard Viśvāmitra's words, Rāghava, along with Lakṣmaṇa, was filled with supreme wonder and then spoke to Viśvāmitra:
2: "Most wondrous is this supreme account you have narrated, revered sage—the sacred descent of Gaṅgā and the filling of the ocean.
3: "All that night passed for me, together with Saumitri, as I reflected on Viśvāmitra's auspicious story."
4: Then, in the clear morning, Rāghava, subduer of foes, spoke these words to Viśvāmitra, rich in tapas, who had completed his morning rites:
5: "The blessed night has passed, and we have heard what is supreme to hear. This night seemed to pass like a mere moment for the two of us, scorcher of foes, as we pondered your entire story in all its detail.
6: "Let us now cross this foremost of rivers, the sacred Tripathagā.
7: "Here is a boat, comfortably spread with seats, belonging to the r̥ṣis of virtuous deeds—knowing that your revered self has arrived here, it has come quickly."
8: Hearing those words of the noble-souled Rāghava, Kauśika arranged for the crossing along with the assembly of r̥ṣis.
9: Reaching the northern bank and honoring the host of r̥ṣis, they paused on the bank of Gaṅgā and saw the city of Viśālā.
10: Then that foremost of munis, accompanied by Rāghava, went swiftly to the lovely, divine city of Viśālā, which rivaled svarga itself.
11: Then the deeply wise Rāma, joining his palms in reverence, asked the great muni Viśvāmitra about the noble city of Viśālā:
12: "Which royal dynasty rules here in Viśālā, great muni? I wish to hear about it—may all be well with you, for my curiosity is great."
13: Hearing those words of Rāma, that foremost among munis began to narrate the ancient history of Viśāla:
14: "Listen, Rāma, to the story I heard as Śakra himself told it—hear in truth, Rāghava, what took place in this very region.
15: "Long ago in the Kr̥ta-yuga, Rāma, the sons of Diti were mighty in strength, and the sons of Aditi were greatly fortunate, valorous, and deeply righteous.
16: "Then, tiger among men, a thought arose in those noble-souled beings: 'How can we become immortal, ageless, and free from disease?'
17: "As those wise ones pondered this, the idea came to them: 'By churning the Ocean of Milk, we will obtain that vital essence there.'
18: "Having decided upon the churning, those beings of boundless energy made Vāsuki the churning rope and Mount Mandara the churning rod, and began to churn.
19: "Then, after a thousand years, the serpent heads serving as the rope began to spew fierce venom and bit into the rocks with their fangs.
20: "From this, the great poison Hālāhala, blazing like fire, surged forth, and by it the entire world—with its devatās, asuras, and humans—was scorched.
21: "Then the devatās, seeking refuge, went to Mahādeva Śaṅkara, Paśupati Rudra, and praised him, crying, 'Protect us! Protect us!'
22: "When the devatās spoke thus, the supreme ruler of the devatās, the mighty Hari, bearing the conch and discus, appeared right there.
23: "With a smile, Hari spoke to Rudra, bearer of the trident: 'While the ocean is being churned by the devatās, this has emerged first.
24: "'Therefore it belongs to you, foremost of the devatās, since it came forth before all the others. Accept this poison here as your first offering, mighty one.'
25: "Having spoken thus, that foremost of the devatās vanished right there. Seeing the terror of the devatās and hearing the words of Śārṅgī,
26: "the illustrious Hara, ruler of the devatās, took up the dreadful Hālāhala poison as though it were amr̥ta itself, and after dismissing the devatās, departed.
27: "Then all the devatās and asuras resumed churning, Raghunandana, when suddenly that foremost of mountains, the churning rod, sank into Pātāla.
28: "Then the devatās, along with the Gandharvas, praised Madhusūdana: 'You are the refuge of all beings, and especially of the dwellers of heaven.
29: "'Protect us, mighty-armed one—please lift up the mountain!' Hearing this, Hr̥ṣīkeśa assumed the form of a great tortoise.
30: "Supporting the mountain on his back, Hari lay there in the ocean, while as the soul of all worlds, Keśava—Puruṣottama—pressed down the peak of the mountain with his hand and, standing among the devatās, churned.
31: "Then, after a thousand years, there first emerged a radiant personage embodying Āyurveda, named Dhanvantari, along with the resplendent Apsarās.
32: "From the churning of those waters—from the very essence of the sea—those lovely women sprang forth, foremost of men, and therefore they became known as Apsarās.
33: "Sixty crore of those resplendent Apsarās came forth, Kākutstha, while their attendants were beyond counting.
34: "None of those devatās or dānavas took them exclusively as wives, and because none claimed them, they came to be known as belonging to all in common.
35: "Next, Raghunandana, Varuṇa's illustrious daughter Vāruṇī rose up from the ocean, seeking someone to accept her.
36: "The sons of Diti did not accept Varuṇa's daughter, Rāma, whereas the sons of Aditi, hero, accepted that blameless maiden.
37: "Because they refused her, Diti's sons became known as Asuras, while Aditi's sons became known as Suras; and the Suras were thrilled and overjoyed at accepting Vāruṇī.
38: "Then Uccaiḥśravas, the noblest of steeds, and the peerless jewel Kaustubha arose, foremost of men, and likewise the supreme amr̥ta.
39: "Then for its sake, Rāma, a great destruction of both lineages ensued, as the sons of Aditi slew the sons of Diti.
40: "All the asuras united with the rākṣasas, hero, and a terrifying war broke out that bewildered all three worlds.
41: "When everything was nearing ruin, the mighty Viṣṇu assumed an enchanting illusion and swiftly carried off the amr̥ta.
42: "Those who marched against the imperishable Viṣṇu, Puruṣottama, were crushed in that battle by the all-powerful Viṣṇu.
43: "In that fierce, great war between the Daityas and the Ādityas, the heroic sons of Aditi slaughtered the sons of Diti relentlessly.
44: "Having slain Diti's sons and regained sovereignty, Purandara ruled joyfully over all the worlds, together with the assemblies of r̥ṣis and Cāraṇas."
