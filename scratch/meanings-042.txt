1: "When Sagara passed away in due time, Rāma, the people chose the deeply righteous Aṁśumān as their king.
2: "King Aṁśumān ruled as a truly great monarch, Raghunandana, and he had a noble son famed by the name Dilīpa.
3: "Entrusting the kingdom to Dilīpa, Raghunandana, Aṁśumān retired to a serene peak of Himavān and practiced fierce tapas.
4: "After spending thirty-two thousand years in that tapas-grove, the illustrious king—rich in austerity—attained svarga.
5: "Meanwhile, the radiant Dilīpa learned of his grand-uncles' destruction, and with his mind clouded by grief, he could find no way forward.
6: "'How can Gaṅgā be brought down? How can their water-rites be performed? How can I deliver them?'—he became absorbed in this worry.
7: "While he lived in dharma with a disciplined mind, constantly brooding over this, a supremely righteous son named Bhagīratha was born to him.
8: "The radiant King Dilīpa performed many sacrifices and ruled his kingdom for thirty thousand years.
9: "Yet without discovering a way to deliver his ancestors, tiger among men, the king succumbed to illness and met his appointed end.
10: "After consecrating his son Bhagīratha on the throne, that foremost among men ascended to Indraloka by the merit of his own deeds.
11: "Bhagīratha was a righteous rājarṣi, Raghunandana—radiant and longing for an heir, yet he had no children.
12: "Entrusting the kingdom to his ministers and dedicating himself to bringing Gaṅgā down, he began a long tapas at Gokarṇa, Raghunandana.
13: "Standing with his arms raised, surrounded by five fires, eating only once a month with all his senses mastered, he endured fierce tapas for thousands of years—
14: "until a thousand years and more had passed for that noble-souled king, mighty-armed Rāma, and the revered Brahmā, sovereign creator of all beings, was deeply pleased.
15: "Accompanied by the hosts of suras, Pitāmaha came to the noble-souled Bhagīratha as he stood in tapas and spoke:
16: "'Illustrious Bhagīratha, ruler of men, I am pleased with you and your intense tapas—choose a boon, steadfast one.'
17: "Standing with hands joined in añjali, the radiant, illustrious Bhagīratha replied to the grandfather of all worlds:
18: "'Revered one, if you are pleased with me and my tapas has borne fruit, let all of Sagara's sons receive the sacred waters from my hands.
19: "'Once the ashes of those noble-souled ancestors are bathed in Gaṅgā's waters, may all my forebears ascend forever to svarga.
20: "'And grant me an heir, divine Brahmā, so that our Ikṣvāku line does not die out—let this be my supreme boon.'
21: "To the king who had spoken thus, the grandfather of all worlds replied in auspicious, gentle, melodious words:
22: "'This is a noble wish, Bhagīratha, mighty mahāratha. So let it be—blessings upon you, upholder of the Ikṣvāku line!
23: "'Here is Gaṅgā, eldest daughter of Himavān. To receive and hold her descent, king, you must seek the help of Hara.
24: "'The earth cannot withstand the impact of Gaṅgā's fall, king; brave prince, I see no one besides the trident-bearing Śiva who can hold her.'"
25: "Having spoken thus to the king and addressed Gaṅgā as well, the creator of the worlds returned to heaven together with all the hosts of Maruts.
