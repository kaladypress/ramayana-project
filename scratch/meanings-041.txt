1: "Realizing that his sons had been gone for a long time, Raghunandana, King Sagara spoke to his grandson, who shone with his own tejas:
2: "'You are brave, accomplished in learning, and equal to your ancestors in tejas. Go search for what became of your uncles, and for the one who stole the horse.
3: "'Mighty and powerful creatures live beneath the earth; take your bow and sword to fend them off.
4: "'Pay respect to those who deserve reverence, strike down any who obstruct you, and return successful—bringing my sacrifice to completion.'"
5: "Thus instructed by the noble-souled Sagara, Aṁśumān took up his bow and sword and set out with swift strides.
6: "Spurred on by the king, foremost among men, he entered the underground passage dug by his noble-souled uncles.
7: "There, the radiant prince saw the quarter-elephant being worshipped by Daityas, Dānavas, Rākṣasas, Piśācas, birds, and serpents.
8: "Walking clockwise around him and asking after his well-being, Aṁśumān inquired about his uncles and the thief who had taken the horse.
9: "Hearing his question, the quarter-elephant spoke warmly to Aṁśumān: 'Son of Asamañja, you will achieve your goal and soon return with the horse.'
10: "Hearing those words, he proceeded in due order and with proper courtesy to question all the other quarter-elephants as well.
11: "Honored by all those eloquent, wise guardians of the quarters, he was cheered on with the words, 'You will certainly return with the horse.'
12: "Hearing their assurance, he hastened with swift strides to the spot where his uncles, the sons of Sagara, lay reduced to heaps of ash.
13: "Overcome by grief at their destruction, the son of Asamañja broke down and wept aloud in deep anguish.
14: "Even in the midst of his sorrow and grief, that tiger among men spotted the sacrificial horse grazing not far away.
15: "Wishing to offer the funeral water-rites for his uncles, the radiant prince searched for water, but saw no stream or pool anywhere.
16: "Casting a keen gaze all around, he then saw Suparṇa, king of birds—swift as the wind and maternal uncle to his fathers' brothers, Rāma.
17: "The mighty Vainateya spoke to him: 'Do not grieve, tiger among men—their death was for the good of all the worlds.
18: "'These mighty princes were consumed by the immeasurable Kapila; wise prince, you must not offer them ordinary earthly water.
19: "'Gaṅgā, the eldest daughter of Himavān and purifier of the worlds, foremost among men—she alone can purify these ashes.
20: "'When these ashes are bathed by Gaṅgā, beloved of all worlds, she will bear the sixty thousand sons straight to svarga.
21: "'Now take the horse and go, illustrious prince, foremost among men—complete your grandfather's sacrifice.'"
22: "Hearing Suparṇa's words, the immensely powerful and famous Aṁśumān swiftly took the horse and returned.
23: "Approaching the consecrated king, Raghunandana, he reported everything just as it had happened, along with what Suparṇa had told him.
24: "Hearing Aṁśumān's dreadful report, the king nonetheless completed the sacrifice in strict accordance with every rule and sacred ordinance.
25: "His sacrifice fulfilled, the illustrious monarch returned to his capital, yet he could find no way to bring Gaṅgā down to earth.
26: "Unable to find a way even after a long passage of time, the great king ruled for thirty thousand years and then ascended to heaven.
