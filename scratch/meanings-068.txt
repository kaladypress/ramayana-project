1: Tiger among men, out of her deep affection and love for you, the devī spoke to me again with great emotion, showing me great respect:
2: "You must speak to Rāma, son of Daśaratha, in every possible way, so that he may kill Rāvaṇa in battle and rescue me quickly.
3: "Or if you think it wise, hero, tamer of enemies, stay here for just one day in some secluded spot. After you have rested, you can leave tomorrow.
4: "Vānara, for an unfortunate woman like me, having you near would bring even a moment's relief from this overwhelming grief.
5: "For once you, heroic one, leave to return, there is no doubt that my very life will be in peril.
6: "The sorrow of not seeing you will torment me even further, plunged as I am from sorrow into deeper sorrow, helpless and miserable.
7: "Hero, a very grave doubt stands before my mind regarding your companions, the Haris and the R̥kṣas:
8: "How will those armies of Haris and R̥kṣas, or those two sons of the foremost of men, cross this vast ocean that is so difficult to bridge?
9: "Faultless one, only three beings have the power to cross this ocean: Vainateya, the wind, or you.
10: "Therefore, hero, foremost among those skilled in action, when this task is so hard to accomplish, what solution do you see? Tell me!
11: "Slayer of enemy heroes, you alone are certainly enough to accomplish this mission; displaying your strength will bring you great renown.
12: "Yet if Rāma kills Rāvaṇa in battle with his entire army, and takes me back to his city in triumph, that will bring him true glory.
13: "Just as that Rākṣasa stole me away from that hero from the forest through deceit out of sheer fear of him, it is not fitting for Rāghava to act in that way.
14: "If Kākutstha, crusher of enemy armies, overruns Laṅkā with his troops and leads me away, that will be truly worthy of him.
15: "Therefore, arrange matters in such a way that it befits the prowess of that great soul, a warrior renowned in battle."
16: Hearing those meaningful, humble, and well-reasoned words of hers, I gave her the rest of my reply:
17: "Devī, Sugrīva, the master of the armies of Haris and R̥kṣas and foremost among all who leap, is endowed with immense courage and is firmly resolved on your rescue.
18: "Under his command stand Haris of mighty prowess, courageous and immensely strong, who can fly with the speed of thought.
19: "Their course cannot be blocked above, below, or sideways; beings of boundless energy, they never falter in monumental tasks.
20: "Following the pathway of the wind, those illustrious and mighty Vānaras have circled the entire earth again and again.
21: "In Sugrīva's presence there are forest-dwellers superior to me and equal to me; there is none there who is inferior to me!
22: "If someone like me could reach here, what of those mighty warriors? For the foremost are never sent on reconnaissance missions — only lesser ones are sent!
23: "Therefore, devī, enough of grieving! Cast away your despair. In a single leap, those Hari troop leaders will reach Laṅkā.
24: "And riding upon my back like the rising sun and moon, those two lions among men will come to you, blessed lady!
25: "You will soon see Rāghava, slayer of foes, lion-like, together with Lakṣmaṇa, bow in hand, standing right at the gates of Laṅkā.
26: "You will soon see the gathered Vānara heroes — armed with nails and fangs, possessing the prowess of lions and tigers, majestic like kings of Vānaras.
27: "Soon you will hear the roar of the Kapi chiefs, resembling mountains and clouds, bellowing upon the ridges of Mount Malaya in Laṅkā.
28: "And having completed his exile in the forest, you will soon see Rāghava, tamer of enemies, consecrated as king in Ayodhyā together with you."
29: Comforted thus by my auspicious and reassuring words, that noble-spoken daughter of Mithilā found peace of mind, deeply tormented though she was by grief in separation from you.
