1: Addressed thus by the great-souled Rāghava, Hanumān reported to Rāghava everything Sītā had said:
2: "Foremost of men, Devī Jānakī spoke these words, recalling in full truth the past token of recognition that took place on Citrakūṭa:
3: 'Having slept peacefully with you, Jānakī woke up first. Suddenly a crow flew down and clawed at her breast.
4: In turn you fell asleep, elder brother of Bharata, resting in the devī's lap. Then that bird came back and began tormenting the devī again.
5: Returning once more, he tore into her flesh viciously. You were awakened then, sprinkled by drops of her blood.
6: Scorcher of foes, sleeping so peacefully, you were awakened by the devī as she was relentlessly harassed by that crow.
7: Mighty-armed hero, seeing her torn at the breast, you hissed in fury like a venomous serpent and said:
8: "Gentle lady, who has ripped your breast with sharp claws? Who is trifling with an enraged five-hooded serpent?"
9: Glancing around, you suddenly caught sight of the crow standing right in front of me, its sharp claws stained with blood.
10: That crow, foremost among winged creatures, was none other than the son of Śakra, capable of darting through the earth and swift as the wind.
11: Mighty-armed prince, best among the wise, your eyes rolling in wrath, you resolved on a dreadful punishment for that crow.
12: You pulled a blade of darbha grass from your mat and charged it with the Brahmāstra. Blazing like the fire of cosmic dissolution, it flared toward the bird.
13: You hurled that blazing grass blade at the crow, and the burning darbha pursued the bird relentlessly.
14: Abandoned by his father, by all the gods, and by the great r̥ṣis, he traversed all three worlds but found no protector anywhere.
15: When he fell to the ground seeking refuge, you, the refuge of all, spared his life out of mercy, Kākutstha, even though he deserved death.
16: Knowing that the divine weapon could never go in vain, Rāghava, you had it strike the crow's right eye.
17: Bowing low to you and to King Daśaratha, the crow was released and flew back into the sky to his home.
18: Rāghava, you are foremost among masters of weapons, powerful and righteous — why then do you not unleash your weapons upon the Rākṣasas?
19: Neither Nāgas, nor Gandharvas, nor Asuras, nor the hosts of the Maruts are able to stand before you and counter you in battle, Rāma!
20: If you, full of valor, still have any tender regard for me, let Rāvaṇa be slain swiftly in battle with your sharp arrows!
21: Or Lakṣmaṇa, the scorcher of foes, acting on his brother's command — why does that best of men, born of Raghu, not rescue me?
22: Those two tigers among men are capable and match wind and fire in brilliance, unassailable even by the gods — why do they ignore me?
23: Surely I must have committed some grave past sin, for both those scorchers of foes, powerful though they are, do not look after me.'
24: Hearing these piteous words spoken in tears by Vaidehī, I spoke once more to that noble lady:
25: 'Devī, I swear to you in truth: Rāma has turned his back on all joys in his grief for you; and with Rāma overwhelmed by sorrow, Lakṣmaṇa grieves bitterly too.
26: Now that you have been found after great effort, this is no time to despair; beautiful lady, you will soon see the end of all your sorrows!
27: Those two princes, tigers among men and tamers of enemies, fueled by the eagerness to see you, will burn Laṅkā to ashes.
28: Slaying the ferocious Rāvaṇa in battle along with all his kinsmen, the mighty-armed Rāghava will surely escort you back to his own city.
29: Blameless lady, please give me a token of recognition that Rāma will recognize, something that will bring joy to his heart.'
30: Mighty hero, glancing in all directions, she untied this splendid jewel from her upper garment — once worn in her braid — and handed it to me.
31: Best of Raghus, receiving that divine jewel for your sake, I bowed my head to her in reverence and hastened to depart.
32: Seeing me expand my body, eager to set off on the return leap, the radiant daughter of Janaka spoke to me,
33: her face bathed in tears, brokenhearted, her voice choked with sobs.
34: Alarmed at my impending leap and overcome by a fresh surge of grief, Sītā said to me, 'Great Kapi, you are fortunate indeed,
35-36: for you will see the mighty-armed, lotus-eyed Rāma, and my glorious brother-in-law, the mighty-armed Lakṣmaṇa.' When Sītā said this to me, I replied to Maithilī, 'Devī, joy of Janaka, climb onto my back at once!
37: Noble, dark-eyed lady, today itself I will bring you before Sugrīva, Lakṣmaṇa, and your husband Rāghava.'
38: Then the devī said to me, 'Great Kapi, bull among Haris, it is against dharma for me to mount your back of my own free will.
39: Hero, when the Rākṣasa touched my body earlier, what could I do, helpless as I was and crushed by circumstance?
40: Go, tiger among Kapis, to where those two sons of the king are.' Speaking thus to me, she proceeded to give me another message:
41: 'Hanumān, greet both lion-like brothers, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, and Sugrīva with his ministers, wishing them all good health.
42: And you must present matters in such a way that the mighty-armed Rāghava rescues me from this ocean of misery.
43: When you reach Rāma's side, tell him of the intense flood of my grief and the constant threats from these Rākṣasas. May your journey be blessed and safe, heroic Hari!'
44: Lion among kings, your noble Sītā spoke these sorrowful words. Hearing what I have recounted, you may rest assured that Sītā is entirely safe and unharmed."
