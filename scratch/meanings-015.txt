1: Perched there, searching for Maithilī and surveying the ground below, he looked all around that entire grove.
2: It was adorned with santānaka trees and climbing creepers, blessed with celestial fragrances and juices, and richly decorated on all sides.
3: Resembling the Nandana garden of Indra, it was filled with deer and birds, crowded with mansions and palaces, and echoing with the sweet calls of cuckoos.
4: It was beautified by ponds adorned with golden water lilies and lotuses, furnished with numerous seats and embroidered rugs, and featured many underground chambers.
5: With delightful trees bearing flowers and fruits of every season, and glowing with the beauty of blooming aśoka trees, it shone like the rising sun.
6: Stationed there, Māruti gazed at the grove, which appeared ablaze as the branches were repeatedly stripped of leaves by swarms of settling birds.
7: As hundreds of birds darted away carrying colorful flower ornaments in their beaks, the sorrow-dispelling aśoka trees blossomed with clusters of flowers from their very roots.
8: The blooming karṇikāra trees, heavily laden with flowers, seemed almost to touch the earth, alongside the brightly blossoming kiṁśuka trees.
9: That entire region seemed ablaze with their brilliance; there stood punnāga, saptaparṇa, campaka, and uddālaka trees,
10: with sturdy, deep roots, all flowering splendidly; some aśoka trees shone like gold, others like flames of fire,
11: and some of the thousands of aśoka trees there were dark like blue collyrium, making the varied grove look wondrous like Indra's Nandana or Kubera's Caitraratha garden.
12: Surpassing all imagination, celestial, delightful, and bathed in splendor, it looked like a second sky studded with constellations of starry blossoms.
13: Wondrous with hundreds of floral jewels, it appeared like a fifth ocean, filled with trees of every season bearing sweet honey scents.
14: Echoing with varied sounds of herds of deer and flocks of birds, wafting multiple pure and enchanting aromas, that garden was laden with scents like a second Mount Gandhamādana, the king of mountains.
15: In that Aśoka grove, the foremost among Vānaras saw a towering Caitya palace standing not far away.
16: Supported in the center by a thousand pillars, it gleamed white like Mount Kailāśa, with stairs crafted of coral and platforms of molten gold.
17: Dazzling the eyes and blazing with beauty, its pure, soaring height seemed to scrape the sky.
18: Then he saw a woman clad in soiled clothes, surrounded by Rākṣasīs,
19: emaciated from fasting, forlorn, sighing repeatedly, looking like the pure crescent moon at the start of the bright fortnight.
20: Though her radiant charm was faintly discernable beneath her faded appearance, she looked like a tongue of fire veiled in dense smoke.
21: Wrapped in a single, worn, yet noble yellow silk garment, stained with dust and devoid of adornments, she was like a lotus pool stripped of its blossoms.
22: Bashful, consumed by grief, withered and ascetic in demeanor, she appeared like the constellation Rohiṇī eclipsed by the planet Mars.
23: With her face bathed in tears, she was miserable and wasted by abstaining from food, immersed in sorrowful contemplation, forever resigned to grief.
24: Unable to see her loved ones and looking only upon the band of Rākṣasīs, she resembled a doe separated from her herd and surrounded by a pack of hounds.
25: With her hair gathered in a single dark braid reaching her hips like a black serpent, she looked like the earth flanked by a dark line of forests at the end of the monsoon.
26: Worthy of every comfort yet scorched by anguish, completely unaccustomed to calamity, that wide-eyed lady sat deeply soiled and emaciated.
27: Seeing her, he deduced by sound reasoning that this must be Sītā; for when she was carried away by that form-shifting Rākṣasa,
28: she had appeared of this very likeness. With a face like the full moon, lovely brows, and well-rounded breasts,
29: that devī dispelled darkness from every quarter with her brilliance. With dark hair, lips red like the bimba fruit, a slender waist, and a noble bearing,
30: with eyes like lotus petals, she looked like Rati to Manmatha, beloved by the entire world like the radiance of the full moon.
31: That lady of exquisite form sat upon the bare ground like an austere ascetic; sighing heavily and trembling with fear, she looked like the captive bride of a serpent king.
32: Her radiance was dimmed by the vast web of sorrow spread around her, like a flame of fire wrapped in dense coils of smoke.
33: She was like a traditional memory grown doubtful, like fallen prosperity, like shattered faith, or like dashed hope.
34: She seemed like success marred by obstacles, like an intellect clouded with confusion, or like high renown tarnished by unfounded slander.
35: Grieving at being kept from Rāma, worn down by her abduction by the Rākṣasa, that helpless woman with eyes like a young fawn glanced fearfully this way and that.
36: Her face was clouded with grief and her dark eyelashes were drenched in tears as she sighed again and again.
37: Covered in dust and grime, forlorn, worthy of royal ornaments yet unadorned, she looked like the radiance of the moon veiled by dark storm clouds.
38: Looking at her repeatedly, Hanumān's mind was clouded with doubt, as sacred learning decays when unpracticed through neglect of tradition.
39: Hanumān recognized the unadorned Sītā only with great difficulty, just as speech lacking grammatical refinement loses its clear meaning.
40: Gazing at that wide-eyed, faultless princess, he reasoned that she was indeed Sītā, establishing it through clear evidence.
41: He observed on her the very suite of ornaments enhancing her form that Rāma had spoken of when describing Vaidehī.
42: He saw her finely crafted earrings, the well-fitted ear-ornaments shaped like wild dog fangs, and the wondrous jewelry studded with gems and coral upon her arms and wrists.
43: Though darkened and tarnished from being worn constantly for so long, they still held their distinct designs; he thought, "These are surely the very ornaments that Rāma described.
44: Those which she dropped, I do not see here; but those which she did not cast off are undoubtedly these before me.
45: That auspicious, shining yellow upper garment like molten gold, which had slipped down and caught on the mountain tree, was seen that day by the Vānaras.
46: And the precious, resonant ornaments that were seen fallen upon the earth were certainly cast away by her as well.
47: This garment she wears, though much worn and frayed from long use, is indeed of the same hue and splendor as that other piece.
48: This lady of golden complexion is the beloved queen of Rāma; though lost to his sight, this chaste woman has never vanished from his heart.
49: This is she for whose sake Rāma is tormented by four feelings: compassion, tenderness, grief, and longing love.
50: Compassion because a woman is lost, tenderness because she depended entirely on him, grief because his wife is gone, and love because she is his cherished beloved.
51: Judging by this devī's beauty and the exquisite perfection of her every feature, and considering Rāma's noble form, this dark-eyed lady is perfectly matched to him."
52: This devī's mind is anchored wholly in him, and his in her; it is because of this that she and that righteous soul continue to live even for a moment.
53: Rāma, that capable master, has accomplished what is nearly impossible: deprived of her, he still sustains his body and does not sink beneath despair.
54: Mighty-armed Rāma performs an impossible feat in surviving even for a single moment without Sītā, this enchanting lady.
55: Having thus seen Sītā, the son of Pavana rejoiced in his heart; he turned his thoughts to Rāma and praised that noble master.
