1: The wise sage Ṛśyaśṛṅga, who knew the Vedas, meditated for a little while. When he came out of his meditation, he spoke these excellent words to the king:
2: "I will perform the Putrakāmeṣṭi rite for you for the sake of sons. It will be carried out according to the prescribed rules, with the mantras taught in the Atharvaśiras, and it will surely bear fruit."
3: Then the radiant sage began that Putrakāmeṣṭi rite for the sake of sons. He offered oblations into the sacred fire, following the rites revealed in the mantras.
4: Then the devatās, along with the Gandharvas, the Siddhas, and the great ṛṣis, gathered there in the prescribed manner to accept their offerings.
5: Taking their assigned places in that sacrificial assembly, the devatās addressed these momentous words to Brahmā, the creator of the worlds:
6: "Bhagavān, empowered by the grace you bestowed on him, a Rākṣasa named Rāvaṇa is oppressing all of us with his might, and we are unable to restrain him.
7: Long ago, Bhagavān, you were pleased with him and gave him a boon. Out of respect for that boon, we always put up with everything he does.
8: That wicked-minded one terrorizes the three worlds and hates anyone who rises high. He even wants to overpower Śakra, the king of the devatās.
9: Unassailable and blinded by the boon, he tramples over ṛṣis, Yakṣas, Gandharvas, Asuras, and Brāhmaṇas alike.
10: The sun reduces his burning heat when shining on him, and the wind does not blow harshly near him. Even the ocean, garlanded with rolling waves, stills its waters when it sees him.
11: So we live in great fear of that terrible-looking Rākṣasa. Bhagavān, please find a way to kill him."
12: When all the devatās spoke to him like this, Brahmā reflected for a moment and said: "Ah! The means to destroy that evil-minded one has occurred to me.
13: He asked me, 'Let me be unkillable by Gandharvas, Yakṣas, Devas, Dānavas, and Rākṣasas,' and I said, 'So be it.'
14: Out of contempt, that Rākṣasa did not mention humans at the time. So he can be killed by a human. There is no other death for him."
15: When they heard these welcome words spoken by Brahmā, all the devatās and great ṛṣis were overjoyed.
16: Just then, Śrī Mahāviṣṇu, the protector of the worlds, arrived there glowing with immense radiance, holding the conch, discus, and mace in his hands, and dressed in yellow silks.
17: Meeting with Brahmā, he stood there composed. Then all the devatās bowed low in reverence, praised him with hymns, and said:
18: "Śrī Mahāviṣṇu, for the welfare of the worlds, we pray that you undertake a mission. O all-pervading one, to King Daśaratha, ruler of Ayodhyā—
19: who knows dharma, is generous, and equals the great ṛṣis in radiance—through his three wives, who resemble Hrī, Śrī, and Kīrti—modesty, prosperity, and fame—
20: Viṣṇu, manifest yourself in four forms and be born as his sons! Taking human form there, destroy in battle Rāvaṇa—that overgrown thorn to the worlds, whom the devatās cannot kill."
21: That foolish Rākṣasa Rāvaṇa, drunk on his own strength, torments the devatās, the Gandharvas, the Siddhas, and the best of the ṛṣis.
22: That savage one has struck down ṛṣis, and attacked Gandharvas and Apsarases wandering at ease in the Nandana gardens.
23: We have come here with the munis to bring about his death. The Siddhas, Gandharvas, and Yakṣas have also come to you for refuge.
24: Deva, scorcher of enemies, you are the supreme refuge for all of us. Set your mind on being born in the world of humans to destroy the enemies of the devatās."
25: When they had spoken to him like this, Viṣṇu, the ruler of all devatās and foremost among the thirty-three crore devatās,
26: honored by all the worlds, spoke to all the devatās gathered there, led by Pitāmaha Brahmā and devoted to dharma:
27: "Let go of your fear. May all be well with you. For your good, I will kill Rāvaṇa in battle—cruel, hard to overcome, the terror of devatās and ṛṣis—along with his sons and grandsons, his ministers, his friends, his kinsmen, and his relatives.
28: Then for ten thousand years, and ten hundred years more, I will live in the human world and protect this earth."
29: Having given this boon to the devatās, the self-possessed god Viṣṇu considered where among humans he would take birth.
30: Then the lotus-eyed one, whose eyes were like lotus petals, divided himself into four and chose King Daśaratha as his father.
31: Then the devarṣis, the Gandharvas, the Rudras, and the hosts of Apsarases praised Madhusūdana with divine hymns:
32: "Rāvaṇa is arrogant and fierce in his power, swollen with pride, and an enemy of Indra, king of the devatās. He makes the worlds cry out. He is a thorn to the good and to ascetics, and a terror to tapasvins. Root him out.
33: Kill that Rāvaṇa of fierce valor, who makes the worlds cry out, along with his army and his kin. Then, free of all anguish, return for a long time to your heavenly realm, guarded by Indra and free of every fault and stain."
