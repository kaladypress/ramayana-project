1: Shaking off that mistaken thought, the great Kapi regained his composure and turned his mind to another line of reasoning concerning Sītā.
2: "Separated from Rāma, that noble lady could never sleep like this; she would not eat, nor adorn herself, nor partake of wine.
3: Nor could she ever attend upon another man, even if he were the ruler of the gods — for among all the devatās, there is no one equal to Rāma."
4-5: Concluding that this woman was someone else, the best of Haris, eager to find Sītā, moved about the banquet hall. There he saw women worn out from sporting, singing, dancing, and drinking.
6: Some women were leaning upon their muraja and mr̥daṅga drums, some rested against stools, while others lay stretched out upon luxurious rugs.
7: The Hari troop leader saw a thousand women adorned with fine jewelry, gifted in beauty and charming conversation, skilled in singing meaningful songs,
8: speaking fitting words suited to time and place, now fast asleep after their passionate revels.
9: In another section, he saw thousands of other lovely young women, graceful in appearance and speech, sleeping soundly.
10: In their midst, the mighty-armed king of Rākṣasas shone like a bull amidst a herd of choice cows in a vast cattle fold.
11: Surrounded by them, that king of Rākṣasas looked like a great wild elephant encircled by female elephants in the forest.
12: In the mansion of that king of Rākṣasas, the tiger among Kapis observed the banquet hall, provided with every object of pleasure.
13: He saw portions of venison, buffalo meat, and wild boar laid out neatly in courses in that banquet hall.
14: In wide golden vessels, the tiger among Kapis saw half-eaten peafowl and fowl.
15: Hanumān noticed dishes of wild boar and rhinoceros seasoned with curds and rock salt, as well as porcupines, deer, and peafowl.
16: He saw various roasted partridges, half-eaten cakora birds, buffalo meat roasted on skewers, and well-prepared goat meat.
17: There were various delicacies to lick, drinks to sip, and diverse foods to chew, served with tangy sour and salty sauces and spiced fruit preserves.
18: With costly necklaces, anklets, and armlets tossed aside, cups strewn about, and various fruits scattered across the floor,
19: strewn with offerings of fresh flowers and furnished here and there with well-crafted couches and seats, the floor displayed extraordinary elegance.
20: That banquet hall seemed ablaze even without fire, brilliant with meats of many kinds expertly seasoned in diverse styles.
21-22: Beside the expertly prepared meats arranged separately in the banquet hall, there were various divine, clear wines and brewed liquors — sugar wines, honey wines, flower-infused spirits, and fruit brandies — each delicately refined with fragrant aromatic powders.
23: The floor shone brightly, strewn with strands of garlands, golden pitchers, and crystal flagons.
24: Surrounded by other golden ewers, and contained in jars of silver and gold,
25: the Kapi saw abundant quantities of choice wine; he saw golden goblets and gem-encrusted flagons filled with liquor.
26: The great Kapi saw silver vessels filled to the brim — some half-empty, some drained completely dry,
27: and some drinks untouched. He wandered through, observing diverse delicacies in one spot, portions of drinks in another, and leftover foods here and there.
28: Here ewers were tipped over, there flagons were overturned, and in places spilled water and fruits were mixed with fallen garlands.
29: In many places the couches were vacant, as several lovely women slept embracing one another on the floor.
30: One woman, pulling another's garment over herself and wrapping it around her body, had fallen asleep beside her, overcome by the power of sleep.
31: Stiffened by their perfumes, the garments and garlands on their bodies barely stirred, gently fanned by the soft breeze of their breath.
32: The scent of cooling sandalwood, the sweet fragrance of wine, and the perfume of varied garlands and blooming flowers
33: were carried about in diverse ways by the breeze, blending with the aromas of bath powders, sandalwood pastes, and rich incense.
34: That delightful fragrance wafted throughout the Puṣpaka Vimāna; there lay lovely women — some fair-skinned, some dark-complexioned, and some of golden hue.
35: In that Rākṣasa palace, some women possessed golden-hued forms, languid with sleep and faint with passion.
36: They looked like sleeping lotus beds closed for the night. Thus the glorious Kapi examined every part of Rāvaṇa's inner quarters, yet he did not find Jānakī.
37: Beholding those sleeping women, the great Kapi fell into deep reflection, troubled by anxiety over dharma:
38: "Looking upon the sleeping wives of another man in their private quarters — will this not cause a serious violation of dharma?
39: Never has my gaze wandered after another's wife with lustful desire; yet here I have scrutinized another man's harem."
40: Then another thought arose in the mind of that thoughtful hero, whose intellect was resolute and single-minded in discerning duty:
41: "Granted that I have seen all these wives of Rāvaṇa sleeping without reserve, yet not the slightest impure agitation has arisen in my mind.
42: The mind alone is the prompter of all the senses in either virtuous or sinful states, and my mind is firmly anchored in purity.
43: Moreover, I could not search for Vaidehī anywhere else; when searching for a woman, she can only be looked for among other women.
44: A creature must always be sought among its own kind; a missing woman cannot be looked for among female deer.
45: With an immaculate mind I have searched through the whole of Rāvaṇa's inner quarters, yet Jānakī is nowhere to be seen."
46: Inspecting the daughters of devas, Gandharvas, and Nāgas, the valiant Hanumān still did not see Jānakī.
47: Failing to see her there while viewing those other noble women, the heroic Kapi withdrew and began to reflect deeply.
48: Summoning greater effort once more, the glorious son of Māruta left the banquet hall and set out to search for her in other places.
