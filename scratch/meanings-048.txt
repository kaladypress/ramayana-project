1: After they had met and inquired after one another's well-being, at the close of their conversation Sumati spoke to the great muni:
2: "May all be well with you! These two young princes have the valor of devatās, the stride of elephants and lions, and the bearing of tigers and bulls—both true heroes.
3: "With large eyes like lotus petals, bearing swords, quivers, and bows, they resemble the Aśvins in beauty and have just reached the threshold of youth.
4: "Muni, how is it that these two, who look like immortals come down to earth from Devaloka of their own accord, have arrived here on foot? For what purpose have they come, and whose sons are they?
5: "Gracing this land just as the moon and the sun adorn the sky, they match one another in stature, expression, and bearing.
6: "Why have these two heroes—foremost of men, bearing fine weapons—traveled along this difficult road? I wish to hear the truth of it."
7: Hearing his words, Viśvāmitra recounted everything as it had happened—their stay at Siddhāśrama and the slaying of the rākṣasas.
8: On hearing Viśvāmitra's account, the king was overjoyed to have Daśaratha's two mighty sons arrive as supreme guests and honored them with full rites, for they were worthy of every hospitality.
9: After receiving the highest hospitality from Sumati and staying there for one night, the two Rāghavas set out for Mithilā.
10: Catching sight of Janaka's auspicious city of Mithilā, all the munis praised and honored it, exclaiming, "Wonderful! Wonderful!"
11: In a grove on the outskirts of Mithilā, Rāghava saw an ancient, deserted, yet lovely āśrama and asked the foremost of munis:
12: "Revered sage, why is this place, which looks like a splendid āśrama, devoid of munis? I wish to hear whose āśrama this once was."
13: Hearing the words spoken by Rāghava, the deeply radiant great muni Viśvāmitra, eloquent in speech, replied:
14: "Rāghava, listen—I will tell you the true story of whose āśrama-site this was, and how its great-souled master cursed it in anger.
15: "Foremost of men, long ago this was the āśrama of the great-souled Gautama—divine in appearance and honored even by the suras.
16: "Illustrious prince, here in days of old he practiced tapas together with Ahalyā for many thousands of years.
17: "Watching for an opportunity when the sage was away, the thousand-eyed husband of Śacī assumed the guise of the muni and spoke these words to Ahalyā:
18: "'Devoted lady, those driven by desire do not wait for the proper season; slender-waisted one, I long to unite with you.'
19: "Raghunandana, even though she recognized the thousand-eyed Indra in the guise of the muni, in her poor judgment she made up her mind to yield, out of curiosity toward the king of the devatās.
20: "Then, her inner desire fulfilled, she said to the foremost of the suras: 'Foremost of the suras, your desire is fulfilled; leave this place quickly, mighty one!
21: "'Ruler of the devatās, giver of honor, protect yourself and me at all times!'
22: "Laughing, Indra replied to Ahalyā: 'Graceful one, I am well pleased; I will depart just as I came.'
23: "Rāma, after uniting with her in this way, he stepped out of the leaf-hut, hurrying in anxious haste out of fear of Gautama.
24: "Just then he saw the great muni Gautama entering—endowed with the power of tapas and unassailable even by devatās and dānavas.
25: "Soaked with water from the sacred bathing-place and blazing like fire, the foremost of munis was carrying samidhs and kuśa grass; seeing him, the ruler of the suras was terrified and his face turned pale.
26: "Seeing the thousand-eyed Indra standing there in the guise of a muni after his wicked deed, the righteous sage spoke to him in anger:
27: "'Fool! Because you took on my form and committed this act that should never be done, you will become emasculated!'
28: "The moment the great-souled Gautama spoke thus in fury, the testicles of the thousand-eyed Indra fell to the ground.
29: "Having cursed Śakra in that manner, he then cursed his wife as well: 'You will live here for many thousands of years,
30: "'subsisting on wind alone, without food, doing penance and lying in ashes; unseen by all beings, you will remain in this āśrama.
31: "'When Rāma, the unassailable son of Daśaratha, comes to this desolate forest, you will be purified.
32: "'Wicked woman, through offering him hospitality you will become free of greed and delusion, and filled with joy, you will regain your own form in my presence.'
33: "Having spoken thus to his erring wife, the deeply radiant, great ascetic Gautama abandoned this āśrama and practiced tapas on a lovely peak of Himavān frequented by Siddhas and Cāraṇas."
