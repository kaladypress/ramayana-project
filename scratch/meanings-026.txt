1: Hearing the sage's resolute words, Rāghava, son of the best of men and firm in his vow, joined his palms in reverence and replied:
2: "Because of my father's explicit command and out of respect for his word, whatever Kauśika says must be done without hesitation.
3: "In Ayodhyā, in the presence of my teachers, I was instructed by my great-souled father, Daśaratha, and his words must never be disregarded.
4: "Having heard my father's words, and at the command of this knower of Brahman, I will without doubt carry out this noble slaying of Tāṭakā.
5: "For the welfare of cows and brāhmaṇas, and for the good of this land, I am ready to carry out your command, whose greatness is beyond measure."
6: Having spoken thus, the subduer of enemies gripped the middle of his bow tightly and twanged the bowstring sharply, making all the directions resound with the sound.
7: Terrified by that sound, the inhabitants of Tāṭakā's forest trembled, while Tāṭakā herself grew furious and bewildered by it.
8: Focusing on that sound, the rākṣasī, blinded with rage, heard where it came from and charged furiously toward the spot from which it had arisen.
9: Seeing her—enraged, deformed, hideous-faced, and gigantic in stature—Rāghava spoke to Lakṣmaṇa:
10: "Look, Lakṣmaṇa, at the yakṣiṇī's terrifying, dreadful form! Just seeing her would shatter the hearts of the timid.
11: "Look at this formidable creature, endowed with the power of illusion (māyā). Today I will turn her back, with the tips of her ears and her nose severed.
12: "I do not wish to kill her, protected as she is by her womanhood. My intention is only to destroy her strength and her ability to move about."
13: Even as Rāma was speaking, Tāṭakā, senseless with rage, raised both her arms and, roaring, rushed straight at Rāma.
14: The brahmarṣi Viśvāmitra rebuked her with a loud roar of "huṁ!" and pronounced a blessing on the two Rāghavas: "May all be well with you, and may victory be yours!"
15: Stirring up a dreadful storm of dust, Tāṭakā bewildered both the Rāghavas for a moment with a vast cloud of dust.
16: Then, resorting to her māyā, she pelted the two Rāghavas with a massive shower of rocks. At that, Rāghava grew angry.
17: Blocking her heavy shower of rocks with a volley of arrows, Rāghava severed both hands of the charging Tāṭakā with his feathered arrows.
18: Then, seeing her with severed arms, exhausted, yet still roaring nearby, Saumitri angrily sliced off the tips of her ears and her nose.
19: Able to take any form at will, the yakṣī assumed many different shapes, vanished from sight while bewildering them with her māyā, and roamed about hurling a terrifying rain of stones.
20: Seeing the two brothers pelted from every side by that rain of stones, the illustrious son of Gādhi spoke these words:
21: "Enough of your pity, Rāma! This wicked, evil-natured yakṣī, a destroyer of sacrifices, may soon grow even stronger through her māyā.
22: "Kill her right now, before twilight sets in—for rākṣasas become invincible at twilight!"
23: Thus urged, Rāma demonstrated his skill at shooting by sound and checked that yakṣī, who was pouring down a rain of stones, with his arrows.
24: Hemmed in by that net of arrows, the yakṣī, still wielding her power of māyā, rushed at Kākutstha and Lakṣmaṇa.
25: As she came hurtling with tremendous speed, fierce as a bolt of lightning, he pierced her in the chest with an arrow; she fell and died.
26: Seeing that terrifying creature slain, the king of the devatās and all the devatās praised Kākutstha, crying, "Well done! Well done!"
27: Overjoyed, the thousand-eyed Purandara and all the delighted devatās then spoke to Viśvāmitra:
28: "Sage Kauśika, may blessings be upon you! All the hosts of the Maruts, together with Indra, are deeply pleased with this deed. Show your affection to Rāghava!
29: "Bestow upon Rāghava the sons of Prajāpati Kr̥śāśva—those beings of unfailing prowess, sustained by the power of austerity, brāhmaṇa.
30: "He is truly worthy of them, brāhmaṇa, and devoted to following you. Moreover, this prince has a mighty task to accomplish for the devatās."
31: Having spoken thus, all the devatās departed joyfully into the sky, honoring Viśvāmitra. Then twilight set in.
32: Then the foremost of sages, delighted and satisfied by the slaying of Tāṭakā, kissed Rāma affectionately on the crown of his head and spoke these words:
33: "Fair-featured Rāma, let us spend tonight right here. Tomorrow at dawn we will set out for my āśrama."
34: Hearing Viśvāmitra's words, the son of Daśaratha was pleased and spent the night happily there in Tāṭakā's forest.
35: Freed from its curse that very day, the forest shone with loveliness, like the Caitraratha grove.
36: Having slain that daughter of the yakṣa, Rāma, praised by hosts of devatās and siddhas, stayed there with the sage until he was awakened at the break of dawn.
