1: Addressed thus by Hanumān, Rāma, the son of Daśaratha, pressed the jewel to his heart and wept aloud together with Lakṣmaṇa.
2: Looking at that finest of jewels, Rāghava, wasted with sorrow, spoke to Sugrīva with eyes brimful of tears:
3: "Just as an affectionate cow lets down milk out of tender love for her calf, so my heart melts at the sight of this jewel.
4: This jewel was given to Vaidehī by my father-in-law at the time of our wedding, and it looked even more exquisite fastened upon her head.
5: Born of the sea and revered by the foremost of beings, this gem was given by the wise Śakra, immensely pleased during a sacrifice.
6: Gentle friend, seeing this finest of jewels today, I feel as if I am seeing my father, and also the illustrious king of Videha.
7: Truly this jewel shone on my beloved's head; looking upon it today, I feel as if I have her back with me.
8: Gentle friend, tell me again and again what Sītā Vaidehī said — your words are like cool water reviving a dying man!
9: Saumitri, what greater sorrow could there be than this: seeing this sea-born gem arrived here, but without Vaidehī?
10: If Vaidehī can survive for a whole month, she is living long indeed; for without that dark-eyed lady, gentle friend, I cannot live even for a moment!
11: Take me too to that place where my beloved was seen! Having received news of her, I cannot wait even for an instant.
12: How can my graceful, gentle beloved, so timid and chaste, stay there in the midst of those terrifying, dreadful Rākṣasas?
13: Surely her face, surrounded by Rākṣasas, is robbed of its brilliance, like the autumn moon obscured by dark clouds when freed from the dark fortnight.
14: Hanumān, tell me truthfully what Sītā said. By your words I will keep living, like a sick man revived by medicine.
15: Hanumān, tell me what my sweet, soft-spoken beloved said! Bereft of me, how does that graceful lady go on living? Plunged from one sorrow into a deeper sorrow, how does Jānakī survive?"
