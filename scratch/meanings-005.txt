1: Then the wise Hanumān saw the radiant moon climbed to mid-sky, spreading a vast canopy of moonlight and roaming through the heavens like an impassioned bull wandering in a pen.
2: He watched the cool-rayed moon gliding on, washing away the sins and sorrows of the world, swelling the great ocean with its tides, and illuminating all living beings.
3: That very splendor which graces Mount Mandara on earth, which glows upon the ocean at dusk, and which rests upon lotuses in the water, now blazed forth upon that lovely moon.
4: Like a royal swan resting in a cage of silver, like a proud lion in a cave of Mount Mandara, or like a valiant warrior mounted upon a spirited elephant, the moon shone high in the heavens.
5: With its crescent horns sharp and full, the moon shone like a horned bull, like a towering white mountain with lofty peaks, or like an elephant with tusks tipped in gold.
6: Cleansed of the cold dews and chill mists, freed from the grasp of eclipsing planets, the divine moon, adorned with its spotless rabbit-mark, shone as the very sanctuary of radiant splendor.
7: Like a king of beasts surveying its rocky plateau, like an elephant king entering a vast battle, or like a sovereign monarch crowned in his realm, the luminous moon shone in all its majesty.
8: With its shadows banished by the bright moonrise, the night was alive with fierce flesh-eating Rākṣasas; yet as passion stirred in lovers' hearts, that evening hour sparkled with the splendor of heaven.
9: Sweet strains of lute strings, pleasing to the ear, echoed softly as faithful wives slept beside their husbands; meanwhile, night-prowlers of astonishing and terrifying deeds set out on their nocturnal revels.
10: The wise Kapi observed noble mansions bustling with people carefree and intoxicated, packed with chariots, horses, and splendid thrones, overflowing with the martial pride of warriors.
11: In their drunkenness, the demons jostled and challenged one another, flinging their muscular arms about, babbling loudly, and hurling insults at one another.
12: The Rākṣasas puffed out their chests, threw their arms around their beloved women, assumed diverse colorful forms, and brandished their sturdy bows.
13: He saw women anointing themselves with fragrant sandal paste, others drifting back to sleep, lovely-faced ladies laughing merrily, and some sighing in petulant anger.
14: With trumpeting great elephants, well-honored noble warriors, and breathing heroes of great strength, the city shone like a deep mountain lake filled with hissing serpents.
15: In that city he saw Yātudhānas of sharp intellect, bearing pleasing names, steadfast in loyalty, honored as leaders of their people, and distinguished by various forms and titles.
16: He was pleased to see many of handsome appearance, endowed with various virtues matching their nature, glowing with noble bearing, while also seeing others of hideous and distorted form.
17: Then he saw their noble women, worthy of the finest luxuries, pure in emotion, devoted to their lovers and fond of sweet wine, gleaming with radiant beauty like constellations of stars.
18: Radiant with beauty and veiled in modesty, some were embraced by their lovers in the dead of night, while others were lost in passionate joy, like birds nestled within dense blossoms.
19: The wise Kapi saw still other women sitting on rooftop terraces, resting happily in the laps of their beloveds—devoted to virtue, cherished by their husbands, and overcome by deep affection.
20: Some were uncovered, glowing with complexions like stripes of gold; some of the highest beauty shone like molten gold; while others, deserted by their lovers, were pale as the moon's mark despite their charming forms.
21: The heroic Hari saw enchanting women in their homes, overflowing with delight upon being reunited with the cherished lovers of their hearts, gazing with deep affection upon their partners.
22: He saw rows of moon-like faces with curved eyelashes and luminous eyes, and sparkling garlands of jewelry flashing like chains of lightning.
23: But he did not find Sītā—born of an illustrious royal house established in righteousness, delicate like a flowering creeper of noble stock, and exquisite in mind and spirit.
24: He did not find her who was anchored on the eternal path of virtue, her gaze fixed solely on Rāma, consumed by love for him, dwelling perpetually in her husband's noble heart, surpassing the finest women of the world.
25: He did not see her who was distressed by grief with tears streaming down her neck, who once wore a precious necklace of gold coins; her eyelashes were graceful and her voice was melodious, like a peahen dancing in the forest.
26: She was like a faint crescent moon obscured by clouds, like a streak of pure gold covered in dust, like an arrow-wound healing over, or like a wisp of cloud scattered by the wind.
27: Failing to see Sītā, the wife of Rāma, the ruler of men and foremost of eloquent speakers, the Plavaṅgama was overcome by deep sorrow, his spirit weighed down in despondency.
